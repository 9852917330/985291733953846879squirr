Technology & Science

Doing business without meeting face to face

Causes / Effects

Prompt

Nowadays people can carry out tasks such as shopping, banking and even business transactions without meeting each other face to face. What are the effects of this on individuals and on society?

Model answer

Shopping, banking and business transactions can increasingly be completed without meeting another person face to face. This brings major practical benefits, but it can also weaken social contact and exclude people who cannot use digital services easily. In my view, society should develop these services while preserving suitable alternatives.

For individuals, convenience is the clearest advantage. A task that previously required travel and queuing may now take only a few minutes. This can be particularly useful for people with mobility difficulties, caring responsibilities or homes far from a town centre. Comparing products and prices is also easier online. However, the benefit depends on access, confidence and reliable systems. A person who cannot resolve an account problem without using an unfamiliar app may find the new process less convenient than the old one. Remote transactions also create particular fraud risks when identities or payment details are misused.

The consequences for society are similarly mixed. Digital services can reduce some operating costs and allow small firms to reach customers beyond their local area. These opportunities may support competition and new kinds of employment. At the same time, closing branches and shops can remove both jobs and useful meeting places. Workers who need different skills may require training rather than simply being expected to adapt without assistance.

The best response is not to reject online transactions, but to design a fair transition. Accessible interfaces, effective customer support and appropriate safeguards are essential. Where a service is important to everyday life, people should have a realistic way to use it even if a digital-only approach does not meet their needs.

In conclusion, less face-to-face contact can improve efficiency and widen opportunities, but the benefits are strongest when convenience is balanced with inclusion, security and continued human support.

Key words and phrases

practical benefits
Useful advantages in ordinary situations.
suitable alternatives
Other options that meet the relevant needs.
mobility difficulties
Problems with moving around or travelling.
caring responsibilities
Duties involved in looking after another person.
operating costs
The regular expenses of running a business or service.
a fair transition
A change managed so that affected groups are treated reasonably.
continued human support
Ongoing help from people rather than only automated systems.
