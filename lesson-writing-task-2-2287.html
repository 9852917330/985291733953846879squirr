Language & Communication

Must every child learn a foreign language?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Because some children do not seem to have a natural ability to learn another language, schools should not force those children to study a foreign language. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Every class contains a few students who make almost no progress in a second language despite years of lessons. I disagree that they should be excused, because the argument rests on a false view of how languages are learned.

The case for excusing them is not absurd. Time spent failing at a subject is time taken from one where the same student might succeed, and repeated failure damages confidence in a way that spreads to other lessons. Where a child struggles with literacy in their first language, adding a second can reasonably be delayed.

The reasoning nevertheless fails at its foundation. Almost every human being acquires a language successfully as a child, which shows the capacity is general rather than exceptional. What differs is not ability but the method and the amount of exposure: classroom teaching of grammar for three hours a week suits some learners and defeats many others, and a student written off as untalented frequently makes rapid progress once immersed in the language or taught differently. Judging a twelve-year-old to have no aptitude for something they have barely practised is a prediction dressed as a diagnosis.

The consequences of excusing them would also be unequal. Decisions of that kind fall disproportionately on children from poorer families, whose difficulty is usually a lack of exposure rather than of capacity, and removing them from language study closes doors they do not yet know exist, since languages affect university options and employment abroad. The wider benefits would be lost too: learning a second language improves understanding of one's own, and it teaches that other ways of describing the world are different rather than wrong.

In conclusion, no child should be released from language study on the basis of apparent talent. Where progress is poor the sensible response is to change the teaching, since the failure usually belongs to the method.

Key words and phrases

no aptitude for something they have barely practised
Mistaking little practice for lack of natural ability.
closes doors they do not yet know exist
Removes future opportunities they cannot yet recognise.
different rather than wrong
An alternative style, not necessarily an error.
the failure usually belongs to the method
The teaching approach is usually the problem.
