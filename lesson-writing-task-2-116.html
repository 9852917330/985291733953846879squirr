Government & Politics

Is it better to save than to spend?

Essay

Prompt

It is better to save money than to spend it. How far do you agree with this statement? Is saving more important than spending in today's world? Give reasons for your answer, and provide ideas and examples from your own experience. You should write at least 250 words.

Model answer

With the world in such economic turmoil, many of us face difficult choices in managing our money. Personally, I feel that saving is preferable to high spending, and I will explain why.

The clearest reason to save is that the future is unpredictable. Saving is a sensible precaution against unemployment, illness or a sudden repair bill, any of which can appear with almost no warning. A household with funds in reserve can absorb a shock like this without borrowing at a punishing rate of interest; one without any savings is often pushed onto a credit card, and a temporary setback turns into years of debt.

This habit of setting money aside also builds a discipline that spending does not. Someone who transfers a fixed share of each pay cheque, even ten per cent, into a separate account is forced to plan ahead rather than spend whatever is left at the end of the month. Treating that transfer as a fixed bill rather than an optional extra is usually enough, over several years, to turn an ordinary salary into a house deposit or a comfortable retirement fund.

The 2008 financial crisis illustrates what happens when this discipline disappears on a larger scale: banks, governments and households had all borrowed heavily against future income, and when that borrowing could no longer be rolled over, the resulting crash cost millions of people their jobs and homes. Some argue that life is short and money should be enjoyed now rather than hoarded, which is fair advice for everyday spending. Treating saving as optional, however, assumes that good times will continue indefinitely, an assumption the last two decades have repeatedly shown to be unwise.

To conclude, I believe that saving is the wiser course, not just for financial but also for personal and political reasons. Sensible saving is an invaluable skill and a buffer against uncertainty.

Key words and phrases

a sensible precaution
A reasonable action to prevent possible harm.
borrowing at a punishing rate of interest
Taking loans with extremely expensive interest.
a temporary setback turns into years of debt
A brief problem causes long-lasting financial obligations.
a buffer against uncertainty
Resources protecting against unexpected problems.
