Place

A city I would like to live in

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a city or country where you would most like to live in the future.

You should say: - where it is - what you know about it - what you would do there - and explain why you would like to live there.

Simple Model Answer

A city I would like to live in is Tokyo, the capital of Japan. What attracts me most is the way modern buildings and traditional places exist together. You might see an old shrine beside a glass tower, or a quiet street close to a busy shopping area.

If I lived there, I would spend weekends exploring different neighbourhoods. I would also take Japanese classes so I could have real conversations rather than just order food. Learning to cook ramen would be another enjoyable goal.

I like busy cities, and Tokyo seems to offer plenty to do. Its railway network would make travelling around convenient. I would enjoy looking at the food in department stores, especially the cakes and sushi. Living there would certainly bring challenges, including learning the language, but that is part of the attraction. I think Tokyo would give me a chance to experience a different way of life while discovering something new almost every day.

Key words and phrases

railway network

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to tell you about Tokyo, the city I want to live in the most in the future. It's the capital city of Japan and the most populous metropolitan area in the world.

The main reason I'd love to settle down in Tokyo is the co-existence of modern and traditional values. It's the kind of city where the red gates of a centuries-old Shinto shrine may well be found just next door to a glass skyscraper showcasing the finest 21st-century technology. Or where a quiet green lane lined with wooden low-rise houses and an old-school tofu shop might sit just a short stroll from a neon-lit square packed with flickering billboards and rainbow-bright street fashion.

If I actually lived there, I think I'd spend most weekends just wandering — a different neighbourhood each time, no real plan, just seeing where the back streets take me. I'd want to get properly good at Japanese rather than just knowing enough to order food, maybe take an evening class, and I'd definitely try to learn how to make ramen properly instead of always eating it out.

The hectic pace of life in Tokyo is also an irresistible attraction for someone like me. Tokyo's railway system seems like it was designed to win world records — it's hard to find a location in the metropolitan area that can't be reached with a train ride and a short walk. And you could easily spend hours drooling over the elaborate pastries and picture-perfect sushi rolls in the basement of any department store.

I feel like the city's always moving towards the future rather than looking back, and that's exactly the kind of energy I want around me every day. I honestly believe Tokyo would never stop surprising me.
