A crowded place

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a crowded place

You should say: Where it was When you went there What you did there And explain how you felt about the crowded place.

Simple Model Answer

A crowded place I remember is Ba Den Pagoda in Tay Ninh province. My family visited during the Tet holiday last year, while we were staying with my grandfather. We took a cable car up Ba Den Mountain to reach it.

I expected plenty of visitors, but the crowd was larger than I imagined. People were carrying flowers and candles, waiting to light incense and praying inside. The room was so full that moving around was difficult. My mother and I eventually reached a place where we could light incense and say a short prayer. My grandfather stayed outside because the smoke and crowded space made him uncomfortable.

By the time we returned to the car, I felt tired. I understood why people wanted to visit a religious place and wish for a good year, but I found the experience stressful. I would like to see the pagoda again on a quieter day. That would give me more time to enjoy the surroundings without feeling pushed by people on every side.

Key words and phrases

light incense
Set a scented stick burning, often as part of a religious practice.
on every side
All around someone or something.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a trip to Ba Den Pagoda that I still remember vividly, mainly because of the sheer number of visitors I ran into while I was there.

So usually, during the Tet holiday, my family drives out to Tay Ninh province to visit my grandfather, and last year we decided to take a short trip to Ba Den Pagoda on Ba Den Mountain.

Before we even got on the cable car up to the pagoda, I had a feeling there'd be a lot of Buddhist worshippers there, since it's a sacred place that draws huge crowds all year round — let alone during Tet, which is the biggest holiday in Vietnam. And I was right. There were people carrying flowers and candles everywhere you looked. In one corner, there was a whole queue of people trying to light incense sticks, and inside the pagoda itself people were kneeling shoulder to shoulder, praying with their eyes closed. It was so packed that we could barely find room to stand, let alone move.

In the end, my mum and I pushed our way through the crowd and managed to get close enough to light some incense of our own and say a quick prayer, but my grandfather stayed outside because he can't stand all the smoke and the lack of oxygen in there. Honestly, by the time we got back to the car, I felt completely drained — it wasn't fear exactly, more just that sense of being crushed on every side for almost an hour.

I understand why so many Vietnamese people love visiting pagodas and temples on these special days, to wish for good luck and happiness in the year ahead, but after that experience, I don't think I'll be rushing back to do it again anytime soon — maybe I'll visit on a quieter day instead, when there's actually room to breathe.

take a short trip to
