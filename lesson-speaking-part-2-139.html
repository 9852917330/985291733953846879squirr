A happy family event that you remember well

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a happy family event that you remember well

You should say: what the event was when and where it happened What you saw or did and explain why you remember this event so well.

Simple Model Answer

A happy family event I remember happened when I was about ten. My eldest sister had given birth to a healthy baby boy, and our family held a celebration at my parents' home. He was the first grandchild on both sides, so everyone was excited.

After seeing him, my father decided we should invite the relatives. My parents organised the shopping and invitations, while my grandmother and sister prepared plenty of food. Aunts, uncles and neighbours arrived with presents, and by the evening our living room was full.

I spent much of the afternoon beside the baby. I wanted to hold him and show him toys, although he was far too small to play. I mostly watched him sleep and noticed how tiny his face and hands were. The day was noisy and busy, but in a happy way. What I remember most is how one new family member brought everyone together. It was one of the first times I understood why adults felt so emotional about a baby's arrival.

Advanced Model Answer

This is a lovely topic for me, because one of my most memorable childhood family events happened when I was about ten, one weekend at my parents' house — the celebration we held to welcome my nephew into the world. My eldest sister gave birth to a healthy baby boy, and he was the very first grandchild on either side of the family, so you can imagine how excited everyone was.

After my father saw the baby for the first time, he announced that we simply had to celebrate properly, so the whole family got to work straight away. My parents set out to invite all the relatives and do the shopping, while my grandmother and my sister spent the whole morning in the kitchen cooking enough food for what felt like the entire street.

I was so excited that I barely left the baby's side all afternoon. I kept wanting to hold him and play with him, but he was obviously far too small for any of the toys I'd bought, so I mostly just sat there staring at his tiny face, which didn't bother me at all — I could have watched him sleep for hours.

It turned into a day full of noise, laughter and general chaos in the best possible way. Aunts, uncles and neighbours kept arriving with gifts, and by the evening our living room was completely packed. What I remember most is how one small baby managed to pull the whole family together, and that's why this event has stayed with me ever since.
