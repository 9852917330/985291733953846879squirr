Cities & Urban Life

Should new buildings copy traditional styles?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that new buildings should be built in traditional styles. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

Model answer

It is sometimes argued that new buildings should be constructed in the traditional style of the country in which they are built. I disagree with this view, because although historic architecture is certainly worth protecting, imitating it indefinitely would leave cities with no character of their own.

The argument for traditional design is understandable. A city whose buildings share a consistent style has a visual identity that visitors recognise immediately, and a modern glass tower inserted into a row of old houses can genuinely spoil a street. Traditional materials such as stone, brick and timber also age well, whereas much of the concrete used in the 1960s has deteriorated badly.

Nevertheless, this approach has serious weaknesses. Every style that we now call traditional was modern when it was built, and the districts we admire are valued precisely because each generation added something of its own. A rule requiring imitation would freeze a city at an arbitrary date and produce buildings that are neither genuinely old nor honestly new. There is also a practical objection: traditional construction depends on skills and materials that are now scarce and expensive, so it could never be applied to housing estates, hospitals or schools.

Modern buildings can also do things that older ones cannot. Contemporary design allows far better insulation, natural light and access for disabled people, and these matter more to the people who use a building every day than its exterior does.

In conclusion, historic buildings should certainly be preserved, but I disagree that new ones must copy them. New architecture should respect the scale and materials of its surroundings rather than reproduce the past.

Key words and phrases

no character of their own
Lack distinctive features or identity.
added something of its own
Contributed a new and distinctive feature.
freeze a city at an arbitrary date
Prevent change by choosing an arbitrary historical appearance.
neither genuinely old nor honestly new
Looks historical without authenticity or clearly modern design.
access for disabled people
Ways for disabled people to enter and use places.
