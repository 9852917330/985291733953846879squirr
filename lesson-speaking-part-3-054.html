Presents children ask for, and advertising to children

Part 3

Prompt

What kinds of presents do children ask for today? What effect has advertising had on children's preferences? Is there a case for banning advertising directed at children?

What kinds of presents do children ask for today?

Simple Model Answer

Many ask for phones, tablets, games and online game items. Older children may want branded clothes or trainers. Influencers can create demand for a particular brand instead of just a type of toy. However, younger children often still want something simply because a friend has it, so technology and branding do not explain every request.

Key words and phrases

demand
The desire to buy or obtain a product.

Advanced Model Answer

Mostly electronics and things connected to them — phones, tablets, games and in-game purchases, which parents often find harder to understand than a physical toy. Branded clothing and trainers matter a great deal to older children. What's changed is where the requests come from: children now ask for specific items they've seen an influencer using, so the demand arrives already attached to a brand rather than to a general idea of what they'd enjoy. It's not universal, though — plenty of younger children still just want whatever toy a friend happens to have, brand aside.

Key words and phrases

already attached to a brand
already loyal to a particular company
whatever toy a friend happens to have
a toy their friend owns by chance

What effect has advertising had on children's preferences?

Simple Model Answer

Adverts make children recognise brands early and can turn an unfamiliar product into something they feel they need. Younger children may believe claims without understanding that someone is trying to sell to them. This creates pressure on parents. Older children usually recognise advertising more easily, though knowing its purpose does not always stop it influencing them.

Key words and phrases

claims
Statements saying that something is true or has a particular benefit.

Advanced Model Answer

It's made them extraordinarily brand-aware — young children can identify logos before they can read. The more serious effect concerns younger children specifically, because below about eight they don't reliably understand that an advertisement is trying to sell them something. They take the claims as straightforward fact. Repeated exposure then turns something they'd never heard of into something they feel they need, and that pressure lands on the parents. Older children are more resistant, since by their teens most can recognise persuasion for what it is, even if they don't always act on that awareness.

Key words and phrases

trying to sell them something
attempting to persuade them to buy a product
Older children are more resistant
children who are older are less easily persuaded

Is there a case for banning advertising directed at children?

Simple Model Answer

I support strong limits, especially for unhealthy food and games that encourage gambling-like spending. Young children cannot judge advertising claims as well as adults. A complete ban would be difficult online because content comes from different countries. I would focus on the youngest audiences and the most harmful products, where protection is most clearly needed.

Key words and phrases

limits
Rules controlling how much something can happen or what is allowed.

Advanced Model Answer

There's a strong case for restricting it, particularly for unhealthy food and for games using gambling-like mechanisms. The argument is straightforward: advertising assumes an audience capable of evaluating a claim, and small children aren't. A total ban would be difficult to enforce given how much content crosses borders online, so I'd focus regulation on the youngest age groups and the most harmful categories rather than attempting to prohibit everything.

Key words and phrases

gambling-like mechanisms
features that imitate gambling, such as chance-based rewards
the youngest age groups
the smallest or least old groups of children
