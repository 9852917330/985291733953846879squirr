#050
Children & Family

Nature or nurture?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Opinions differ as to whether nature or nurture plays the more important role in the development of personality. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

People disagree about whether personality is determined mainly by inherited characteristics or by upbringing and environment. In my opinion both matter, but the evidence suggests that environment has the greater influence over how a person actually behaves.

The argument for nature is not weak. Studies of identical twins raised in separate families have repeatedly found striking similarities in temperament, and any parent with more than one child will confirm that babies differ from birth in how anxious, sociable or persistent they are. Nobody chooses to be naturally shy or naturally energetic, and these tendencies remain recognisable throughout life.

Nevertheless, inherited temperament sets a range rather than a fixed outcome. A naturally shy child who is encouraged to speak in class and never mocked for hesitating usually becomes a reserved but confident adult; the same child, criticised for shyness, may become genuinely withdrawn. Language, values, ambition and attitudes to work are also learned almost entirely from family and society, which is why children raised in different countries develop such different assumptions about what is normal.

The most convincing explanation is that the two interact continuously rather than competing. Inherited characteristics influence how a child is treated, since an outgoing baby receives more attention and a difficult one receives more correction, and that treatment then reinforces the original tendency. Separating the two influences is therefore almost impossible in practice.

In conclusion, personality begins with inherited temperament, but I believe upbringing determines far more of the final result. This matters practically, because it means that good parenting and good schooling can genuinely change how a child's life turns out.
