Education

Employable skills or knowledge for its own sake?

Opinion

Prompt

Nowadays, some universities teach graduates practical skills that help them find employment, while others believe that the main function of university is to pursue knowledge for its own sake. What is your opinion?

Model answer

Universities have two important roles: they develop knowledge and prepare students for adult working life. In my view, these aims are not in conflict. A strong university education should combine academic understanding with practical skills.

Knowledge for its own sake remains valuable. Subjects such as history, mathematics and philosophy train students to analyse evidence, question assumptions and build logical arguments. These abilities are useful far beyond one job. They also allow universities to conduct research that may have no immediate commercial use but can later produce major social benefits. Reducing higher education to short-term job training would therefore narrow the purpose of university.

At the same time, graduates need to enter a competitive labour market. Universities should teach students how to communicate clearly, work in teams, solve unfamiliar problems and use relevant technology. Internships, project work and careers advice can help students connect theory with practice. This does not mean that employers should dictate the entire curriculum; rather, practical experience should support serious academic study.

Practical preparation already works well in fields such as medicine and engineering, where students spend years on hospital wards or in industrial placements without losing scientific rigour. Some countries extend this idea at an institutional level, running applied-science universities alongside research universities so that students who want a profession quickly and those who want an academic career can choose the right kind of course. Expanding this sort of structured choice, rather than diluting every degree equally, would let more graduates leave with skills that transfer between different careers.

In conclusion, universities should not choose between knowledge and employability. Their best contribution is to give students a deep understanding of a subject while also developing skills that can be transferred to different careers. This balanced approach would produce graduates who are both intellectually independent and work-ready.

Key words and phrases

narrow the purpose of university
Limit what university education aims to achieve.
connect theory with practice
Link academic ideas with actual situations.
practical experience should support serious academic study
Hands-on learning should strengthen academic understanding.
without losing scientific rigour
While maintaining careful, reliable scientific methods.
diluting every degree equally
Reducing the depth of every degree programme.
skills that transfer between different careers
Abilities useful across several types of work.
