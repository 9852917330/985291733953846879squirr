Education

University places for the many

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

It is neither possible nor useful for a country to provide university places for a high proportion of young people. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Half of all school leavers now enter higher education in many countries, and critics argue that this is both unaffordable and pointless. I partly agree: mass expansion has been achieved, so it is clearly possible, but its usefulness depends entirely on how it is done.

The claim that it cannot be done is the weaker half. Several countries already educate more than half their young adults, and the cost, while large, is smaller than the tax revenue graduates return over a working life. What is genuinely difficult is expanding without diluting: hurried growth produces overcrowded lectures, unqualified teaching and degrees that no employer treats as evidence of anything.

The usefulness argument carries more weight. Many graduates now work in jobs that never required a degree, which suggests the qualification is being used to sort applicants rather than to prepare them. Students pay years of fees and lost earnings for that sorting, and countries with strong technical training produce skilled workers faster and at lower cost. There is a real shortage of electricians and nurses alongside a surplus of graduates in crowded fields, so the money has been spent on the wrong training.

The conclusion I draw is not that fewer people should be educated, but that education after school should be broader than a single model. Apprenticeships, technical colleges and short professional qualifications deserve the funding and the respect currently reserved for universities, and school leavers need honest information about where each route leads. A country also benefits from graduates beyond their wages, because an educated population argues better and is harder to mislead.

In conclusion, providing university places for many young people is possible and has already happened. It is useful only where the teaching is genuine and the alternatives are properly funded, and pushing everyone through the same route wastes both money and talent.

Key words and phrases

wastes both money and talent
Loses financial resources and people's potential.
