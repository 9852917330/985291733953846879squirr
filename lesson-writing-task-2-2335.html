Animals & Wildlife

Protect every species or only some?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people believe that all wild animals should be protected. Others say that only a few wild animals should be protected. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Conservation budgets are small and the number of threatened species is enormous, which forces an uncomfortable question about where the money goes. Both positions here are defensible, and my own view is that protecting habitats resolves most of the disagreement.

The case for protecting everything rests on how ecosystems actually work. Species are connected in ways that are rarely obvious until something is removed: insects pollinate crops, predators keep grazing animals from stripping grassland, and unremarkable plants hold soil in place. Choosing which creatures matter also assumes a knowledge we do not have, since medicines have repeatedly come from organisms nobody valued. There is a moral argument as well, in that a species which took millions of years to appear should not disappear for the sake of a short-term convenience.

Selective protection answers with arithmetic. There is not enough money to save everything, and spreading it thinly saves nothing, so it is better to concentrate on species whose loss would collapse an ecosystem or that can realistically be recovered. Some animals are already beyond saving in the wild, and funds spent on them are taken from cases where the same money would work. Conservation also competes with schools and hospitals, which cannot always lose.

My own position is that the choice is largely a false one, because animals are rarely saved individually. Protecting a forest or a reef preserves thousands of species at once, most of which have never been catalogued, and doing so is cheaper than any programme aimed at a single animal. Well-known species still deserve attention, since they attract the public support that funds everything else.

In conclusion, complete protection is beyond any budget and rigid selection risks losing species we do not yet understand. Concentrating on habitats protects the greatest number for the least money, and that is where the effort belongs.

Key words and phrases

disappear for the sake of a short-term convenience
Be lost for a brief practical benefit.
