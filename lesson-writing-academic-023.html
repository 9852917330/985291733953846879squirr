Transport

Urban traffic speeds and urban zones

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The charts show (Chart a) the average speed of urban zone traffic (in kilometres per hour or kph) in a number of countries over a fifty year period, including a future estimate; and also (Chart b) the total number of urban zones per country (in thousands). Write a report summarising the information. Select and describe the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer

The charts show the average speed of city traffic in a number of countries over a fifty-year period, including projected figures for 2020, and also the number of urban zones by country.

Overall, the general trend is for the speed to increase and then decrease, although speeds in three of the countries rise over the period as a whole. The data does not suggest any correlation between increasing urban zones and average traffic speeds.

In France, for example, the average speed increased from 60 kph in 1970 to a peak of 86 kph in 1990, tailing off to a forecast 52 kph in 2020. The USA also peaked in 1990, at 57 kph, before declining to 43 kph at the end of the period. The UK and Russia have a similar trend, rising to 56 and 72 kph respectively in 2010 before falling to below the starting level, and Canada shows a decline from 69 kph to 64 kph despite a peak of 73 kph. Chile, however, fluctuates around 51 kph and remains static over the period, while Italy, Brazil and Spain, by contrast, show a clear rise, finishing at 58, 57 and 61 kph respectively.

Regarding urban zones, the figures for all countries except Russia are projected to more than triple between 1970 and 2020. The exception is Russia, which sees a fall of about half, to 3,200.
