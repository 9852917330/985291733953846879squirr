Children & Family

The effects of peer pressure

Advantages & Disadvantages

Prompt

Young people are often influenced in their behaviour and attitudes by others of the same age. This is called peer pressure. Do the disadvantages of peer pressure outweigh the advantages?

Model answer

Young people are strongly affected by the expectations of others their own age, and this influence is usually described as a danger. In my view the disadvantages do outweigh the benefits during adolescence, though the effect is not as one-sided as it is often presented.

The harm is easiest to see in behaviour that carries real risk. Smoking, drinking and dangerous driving almost always begin in a group rather than alone, because a teenager who refuses fears exclusion more than the risk itself, and the part of the brain that weighs consequences is still developing at that age. The pressure also narrows what young people allow themselves to be: a boy who enjoys music or a girl who is good at mathematics may abandon the subject rather than be seen as different, and the cost of that is paid for years afterwards.

The positive side is genuine but weaker. The same mechanism works in the opposite direction when the group values effort: a student whose friends intend to go to university is far more likely to apply, and teams and clubs use exactly this force to build commitment. Peers also teach social skills that adults cannot, since learning to negotiate and apologise happens among equals rather than under adult supervision.

The imbalance lies in what each side risks. A beneficial influence produces better habits, while a harmful one can produce an addiction, a criminal record or a fatal accident, so the consequences are not symmetrical.

In conclusion, peer pressure can encourage effort and teaches young people how to live among others. Its drawbacks are nonetheless greater, because the damage it causes is often difficult to undo later.

Key words and phrases

fears exclusion more than the risk itself
Is more afraid of rejection than danger.
still developing at that age
Has not yet finished growing or maturing.
is paid for years afterwards
Causes costs or harm long afterwards.
the consequences are not symmetrical
The two choices have unequal consequences.
difficult to undo later
Hard to reverse in the future.
