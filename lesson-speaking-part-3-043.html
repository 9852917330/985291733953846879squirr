Environment & Nature

Human effects on the environment and what can be done

Part 3

Prompt

What are the effects of human activities on the environment? What can be done to protect the environment? What would be the impact of global warming on the environment?

What are the effects of human activities on the environment?

Simple Model Answer

Cutting down forests, burning fossil fuels, intensive farming and waste damage both nature and the climate. They can destroy habitats, pollute rivers and make the air unhealthy. Changing weather is especially worrying because it affects food production. Some damage can improve when pollution falls, but losing a species may be permanent, so prevention matters.

Key words and phrases

prevention
Taking action to stop a problem before it happens.

Advanced Model Answer

The largest are deforestation, burning fossil fuels, intensive agriculture and waste. Between them they've altered habitats and the climate itself, and the consequences are visible: species disappearing at an unprecedented rate, air quality in major cities that measurably shortens lives, and rivers carrying plastic and agricultural runoff. What concerns me most is the change in weather patterns, because that affects food production, and food security affects almost everything else in a society. Not all of this is equally permanent, though — air quality can bounce back within years once emissions drop, whereas a lost species or a collapsed fishery doesn't come back at all.

Key words and phrases

an unprecedented rate
a speed never seen before
air quality can bounce back within years
the air can become clean again in a few years

What can be done to protect the environment?

Simple Model Answer

Governments need to lead by setting pollution limits and investing in clean energy and public transport. People cannot choose a greener option if none is available. Companies should also pay for the damage they cause. Individual habits matter, but asking consumers to solve everything while the wider system stays the same is not enough.

Key words and phrases

consumers
People who buy or use goods and services.

Advanced Model Answer

The decisive action has to come from governments, because individuals can't buy clean options that don't exist. That means enforceable emissions standards, investment in public transport and renewable energy, and making polluters pay for the damage they cause rather than passing it to the public. Individual behaviour matters and shouldn't be dismissed, but I'm impatient with campaigns that place the whole responsibility on consumers while the underlying system stays identical.

Key words and phrases

making polluters pay for the damage they cause
requiring those who pollute to pay for the harm
the underlying system stays identical
the basic way things work has not changed

What would be the impact of global warming on the environment?

Simple Model Answer

It can cause stronger heatwaves, rising seas, droughts and floods. Animals and plants may struggle because their habitats change faster than they can move. Poorer countries may suffer most despite contributing less to the problem, so fairness matters too. Some cold areas might briefly benefit from warmer growing conditions, but that does not balance the wider damage.

Key words and phrases

droughts
Long periods with too little rain.

Advanced Model Answer

More intense heatwaves, rising sea levels, longer droughts in some regions and heavier flooding in others. Ecosystems will be disrupted because species cannot relocate as fast as their habitats change. The part that receives less attention is the human distribution of it: the countries that will suffer worst contributed least to the emissions and have the fewest resources to adapt. That's what makes it a question of fairness, not only of science. It isn't uniformly bad news, either — a handful of colder regions may see longer growing seasons, though that's a small and temporary upside next to the disruption everywhere else.

Key words and phrases

cannot relocate as fast as their habitats change
cannot move quickly enough when their natural homes change
a question of fairness
an issue of treating people equally and justly
