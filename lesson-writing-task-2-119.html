Government & Politics

Should people pay taxes?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that individuals should keep all the money they earn and should not pay taxes to the state. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Some people argue that individuals should keep everything they earn and pay nothing to the state. I strongly disagree with this view, because almost everything that allows a modern society to function depends on taxation.

The most obvious objection is that the services people rely on cannot be produced individually. Roads, police, courts, street lighting, clean water and national defence are used by everyone, and it is impossible to supply them only to those who choose to pay. Without compulsory taxation, each person would have an incentive to let others fund them, and the services would simply cease to exist. Even the business owner who resents paying tax depends on educated employees, functioning roads and a legal system that enforces contracts, none of which the private market would supply on its own.

Taxation also protects people against risks they cannot predict. Serious illness, disability, unemployment and old age can affect anyone, and very few individuals could meet the cost of major surgery or decades of retirement from savings alone. A tax-funded system spreads that risk across the whole population, which is why countries without one see families ruined by a single accident.

I would accept that taxes must be reasonable and spent efficiently, and that waste and corruption justify criticism. That, however, is an argument for better government rather than for abolishing taxation altogether.

In conclusion, I disagree entirely with this proposal. Taxation is the price of living in an organised society, and a country that abandoned it would quickly find that almost everyone, including the wealthy, was considerably worse off.

Key words and phrases

let others fund them
Expect other people to cover the expense.
spreads that risk
Shares possible losses among more people.
ruined by a single accident
Financially devastated by one unexpected event.
the price of living in an organised society
A necessary contribution to shared public services.
