Place

An interesting place that few tourists visit

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an interesting place that few tourists visit.

You should say: - Where it is; - When you went there; - What you can see there; - Why there is a few people to go.

OUTLINE

>>> "Grandma May's" restaurant
hard to think of somewhere interesting hardly anyone knows about; favourite restaurant, suburbs, no proper name
been going since primary school, right next to old school gate, popped in after class
busy little street, small/chaotic, run-down outside, old wooden tables/mismatched chairs inside, full of regulars
no menu, walk up to kitchen and point at what catches your eye, best dishes go fast
tiny but food good enough everyone falls in love with it, best restaurant in my mind
hardly anyone's heard of it, never had a proper name, hometown too small for word to spread

Simple Model Answer

An interesting place that few tourists know is a restaurant people call Grandma May's. It is in the suburbs of my hometown, beside my old primary school. May is the elderly owner and cook, and she only serves lunch.

I have eaten there since I was a child. From outside, it looks small and rather old. Inside, there are wooden tables and chairs that do not all match. There is no printed menu. Customers look at the food May has prepared and choose what they would like. The most popular dishes sell out quickly, so arriving early helps.

Whenever I return home, I try to go with friends or family. I enjoy both the food and the familiar atmosphere. Although the place is simple, the cooking is excellent. I think few tourists visit because the restaurant has no clear business name and my hometown is not a major destination. To me, that makes it feel like a local secret: an ordinary-looking place with food that is worth remembering.

Key words and phrases

sell out
Have none left because everything has been bought.
familiar atmosphere
A feeling of comfort because a place is well known to you.

Advanced Model Answer

To be honest, it's really hard to think of somewhere that's genuinely interesting but that hardly anyone knows about. So today I'm going to talk about one of my favourite restaurants — it's out in the suburbs of my hometown, and it doesn't even have a proper name. Everyone just calls it "Grandma May's", after the owner, who's also the cook — an elderly woman called May who only ever serves lunch.

I've been going there since I was at primary school, actually, because it's right next to my old school gate, so my classmates and I used to pop in after class. It's on a busy little street, small and a bit chaotic inside, and from the outside it honestly doesn't look like much — a bit run-down, if I'm being honest. Inside, though, there are rows of old square wooden tables, each with four mismatched chairs, and it's always full of regulars.

There's no menu at all — you just walk up to the kitchen, see what May's cooked that day, and point at whatever catches your eye. Everything's delicious, but the best dishes go fast, so if you turn up too late, you'll miss out. Whenever I've got some free time back home, I still go there with my friends or family to tuck into my favourite dishes.

Even though it's tiny, the food is good enough that everyone who's ever eaten there ends up falling in love with it. Honestly, in my mind it's one of the best restaurants anywhere. But hardly anyone's heard of it, probably because it's never had a proper name, and my hometown's just too small for word to spread very far.

Key words and phrases

run-down
in poor condition because it has not been cared for
catches your eye
immediately attracts your attention
tuck into
start eating something eagerly
falling in love with it
starting to like it very strongly
heard of it
known that it exists
