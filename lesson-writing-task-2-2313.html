Cities & Urban Life

Moving companies out of the cities

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Traffic and housing problems could be solved by moving large companies, factories and their employees to the countryside. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Congested roads and unaffordable housing are the standard complaints of every large city, and moving employers out of them sounds like a direct cure. I agree that relocation would ease both problems, but I disagree that it would solve them, and it would create new difficulties of its own.

The logic behind the idea is sound as far as it goes. Traffic and housing pressure are both consequences of too many people needing to be in one place, so removing a large employer removes thousands of daily journeys and thousands of competing tenants at once. Rural areas gain from the same movement, since a factory brings wages, customers for local shops and a reason for young people to stay in the town they grew up in.

The plan is weaker than it looks, however. Firms cluster in cities because they need suppliers, specialists, airports and each other, and a company that moves alone loses those advantages and often loses staff, since employees have partners with careers and children in school. Rural roads and railways are also thin, so a relocated workforce drives where it used to take a train, and the traffic reappears in a less efficient form. Housing pressure tends to follow the jobs rather than disappear, which is how expensive commuter towns are created.

More effective measures work on the city itself: building densely near stations, taxing empty property, improving public transport and allowing people to work from home for part of the week. These reduce the need to travel without dismantling what makes a city productive.

In conclusion, moving some employers out of crowded cities is a reasonable part of a wider plan, particularly for factories that need space rather than proximity. On its own it would relocate the problems rather than remove them.

Key words and phrases

stay in the town they grew up in
Remain in their childhood hometown.
drives where it used to take a train
Now uses cars instead of its former rail travel.
