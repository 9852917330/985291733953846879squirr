Education

Too few students studying science

Reasons / Effects

Prompt

It is observed that in many countries not enough students are choosing to study science subjects at university. What are the causes, and what will be the effects on society?

Model answer

Applications to physics, chemistry and engineering have fallen in many developed countries even as demand for those skills has risen. The causes lie in school experience and in the returns students expect, and the consequences reach further than a shortage of laboratory staff.

School is where most of the decision is made. Science taught as facts to be memorised, with little laboratory work and often by teachers trained in another subject, convinces capable students that it is dull and difficult. The subjects are also marked more severely than many alternatives, so students protecting their grades choose the safer route to the same university place. A shortage of specialist teachers makes this self-reinforcing, since fewer science graduates means fewer science teachers.

Expectations about work matter too. A science degree is demanding and often leads to research posts that are insecure and modestly paid, while finance, law and business offer higher salaries for less mathematics. Students also see very few scientists in public life and have little idea what the career actually involves, and girls in particular receive the impression early that the field is not for them.

The effects on society are serious. Countries without enough engineers and scientists cannot build the energy systems, medical capacity and digital infrastructure their economies depend on, and they end up importing both the technology and the specialists, which is expensive and unreliable. Innovation slows, since new industries form where the expertise already exists. There is also a wider cost to public life: a population with little scientific understanding struggles to judge claims about health, climate or risk, and decisions on those subjects then follow whoever argues most loudly.

In conclusion, students avoid science because it is taught poorly, graded harshly and rewarded modestly. The result is a country that depends on expertise it has not produced and a public less able to evaluate the questions that matter most.

Key words and phrases

depends on expertise it has not produced
Relies on specialists trained elsewhere.
