Work & Employment

One career or several?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people think that people who choose a job early and keep doing it are more likely to get a satisfying career life than those who frequently change jobs. Do you agree or disagree with this belief?

Model answer

Some people believe that those who commit to a single occupation early in life end up more satisfied than those who move between jobs. I disagree with this view, because satisfaction depends on how well the work suits the individual rather than on how long it has been done.

There is some force in the argument for stability. A person who remains in one field accumulates deep expertise, builds a professional reputation and usually reaches a senior position with a good salary and a secure pension. Surgeons, lawyers and academics all require many years of continuous experience, and the sense of mastery this produces is a genuine source of satisfaction. Constant movement, by contrast, can leave someone permanently a beginner.

Nevertheless, this argument assumes that the original choice was a good one. Most people select a career at seventeen or eighteen, with almost no experience of the working world, and it would be surprising if they always chose correctly. Someone who discovers that a profession does not suit them, but who stays out of habit or fear, is likely to spend decades in quiet frustration. Changing direction is precisely what allows such a person to find work that matches their abilities and interests.

There is also a practical point. Industries now change so quickly that lifelong loyalty may not be rewarded. Workers with varied experience adapt more easily when their sector declines, whereas those with a single narrow specialism can find themselves unemployable in middle age.

In conclusion, I disagree that staying in one job produces a more satisfying career. What matters is finding work that genuinely suits the person, whether that takes one decision or several.

Key words and phrases

accumulates deep expertise
Builds extensive specialist knowledge over time.
sense of mastery
Feeling highly capable at a particular skill.
permanently a beginner
Always starting again without developing deep expertise.
lifelong loyalty
Remaining faithful to an employer throughout life.
a single narrow specialism
Expertise limited to one small professional area.
unemployable in middle age
Unable to obtain work in middle adulthood.
