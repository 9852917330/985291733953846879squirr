Object & Favourite

Something you bought according to an advertisement you saw

Part 2

Prompt

Describe something you bought according to an advertisement you saw.

You should say: - What it is; - When and where you see the advertisement; - Why you want to buy it; - and how you felt about it.

Simple Model Answer

Something I bought after seeing an advertisement was a pair of running shoes. I noticed the advert online a couple of months ago. The photograph caught my attention because the shoes looked different from the ones I normally wore.

The upper part fitted closely around the foot, and the flexible sole was designed to move with each step. A short video showed someone running up a rocky hill. It explained how the shoes could help the runner feel the ground while still providing grip and protection.

The advertisement was fairly simple and informative. It did not tell a dramatic story, but it explained the design clearly enough to make me curious. I ordered a pair in my size and have worn them several times. They were more expensive than ordinary running shoes, but I find them comfortable and enjoy the different feeling when I run. I am pleased with the purchase because the actual experience matched what interested me in the advertisement. In this case, learning about a product's design was more convincing than a flashy promotion.

Key words and phrases

flexible sole
The bottom part of a shoe that can bend easily.
providing grip
Helping something hold onto a surface without slipping.

Advanced Model Answer

About a couple of months ago I came across an advert on the internet for a rather unusual pair of running shoes. It was the picture that caught my attention first, because they looked completely different from any running shoe I'd seen before.

They're made from some kind of special material, with a synthetic sole that's moulded onto the upper so the whole shoe fits like a glove. The sole itself isn't too different from a normal running shoe underneath — it still grips well and protects my feet properly — but the way the upper's built is what really stood out to me in the photo.

The advert itself also had a short video, maybe three or four minutes long, showing a runner going up a rocky hillside. It explained how the shoes let you feel more of the ground under your feet and grip better, because of the way the sole flexes with each step.

It was more of an informative ad than anything flashy or creative, but it worked on me — I hadn't seen shoes built quite like that before, and I wanted to try a pair for myself, so I ordered them in my size. I've worn them a handful of times now and they've genuinely impressed me. They cost a bit more than an ordinary pair of running shoes, but they're worth every penny as far as I'm concerned — running in them just feels completely different, and honestly, I prefer it.
