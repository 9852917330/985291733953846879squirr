Work

Technology at work and whether it costs jobs

Prompt

Which technologies are commonly used in workplaces? Does technology make work easier? Does technology cause people to lose their jobs?

Which technologies are commonly used in workplaces?

Simple Model Answer

Computers, phones, printers and shared online documents are common in offices. Computers support emails, reports, data and research, while video calls connect teams in different locations. However, needs vary by job. A mechanic or electrician still depends mainly on physical tools, so an office's most important technology is not necessarily essential in every workplace.

Key words and phrases

shared online documents
Files that several people can access or edit through the internet.

Advanced Model Answer

Most workplaces run on a fairly standard set of equipment — computers, phones, printers and photocopiers, with fax machines still hanging on in a few offices. The computer's probably the most essential piece of equipment because we rely on it for almost everything: email, reports, organising data, finding information online. Video calls have become just as essential now that teams are spread across different offices, and shared documents in the cloud have largely replaced sending files back and forth. The one exception is anything hands-on — a mechanic or an electrician still depends far more on physical tools than on a laptop.

Key words and phrases

essential piece of equipment
a tool or device that you cannot do the activity without
rely on it for almost everything
depend on it for nearly every task

Does technology make work easier?

Simple Model Answer

Overall, yes. Technology makes ordinary tasks quicker, such as sending a report to another country in seconds. However, an internet failure or computer crash can stop work completely. It also allows employers to contact staff at home. So it improves efficiency but can bring dependence and make it harder to separate work from personal time.

Key words and phrases

efficiency
Doing work with less wasted time or effort.

Advanced Model Answer

It does, on balance, because it removes a lot of friction from ordinary tasks. Email alone means a report or a query reaches a colleague in a different office, or a different country, in seconds rather than days. On the other hand, technology can make life more difficult too, especially when it goes wrong — a dead internet connection or a crashed computer can bring an entire office to a stop for an afternoon. It also means work follows you home, because the same tools that let you work anywhere let your employer reach you anywhere.

Key words and phrases

make life more difficult
cause ordinary living to become harder

Does technology cause people to lose their jobs?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, machines and software can replace jobs in areas such as farming and manufacturing. New technology also creates roles, including programming. The problem is that these changes do not happen at the same speed or help the same people. A worker may lose a job long before they can train for one of the new opportunities.

Key words and phrases

replace jobs
Perform work that people were previously employed to do.

Advanced Model Answer

I think technology's often responsible for people losing their jobs. Machines have replaced people in areas like manufacturing and agriculture, and whenever a new technology's introduced, there are redundancies. At the same time, jobs might be created thanks to a new technology — there'd be no computer programmers if the computer hadn't been invented. The real problem is timing. Jobs disappear quickly and new ones appear slowly, and they rarely go to the same people, so an individual can be displaced permanently even while total employment holds up.

Key words and phrases

Machines have replaced people
devices now do work once done by humans
jobs might be created
new positions may become available
