Crime & Punishment

Is corporal punishment ever acceptable?

Essay

Prompt

Although it is generally prohibited, corporal punishment persists in many families. Do you think corporal punishment is an acceptable way to regulate children's behaviour?

Model answer

Corporal punishment is still used in some families because it can stop bad behaviour immediately. However, I do not believe that hitting a child is an acceptable form of discipline. It may produce short-term obedience, but it creates serious physical, emotional and educational risks.

The first problem is that physical punishment can easily blur the line between discipline and abuse. A parent who is angry or tired may use more force than intended, and a child has little power to protect himself or herself. Even when no visible injury occurs, repeated punishment can cause lasting psychological harm. Children may become anxious, secretive or resentful, rather than learning why their behaviour was wrong.

A second concern is the lesson that physical punishment teaches. When adults solve conflict by hitting, they model aggression as a legitimate response to frustration. Children may then copy this behaviour with siblings or classmates. Fear can also damage trust between parents and children, making honest communication less likely precisely when young people need guidance.

Parents still need effective ways to control unacceptable behaviour. The better approach is to set clear and consistent boundaries, explain the consequences of breaking them, and use proportionate measures such as loss of privileges or additional responsibilities. Praise for good behaviour is equally important because it shows children what they should do, not merely what they should avoid.

In conclusion, corporal punishment is neither necessary nor defensible. Non-violent forms of discipline can correct behaviour while preserving a child's dignity, safety and relationship with the family.

Key words and phrases

blur the line between discipline and abuse
Make reasonable correction hard to distinguish from mistreatment.
cause lasting psychological harm
Create emotional damage that lasts a long time.
model aggression as a legitimate response
Show children that aggression is an acceptable response.
set clear and consistent boundaries
Establish understandable rules applied in the same way.
proportionate measures such as loss of privileges
Fair penalties, such as losing special freedoms.
