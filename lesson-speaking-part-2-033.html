Part 2

Prompt

Describe a city or town you have been to

You should say: Where it is When you visited it What you did there And explain why you like it

Simple Model Answer

A city I have visited is Singapore. I went there last summer with my sister, Min. We flew from Hanoi, which took about two hours, and stayed for five days.

My first impression was how clean the streets were. We spent a lot of time walking around, shopping and visiting famous places. These included the Merlion statue, the zoo and the botanical gardens. I particularly enjoyed seeing different parts of the city rather than staying in just one area.

Another thing I liked was the public transport. The buses and trains were easy to use, and the staff were helpful when we needed directions. I also enjoyed meeting people from different countries. Because English was widely used, I had plenty of opportunities to practise speaking with local people and other visitors. The five days passed very quickly. I wished we had stayed longer because there were still places to explore. It was a convenient and enjoyable holiday, and I would happily return.

Key words and phrases

first impression
Your first opinion of a person or place.
public transport
Buses, trains and other services that many people can use.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about Singapore, one of several cities I've visited and really enjoyed. I went there on holiday last summer with my sister, Min, and we stayed for 5 days.

Singapore's a city-state just to the southeast of Vietnam, and it only took us about 2 hours to fly there from Hanoi.

When I arrived in Singapore, I was quite impressed by how clean the city looked. I could walk around all day and come back without a speck of dirt on my shoes. While I was there, I did some shopping and visited a few famous landmarks, like the Merlion statue, Singapore Zoo, and the botanical gardens.

For me, Singapore's well worth visiting for a few reasons. One of the main ones is that the public transport system there is extremely reliable — you don't have to spend hours waiting for buses or trains, and the staff are always friendly and helpful. What I miss most about Singapore, though, is how multicultural it is. Over the 5 days I was there, I bumped into people from all sorts of different countries. English is the first language, so it was great to get the chance to practise my English-speaking skills with locals and other tourists alike. Five days in Singapore passed by so fast that I wish I'd stayed a bit longer.
