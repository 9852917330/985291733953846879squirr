Society

Is competitiveness a good quality?

Effects / Beneficial or Harmful

Prompt

In many societies, competitiveness is regarded as an important personal quality. How does this affect individuals? Do you think this is a beneficial or harmful trend?

Model answer

Many cultures treat the desire to outperform others as a virtue and reward it from primary school onwards. The effects on individuals run in both directions, and I regard the trend as harmful once competition becomes the main measure of a person.

The positive effects are worth acknowledging. Competition supplies motivation that is difficult to generate any other way, since most people work harder when their performance is visible and compared. It also produces useful information: an athlete or a researcher discovers where they actually stand, which allows them to improve rather than assume they are already good enough. Societies that reward achievement rather than family connections also allow talented people to rise from unpromising beginnings.

The harmful effects appear when competitiveness becomes an identity. A person who measures their worth by ranking is anxious whenever they are not winning, and since there is always somebody ahead, the anxiety is permanent. This shows in the mental health of students in the most competitive education systems, where rates of exhaustion and depression are highest. Highly competitive individuals also cooperate poorly, because sharing an idea with a colleague means giving away an advantage, and most valuable work now depends on precisely that sharing.

There is a further cost. Competition rewards what can be measured, so people abandon work that matters but cannot be scored, and choose safe targets they are confident of hitting rather than difficult problems where they might fail in front of others.

In conclusion, competitiveness raises effort and identifies talent, and a society without it would stagnate. The current emphasis is nonetheless harmful, because treating comparison as the measure of a person produces anxious individuals who cannot work together and who avoid the risks that real progress requires.
