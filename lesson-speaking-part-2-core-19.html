Core Story · Habit

A Phone-Free Morning Walk

Core Story

Prompt

Describe a positive habit that you have developed.

You should say: - what the habit is - when you started it - how often you do it - and explain how it has improved your life.

OUTLINE

>>> A Phone-Free Morning Walk
positive habit • phone-free morning walk • about 30 minutes
started after checking messages immediately on waking
phone stays in pocket • quiet thinking • notice surroundings
clearer head • fewer reactive notifications • easy and free to maintain
mental transition from sleep to work • sustainable routine

Simple Model Answer

A positive habit I have developed is taking a morning walk without using my phone. I started because I noticed I was checking messages almost as soon as I woke up. Nothing was urgent, but I already felt busy before the day had really started.

I decided to walk for about half an hour with my phone left in my pocket. At first, it felt strange not to fill the time with information. After a week or two, I began enjoying the quiet. I could think about my plans, notice my surroundings and sometimes find answers to small problems.

The main benefit is a clearer mind before work. I do not feel as much need to react to every notification immediately. Walking also gives me some exercise, but the mental break matters most. This habit costs nothing and needs no special equipment. Because it is easy to fit into daily life, I have managed to keep doing it.

Key words and phrases

surroundings
The places and things around you.
notification
A message or alert sent by a device or app.

Advanced Model Answer

A positive habit I have developed is taking a short morning walk without looking at my phone.

The habit started when I noticed that I was checking messages almost immediately after waking up. Nothing urgent was happening, but the habit made the day feel busy before it had properly begun. I decided to leave my phone in my pocket and walk for about half an hour instead.

At first, it felt strangely unproductive because I was used to filling every spare minute with information. After a week or two, however, I started to value the quiet. I use the walk to think through the day, notice what is happening around me and sometimes solve small problems that felt confusing the night before.

The biggest benefit is not physical, although walking obviously helps with that. It is the mental transition between sleep and work. I begin the day with a clearer head and I am less likely to react immediately to every notification. The habit costs nothing, requires no special equipment and is easy to maintain, which is probably why it has lasted.
