An important river/ area of water in your country

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an important river/ area of water in your country

You should say: How long the river is Where it is What it looks like Why it is important

Simple Model Answer

An important river in Vietnam is the Mekong. It passes through several countries before reaching southwestern Vietnam and flowing into the sea. In Vietnam, it is also associated with the name River of the Nine Dragons.

The part I know best is the Delta. There are green rice fields, fruit gardens and many small canals. Boats travel between homes and markets along the waterways. I visited with my family a couple of summers ago and went on a boat trip. My clearest memory is passing a floating market at sunrise, with sellers calling to customers from their boats.

The river is important because so many people depend on it for farming and fishing. The Delta produces a large amount of rice and freshwater fish. It is also part of local culture, appearing in songs and stories. Visiting helped me understand that it is more than a beautiful view. It supports everyday life and work for many communities, which is why I consider it such an important river.

Key words and phrases

floating market
A market where goods are sold from boats.
depend on it
Need it in order to live or work successfully.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about the Mekong River, one of the biggest and most important rivers in Vietnam, flowing through the southwestern part of the country before it reaches the sea.

It's about 4,300 kilometres long in total, which makes it one of the longest rivers in Asia, though most of that length is actually in other countries before it even reaches Vietnam. Locally, people call it the River of the Nine Dragons, because since time immemorial, its smaller branches have been linked to the image of nine dragons — it even shows up in poems and songs because of that.

What it looks like really depends where you are. Up close, in the Delta, it's an incredible patchwork of green — rice paddies, orchards and little canals everywhere — with houses, markets and boats floating along waterways that criss-cross the landscape almost like veins. I went on a boat trip through part of it with my family a couple of summers ago, and I still remember drifting past a floating market at sunrise, with vendors calling out prices from their boats.

It's important mainly because of agriculture and fishing — the Delta's often called the rice bowl of the whole country, and it's Vietnam's most important region for freshwater fishing too. Honestly, without the Mekong, a huge part of Vietnam's food supply just wouldn't exist.

floating market

rice bowl
