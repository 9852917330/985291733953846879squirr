Situation

A situation that made you angry

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a situation that made you angry.

You should say: - when it happened - what happened - how you felt

Simple Model Answer

A situation that made me angry was getting stuck in traffic while going to meet friends last Sunday. It was lunchtime, and I had expected quiet roads because many people were not working.

At first, the journey was easy. Then I reached a long queue of cars and could not turn away. Roadworks had reduced the road to one lane, and getting past them took an extra thirty minutes. I felt frustrated because my friends were waiting and I did not know how long the delay would last.

What annoyed me most was the lack of warning. There had been no sign where I entered the road, and my map app had not mentioned the work. With earlier information, I could have chosen another route. When I finally arrived, my friends were relaxed about the delay. Their reaction made me realise that getting so angry had not helped. Since then, I have tried to check traffic conditions before leaving and allow more time for unexpected problems. It is a small habit that makes journeys less stressful.

Key words and phrases

Roadworks
Repairs or construction taking place on a road.
lack of warning
Not being told beforehand that a problem may happen.

Advanced Model Answer

A recent situation that made me angry was getting stuck in a traffic jam on the way to meet some friends. It happened at lunchtime last Sunday, and I hadn't expected the roads to be busy since most people weren't working.

Everything was going smoothly until I suddenly saw a long queue of cars ahead. I had no choice but to join it and wait to get past whatever was causing the delay. Eventually, I discovered that roadworks had reduced the road to a single lane, and it took me an extra thirty minutes to get past them.

Traffic congestion doesn't usually make me angry, but this time I felt frustrated and powerless. I knew my friends were waiting for me, yet there was nothing I could do and no way of knowing how long the delay would last. When I finally got past the roadworks, I was relieved to be moving again, but I was still stressed because I was already half an hour late. What annoyed me most, thinking about it afterwards, wasn't the delay itself but the lack of any warning. There was no sign at the junction where I'd turned in, and no notification on the map app I was using, even though the roadworks had apparently been there for several days already. Ten seconds of information would have let me take a completely different route.

My friends were totally relaxed about it when I finally arrived, which made me feel slightly foolish for having been so wound up. Since then I've made a point of checking the traffic before I set off, and it's saved me from the same situation more than once.
