Photography

Taking photographs and looking at other people's

Part 3

Prompt

Are you a keen photographer? Do you like looking at other people's photographs? Do you prefer to take a photo or film and why?

Are you a keen photographer?

Simple Model Answer

I take photos regularly, mainly of trips and family events, but I am not a serious photographer. I used to take huge numbers and never look at them. Now I try to take fewer and choose the best afterwards. That habit still disappears sometimes when I get excited and take many shots of the same thing.

Key words and phrases

shots
Individual photographs taken with a camera.

Advanced Model Answer

I take photographs fairly regularly, but I wouldn't call myself a photographer in any serious sense — it's mostly travel and family occasions. What's changed is that I've become far more selective. When phones made it free to take a thousand pictures, I did exactly that, and then never looked at any of them again. Now I take far fewer and actually choose between them afterwards, though I'll admit that discipline slips the moment something exciting happens and I end up back at fifty shots of the same view.

Key words and phrases

choose between them afterwards
make a decision among the options later

Do you like looking at other people's photographs?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, when they have been chosen carefully and tell a story. Older photos are especially interesting because they show how people and places have changed. Hundreds of similar holiday pictures are harder to enjoy because nothing stands out. I make an exception for close family, though, since even ordinary pictures of them can mean something to me.

Key words and phrases

stands out
Is more noticeable or memorable than the things around it.

Advanced Model Answer

It depends entirely on how they're presented. If someone's chosen twenty images that tell a story, I'm genuinely interested, especially photographs from an earlier period, because you can see how people dressed and what places looked like before they changed. What's difficult is being shown four hundred unedited pictures from someone's holiday, because the problem isn't the photographs themselves, it's that no selection's been made, so nothing stands out. The one exception is close family — I'll happily sit through all four hundred of those, because even a handful of good ones makes it worthwhile.

Key words and phrases

that tell a story
that communicate events or a clear idea
unedited pictures
photographs without changes made afterwards

Do you prefer to take a photo or film and why?

Simple Model Answer

Usually photos, because they are quick to look at again and leave room for imagination. Video is better for saving voices and movement, especially of relatives. However, it takes more storage and time to watch. Filming can also distract me from enjoying an event, so I choose it more carefully than taking a few pictures.

Key words and phrases

storage
Space available for keeping digital files.

Advanced Model Answer

Photographs, generally. A still image's quick to revisit — you take it in at a glance — and it leaves something to the imagination, because you don't know what happened just before or after. Video captures movement and voices, though, which is irreplaceable for recordings of relatives in particular — that's the one area where I'd choose it every time. But it demands time to watch and a lot of storage, and I've noticed that filming an event can stop you experiencing it properly.

Key words and phrases

at a glance
after a quick look
captures movement and voices
records things moving and the sounds people make
stop you experiencing it
prevent you from enjoying it directly
