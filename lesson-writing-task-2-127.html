Food & Diet

Is killing animals for food cruel?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people think that killing animals for food is cruel, while others claim that animals are a necessary part of the human diet. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Some people regard the killing of animals for food as fundamentally cruel, while others consider meat a necessary part of the human diet. In my view the objection to cruelty is justified, but it applies to how animals are raised and killed rather than to eating meat itself.

Those who object have a serious case. Most meat now comes from intensive farms where animals spend their lives in confined spaces, unable to move naturally, and are transported long distances before slaughter. Animals of this kind demonstrably feel pain and fear, and inflicting that on a large scale for the sake of a cheaper product is difficult to defend. Critics also point out that the necessity argument is weak in wealthy countries, where a varied plant-based diet is readily available.

The opposing view rests on biology and culture. Humans have eaten meat throughout their evolution, and it remains the most reliable source of iron, vitamin B12 and complete protein, which matters particularly for children and for people in regions where alternatives are scarce. Livestock also supports the livelihoods of hundreds of millions of small farmers.

My own position is that killing an animal for food is not in itself wrong, but causing unnecessary suffering is. The practical conclusion is that intensive farming should be reformed rather than that meat should be abandoned.

In conclusion, I do not accept that eating meat is cruel by definition, but I do believe that the way most meat is currently produced cannot be morally justified.

Key words and phrases

demonstrably feel pain and fear
Clearly capable of suffering and being afraid.
inflicting that on a large scale
Causing that suffering to many animals.
the necessity argument is weak in wealthy countries
Claiming it is essential is less convincing in rich countries.
a varied plant-based diet
Eating many different foods mainly from plants.
cannot be morally justified
Cannot be defended as ethically acceptable.
