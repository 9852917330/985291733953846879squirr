Ageing

Is growing old worse than it used to be?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people say that in the modern world, getting old is entirely bad. Others believe that old people's lives nowadays are much better than in the past. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Old age is described either as a period of neglect in a society that values youth or as the most comfortable it has ever been. My own view is that the second description is closer to the truth in material terms, while the first identifies something real about status.

The pessimistic account has evidence behind it. Older people once held authority because they carried knowledge nobody else had, and in a world where skills change every few years that authority has gone; an experienced worker is now more likely to be regarded as expensive than as valuable. Families have dispersed, so the daily contact that once made age bearable has been replaced by occasional visits, and loneliness among the elderly is now common enough to appear in health statistics. Pensions are under pressure, and a longer life is not obviously a gift if the money runs out first.

The optimistic account rests on comparison. Life expectancy has risen by decades, and those years are healthier: hips are replaced, cataracts removed and heart conditions managed, so people remain active at seventy in ways their grandparents were not at fifty. State pensions and public health care, which barely existed a century ago, mean far fewer older people live in poverty. Technology keeps distant families in contact, and travel, study and second careers are now normal after retirement rather than eccentric.

Weighing these, the material improvement is beyond argument, and few would exchange today's old age for that of a hundred years ago. What has genuinely worsened is position: the elderly are comfortable but consulted less, and a society that treats them as a cost rather than a resource wastes them.

In conclusion, growing old today is healthier, longer and more secure than at any earlier time, so calling it entirely bad is wrong. The loss of respect and daily company is real, and that, rather than the condition of the body, is what most needs repairing.

Key words and phrases

comfortable but consulted less
Living well but asked for opinions less.
the condition of the body
A person's physical health.
