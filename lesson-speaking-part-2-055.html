Place

A street you know well

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a street you know well

You should say: Where it is What the main buildings are How often you go there And explain how you feel about this street

OUTLINE

>>> Ly Thai To Street
few streets could talk about, one enjoy most is Ly Thai To, popular tourist area Hanoi
centre of city, near Hoan Kiem Lake, 15-20 min drive, go by scooter
main buildings: old French colonial villas, short walk from Hanoi Opera House (grandest building)
how often: once/twice a month, don't have more time
mainly go for street food (squid/bacon sandwiches/chicken wings), souvenir shops overpriced
like it bc food, closed off to traffic (space to walk), old buildings give old-world charm

Simple Model Answer

A street I know well is Ly Thai To Street in central Hanoi, near Hoan Kiem Lake. It takes me about fifteen to twenty minutes to get there by scooter. I usually visit once or twice a month.

The street has many attractive old buildings, including French-style villas with high ceilings and shutters. It is also near the Hanoi Opera House. I enjoy walking there and looking at the architecture, but my main reason for visiting is the food. Stalls sell things such as grilled squid, chicken wings and sandwiches.

There are souvenir and craft shops too, although I rarely buy anything because prices seem high. For example, postcards can cost much more than in an ordinary bookshop. What I appreciate most is having space to walk when the street is closed to traffic. I can enjoy the food and old buildings without constantly watching for scooters. The combination of good snacks and a relaxed walk makes this one of my favourite parts of the city.

Key words and phrases

architecture
The design and style of buildings.
closed to traffic
Not allowing cars, motorbikes or other vehicles to enter.

Advanced Model Answer

Well, there are quite a few streets I could talk about, but the one I most enjoy going to is Ly Thai To Street, which is one of the most popular tourist areas in Hanoi.

It's right in the centre of the city, close to Hoan Kiem Lake, about fifteen to twenty minutes' drive from where I live, so I normally just hop on my scooter and go. In terms of buildings, it's mostly lined with colonial-style architecture — old French villas with high ceilings and shuttered windows — and it's only a short walk from the Hanoi Opera House, which is easily the grandest building in that part of town.

As for how often I go, honestly, probably only once or twice a month. I know that's not a lot, but I just don't have the time to go as often as I'd like.

Most of the time I go for the street food, if I'm being honest — there are loads of stalls doing really good barbecued squid, bacon sandwiches, chicken wings, that kind of thing, so that's basically my main reason for going. There are also a few little shops selling souvenirs and handicrafts, but I don't spend long in them because everything's wildly overpriced — a pack of postcards there will probably set you back about 30,000 dong, whereas the same thing in any bookshop would only cost about a third of that.

But in terms of why I actually like the street, it's mainly the food, like I said, plus the fact that it's closed off to traffic, so you've got loads of space to walk around without worrying about getting hit by a scooter. And because most of the buildings are so old, the whole street's just got this really nice, old-world charm to it.

colonial-style architecture

the grandest building

wildly overpriced
