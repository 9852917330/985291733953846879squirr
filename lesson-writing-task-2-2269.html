Environment

The cost of making more goods

Causes / Solutions

Prompt

The increase in the production of consumer goods results in damage to the natural environment. What are the causes of this? What can be done to solve this problem?

Model answer

Factories now produce more clothing, electronics and furniture than at any point in history, and the environmental cost is visible in emissions, landfill and polluted rivers. The causes lie in how goods are designed and priced, and the solutions must change both.

The first cause is that products are built to be replaced. Clothing is made cheaply enough to be worn a few times, and appliances are designed so that a failed component cannot be replaced without buying the whole machine, which means each purchase must be manufactured, shipped and eventually discarded. Marketing reinforces this by presenting last year's model as embarrassing rather than adequate, so goods are thrown away while still perfectly capable of working.

The second cause is that prices do not reflect damage. A manufacturer pays for materials and labour but not for the river it pollutes or the carbon it releases, so the cheapest production method is usually the dirtiest and the cost is carried by the public instead.

The solutions follow directly. Making producers legally responsible for their products at the end of life gives them a financial reason to design goods that can be repaired and dismantled, and this has already reduced electronic waste where it has been applied. Pricing pollution through carbon taxes removes the advantage currently enjoyed by dirty production. Requiring spare parts to be available for a fixed number of years would also make repair a realistic option again.

In conclusion, the damage arises because goods are designed for a short life and their true cost is never charged. It can be reduced by making manufacturers responsible for what they produce and by ensuring that the price of an item includes the harm it causes.

Key words and phrases

perfectly capable of working
Fully able to function properly.
carried by the public instead
Paid for by society rather than businesses.
make repair a realistic option
Make fixing things affordable and practical.
includes the harm it causes
Takes account of the resulting damage.
