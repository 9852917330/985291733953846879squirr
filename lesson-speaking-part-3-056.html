History

Whether studying history prevents repeated mistakes

Part 3

Prompt

Studying history can help countries to avoid repeating past mistakes. Do you agree or not? How can studying your own country's history help you understand present-day situations? How important do you consider it to study the history of other countries?

Studying history can help countries to avoid repeating past mistakes. Do you agree or not?

Simple Model Answer

I agree that it can help, but only if people use it honestly. History shows how crises and conflicts develop. The difficulty is deciding whether a past situation really matches the present. Leaders may choose only examples supporting their existing plans. Lessons are useful when people are willing to question their own views, not just defend them.

Key words and phrases

existing plans
Plans that have already been made.

Advanced Model Answer

I agree in principle, though the record suggests it works less often than it should. History does reveal patterns — how financial crises build, how conflicts escalate, what happens when a country isolates itself economically. The difficulty is recognising which past situation actually resembles the present one, because leaders tend to select the analogy that supports what they'd already decided to do anyway. So the knowledge only helps where there's a genuine willingness to be corrected.

How can studying your own country's history help you understand present-day situations?

Simple Model Answer

It explains why institutions, regions and political arguments are the way they are. Without that background, current events can seem to appear from nowhere. However, history needs to include mistakes and uncomfortable events, not only achievements. A story designed just to make people proud may hide information they need to understand their country today.

Key words and phrases

institutions
Established organisations or systems that shape public life.

Advanced Model Answer

It explains things that otherwise look arbitrary — why institutions are organised as they are, why particular regions are wealthier, why certain political arguments recur every generation. Without that background, current events seem to come from nowhere. The important condition is that it's taught critically, including the uncomfortable episodes. A national history presented purely as a patriotic story doesn't explain the present; it simply produces people who are easy to mislead about it.

Key words and phrases

come from nowhere
appear unexpectedly
a patriotic story
a tale designed to create love for one's country

How important do you consider it to study the history of other countries?

Simple Model Answer

It is very important because trade, migration and war have connected countries for centuries. Another country's account can also challenge our own assumptions about the same event. Nobody can study every place deeply, so I would start with neighbours, major trading partners and countries closely linked to our history. That makes learning both useful and manageable.

Key words and phrases

assumptions
Ideas accepted as true without being fully checked.

Advanced Model Answer

Very important, because no country developed on its own. Trade, migration and war connected societies long before anyone used the word globalisation, and you can't understand your own country's position without that. There's a second benefit as well: reading how another society understands the same events is the fastest cure for assuming that your own version is simply the truth rather than one account among several. Nobody can study every country in depth, of course, so the practical priority should be the histories most entangled with your own — former colonisers, close neighbours, major trading partners — rather than an impossible attempt at everything.

Key words and phrases

simply the truth
only what actually happened
the practical priority
the most important thing to do in real life
