Environment & Nature

Human effects on wild animals and protecting wildlife

Prompt

What effects do you think humans have on wild animals? What measures could we take to protect wildlife? Is it the responsibility of schools to teach children about protecting wildlife?

What effects do you think humans have on wild animals?

Simple Model Answer

Humans destroy habitats by clearing forests or changing land for farming and building. Pollution, fishing and climate change cause further harm. Roads also separate animal groups, making movement and breeding harder. Some adaptable animals do well in cities, but many cannot use those environments. Protecting a species is difficult if it no longer has a suitable place to live.

Key words and phrases

habitats
The natural places where particular animals or plants live.

Advanced Model Answer

Habitat destruction's the largest by a long way — clearing forests and draining wetlands for agriculture removes the places animals actually live, and no amount of protection helps a species with nowhere left to go. Beyond that, pollution, overfishing and climate change all take a heavy toll. There's a subtler effect too: roads and cities fragment habitats into isolated patches, so populations can no longer migrate or interbreed, and small isolated groups tend to die out gradually without anyone really noticing. It's not universally bad news, though — a handful of adaptable species, foxes and certain birds, have actually thrived alongside humans by exploiting urban environments most others can't use.

Key words and phrases

fragment habitats into isolated patches
divide animals' natural homes into small separate areas
urban environments
places in towns and cities

What measures could we take to protect wildlife?

Simple Model Answer

We should protect habitats, support parks properly and enforce laws against illegal hunting. Reducing demand for wildlife products is also important. Local people need benefits from conservation, especially if animals damage their crops or land use is restricted. Protection is more likely to succeed when communities have a reason to support it, rather than only being told what not to do.

Key words and phrases

conservation
Protecting nature and using its resources carefully.

Advanced Model Answer

Protecting habitat's the foundation, since everything else is secondary to having somewhere to live. That means properly funded national parks with actual rangers, not simply lines drawn on a map. Enforcing anti-poaching law matters too, along with reducing demand in the countries that buy wildlife products. And local communities have to benefit financially from it, because conservation imposed on people who lose crops or land to it will always be resisted, whatever the law says on paper — a farmer who's lost his harvest to elephants isn't going to feel any goodwill towards the park next door.

Key words and phrases

not simply lines drawn on a map
more than boundaries shown on a map
feel any goodwill
have any friendly feeling towards them

Is it the responsibility of schools to teach children about protecting wildlife?

Simple Model Answer

Schools should help, but they are not the only ones responsible. Learning about nature can build concern that lasts into adulthood, and children may influence family habits too. Visits to forests or nature reserves can make the subject more meaningful than textbooks alone. It is easier to care about something after seeing and experiencing it.

Key words and phrases

nature reserves
Areas protected so that wildlife and natural habitats can survive.

Advanced Model Answer

Schools have a clear role, though I wouldn't say the responsibility's theirs alone. Children who learn about ecosystems and endangered species early tend to keep that concern into adulthood, and they often influence their parents' behaviour at home, which is a genuinely useful side effect. What works better than classroom lessons, though, is direct contact: a single trip to a forest or a nature reserve tends to do more than a term of textbook material, simply because it's harder to care about a species you've never actually seen.

Key words and phrases

influence their parents' behaviour at home
change what their parents do in the household
a term of textbook material
a school term spent studying from a book
