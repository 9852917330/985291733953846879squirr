Situation

A time when you were not allowed to use your cell phone

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you were not allowed to use your cell phone

You should say: Where you were When it happened What you were doing And why you were not allowed to use your cell phone

Simple Model Answer

A time I was not allowed to use my phone was during my high school graduation exam two years ago. It was an important test because it affected both finishing school and my future university plans.

Phones were not permitted in the exam room because they could be used to gain an unfair advantage. I understood the reason, but being without mine still felt uncomfortable. When practising at home, I had developed a habit of checking answers on my phone whenever I felt unsure.

During the real exam, I sometimes wanted to reach for it automatically. Instead, I had to trust the answers I had worked out myself. I remained uncertain about some questions until I returned home and checked the answer key. Looking back, the experience taught me something useful about my habits. A phone can support learning, but relying on it for immediate reassurance can reduce confidence. I realised I needed to become more comfortable making decisions without checking everything at once. The rule was sensible, even though following it made me notice my dependence on the device.

Key words and phrases

unfair advantage
Help that makes a competition or test unequal.
reassurance
Something that makes a person feel less worried or uncertain.

Advanced Model Answer

Mobile phones have basically become the must-have item for everyone these days - we can't seem to live without them. But I still remember one time I had to be apart from my phone completely: during my high school graduation exam two years ago, and it honestly felt quite uncomfortable.

That exam is compulsory in Vietnam - you have to sit it at the end of grade 12 to get your diploma, and your score also decides whether you can get into university. It's one of the biggest events in a student's life, really, because it doesn't just shape your future, it's also a way of proving to yourself what you're capable of.

Naturally, phones aren't allowed anywhere near the exam room, so nobody can get an unfair advantage by cheating. If your phone even makes a noise, or if you're caught using it at any point, including during breaks, you can be disqualified on the spot, and your results get cancelled, no exceptions.

I'll admit I had a bit of a habit of checking my phone whenever I was doing practice tests at home, just to see if I'd got the right answers. So during the actual exam, I kept feeling this urge to reach for it, but obviously I couldn't, so I just had to sit with the uncertainty. Not knowing whether I'd got things right was honestly more stressful than the exam itself, and I didn't really relax until I'd got home and checked everything against the answer key.

Looking back, that experience made me realise I rely too much on my phone for reassurance. It's a brilliant tool for studying, but I think it also taught me to trust my own judgement a bit more, instead of needing to double-check everything straight away.
