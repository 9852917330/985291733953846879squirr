Advertising & Consumerism

Does more advertising do more good than harm?

Essay

Prompt

Do the positive effects outweigh negative effects, when advertising gains in popularity?

Model answer

Advertising has become almost impossible to avoid, and its growth is more often treated as a problem than as a benefit. In my view, although some of the criticism is deserved, the advantages of widespread advertising outweigh its drawbacks.

The clearest benefit is information. A consumer who has never heard of a product cannot buy it, and without advertising people would be limited to the few brands sold in their nearest shop. Comparison of prices, features and guarantees is what allows a buyer to make an informed choice, and public prices also force sellers to compete rather than quietly charge more than their rivals. The frequent claim that advertising simply manipulates people is hard to sustain when most new products fail despite large campaigns.

Advertising also pays for a great deal that the public enjoys without noticing. Commercial television, radio, most news websites and nearly all free online services are funded by advertisers rather than by their audiences, and sport at every level depends on sponsorship. Without that income, either these services disappear or the public pays for them directly. By moving goods quickly, advertising also supports the steady production and employment on which economic development depends.

The strongest objection is that advertising persuades people to buy things they do not need, and that some of it rests on misleading claims about what a product will do. This is a real cost, but a manageable one. Most countries now regulate advertising directly, requiring evidence for health claims and banning tobacco promotion outright, and audiences are far more sceptical than they were: reviews written by other customers now influence a purchase more than the campaign that preceded it.

In conclusion, advertising does encourage spending, and it is not the neutral public service its defenders describe. Its growth nevertheless brings better information, sharper competition and the funding of services that audiences would otherwise pay for themselves, while regulation deals with its worst excesses. On balance, the positive effects are the greater.
