Crime & Punishment

Should young offenders be treated as adults?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that young people who commit serious crimes should be punished in the same way as adults. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

When a teenager commits a violent crime, the demand that age should make no difference is understandable. I disagree with it, however, because the reasons we punish adults as we do apply only partly to the young.

The argument for equal treatment rests on the harm done. A victim is no less injured because the attacker was sixteen, and a system that hands down light sentences for serious offences looks unjust to the family involved. There is also a fear that lenient courts will be exploited, with older criminals using teenagers to carry out the work. These points deserve a serious answer rather than dismissal.

The answer is that responsibility depends on judgement, and judgement is not complete at sixteen. The parts of the brain governing impulse and consequence continue developing into the mid-twenties, which is why young people offend in groups, on impulse and with little thought of what follows. Most also stop: the great majority of teenage offenders commit no crimes as adults, so a long sentence punishes a person who would have grown out of it anyway. Prison makes that worse, since a young man placed among experienced criminals learns from them and leaves with no qualifications and a permanent record.

This is not an argument for doing nothing. Serious crimes should carry serious consequences, including detention where the public must be protected. The difference is that a young offender's sentence should be built around education, treatment and supervision, and courts should retain the power to punish the most extreme cases severely.

In conclusion, treating children exactly as adults ignores what is known about how they think and how they change. Firm but separate treatment, aimed at stopping the next offence rather than matching the last one, protects the public more effectively.

Key words and phrases

using teenagers to carry out the work
Employing young people to perform criminal tasks.
would have grown out of it anyway
Would naturally have stopped the behaviour while maturing.
no qualifications and a permanent record
Lacking certificates and having a lasting criminal history.
