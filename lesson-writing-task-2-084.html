Education

Music, art and drama against other subjects

opinion

Prompt

Some say that music, art and drama are as important as other school subjects, especially at the primary level. Do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Music, art and drama deserve an important place in primary education. They should not replace literacy, mathematics or science, but they support children's development in ways that academic subjects alone cannot.

Creative subjects help young children express ideas before they have the vocabulary to explain them fully. Drawing, singing and acting also develop concentration, coordination and confidence. A child who performs in a short play must listen to others, remember a sequence and manage nervousness. These are transferable skills that support learning across the curriculum.

The arts also make school more inclusive. Some pupils who struggle with written tests may show strong visual, musical or practical ability. Giving these children a chance to succeed can improve motivation and attendance. In addition, creative activities introduce students to their own culture and to unfamiliar traditions, helping them develop curiosity and cultural awareness.

There is also a longer-term argument about talent. Creative industries such as design, film and digital media now employ a significant share of the workforce, and ability in these fields often first appears at primary age. A pupil who never draws, performs or composes may simply never discover a talent worth developing, much as a footballer would never be discovered without ever being given a ball. Spotting this kind of talent early is arguably as valuable as anything taught in a standard lesson, provided timetables protect enough regular time for these subjects rather than treating them as an occasional extra.

In conclusion, primary schools should treat the arts as a core part of a balanced education, while still protecting enough time for basic academic skills. The aim is not to train every child as an artist, but to use creative work to build confident, observant and cooperative learners.
