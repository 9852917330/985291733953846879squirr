Work & Employment

Would younger leaders be better?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Leaders and directors in organisations are normally older people. Some people think that having a younger leader would be better. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Boardrooms and cabinets are dominated by people in their fifties and sixties, and the suggestion that younger leaders would do better is heard more often each year. I partly agree: age is a poor qualification in itself, but replacing it with youth would repeat the same mistake.

The case for younger leaders is strongest where the world is changing quickly. Someone who grew up with the technology their company sells understands its customers without translation, and younger leaders tend to be less attached to methods that once worked. They also have no reputation to protect from a change of direction, which makes difficult decisions easier, and they will personally live with the long-term consequences of choices about climate, debt and pensions, so they weigh them differently.

Experience nonetheless earns its position. Judgement is largely a store of remembered failures, and a leader who has managed a recession, a scandal or a lost contract recognises the early signs. Much of leadership is also negotiation, and knowing which people to call and how far they can be pushed is knowledge that cannot be acquired quickly. Confidence without that store produces expensive errors, as several young founders have demonstrated.

The real objection to the present arrangement is not that leaders are old but that promotion often follows length of service rather than ability, so capable people wait for their turn while weak leaders remain in place until retirement. The answer is to select on evidence of judgement and to build teams of mixed ages, where the young supply energy and current knowledge and the experienced supply pattern recognition.

In conclusion, younger leaders would improve many organisations, particularly fast-moving ones. Youth is not a qualification either, and the sensible reform is to stop treating age as a proxy for competence in either direction.

Key words and phrases

knowledge that cannot be acquired quickly
Understanding that needs years to develop.
weak leaders remain in place until retirement
Ineffective managers keep their positions until they retire.
stop treating age as a proxy for competence
Stop assuming age reliably indicates job ability.
