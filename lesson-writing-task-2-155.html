Cities & Urban Life

Knocking down old buildings

Opinion

Prompt

People think that old buildings should be knocked down and give way to the new buildings. Do you agree or disagree? How important are old buildings to us?

Model answer

Some old buildings should be protected, while others should be replaced when they are unsafe or no longer useful. I therefore disagree with the idea that age alone is a reason either to preserve or demolish a structure. Decisions should be based on historical value, condition and the needs of the surrounding community.

Important old buildings connect people with the history of a place. A traditional market, theatre or government building may show how earlier generations lived and what materials or techniques they used. Such structures can strengthen local identity, support tourism and provide architects with practical examples of earlier design. Once a unique building is destroyed, its cultural value cannot be recreated by a modern copy.

Nevertheless, preservation has limits. Some buildings are structurally dangerous, extremely expensive to maintain or poorly suited to modern needs. Keeping every ageing structure can prevent the construction of housing, schools or transport links in areas where land is scarce. In these cases, demolition may be justified after an independent assessment. Useful parts of the original building, such as a façade or significant interior features, can sometimes be incorporated into a new development.

The best policy is a clear classification system. Buildings of genuine architectural or historical importance should receive legal protection and realistic maintenance funding. Ordinary structures should be assessed more flexibly, with public consultation and a full comparison of renovation and replacement costs. This creates a balance between heritage and present-day needs.

In conclusion, old buildings are important when they carry distinctive cultural, historical or design value. Others may reasonably give way to safer and more useful development, provided that the decision is based on evidence rather than short-term profit.

Key words and phrases

its cultural value cannot be recreated
Its historical or cultural importance cannot be reproduced.
poorly suited to modern needs
Not appropriate for how people live today.
incorporated into a new development
Included as part of a new building project.
based on evidence rather than short-term profit
Decided using facts rather than quick financial gains.
