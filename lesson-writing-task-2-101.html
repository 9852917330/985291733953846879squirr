Advertising & Consumerism

Does advertising promote quantity over quality?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Advertising encourages consumers to buy in quantity rather than in quality. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes argued that advertising persuades people to buy as much as possible rather than to buy well. I largely agree with this view, although the criticism applies to some kinds of advertising far more than to others.

Most advertising is designed to increase the volume of sales, and its methods reflect that aim. Campaigns emphasise price reductions, multi-buy offers and limited-time deals, all of which encourage people to purchase more than they intended. Fashion and technology advertising works differently but towards the same end, by presenting last year's perfectly functional clothes or phones as embarrassing, so that consumers replace items that are nowhere near worn out. The result is visible in the quantity of clothing, electronics and food that households throw away every year.

Advertising also tends to avoid the information that would allow a genuine judgement of quality. Very few advertisements mention durability, repairability or running costs, because these do not encourage frequent repurchase, and attention is directed instead towards image, celebrity endorsement and lifestyle.

I would accept that this is not universal. Advertising for cars, insurance and household appliances often does compare specifications, safety ratings and reliability, because buyers of expensive items research them carefully. Competition in these markets has arguably raised standards, since a manufacturer with a poor reliability record cannot conceal it.

In conclusion, I agree that most advertising promotes consumption rather than careful choice, and that this contributes to considerable waste. The exception is expensive goods, where consumers demand real information and advertisers are obliged to provide it.

Key words and phrases

nowhere near worn out
Still in very good usable condition.
durability, repairability or running costs
How long it lasts, repair options and costs.
celebrity endorsement
A famous person promoting a product.
cannot conceal it
Cannot hide it.
obliged to provide it
Required to supply it.
