Arts & Museums

Returning historical objects

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Historical objects in museums all over the world should be returned to their country of origin. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Many of the most famous objects in European museums were removed from countries under colonial rule, and pressure to return them has grown. I largely agree that they should go back, though I would not apply the principle to every object without exception.

The central argument is about how the objects were obtained. A sculpture taken during a military occupation, from a people who had no power to refuse, was not acquired in any meaningful sense, and continuing to display it treats that history as settled. Many of these objects were also religious or ceremonial rather than decorative, so removing them stripped them of their purpose as well as their setting. Returning them acknowledges a wrong in the only way that costs anything.

There is a practical argument too. Museums hold far more than they can exhibit, and objects that sit in storage for decades in one country would be central exhibits in another, seen by the people whose ancestors made them and studied by scholars who understand the context. Access is currently reversed for the communities most concerned, who must travel abroad to see their own heritage.

I would accept two limits. Where a country genuinely cannot yet protect an object from war or theft, delay is reasonable, provided it is a delay rather than a refusal. Objects bought openly and fairly also stand in a different position from those seized, and treating the two as though they were identical weakens the case.

In conclusion, I agree that historical objects should generally be returned, because ownership acquired by force is not ownership. The argument is strongest for objects taken without consent, and it can be met while keeping the world's collections connected through long-term loans in both directions.

Key words and phrases

stripped them of their purpose
Removed their original meaning or function.
reversed for the communities most concerned
Changed back for the people most affected.
as though they were identical
As if there were no differences.
long-term loans in both directions
Extended loans exchanged between both sides.
