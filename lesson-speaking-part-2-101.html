Object & Favourite

An important plant in your country

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an important plant in your country.

You should say: - what the plant is - how you know about it - why it is important - and explain how you feel about this plant.

Simple Model Answer

An important plant in Vietnam is bamboo. I have known it since childhood because it grows around villages, fields and rivers. My grandfather planted a bamboo hedge beside his house to reduce wind and help hold the soil in place.

Bamboo has many practical uses. People make baskets, furniture, fences and chopsticks from it. Young bamboo shoots are also used in cooking. Because it grows quickly, it has long been a useful material for families, especially before metal and plastic became so common.

It is important in culture as well. In Vietnamese stories and writing, bamboo often represents the ability to stay strong during difficult times. It can bend in bad weather and then return upright, which makes that comparison easy to understand. I like bamboo partly because it is so familiar. It is not always treated as a beautiful plant, but it quietly supports everyday life in many ways. If it disappeared from villages, both the scenery and people's practical routines would feel very different.

Key words and phrases

bamboo shoots
The young new stems of bamboo that can be prepared as food.
return upright
Move back into a straight, standing position.

Advanced Model Answer

The plant I've chosen to talk about is bamboo, probably the most useful plant in Vietnam after rice, though it gets far less attention.

I've known about it since I was small because it grows everywhere — around villages, along riverbanks and at the edge of almost every field. My grandparents' house had a bamboo hedge that my grandfather planted as a windbreak, and I remember being told never to cut it, because it was holding the soil together.

It's important for two quite different reasons. The practical one is that almost anything can be made from it: scaffolding, furniture, baskets, fencing, chopsticks, even food, since bamboo shoots are a normal ingredient. It grows extraordinarily fast, so a household could cut what it needed and the plant would replace itself within a couple of years. Before cheap steel and plastic became widely available, a rural family relied on it constantly.

The second reason is cultural. Bamboo turns up throughout Vietnamese literature as a symbol of resilience, because it bends a long way in a storm without snapping and then straightens again. That image gets used so often it's almost become a cliche, but I think it's a fair one.

I'm quite fond of it, partly out of habit. It's not a spectacular plant, and nobody photographs it, but it's one of those things you would notice immediately if it disappeared.

Keeping earth in place with roots.
