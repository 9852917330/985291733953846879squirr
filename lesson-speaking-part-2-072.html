Object & Favourite

A movie you would like to watch again

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a movie you would like to watch again.

You should say: What it was about When and where you watched it Who you watched it with And explain why you would like to watch it again.

Simple Model Answer

A film I would like to watch again is Love, Rosie, a romantic comedy starring Lily Collins and Sam Claflin. It is based on the book Where Rainbows End. I first watched it at the cinema in 2014 with my best friend.

We chose it after seeing a trailer online. The pictures and music attracted us, so we booked tickets that evening. The story follows two childhood friends who have feelings for each other but repeatedly miss the chance to say so. They eventually find their way back together, although many sad things happen first.

I enjoyed the film more than I expected and cried during some scenes. The main reason I want to see it again is the dialogue. Some conversations are funny, while others contain ideas about love and life that I understood personally. A second viewing might help me notice details I missed. I also like films that make me feel several emotions rather than only entertaining me. This one stayed in my mind long after leaving the cinema.

Key words and phrases

based on
Developed from an existing story or idea.
dialogue
The words spoken between characters in a film or story.

Advanced Model Answer

The film I'd love to watch again is Love, Rosie — a romantic comedy starring Lily Collins and Sam Claflin. It's actually an adaptation of a book called Where Rainbows End, which was an Irish best seller back in 2004.

I remember watching it for the first time in 2014, not long after it hit the big screen. I went with my best friend, who's just as much of a film buff as I am. We'd been scrolling through trailers online, and this one caught our attention straight away because of the cinematography and the soundtrack, so we booked tickets that same evening.

It ended up completely exceeding our expectations. The story follows two childhood friends who clearly have feelings for each other but keep missing their chance to say so, over years and years of near-misses. It does end happily, with them finally finding their way back to each other, but there are so many heartbreaking moments along the way that I was properly crying in the cinema by the end.

The main reason I'd watch it again is the dialogue — it's witty, but it's also full of these little lines about life and love that actually stuck with me. I could relate to so much of what the characters said, and I think I'd pick up on even more of it on a second watch. That's why it's stayed one of my favourites.
