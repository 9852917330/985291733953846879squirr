Object & Favourite

A toy that was special to you when you were a child

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a toy that was special to you when you were a child.

You should say: - when you got it - what it looked like - who gave it to you

Simple Model Answer

A special childhood toy was a Lego car my parents gave me for my birthday when I was about ten or eleven. It came in a box containing hundreds of small pieces and instructions showing how to build the finished model.

The car was quite complicated. It had an engine, movable seats and gears, so I needed to concentrate carefully. Building it took me a day or two. I felt proud when I finally completed it, although I did not play with it very much afterwards. The enjoyable part had been making it.

My father helped during the first hour. We sat at the kitchen table, sorted pieces by colour and followed the instructions together. Looking back, that shared time is my strongest memory of the toy. I think my parents chose it because they knew I liked solving problems and could become bored with simpler toys. It taught me patience and gave me something useful to do for more than one afternoon. More importantly, it gave my father and me a project to enjoy together.

Key words and phrases

gears
Parts with teeth that turn together to move a machine or mechanism.
shared time
Time spent doing something together with another person.

Advanced Model Answer

One special toy that I remember getting was a Lego car. It was a birthday present from my parents. I can't remember exactly how old I was, but I was probably about 10 or 11. The special thing about this car was that I had to build it myself out of hundreds of pieces of Lego. The pieces came in a box with a picture of the finished car on the front, and I had to follow step-by-step instructions to put all the pieces together in the correct way.

This wasn't an easy task because the car even had an engine, movable seats and gears. It took me a day or two to make, and required a lot of concentration. When the car was finished it looked great, and I felt a sense of accomplishment. I seem to remember that I didn't play with the car very much; the fun part had been the process of building it.

Looking back, I think that is exactly why my parents chose it. They knew I got bored with toys that did everything for you, and they wanted something that would keep me occupied for more than an afternoon. My father actually sat with me for the first hour, sorting the pieces into piles by colour, and that is probably the part I remember most fondly now — not the car itself, but the two of us working through the instructions together at the kitchen table.
