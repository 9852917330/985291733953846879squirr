Food & Diet

Should countries grow all their own food?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Countries should try to produce all the food for their population and import as little food as possible. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Recent shortages and rising shipping costs have revived the idea that every country should feed itself. I agree that nations should protect a core level of domestic production, but I disagree with the stronger claim that imports should be minimised.

The case for growing your own food is a case about risk. A country that buys most of its grain abroad is exposed to any war, export ban or bad harvest elsewhere, and food is the one commodity a government cannot allow to run short. Domestic farming also keeps rural communities alive, protects the knowledge and land needed to expand production in a crisis, and cuts the emissions of moving food across oceans. These are serious arguments, and no state should depend entirely on somebody else's fields.

Total self-sufficiency, though, is either impossible or ruinous. Climate and soil decide what will grow: coffee, bananas and rice cannot be produced in northern Europe, and Gulf states have neither the water nor the land. Forcing production against nature means heated glasshouses, heavy irrigation and chemical inputs, so the environmental cost usually exceeds the cost of shipping. Diets would narrow, prices would rise, and the poorest households would feel it first.

There is also a hidden danger in relying on one country's harvest alone. A single drought or plant disease then affects everything at once, whereas buying from several regions spreads the risk across different climates. Trade is not only cheaper; it is often safer.

In conclusion, every country should retain enough farming to survive a disruption and to keep its rural economy working. Aiming to grow everything would raise prices, damage the environment and, by concentrating the risk at home, make the food supply less secure rather than more.

depend entirely on somebody else's fields
Rely completely on food grown elsewhere.
exceeds the cost of shipping
Is greater than transport expenses.
spreads the risk across different climates
Avoid relying on one region's weather.
concentrating the risk at home
Making the country depend on its own weather.
