Children & Family

Limiting screens and encouraging books

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people say that parents should place restrictions on the hours their children spend watching television and playing computer games, and should encourage them to spend this time reading books instead. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Most children would happily spend every free hour in front of a screen, and many now do. I agree that parents should set limits, though I think the second half of the suggestion, swapping those hours directly for books, works only if it is handled with some care.

Limits are justified because screen time crowds out everything else. A child absorbed in a game is not sleeping, moving, talking to anyone or being bored, and boredom is where children invent things. Games and streaming services are also built to hold attention: episodes play automatically and rewards arrive on a schedule, so stopping requires more willpower than a child has. Setting an hour is not a punishment but a substitute for self-control the child has not yet developed.

Reading is a reasonable thing to steer them towards. It builds vocabulary and concentration in a way no other activity matches, and a child who reads fluently by ten finds every school subject easier. Sitting with a difficult page also teaches patience, because the reward comes slowly rather than every few seconds.

The plan fails, however, when reading is imposed as the price of the screen being switched off. Forced reading teaches children that books are a chore, which is the opposite of the intention. What works is making books available and attractive, letting children choose whatever they enjoy, comics included, and having parents read visibly themselves. Nor should the freed hours all go to reading; sport and unstructured play deserve a share.

In conclusion, parents are right to cap the hours their children spend on screens, since those devices are designed to be hard to put down. Books should be offered generously rather than enforced, because a child who is made to read stops wanting to.

Key words and phrases

stopping requires more willpower than a child has
Resisting the habit needs more self-control than children possess.
the reward comes slowly
The benefit takes time to appear.
read visibly themselves
Let children see them reading regularly.
made to read stops wanting to
Forced reading can remove the desire to read.
