Emotions

Showing anger, and whether men and women differ

Prompt

Do you think it is good to show your emotions when you are angry? In your opinion, do women show their emotions more than men? Why do you think men tend to show their emotions less than women?

Do you think it is good to show your emotions when you are angry?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, if you explain the anger calmly. Hiding it does not necessarily solve the problem and may lead to another argument later. Saying what upset you can help. Shouting or insulting someone usually makes them defend themselves instead of listen, though. The important thing is expressing the feeling clearly without attacking the other person.

Key words and phrases

defend themselves
Try to protect themselves from criticism or blame.

Advanced Model Answer

It's better than concealing it, but how you show it decides everything. Suppressing anger doesn't remove it — it usually resurfaces later as resentment, or comes out in some completely unrelated argument a week later over something trivial like the washing-up. Saying clearly what caused the problem, while you're still able to speak calmly, generally resolves it. What doesn't work is shouting or insulting somebody, because the other person stops listening to the complaint and starts defending themselves against the attack instead — at which point nothing productive is actually being said.

Key words and phrases

resurfaces later as resentment
returns later as lasting anger
starts defending themselves against the attack
focuses on protecting themselves from criticism

In your opinion, do women show their emotions more than men?

Simple Model Answer

Women may sometimes show emotion more openly, but that does not prove they feel more. Children often learn different rules about expressing feelings: girls may be allowed more freedom, while boys are discouraged. Individual differences are large, though. I know expressive men and quiet women, so I would avoid judging a person mainly by gender.

Key words and phrases

expressive
Showing thoughts and feelings openly.

Advanced Model Answer

Outwardly, often yes, but I'd question whether they actually feel more. The difference looks largely learned to me — in most cultures girls are permitted a wider range of emotional expression, while boys are steered towards a narrower one from a young age. So the same feeling gets displayed differently rather than experienced differently. The variation between individuals is far larger than the variation between the sexes, though; I know men who are highly expressive and women who reveal almost nothing, my own brother and sister being a perfect example of exactly that.

Key words and phrases

a wider range of emotional expression
more ways of showing different feelings

Why do you think men tend to show their emotions less than women?

Simple Model Answer

Boys are often taught not to cry and may fear being laughed at if they admit difficulties. When no one in a group talks openly, asking for help can feel like weakness. However, this is changing among some younger men as discussing mental health becomes more normal. I think upbringing and social expectations matter more than a simple natural difference.

Key words and phrases

upbringing
The way a person is taught and cared for while growing up.

Advanced Model Answer

Mostly upbringing, I'd say. Boys are told very early not to cry, often by well-meaning adults, and they quickly learn that visible distress invites mockery from other boys. That becomes self-reinforcing: if nobody in the group admits to struggling, admitting it feels like being the only weak one. It has real consequences too, since men are noticeably less likely to ask for help when they need it. That's shifting a bit in younger generations, though, where talking openly about mental health has become far more normal among male friend groups than it was even ten years ago.

Key words and phrases

invites mockery from other boys
encourages other boys to laugh at him
ask for help when they need it
request support at the point they require it
talking openly
discussing feelings honestly and without secrecy
