Work & Employment

Unemployment or a job you dislike?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

It is better for people to be unemployed than to be in a job they do not enjoy. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Advice to leave any job that makes you unhappy is now common, particularly among younger workers. I disagree with it as a general rule, although there are situations where walking away is clearly the right decision.

Unemployment is far more damaging than the phrase suggests. It removes income first, and financial fear quickly reaches the family, the housing and the ability to plan anything. It also removes structure, colleagues and the sense of contributing, which is why long spells out of work are linked to depression far more strongly than a disliked job is. Skills fade, and employers treat a long gap with suspicion, so each month out makes the next job harder to obtain.

A job that is merely unenjoyable still supplies a good deal. It pays for the parts of life that do give pleasure, and most people find their satisfaction outside work in any case. It teaches things the worker did not expect to learn, and it is a far better position from which to look for something better, because searching from inside employment carries neither desperation nor a gap in the record.

There are limits to this. Work that damages health, involves abuse or requires dishonesty should be left, and where savings or family support exist, a short deliberate break to retrain can be a sound investment rather than an escape. The distinction is between leaving towards something and leaving away from something.

In conclusion, quitting is justified when a job harms a person or when a real plan is waiting. In the ordinary case of boredom or mild dislike, staying while looking is the wiser course, since unemployment usually costs more than the dissatisfaction it was meant to cure.

Key words and phrases

financial fear quickly reaches the family
Money worries soon affect relationships at home.
treat a long gap with suspicion
View an extended employment break as concerning.
searching from inside employment
Looking for a new job while still working.
a short deliberate break to retrain
A planned brief pause to learn new skills.
