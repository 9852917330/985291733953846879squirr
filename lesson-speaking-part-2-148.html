Situation

An occasion when you helped a person

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an occasion when you helped a person.

You should say: - What the situation was - Who you helped - How you helped - How you felt after giving help.

Simple Model Answer

An occasion when I helped someone began at a street fair on a day off. I noticed a young boy selling balloons and toys. He looked unhappy because few people were stopping, so I bought a couple of items and spoke to him.

He explained that his father had left and his mother was having a difficult time. He was trying to earn money for his family, including his younger sister. His situation stayed in my mind after I returned home.

I posted a photo of the toys on Facebook and told friends about his stall, asking them to buy something if they passed. I did not expect a large response, but several people visited or offered help. A few months later, I saw him again. He and his mother thanked me and said the extra support had made a difference. I felt happy because the action had taken little effort from me but had been useful to them. It reminded me that helping does not always require a complicated plan or a large amount of money.

Advanced Model Answer

There are plenty of times I've asked myself what it actually takes to make a difference for someone else. But it wasn't until I got a heartfelt thank-you from a complete stranger, a little boy, that I realised how simple helping someone can actually be. Funnily enough, I helped him without even meaning to.

I can't remember the exact date, but it was on a day off work, and I came across a young boy selling balloons and toys at a street fair, looking pretty dejected because nobody was paying him any attention. When I walked over, his whole face lit up. I bought a couple of toys from him and asked a few questions, and from what he told me, it was clear he was going through a rough patch: his father had left the family out of the blue, and his mother hadn't been coping well since, so he'd more or less had to start earning money himself to support her and his younger sister.

After I got home, I posted a photo of his toys on Facebook along with a short caption about his situation, mentioned roughly which street he sold on, and asked my friends to buy from him if they got the chance. I honestly hadn't expected much, but it's amazing what a bit of social media can do, a lot of people ended up going to find him and buying his stuff, or just offering to help however they could.

A few months later I went back to see him. He and his mother were incredibly grateful and told me that one small gesture had genuinely changed things for them. It wasn't much on my part, but knowing it meant the world to them made me happier than almost anything else I've done.
