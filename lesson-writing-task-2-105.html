Sport & Leisure

The future of major sporting events

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

International sporting events such as the Olympic Games are becoming increasingly expensive to host. Some people believe that they will gradually lose their importance. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

International sporting events such as the Olympic Games now cost host cities billions of dollars, and some people predict that they will gradually lose their importance. I disagree with this view, although I accept that the way such events are organised will have to change.

There are certainly reasons to be pessimistic. Recent Games have left several host cities with enormous debts and stadiums that stand empty afterwards, and a number of cities have withdrawn their bids after residents voted against them. Doping scandals and disputes over political influence have also damaged public confidence. Meanwhile, audiences can now watch elite sport of every kind at any time, so a single event no longer feels as rare as it once did.

Nevertheless, the underlying appeal remains extremely strong. The Olympic Games attract billions of viewers because they offer something no ordinary competition can: the world's best athletes, in dozens of sports, competing under a national flag every four years. Broadcasters and sponsors continue to pay record sums for the rights, which is the clearest evidence that interest has not declined. These events also give minority sports such as fencing and rowing their only global audience.

What is likely to change is the model rather than the event itself. Future competitions will probably use existing venues, be shared between several cities and be organised on a far more modest budget, as recent hosts have already begun to do.

In conclusion, I do not believe that major international sporting events will fade away. Their financial structure will have to become more realistic, but their appeal to audiences shows no sign of weakening.

Key words and phrases

withdrawn their bids after residents voted against them
Stopped seeking to host after local opposition.
continue to pay record sums for the rights
Keep paying unprecedented amounts for broadcasting permission.
their only global audience
Their sole opportunity to reach viewers worldwide.
be shared between several cities
Divide responsibilities or events across several cities.
shows no sign of weakening
Appears to remain just as strong.
