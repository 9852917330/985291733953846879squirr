Film & Television

How films have changed and the future of cinemas

Prompt

Do you think films have changed since you were a child? As the technology for home viewing improves, do you think people will stop going to the cinema in future?

Do you think films have changed since you were a child?

Simple Model Answer

The technology has changed more than the basic stories, I think. I enjoyed superhero action films such as Superman as a child, and similar films are still popular. Effects now look more realistic, and successful stories may continue for many films. However, the familiar heroes and basic story patterns often remain much the same.

Key words and phrases

realistic
Looking or feeling close to real life.

Advanced Model Answer

No, I don't think films have changed all that much since I was a child. When I was younger I enjoyed watching action films, and the Hollywood formula for this type of film seems to be the same today. For example, I liked the original 'Superman' films, and superheroes are still a popular subject for film-makers. What's changed is the technology rather than the stories: the effects are far more convincing now, and a franchise runs for a decade instead of producing one sequel and moving on.

Key words and phrases

a popular subject
a topic many people want to learn or talk about
a franchise runs for a decade
a series of related films continues for ten years

As the technology for home viewing improves, do you think people will stop going to the cinema in future?

Simple Model Answer

No, cinemas offer a shared atmosphere that home equipment cannot fully replace. Going out for a new film feels special, and people may concentrate more without phones or pauses. However, cinemas still need attractive films and reasonable prices. If visiting becomes too expensive or disappointing, people will naturally choose the comfort of home more often.

Key words and phrases

shared atmosphere
The feeling created by experiencing something with other people.

Advanced Model Answer

No, I don't think that people will stop going to the cinema altogether. People can already buy fantastic home viewing equipment, but it still feels more special to share the experience of watching a new film with a theatre full of people. I don't think that technology will ever fully replicate that cinema atmosphere. There's also the fact that going out is an occasion in a way that staying in simply isn't — you leave the house, you can't pause it, and you aren't checking your phone every ten minutes. Cinemas will have to work harder for that loyalty, though; ticket prices and dull scheduling are exactly the kind of thing that pushes people back towards their sofas.

Key words and phrases

home viewing equipment
devices used to watch films in your house
replicate that cinema atmosphere
create the same feeling as being in a cinema
ticket prices and dull scheduling
the cost of entry and an uninteresting programme
