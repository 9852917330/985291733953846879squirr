News & Media

Newspapers against radio and television for news

Part 3

Prompt

In what ways are newspapers better for learning about the news than radio or television? Do you think newspapers should be completely free to say whatever they want? What do people enjoy reading in your country?

In what ways are newspapers better for learning about the news than radio or television?

Simple Model Answer

Reading lets you choose your own pace, return to difficult passages and check the source of a claim. Newspapers also have room for background and usually separate opinion from reporting clearly. Their weakness is speed: a daily printed paper cannot update as quickly as television or an app when an event is happening right now.

Key words and phrases

choose your own pace
Decide how quickly or slowly to do something.

Advanced Model Answer

The main advantage is control over the pace. With a broadcast you move at the presenter's speed, whereas reading lets you slow down on something complicated, go back over a paragraph and check where a claim actually came from. Print also has more room, so a newspaper can give you the background to a story rather than just the headline, and it's easier to tell reporting from opinion, because the two are physically separated on the page. The trade-off is immediacy, though — if something's breaking right now, a newspaper that's printed once a day can't compete with television or a news app.

Key words and phrases

control over the pace
the ability to decide how fast something happens
tell reporting from opinion
recognise the difference between news and a personal view

Do you think newspapers should be completely free to say whatever they want?

Simple Model Answer

No. A free press is important for questioning governments, but it should still respect privacy and rules against harmful false accusations. Publishing something untrue can seriously damage someone who cannot respond equally. I would distinguish preventing a newspaper from investigating from making it responsible for what it publishes. Freedom and responsibility should work together.

Key words and phrases

false accusations
Untrue statements saying someone has done something wrong.

Advanced Model Answer

No, and I don't think any country actually allows that in practice. A free press is essential, since without it governments aren't held to account. But that freedom has to sit alongside laws on defamation, privacy and incitement, because publishing a false accusation can destroy someone who has no comparable platform to respond. The distinction I'd draw is between restricting what the press may investigate, which is dangerous, and holding it responsible for what it actually prints, which isn't the same thing at all.

Key words and phrases

held to account
required to explain and take responsibility for actions
no comparable platform to respond
no similarly large way to give their reply

What do people enjoy reading in your country?

Simple Model Answer

News, sport, business and celebrity stories attract many readers. People also follow property and other personal interests. Increasingly, they find individual stories through social media rather than read a whole newspaper. That is convenient, but it can narrow their knowledge: someone following only football may rarely encounter important stories about anything else.

Key words and phrases

encounter
Come across or experience something.

Advanced Model Answer

News and sport are probably the most widely followed, along with entertainment and celebrity coverage. Business and property attract a lot of attention too, especially among people my age. What's changed is that people no longer read a whole publication — they follow individual topics through social media feeds instead, so someone might read enormously about football and almost nothing else. That's convenient, but it means most people never encounter a story they did not already care about.

Key words and phrases

social media feeds
streams of posts shown by social networking sites
a story they did not already care about
news about an issue they had not previously followed
