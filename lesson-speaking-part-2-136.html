A dinner you enjoyed with your friends

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a dinner you enjoyed with your friends.

You should say: when and where you had the dinner what you had who you went with and explain why you enjoyed the dinner.

Simple Model Answer

A dinner I enjoyed with friends was a rooftop barbecue at Lan's apartment building in early spring. It was a farewell meal for my best friend Mai, who was leaving to study in Australia the following week.

About eight university friends came. We bought meat and drinks together and spent the afternoon preparing the rooftop. Mai was the best cook, so she prepared the meat with seasoning. We also had grilled corn and salad. For dessert, someone brought a red velvet cake from a bakery near Hoan Kiem Lake, and I enjoyed it enough to have a second slice.

The food was excellent, but the company made the evening special. We stayed until after dark, telling stories and laughing about our university years. At times, we became emotional when we spoke about Mai leaving. We knew it might be a long time before everyone could meet again. I remember the meal because it gave us time to appreciate our friendship before our lives moved in different directions. It felt like a warm and happy way to say goodbye.

Key words and phrases

farewell meal
A meal held to say goodbye to someone who is leaving.
became emotional
Started to feel strong feelings, such as sadness or happiness.

Advanced Model Answer

I've been to plenty of dinners and parties over the years, but there's one that really stands out for me. It was a barbecue on the rooftop of my friend Lan's apartment building, back in early spring, and it was actually a farewell party for my best friend Mai, because she was leaving to study abroad in Australia the following week.

There were about eight of us altogether, all close friends from university. We'd been planning the evening for weeks, and on the day itself we met at Lan's place, went shopping together for the meat and drinks, and spent a couple of hours setting up the rooftop terrace. Mai is a brilliant cook, so she took charge of the marinade, and honestly, I don't think I've ever had grilled pork that tasted so good.

Alongside the meat we had grilled corn, salad, and far too many drinks. The dessert is what I remember best, though. Someone had ordered a red velvet cake from a bakery near Hoan Kiem Lake, with a thick layer of cream cheese frosting, and it was worth every penny — I actually went back for a second slice, which I never normally do.

But what made that evening so good wasn't really the food, it was the company. We stayed up on that rooftop until it got properly dark, grilling, chatting, and telling stories about things we'd done together over the years. At one point the conversation got a bit emotional when we talked about Mai leaving, but mostly we just laughed. It might be years before all of us are in the same place again, and that's exactly why that dinner has stuck in my memory.
