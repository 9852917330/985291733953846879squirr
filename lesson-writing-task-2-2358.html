Environment

A global body for the environment?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that tackling environmental problems should be managed by a global organisation instead of by national governments individually. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Pollution and climate change ignore borders, which makes managing them through separate national policies look inefficient. I partly agree that a global body is needed, but it should coordinate and enforce rather than replace national governments.

The argument for central management is strong where the problem is genuinely shared. Emissions released anywhere affect everyone, so a country acting alone bears the full cost and receives a fraction of the benefit, which is why so many governments promise action and deliver little. A global authority could set binding limits, prevent industries from relocating to wherever rules are weakest, and stop each country waiting for the others to move first. Oceans and the atmosphere belong to nobody in particular, so only an international body can defend them, and problems such as overfishing show what happens when nothing does.

The objection is that such an organisation would have no way of acting. Environmental measures require changing taxes, planning law, farming and energy supply, all of which depend on domestic institutions that no international body possesses. Countries also differ enormously in what they can afford and in what caused the damage, so uniform rules would be unjust as well as unworkable. Any authority strong enough to overrule governments would also lack the accountability that elections provide, and states would simply refuse to join.

The workable arrangement is division of labour. An international body should set targets, verify emissions independently, distribute finance to poorer countries and impose consequences for breaking agreements, while national governments choose how to meet the targets in their own conditions. That is roughly what existing agreements attempt, and their weakness is the absence of any penalty for failure.

In conclusion, environmental problems are international and require an international institution with real enforcement powers. Delivery must remain national, because only governments can change what happens on the ground.

Key words and phrases

lack the accountability that elections provide
Do not face voters' power to remove leaders.
the absence of any penalty for failure
No punishment follows failure to deliver.
