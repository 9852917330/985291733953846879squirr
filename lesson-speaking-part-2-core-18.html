Why I Like Dogs

Core Story

Prompt

Describe an animal that you like.

You should say: - what the animal is - what you know about it - whether you have ever kept one - and explain why you like it.

OUTLINE

>>> Why I Like Dogs
animal I like • dogs • social, observant, adaptable
interest grew through a friend's rescue dog
read tone, routines and body language • trust develops gradually
companionship + practical roles • search and rescue • guide dogs
would adopt when lifestyle allows • responsibility matters

Simple Model Answer

An animal I have learned to appreciate is the dog. I was not very interested in animals when I was younger. My opinion began to change when a close friend adopted a rescue dog. At first, it was nervous around strangers, but after several visits it recognised me and waited near the door.

I was impressed by how well dogs notice human behaviour. They respond to voice, daily habits and body language, and some seem to understand when people feel happy or upset. Of course, every dog is different, but this communication without words is interesting.

Dogs can also do important work, such as helping rescue teams or guiding people who cannot see well. I like that they can be both useful and good company. I do not currently own one because it needs time and regular care. If my lifestyle becomes suitable, though, I would seriously consider adopting a dog in the future.

Key words and phrases

rescue dog
A dog taken into care after being abandoned or needing help.
body language
Feelings or messages shown through movement and posture.

Advanced Model Answer

An animal I have gradually come to appreciate is the dog, mainly because of how social, observant and adaptable dogs can be.

I was not especially interested in animals when I was younger, so my opinion changed quite slowly. The turning point came when a close friend adopted a rescue dog. At first the dog was nervous around strangers, but after a few visits it began to recognise me and wait near the door when I arrived.

What I find interesting is how well dogs read human behaviour. They notice tone of voice, routine and body language, and many of them seem to know when somebody is excited or upset. Of course, that varies from one animal to another, but the level of communication can be remarkable even without words.

I also respect the practical roles dogs can perform, from search and rescue to guiding people with visual impairments. That combination of companionship and usefulness is unusual. I do not currently keep a dog because it requires time and responsibility, but if my lifestyle becomes more suitable in the future, I would seriously consider adopting one.
