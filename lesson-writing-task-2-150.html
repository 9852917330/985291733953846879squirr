Technology & Science

Optimism about what science will do this century

Opinion

Prompt

Many people are optimistic about the 21st century and expect scientists to make positive changes to the world. To what extent do you agree or disagree with their optimism? What changes would you like to see in the new century?

Model answer

I am cautiously optimistic about the twenty-first century. Science has the potential to improve health, energy and living standards, but progress will only be positive if governments use new knowledge responsibly. The changes I would most like to see concern clean energy and equal access to healthcare.

There are good reasons for optimism. Medical research has already made many diseases easier to diagnose and treat, while digital technology allows knowledge to spread quickly. Further advances in vaccines, early screening and personalised treatment could extend healthy life expectancy. Science can also make farming more efficient and help cities use water and energy more carefully.

However, scientific progress can create new risks. Automation may remove jobs faster than workers can retrain, and poorly regulated data systems can threaten privacy. Environmental technology will also have limited value if countries continue to depend on fossil fuels. I would therefore like governments to invest heavily in renewable energy, public transport and energy-efficient housing. These measures would reduce emissions at source rather than simply deal with their consequences.

A second priority should be affordable healthcare. Breakthroughs are of little value if only wealthy people can use them, and the gap is already visible: new cancer drugs and gene therapies are priced far beyond what most health systems can afford, and during the recent pandemic richer countries secured vaccine supplies many months before poorer ones. Public funding, international cooperation and fair pricing are needed so that essential treatment is available regardless of income.

Scientific progress alone will not guarantee a better century. New medicines, clean energy and more efficient farming can improve millions of lives, but access and regulation determine who benefits. I would particularly like to see research focused on affordable disease prevention and low-carbon infrastructure. These priorities could turn technical progress into widely shared human progress.

Key words and phrases

extend healthy life expectancy
Increase the years people live in good health.
remove jobs faster than workers can retrain
Eliminate employment before workers can learn new skills.
reduce emissions at source
Stop pollution where it is originally produced.
secured vaccine supplies many months before poorer ones
Obtained vaccines well before less-wealthy countries.
available regardless of income
Accessible whether someone is rich or poor.
turn technical progress into widely shared human progress
Make new technology improve life for many people.
