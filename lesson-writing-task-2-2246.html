Housing

Should housing be provided free?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Housing is a basic necessity for everyone. Therefore, the government should provide free housing for people who cannot afford to pay for it. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Shelter is as fundamental as food and medical care, and it is argued that governments should therefore house those who cannot house themselves. I largely agree, though I believe subsidised housing is a better instrument than housing provided at no cost at all.

The strongest argument is that homelessness is more expensive than housing. A person sleeping outside uses emergency services repeatedly, is admitted to hospital far more often and cannot hold a job without an address, and several cities that simply gave permanent housing to long-term rough sleepers found that the total public cost fell rather than rose. Housing is also the condition for everything else: children in temporary accommodation change schools repeatedly and fall behind, so the cost of not acting is paid by the next generation.

There is a market argument as well. In cities where private rents have risen far faster than wages, nurses, cleaners and bus drivers cannot afford to live where they work, and a city that cannot house the people who run it will eventually fail to function.

My reservation concerns the word free. Housing that costs nothing is expensive to maintain, tends to be built cheaply and can trap tenants, since taking a job may mean losing the home. Systems that work best charge rent capped as a proportion of income, so that a tenant contributes what they can and keeps the gain from earning more.

In conclusion, I agree that governments must house people who cannot house themselves, because the alternative costs more and damages children permanently. The provision should be secure and affordable rather than free, so that it supports people without discouraging work.

Key words and phrases

fell rather than rose
Decreased instead of increasing.
is paid by the next generation
Future adults bear the cost.
keeps the gain from earning more
Retains the benefit of higher pay.
without discouraging work
Without reducing people's willingness to work.
