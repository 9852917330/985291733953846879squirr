Risk & Safety

Managing risk and the impact of natural hazards

Part 3

Prompt

How important is risk management? What can be done to prevent natural disasters? What are the impacts of natural hazards on the surrounding areas?

How important is risk management?

Simple Model Answer

It is essential when mistakes could cause serious harm or expense. We need to identify possible problems, judge their likelihood and seriousness, then take suitable precautions. Simply writing a list is not enough. If warnings are recorded but ignored, a risk assessment may create false confidence instead of improving safety. The actions following the assessment matter most.

Key words and phrases

precautions
Steps taken in advance to reduce danger or prevent problems.

Advanced Model Answer

It's essential in any situation where failure would be expensive or dangerous. The basic idea's simple enough — you identify what could go wrong, rank each possibility by how likely it is and how serious it'd be, and then put safeguards in place proportionate to that. What people underestimate is the cost of getting it wrong: most major industrial accidents were preceded by warnings that somebody had already flagged and nobody had acted on. The danger is doing it badly, though — a box-ticking risk assessment that lists hazards nobody then fixes is arguably worse than having none at all, because it creates a false sense that the problem's been handled.

Key words and phrases

proportionate to that
in a size that matches the situation
a false sense
a mistaken impression

What can be done to prevent natural disasters?

Simple Model Answer

We cannot usually stop an earthquake or storm, but we can reduce the harm. Avoiding risky building locations, using strong construction, providing warnings and teaching people what to do all help. Countries with good preparation can lose fewer lives. However, a very large event may still exceed the protection planned, so preparation reduces risk rather than removing it completely.

Key words and phrases

exceed
Be greater than an expected amount or limit.

Advanced Model Answer

Strictly speaking you can't prevent the natural event itself, since an earthquake or a typhoon's going to happen regardless. What you can prevent is the disaster, which is really a question of how exposed people are. That means not building on floodplains, enforcing construction standards so buildings don't collapse, installing early-warning systems and, crucially, teaching people what to do. Japan's the obvious example, where the same earthquake causes far fewer deaths than it would elsewhere — though even Japan doesn't get it right every time, since a big enough event can still overwhelm defences built for anything smaller.

Key words and phrases

how exposed people are
the degree to which people face a danger
early-warning systems
systems that give people notice before danger arrives
overwhelm defences
be too strong for protective systems to cope with

What are the impacts of natural hazards on the surrounding areas?

Simple Model Answer

They damage homes and roads, but the effects continue much longer. People may lose farms or businesses, children miss school and health services struggle. Some residents move away permanently. Recovery therefore needs jobs, services and community support, not just new buildings. Rebuilding may sometimes improve facilities, but that does not cancel the suffering people have experienced.

Key words and phrases

Recovery
The process of returning to a safer or more normal situation after harm.

Advanced Model Answer

The immediate damage to homes and infrastructure is what gets reported, but the longer-term effects are often worse. Livelihoods disappear when farmland's contaminated or boats destroyed, children lose a year or more of schooling, and health services collapse exactly when they're needed most. There's also displacement, since people move away and frequently never return. That's why recovery has to mean far more than rebuilding the physical structures. The one qualification is that reconstruction aid sometimes leaves a place with better infrastructure than it had before, though that's small comfort to the people who lost everything to get there.

Key words and phrases

rebuilding the physical structures
constructing buildings and roads again after damage
