Situation

A time you felt happy that you used your cellphone

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time you felt happy that you used your cellphone.

You should say: - What you do; - When, where you do it; - Who you do it with; - Why you felt happy using your cell phone.

Simple Model Answer

A time using my phone made me happy began when my mother gave me my first iPhone. It was a reward for passing the university entrance exam. I had used only a basic phone before, so exploring the new settings and apps felt exciting.

At university, it became useful for group messages about lecture changes and assignment deadlines. I could also review study material on the bus instead of carrying several textbooks. These functions made daily life more convenient, although my mother sometimes joked that I spent too long looking at the screen.

The happiest use, however, was video calling my grandmother. She lived several hours away, and before that we mainly spoke when I visited. With the phone, I could see her face and talk almost every weekend. Those small conversations helped us feel closer despite the distance. Looking back, the present became more valuable for the relationships it supported than for its technical features. It started as a reward for one exam, but it gave me many ordinary moments of connection afterwards.

basic phone

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the day my mother gave me my first smartphone, an iPhone, as a reward for getting into university. I'd just found out I'd passed the entrance exam, which felt like a huge milestone at the time, and she surprised me with it that same evening.

I remember being completely fascinated by it at first - I spent almost the whole night just exploring the settings and apps, working out what everything did. Before that I'd had a really basic phone that could barely do more than make calls and send texts, so having something that could keep me connected to everyone, my friends, my family, even my teachers, felt like a huge upgrade.

The thing I appreciated most, though, wasn't really the phone calls. It was being able to stay on top of my university group chats, which back then were mostly on Facebook Messenger, and never missing an update about lecture changes or assignment deadlines. My mother used to joke that I'd become glued to the screen, and honestly, she wasn't wrong - I even started using it to revise for exams on the bus, which I'd never have bothered doing with textbooks.

What made me happiest, though, was something smaller: I used it to video call my grandmother, who lives a few hours away, almost every weekend after that. Before the phone, we mostly only spoke when I visited in person, so being able to see her face on a screen and check in with her so easily made a real difference. It's funny looking back - a gift that started out as a reward for an exam ended up being far more valuable for the little everyday connections it gave me.
