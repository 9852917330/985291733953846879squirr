Children & Family

Is television bad for children?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think watching TV is bad for children while others think that watching TV has more beneficial effects on children. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Few household habits divide parents as sharply as television. Some regard it as damaging while others see it as a source of learning, and my own view is that the argument is really about quantity and content rather than about the screen itself.

The case against television rests on what it replaces. Hours spent watching are hours not spent reading, playing outside or talking to adults, and language develops through conversation, which a broadcast cannot supply because it never responds to the child. Heavy viewing is associated with poorer sleep and lower fitness, and programmes made for adults expose children to violence and to advertising for food they should be eating less of.

Those who defend television point to what good programmes achieve. Well-designed children's series have measurably improved vocabulary and number skills, particularly among children whose parents read to them little. Documentaries also show things a child could never otherwise see, from the deep ocean to the inside of a volcano, and shared viewing gives a family something to talk about together.

My position is that both sides describe real effects of different practices. Two hours of unsupervised viewing is not the same activity as thirty minutes of a nature programme watched with a parent who asks questions afterwards. The evidence on harm concerns quantity and unsuitable content, while the evidence on benefit concerns careful selection, so the sensible response is to limit the hours and choose the programmes.

In conclusion, television is neither harmful nor beneficial in itself. What matters is how much a child watches, what they watch and whether an adult watches with them, which makes this a question of parental judgement rather than prohibition.

Key words and phrases

be eating less of
Consume smaller amounts of something.
limit the hours and choose the programmes
Control both viewing time and viewing content.
parental judgement rather than prohibition
Parents making sensible choices instead of banning everything.
