Arts & Museums

Public art in towns and cities

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

The government should spend money on putting more works of art such as paintings and statues in towns and cities to make them more attractive. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Public sculpture and murals are often dismissed as decoration that a council cannot justify while other services are cut. I agree that governments should fund such work, provided the money is spent in ways that residents actually value.

The first argument is that appearance affects behaviour. Neglected streets invite further neglect, whereas a well-maintained square with something worth looking at is used more, and places that are used are safer. Several cities have found that commissioning murals on walls that were repeatedly defaced stopped the vandalism altogether, because a wall that clearly belongs to somebody is treated differently.

Public art is also the only art that reaches everybody. Entering a gallery requires knowing that one is welcome there, and many people never do, whereas a sculpture in a station is encountered by commuters who would never buy a ticket to see it. For children in areas with no cultural provision, this may be the only art they meet outside a screen. There is an economic return as well, since distinctive public works draw visitors and give a place an identity that no shopping centre can supply.

I would attach one condition. Money is wasted when a council commissions an expensive piece that residents dislike and were never consulted about, and such projects damage the case for all the others. Funding should follow consultation, and should include local artists and maintenance rather than a single purchase.

In conclusion, I agree that public money should pay for art in public spaces, because it improves how places are treated and reaches people galleries never do. The spending is justified only where the work is chosen with the community rather than for it.

Key words and phrases

stopped the vandalism altogether
Completely ended deliberate property damage.
only art they meet outside a screen
The only art encountered in person.
no shopping centre can supply
Something a shopping centre cannot provide.
with the community rather than for it
Involving local people in creating it.
