Education

Educated for society or for yourself?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people say that the purpose of education is to prepare individuals to be useful to society. Others say its purpose is to help individuals achieve their personal ambitions. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Education consumes a large share of public money and a large share of a person's life, so what it is for is a fair question. Both answers describe something real, and my own view is that they are the same aim seen from different sides.

The social argument is the reason education is funded publicly. Societies need doctors, engineers, teachers and farmers, and a country that produced none of them would not survive whatever its citizens had studied. Education also transmits the shared knowledge, language and rules that hold a community together, and taxpayers who pay for a medical degree may reasonably expect a doctor at the end of it. Where shortages are severe, directing students towards needed fields is defensible.

The individual argument answers that people are not instruments. A person spends their own years studying, and a system that assigns them to whatever the economy requires ignores what they might become. Education also does things with no social return at all: it lets someone understand music, argue about history or read for pleasure, and those belong to the person rather than to the state. Ambition is also the strongest motivation available, since students working towards something they chose learn far more than those completing an allocation.

The division dissolves on examination. People who pursue their own interests generally become useful, because they work harder at what absorbs them, and most valuable contributions were made by people following curiosity rather than instruction. In the other direction, useful work is satisfying, so a nurse or an engineer usually finds personal meaning in exactly the work society needed. Conflict arises only when a system forces everybody down a single route.

In conclusion, education must supply the skills a society depends on, and it must also let individuals become what they are capable of. These aims support each other, and the failures occur where one of them is pursued as if the other did not matter.

Key words and phrases

learn far more than those completing an allocation
Learn more than people only fulfilling compulsory hours.
forces everybody down a single route
Makes everyone follow the same path.
