A festival that is important in your country

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a festival that is important in your country

You should say: - when the festival occurs - what you did during it - what you like or dislike about it - and explain why this festival is important

Simple Model Answer

An important festival in Vietnam is Tet, the Lunar New Year. It usually takes place in late January or February. For my family, it is a chance to spend several days together rather than fitting visits around work.

Before Tet, we clean the house, prepare food and buy flowers. On New Year's Eve, we have a family dinner. During the following days, we visit relatives, remember our ancestors and give children lucky money. I especially like the feeling of a fresh start, when people try to put old disagreements aside.

The atmosphere changes dramatically. Before the holiday, markets and stations are crowded. On the first morning, the streets become much quieter because many people are at home with their families. I enjoy that contrast. There are disadvantages, including expensive travel and pressure to spend money on gifts and food. However, Tet remains important because it helps people maintain family connections and traditions. For those living far away, returning home often becomes the most important plan of the year.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to describe Tet, the Lunar New Year, which is the most important traditional festival in Vietnam. It usually falls in late January or February and marks both the beginning of a new lunar year and a rare period when families can spend several uninterrupted days together.

Before Tet, my family cleans the house, prepares food and buys flowers. On New Year's Eve, we have dinner together, and during the following days we visit relatives, give children lucky money and pay respect to our ancestors. What I enjoy most is the sense of renewal: unfinished disagreements are often put aside, and people begin the year with more generous intentions.

The only aspect I dislike is the commercial pressure. Travel becomes expensive, shops are crowded and some families feel obliged to spend more than they can afford. Even so, Tet remains deeply important because it preserves cultural identity and renews family bonds. For many people who live far from home, it's the one occasion when returning to their family takes priority over work.

I should probably add what the atmosphere is actually like, because that's the part outsiders find hardest to picture. In the week beforehand the whole country seems to be in motion — stations and coach terminals are packed, and every market is selling peach blossom and kumquat trees. Then on the first morning of the new year the streets go completely silent, because everyone is indoors with their family. That contrast between the frantic preparation and the sudden stillness is what I associate most strongly with Tet, and it's something I've never experienced with any other holiday.
