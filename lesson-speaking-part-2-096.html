An article you read about healthy life

Describe an article you read about healthy life

Simple Model Answer

An article about healthy living that I remember was called Ten Ways to Live Longer. I read it online in the Guardian a few months ago after finding it while looking through the news late at night.

The article discussed everyday habits connected with health. It mentioned regular check-ups, being physically active, avoiding smoking and eating more vegetables. It also explained why people should pay attention to things such as blood pressure rather than waiting until they feel unwell. The suggestions were presented as ordinary habits, rather than an extreme fitness plan.

What I liked was how practical the advice seemed. The article encouraged readers to make small changes instead of assuming that healthy living requires hours in a gym. It made me think about how much time I spent sitting and how often I chose convenience over movement. After reading it, I began walking to work twice a week. I cannot judge every claim in the article myself, but it gave me a useful reason to look more carefully at my routine.

Key words and phrases

regular check-ups
Health examinations carried out from time to time.
physically active
Moving the body regularly rather than spending most of the time sitting.

Advanced Model Answer

I want to talk about an article called “Ten Ways to Live Longer”, which I read a few months ago in the Guardian, a well-known British newspaper. I came across it almost by accident, scrolling through news online late one night when I probably should have been asleep, and it ended up being one of the more useful things I've read all year.

The article opens with a surprising fact: the country with the longest life expectancy, for both men and women, is Japan. It explains that people there tend to have regular health check-ups and pay close attention to hygiene in daily life. It also points out that avoiding a sedentary lifestyle is one of the simplest rules for good health, even though plenty of countries still struggle with it — basically, get moving instead of sitting around all day.

Another point that stuck with me was about blood pressure — the article says you should get it checked regularly, since high blood pressure is one of the biggest single causes of early death and years of poor health, and a lot of people don't even realise they have it.

The ten tips themselves were pretty simple: things like not smoking, eating more vegetables instead of junk food, drinking in moderation, and managing stress, which all sound obvious but can genuinely add years to your life if you actually stick to them. You don't need intense workouts either — the article suggested small things like carrying your own shopping bags instead of using a trolley, just to improve your muscle strength a couple of days a week.

What I liked most about it was how practical and realistic the advice was — nothing extreme, just small daily habits. It made me rethink a few of my own habits, and I've actually started walking to work twice a week since reading it.
