Education

The advantages and disadvantages of studying abroad

Advantages & Disadvantages

Prompt

Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of studying abroad.

Model answer

Increasing numbers of students now complete part of their education in another country. In my view the experience offers substantial benefits, although the difficulties involved are greater than most students expect before they go.

The clearest advantage is language. Living somewhere the language is spoken constantly produces a fluency that classroom study rarely achieves, because everyday necessities such as shopping, banking and asking directions force students to use it. Cultural understanding develops in the same way: a student who has actually lived in a country learns how people there think and behave, rather than learning about it second-hand.

The second benefit is independence. Students abroad have to manage accommodation, money, cooking and bureaucracy without their families, often in a second language. Most find the first few months genuinely difficult, but the confidence that comes from having coped is lasting, and employers recognise it immediately. The international friendships formed along the way are valuable too, both personally and later in a career.

The drawbacks are real, however. Studying abroad is expensive, which restricts it to families who can afford it or students who win scholarships. Loneliness is the more serious problem: homesickness affects almost everyone at some stage, and a student who is struggling has no family nearby and may not yet have formed close friendships. A minority never adjust and return home early.

In conclusion, studying abroad develops language ability, cultural understanding and self-reliance far more effectively than studying at home ever could. Students should nevertheless prepare for a difficult first term rather than expecting the whole experience to be straightforwardly enjoyable.

Rather than hearing about it from others.

prepare for a difficult first term
