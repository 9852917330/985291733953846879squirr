Object & Favourite

A subject I would like to study

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a subject you would like to study that you never had the opportunity to study.

You should say: What is it What this subject deals with What benefits you would get And explain why you want to study this subject.

Simple Model Answer

A subject I would like to study is Japanese. I became interested in it as a child because I loved reading manga, or Japanese comic books. I often imagined reading the original versions one day, but exams and later work kept delaying my plans.

Studying Japanese would involve learning the hiragana and katakana writing systems, as well as kanji characters. I would also need grammar lessons and regular speaking practice. It sounds challenging, but I would enjoy joining a proper course instead of only learning occasional words online.

There are two main benefits for me. First, I could understand books and other material that may not be translated well. This would help me learn more about Japanese culture. Second, Japanese companies operate in Vietnam, so the language could be useful for future work. It might help me stand out when applying for jobs. For me, this subject combines an old personal interest with a practical goal. I hope to begin before my life becomes even busier.

Advanced Model Answer

If I had the chance, I'd love to study Japanese — it's something I've wanted to do since I was a kid. Back in the day, I was obsessed with manga, Japanese comic books. I couldn't take my eyes off them; I read a new one practically every day, and I used to tell myself I'd study Japanese properly one day. Unfortunately, once I got older, I was buried in exams and then work, so that plan just never happened.

The subject itself would mainly involve learning to read and write the two phonetic alphabets, hiragana and katakana, plus a huge number of kanji characters, on top of grammar and speaking practice. It sounds like a lot, and it is, but now and then I still think about that childhood dream, and I'd love to eventually take part in a proper course.

As for the benefits, I think there'd be two main ones. The obvious one is access — a huge amount of Japanese material never gets translated, or gets translated badly, so reading it in the original would open up a whole side of the culture I currently miss out on. The other is more practical: plenty of Japanese companies operate here in Vietnam, and the language is studied far less than English or Chinese, so it would genuinely set me apart in the job market.

So really, it's a mix of an old childhood dream and a practical calculation, and being fluent would finally make that dream come true. I'd like to act on it before life gets any busier.
