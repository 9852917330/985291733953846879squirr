Children

Toys for boys and girls, and what children should try

Prompt

Do you think it is important for boys to play with boys' toys and for girls to only play with girls' toys? Do you think it is good if parents only buy educational toys for their children?

Do you think it is important for boys to play with boys' toys and for girls to only play with girls' toys?

Simple Model Answer

No, I don't think those limits are necessary. Boys and girls may show different preferences, but parents, adverts and other children can influence them. I had cars and my sister liked dolls, yet that does not mean everyone should choose the same way. Children should feel free to try toys without worrying about what they are supposed to like.

Key words and phrases

preferences
The things people like or choose more than others.

Advanced Model Answer

I think boys do naturally seem to prefer playing with "boys' toys" and girls with "girls' toys", though how much of that is innate and how much is just modelled by parents, adverts and other children is genuinely hard to untangle. When I was young I had toy cars, trucks and toy guns, whereas my sister always wanted dolls, and neither of us was ever pushed towards those choices that I remember. I don't suppose it really matters if a boy plays with a doll or a girl plays with a toy car, but children pick up very quickly what they're "supposed" to like, and that's the part I'd want to loosen up.

Key words and phrases

naturally seem to prefer
appear to favour without having to think about it
hard to untangle
difficult to separate and understand

Do you think it is good if parents only buy educational toys for their children?

Simple Model Answer

No. Educational toys can help, but children also need play without a fixed lesson. Simple things such as blocks or old clothes can become many different games. If every toy feels like a test, children may lose interest. I would offer a mixture and let them experiment, imagine and enjoy themselves.

Key words and phrases

experiment
Try different things to discover what happens or works.

Advanced Model Answer

No. Educational toys can be useful in moderation, but children also learn a great deal through unstructured play. If every toy's chosen for an obvious academic purpose, play can turn into another lesson rather than a chance to experiment, invent rules and just enjoy themselves. Children lose interest fast, too, once they sense a toy's really a test in disguise. In my experience the things they come back to are simple and open-ended, like building blocks or a box of old clothes, because those can become something different every time — a purpose-built "educational" toy usually can't offer that same flexibility.

Key words and phrases

unstructured play
children's games without fixed rules or a lesson plan
an obvious academic purpose
a clear reason connected to school learning
become something different every time
can be used as a different thing on each occasion
