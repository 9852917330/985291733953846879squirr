Advertising & Consumerism

Are advertising methods unethical?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some of the methods used in advertising are unethical and unacceptable in today's society. To what extent do you agree with this view?

Model answer

Advertising is indispensable to a market economy because it informs consumers and allows firms to compete. Nevertheless, I largely agree that a significant proportion of modern advertising is ethically unacceptable, particularly when it conceals commercial intent or targets people who cannot make fully informed choices.

The first concern is that digital advertising can blur the boundary between persuasion and manipulation. Online platforms collect extensive behavioural data and use it to predict when an individual is most receptive to a message. Consumers may therefore see personalised claims repeatedly without understanding why they have been selected. This practice can exploit informational asymmetry: advertisers know far more about the audience than the audience knows about the campaign. The problem is not merely that people are persuaded to buy unnecessary goods, but that their attention and personal data are used without meaningful consent.

A second ethical problem arises when advertising is directed at children, people in financial difficulty or those with health-related anxieties. Such groups may be less able to assess exaggerated promises or long-term consequences. Promotions for high-interest credit, gambling or unproven health products can disproportionately affect vulnerable consumers, even when the wording is technically legal. In these cases, commercial freedom should not outweigh the duty to prevent foreseeable harm.

This does not mean that all advertising is objectionable. Clear product information, verifiable comparisons and creative brand communication can help consumers make better decisions. However, these practices must be subject to meaningful oversight, including transparent data rules, strict protection for minors and penalties for misleading claims.

In conclusion, advertising remains legitimate, but methods based on covert tracking, manipulation or exploitation are ethically unacceptable. Strong regulation is therefore necessary to preserve both consumer autonomy and public trust.
