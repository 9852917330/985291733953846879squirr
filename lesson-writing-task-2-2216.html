Work & Employment

Should social life come from work?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think a job not only provides income but also social life. Others think it is better to develop social life with people you do not work with. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Model answer

Most adults spend more waking hours with colleagues than with anyone else, which raises the question of whether friendships should grow out of work or be kept separate from it. Both arrangements have merit, although I believe a social life built entirely around the office carries real risks.

The case for workplace friendship is practical. Colleagues already share a routine, a location and a set of problems, so conversation starts easily in a way it rarely does among strangers. People who enjoy the company around them also cooperate more readily, ask for help sooner and stay in a job longer, which is why many employers deliberately fund social events. For someone who has moved to a new city for work, the office may be the only place where meeting people happens naturally.

The opposing view is that mixing the two makes both harder. A disagreement over a project becomes personal, a promotion turns a friend into a manager, and confiding in a colleague about money or family can quietly damage a professional reputation. There is a further problem: if every friend is a coworker, redundancy removes an income and a social circle at the same time, which is precisely when support matters most.

My own position is that friendships at work are worth having but should not be the whole of one's social life. Interests pursued outside the office — a sport, a language class, volunteering — put a person among people who have no stake in their career, and those relationships tend to be more honest for that reason.

In conclusion, colleagues can become genuine friends and there is no reason to avoid it. The mistake is to rely on work for everything, because a life with only one source of company is a fragile arrangement.

Key words and phrases

meeting people happens naturally
Social contact occurs without special planning.
a promotion turns a friend into a manager
Career advancement changes a friendship's power balance.
quietly damage a professional reputation
Gradually harm how someone is regarded at work.
have no stake in their career
Have no personal interest in their professional success.
more honest for that reason
Can speak frankly because no career interests are involved.
fragile arrangement
An agreement easily upset by changing circumstances.
