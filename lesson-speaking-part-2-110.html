Object & Favourite

Your favourite flower

Part 2

Prompt

Describe your favourite flower.

You should say: What it is Where it is grown If it is common in your country And explain why it is your favourite flower.

Simple Model Answer

My favourite flower is the rose because it reminds me of my grandmother. She had about twelve rose bushes in her small back garden, mostly red with a few light pink ones. I often helped her when I visited as a child.

We removed old flowers and weeds, and she taught me to be careful with the thorns. I did not always manage that and sometimes hurt my fingers. Still, I remember those afternoons as happy, quiet time together. Roses are common in Vietnam, including in home gardens and flower farms around Da Lat, but my connection with them is personal.

My grandmother died a few years ago. Now, whenever I pass a red rose bush, I think of her garden. I have started growing some roses on my balcony too. Mine are not as healthy or tidy as hers were, but seeing a flower open gives me pleasure. It feels like a small way of continuing something she enjoyed. That memory matters more to me than whether roses are rare or fashionable.

Key words and phrases

thorns
Sharp points growing on the stems of some plants.
my connection with them is personal
They matter to me because of my own experiences and feelings.

Advanced Model Answer

My favourite flower is the rose, which probably isn't the most original answer, but I've got a specific reason for choosing it — my grandmother grew them, and I've loved them since I was small because of her.

She had a small garden behind her house with maybe a dozen rose bushes, mostly red and a few pale pink ones, and every visit as a kid I'd get roped into helping her deadhead them or pull up weeds around the roots. I remember being warned constantly about the thorns, and getting pricked more than once before I learned to handle the stems properly.

Roses are grown pretty much everywhere in Vietnam — you'll find them in home gardens, in flower farms up in Da Lat, where the cooler climate suits them well, and on every street corner around Tet and Valentine's Day, when florists sell out almost completely. So yes, they're extremely common here, not some rare or exotic flower at all.

What makes the rose my favourite, though, is less about the flower itself and more about what it reminds me of. My grandmother passed away a few years ago, but she's still growing roses in my memory — whenever I walk past a rose bush now, especially a red one, I think of her garden and those Sunday afternoons.

I've actually started growing a few myself on my balcony, though nowhere near as successfully as she did — mine are a bit patchy and uneven compared to hers. Still, every time one comes into bloom, it feels like a small connection to her, which is really the whole reason I love roses more than any other flower.
