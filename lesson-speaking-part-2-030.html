A cafe you like or dislike

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a cafe you like or dislike

You should say: Where it was When you went there what you ate or drank And explain why you liked or disliked the cafe.

Simple Model Answer

A cafe I like is Secret Garden in central Hanoi. It is on the roof of a building and has a garden design, with plenty of plants and flowers. Although the streets below are busy, the cafe feels peaceful.

A colleague first took me there about two years ago after a difficult week at work. Since then, I have returned every few weekends. I usually order chamomile tea and lemon cake, then spend a couple of hours reading. If I go with a friend, we share tea and biscuits and have a quiet conversation.

The main reason I like it is the atmosphere. Even when the cafe is full, people speak softly and do not play loud music on their phones. The staff are calm and helpful too. I am quite a quiet person, so I appreciate having somewhere to take a break from the city. After a visit, I normally feel more relaxed and ready to deal with work again. It is a simple place that makes a big difference to my mood.

Advanced Model Answer

The fast pace of modern life in the city can be too much for an introvert like myself, and sometimes I just need somewhere to retreat into my shell for an hour or two. Fortunately, I found the perfect place, a spot called "Secret Garden," which I'd like to talk about now.

"Secret Garden" is a garden-themed coffee shop on the rooftop of a building in the centre of Hanoi. It's well known for its floral, botanical-inspired design, which gives the whole place a real sense of peace and tranquillity — you almost forget you're still in the middle of a busy city.

I first found it about two years ago, when a colleague dragged me along after a rough week at work, and I've been going back every couple of weekends ever since. I usually order chamomile tea and a slice of their lemon drizzle cake, and I'll sit there for a couple of hours with a book, half-reading and half just watching the plants. If a friend comes along instead, we'll share a pot of tea and a plate of biscuits and chat quietly in the corner.

What keeps me going back, though, isn't really the food or drink — it's the mood of the place. Even when every seat is taken, nobody raises their voice, nobody plays music from their phone, and the staff move around so quietly you barely notice them refilling your cup. It's the kind of calm that's hard to find anywhere else in a city as loud and crowded as Hanoi, and that's exactly why I always tell my friends to go there whenever they need a break from the chaos outside.
