Person

A sports person you admire

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a sports person you admire.

You should say: - who this person is - what they have achieved - how you found out about them - and explain why you admire this person.

Simple Model Answer

A sportsperson I admire is Eliud Kipchoge, a marathon runner from Kenya. I first learned about him through a documentary recommended by a friend. He has won two Olympic marathon titles and has held the world record.

In 2019, he completed the marathon distance in less than two hours. That run used special conditions, so it was not an official world record, but I still found it impressive. What surprised me more was his ordinary daily life. He stays in a simple training camp, helps with chores and follows a regular training plan.

I admire his discipline. He continues training even when the weather is unpleasant or the session is repetitive. He also believes that the way we think can affect what we achieve. His example is useful beyond sport. I am unlikely to run a marathon, but I can apply his attitude to studying or work. He shows that regular effort over a long time is often more helpful than working extremely hard for just a short period.

Key words and phrases

beyond sport
In other areas of life as well as in sport.
regular effort
Work that you keep doing steadily over time.

Advanced Model Answer

The sportsperson I most admire is the marathon runner Eliud Kipchoge, partly because of what he's achieved and partly because of how he talks about it.

He is a Kenyan distance runner who has won the Olympic marathon twice and held the world record, and in 2019 he became the first person to run the distance in under two hours, although that particular run was staged under special conditions and doesn't count officially.

I first heard about him through a documentary a friend recommended, and what surprised me was how ordinary the training looked. There weren't any dramatic scenes. He lives in a simple training camp, shares the chores with the other runners, and he's followed almost exactly the same weekly routine for years.

I admire him for two reasons. The first is the discipline, which isn't the exciting kind — it's the willingness to do the same unglamorous session on a wet Tuesday morning for a decade. The second is his attitude to limits. He's said repeatedly that the barrier is usually mental rather than physical, and unlike most people who say that, he actually demonstrated it in front of a stopwatch.

What I find useful is that his approach transfers. Very few of us will run a marathon, but almost anything worth doing rewards showing up consistently rather than working intensely for a fortnight.

Key words and phrases

held the world record
had achieved the best officially recorded result
staged under special conditions
arranged in an unusual, controlled setting
the same unglamorous session

rewards showing up consistently
