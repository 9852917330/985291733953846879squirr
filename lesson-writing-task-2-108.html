Cities & Urban Life

The growth of large cities

Causes / Effects

Prompt

Cities around the world are growing rapidly. What are the causes of this trend, and what consequences might it have?

Model answer

Cities in almost every country are expanding rapidly, and in several regions the population of a single urban area now exceeds that of a small nation. This growth has clear causes, and its consequences are both beneficial and damaging.

The principal cause is economic. Employment is concentrated in cities, wages are higher, and rural work has declined as farming has become mechanised, so millions move in search of a living. Cities also offer services the countryside cannot provide: universities, specialist hospitals and better schools. A further factor is that urban populations grow from within, since migrants are typically young adults who then have families of their own, so growth continues even when new arrivals slow.

Some consequences are positive. Concentrating people makes public transport, water supply and healthcare far cheaper to provide per person, and cities generate the majority of national income and tax revenue. The variety of people living closely together also produces the exchange of ideas on which new industries depend.

The problems, however, are severe. Housing cannot be built quickly enough, so prices rise and the poorest end up in overcrowded or informal settlements. Roads congest, air quality deteriorates, and demand for water and electricity outstrips supply. Rural areas suffer the opposite difficulty, losing the young workers who would have supported them.

In conclusion, urban growth is driven mainly by the concentration of work and services, and it is unlikely to reverse. It brings genuine efficiency, but without serious investment in housing and public transport the costs will steadily outweigh the advantages.

Key words and phrases

far cheaper to provide per person
Costs much less for each person served.
the exchange of ideas
Sharing and discussing different thoughts.
outstrips supply
Exceeds the amount available.
outweigh the advantages
Be more important than the benefits.
