An artist or entertainer you admire

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an artist or entertainer you admire

Simple Model Answer

An entertainer I admire is Ed Sheeran, a British singer and songwriter. A friend first played his song The A Team for me. I liked how a simple song could express such strong feelings.

He often performs with just a guitar and a loop pedal. This device lets him record short sounds and repeat them while he plays other parts. In this way, he can build a full musical performance on his own. I find that both clever and enjoyable to watch.

I also respect the effort behind his success. Before becoming famous, he played in small places, sometimes to very few people, and released his own music. He kept practising songwriting instead of waiting for a perfect idea. Many of his songs feel personal because he writes them himself. I admire his working attitude as much as his music. His career reminds me that a skill develops through regular practice, and that starting with a small audience does not mean you cannot achieve something bigger later.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about Ed Sheeran, a British singer-songwriter who performs mainly with his voice, an acoustic guitar and a loop pedal. I first became aware of him when a friend played “The A Team” for me, and I was struck by how a simple arrangement could carry such a strong emotional message.

He became successful gradually rather than through a single manufactured publicity campaign. Before he was internationally known, he performed in small venues, released music independently and built an audience through constant live work. His breakthrough came when his songs reached a much wider online audience and attracted support from established musicians.

I admire him for two reasons. First, he writes much of his own material, so his music feels personal rather than interchangeable. Second, his live performances show technical control and creativity: he can build a complete arrangement alone by recording rhythms and harmonies in real time. Even though his music is commercially successful, the core of it still depends on songwriting and sustained practice, which makes his career feel earned. I'd add that what I admire is not really the fame but the working method behind it. He's apparently played several hundred gigs in a single year at one stage, many of them to almost empty rooms, and he's said in interviews that he simply treated songwriting as a skill to be practised rather than as something you wait to feel inspired about.

That's a useful attitude well beyond music, and it's the main reason I find him interesting. Plenty of performers are more technically gifted, but very few of them are as visibly the product of their own effort.
