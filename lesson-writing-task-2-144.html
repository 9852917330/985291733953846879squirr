Children & Family

Does growing up poor build resilience?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Children who grow up in families short of money are better prepared to deal with the problems of adult life than children brought up by wealthy parents. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes claimed that children who grow up without money handle adult difficulties better than those raised in wealthy families. I partly agree, since financial hardship can teach resilience, but I believe the claim is exaggerated and overlooks the serious disadvantages of poverty.

There is something in the argument. Children in poorer households usually take on responsibility earlier, whether by working part-time, caring for younger siblings or managing limited resources, and they learn that difficulties must be solved rather than paid away. They may also develop a stronger motivation to succeed, since nothing is guaranteed and no family safety net exists behind them. Conversely, a young adult who has never been refused anything may be poorly equipped for the first serious setback.

Nevertheless, this romanticises poverty considerably. Growing up short of money frequently means poor nutrition, overcrowded housing, weaker schools and parents too exhausted by work to provide attention. Persistent financial stress in childhood is associated with anxiety and lower educational attainment, and these are handicaps rather than preparation. Wealthy families, meanwhile, supply advantages that are genuinely useful in adult life: good health, strong schooling and professional contacts.

In my view what actually builds resilience is being given responsibility and being allowed to fail, and this depends on parenting rather than on income. A wealthy child who is expected to work for what they receive develops exactly the same capacity.

In conclusion, hardship can produce resourcefulness, but I disagree that poverty is a good preparation for adult life. Its disadvantages generally outweigh whatever character it builds.

no family safety net exists behind them
They have no relatives able to support them.
the first serious setback
The first major difficulty or failure.
this romanticises poverty considerably

handicaps rather than preparation
Disadvantages rather than useful training for life.
exactly the same capacity
