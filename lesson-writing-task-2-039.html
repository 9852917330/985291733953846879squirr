Culture & Traditions

Should immigrants adopt the local culture?

Essay

Prompt

Some people argue that immigrants should adopt the local culture when they move to a new country. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Moving to another country requires immigrants to understand local customs, but it should not require them to abandon their own culture. I partly agree that newcomers need to adapt. They should respect the law and learn the language, while remaining free to preserve harmless traditions and community ties.

Adaptation is essential for practical reasons. A person who can communicate in the local language is more likely to find work, use public services and build relationships outside an immigrant community. Understanding everyday conventions also prevents avoidable conflict. For example, expectations about punctuality, personal space or workplace communication may differ greatly between countries. Learning these norms helps newcomers participate fully in public life.

However, integration is not the same as cultural surrender. Family traditions, food, music and religious practice can give immigrants stability during a difficult transition. Minority organisations may also provide language support, childcare and advice for new arrivals. These communities become harmful only when they isolate members from the wider society or reject basic legal principles. In most cases, they can preserve identity while supporting integration.

The host country has responsibilities as well. Schools, employers and public institutions should make participation possible and respond firmly to discrimination. Integration works best when newcomers are expected to contribute but are not treated as inferior for having a different background.

In conclusion, immigrants should adopt the language, laws and civic expectations of their new country. They should not be required to erase their heritage. A successful multicultural society creates a shared public culture with room for private difference.

Key words and phrases

abandon their own culture
Give up their traditions and identity.
participate fully in public life
Take an active part in shared social activities.
integration is not the same as cultural surrender
Joining society does not require abandoning one's culture.
preserve identity while supporting integration
Keep cultural roots while helping people join wider society.
