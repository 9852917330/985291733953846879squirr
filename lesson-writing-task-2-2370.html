Equality

Would a smaller income gap make us happier?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Many believe that the best way to ensure a happier society is to reduce the difference in income between the rich and the poor. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Countries with similar levels of wealth report very different levels of satisfaction, and the distribution of income is one of the clearest things separating them. I largely agree that narrowing the gap raises happiness, though calling it the best single measure claims too much.

The evidence for the connection is substantial. Beyond the point where basic needs are met, additional income adds little to wellbeing, but relative position continues to matter, because people judge their lives by comparison with those around them. Wide gaps therefore produce dissatisfaction at every level except the top. More equal societies also record better health, lower crime and higher social trust, since people are more willing to contribute where they feel treated fairly. Practically, money moved from the wealthy to the poor buys more happiness, as the same sum means far more to a household struggling with rent.

There are limits to the argument, however. Equality achieved by making everyone poorer helps nobody, and countries that suppressed differences entirely produced neither prosperity nor contentment. What people object to is usually unfairness rather than difference: a gap earned through effort is accepted, while one inherited or extracted is resented. Other factors also weigh heavily, since employment, health, safety and the quality of relationships shape happiness at least as much as the income distribution does.

The most effective approach is therefore indirect. Rather than redistributing income alone, governments raise satisfaction by providing services that everyone uses regardless of income — schools, health care, transport — which reduces the practical consequences of the gap while allowing differences in earnings to remain.

In conclusion, reducing the distance between rich and poor genuinely improves health, trust and satisfaction, so the belief is well founded. It works best when it comes from fair opportunity and strong public services rather than from levelling incomes for its own sake.

Key words and phrases

providing services that everyone uses regardless of income
Offer public services to rich and poor alike.
fair opportunity and strong public services
Equal chances and dependable government services.
