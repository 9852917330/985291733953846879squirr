Advertising

Whether adverts change what people buy

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think advertisements influence what people buy? What criteria should be used to assess a good advertisement?

Do you think advertisements influence what people buy?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, but they usually work slowly. Seeing a brand repeatedly makes it familiar, so it may feel safer than another product when we shop. Adverts can also suggest that owning something shows success. However, for a completely new product, people may trust recommendations from friends more than the advert itself.

Key words and phrases

recommendations
Suggestions about what someone should try or choose.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, though not in the direct way people imagine. Very few adverts make somebody buy something immediately. What they do is build familiarity, so when you're standing in front of two similar products, one feels known and therefore safer. That's a subtle advantage but a valuable one, which is why brands spend so much simply keeping their name in front of you. Advertising also attaches products to an image — a car sold as evidence of success rather than as transport — and that works even on people who insist it doesn't. The exception is a genuinely new product with no reputation yet; there, word of mouth often does more than the advert itself.

Key words and phrases

feels known and therefore safer
seems familiar and thus less risky
keeping their name in front of you
making sure people repeatedly see the business name
evidence of success
proof that something achieved its goal

What criteria should be used to assess a good advertisement?

Simple Model Answer

A good advert should be easy to remember and explain clearly what the product offers. Most importantly, it should be honest. An exciting promise may attract a buyer once, but people lose trust if the product disappoints them. Some exaggeration might succeed for a while, but it can damage the business in the long term.

Key words and phrases

in the long term
Over a long period rather than immediately.

Advanced Model Answer

Whether it's memorable is the first test, since an advert nobody recalls has failed regardless of how elegant it is. Clarity comes next: you should know what's being sold and why it might matter to you. But the criterion I'd weight most heavily is truthfulness. An advertisement that exaggerates may work once, and then the customer discovers the product doesn't match the claim, which damages the brand far more than the sale was worth. Occasionally a company gets away with mild exaggeration for years, so it isn't an absolute rule — but it's a risk that rarely pays off in the long run.

Key words and phrases

exaggerates may work once
making claims too strong may succeed once
damages the brand far more
harms people's trust in the company much more
