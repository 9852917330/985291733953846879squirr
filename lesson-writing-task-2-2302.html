Environment

Climate change and business

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people think that climate change could have a negative effect on business, while others think it could create more business opportunities. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Companies now treat climate change as a financial question rather than an environmental one, and their conclusions differ sharply. Both readings are accurate for particular industries, and my own view is that the losses will be larger and will fall on those least able to absorb them.

The case for damage is straightforward. Floods, storms and heat destroy factories, crops and transport links, and insurers in several regions have stopped covering the risk altogether, which makes some businesses impossible to run. Supply chains are disrupted by drought and low rivers, and agriculture, tourism and fishing face conditions their whole business model assumed would not change. Regulation adds a second cost, since carbon pricing and emissions rules make existing methods more expensive to continue.

The opportunity argument is equally grounded. Any problem of this size creates markets: renewable energy, insulation, batteries, electric transport and efficient farming are among the fastest-growing industries anywhere, and companies that moved early have profited substantially. Adaptation is a market of its own, covering flood defences, cooling, drought-resistant seed and insurance products, and firms that reduce energy use cut their costs while reducing emissions.

The two views can be reconciled by asking who gains. The opportunities are concentrated in wealthy countries with capital, technology and stable institutions, while the damage falls hardest on poorer regions whose economies depend on agriculture and which cannot borrow to rebuild. Even where new industries grow, they arrive in different places from the ones that decline.

In conclusion, climate change will destroy some businesses and create others, and the sectors benefiting are already visible. The destruction will be greater in total and unevenly distributed, so treating the situation as an opportunity describes the view from the countries least affected.
