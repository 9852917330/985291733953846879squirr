Children

Encouraging children to try new things

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think children today are encouraged to try new things? What can people do to make the best use of their life? What is the most important event for a teenager?

Do you think children today are encouraged to try new things?

Simple Model Answer

Not enough, in my opinion. Exams can leave little room for other activities, and adults may worry about safety or failure. Children need chances to try something without having to be good immediately. Praising effort can help. Of course, some children are naturally curious and will explore even without much encouragement.

Key words and phrases

encouragement
Support that helps someone feel willing or confident to try.

Advanced Model Answer

Less than they should be, I'd say. Schools have become quite exam-focused, so anything that isn't assessed gets squeezed out, and parents are more anxious about safety than they were a generation ago. There's also a fear-of-failure problem: children praised for being clever often avoid anything they might be bad at, in case it proves them wrong. What works is low-stakes encouragement — letting a child try something with no expectation of being immediately good at it. Some children need none of this pushed on them, of course; a naturally curious kid will try new things regardless of how cautiously they're raised.

Key words and phrases

a fear-of-failure problem
difficulty caused by being afraid of making mistakes
low-stakes encouragement
gentle support when the result is not very important

What can people do to make the best use of their life?

Simple Model Answer

I'd start with health, close relationships and work that gives life some direction. These can make everyday life more satisfying than simply earning more money. However, people have different needs. Someone struggling to pay for food and housing may reasonably focus on income first, before they have the freedom to think about other goals.

Key words and phrases

income
Money received from work or other sources.

Advanced Model Answer

That's a large question, but I'd start with health, because everything else depends on it and it's far easier to protect than to recover once it's gone. Then close relationships — the long-running research is fairly consistent that these predict life satisfaction better than income does; someone with a huge salary and nobody to share dinner with usually isn't the happier one. And work that gives some sense of direction, which needn't be a grand purpose. None of this is a fixed order for everyone, though — someone in real financial hardship reasonably has to prioritise income for a while before any of the rest becomes possible.

Key words and phrases

far easier to protect than to recover
much simpler to prevent losing than to regain
predict life satisfaction better than income does
show future happiness with life more accurately than earnings

What is the most important event for a teenager?

Simple Model Answer

Finishing school and choosing further education is a major event for many teenagers. It can affect their future, although they often know little about what each course or career is really like. For others, a first job or moving away matters more. I don't think one event is equally important for every young person.

Key words and phrases

further education
Study or training after finishing school.

Advanced Model Answer

There's no single event that applies to everybody, but finishing school and making the first real educational choice is significant for most. It's usually the first decision with genuine long-term consequences that a young person makes largely alone. What makes it difficult is that they're asked to choose with very little information about what any of those paths actually involve day to day — nobody really knows what studying engineering is like until they're two years into the degree. For others the defining event is more personal, like a first job or moving away from home for the first time, so it really varies by what a particular teenager's life looks like.

Key words and phrases

with very little information
without knowing many facts
