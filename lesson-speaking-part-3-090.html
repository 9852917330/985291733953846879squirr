Transport

Public transport against the car

Part 3

Prompt

Do you prefer travelling by public transport or by car? What are the benefits of travelling by public transport? In your opinion, in what ways can governments encourage people to use public transport?

Do you prefer travelling by public transport or by car?

Simple Model Answer

I prefer public transport when it is reliable. I can read or work instead of concentrating on driving, and it often costs less after parking and maintenance are included. However, a car is more practical for poorly served places, late journeys or carrying a child and lots of luggage. My choice depends on the actual trip.

Key words and phrases

maintenance
Work needed to keep something in good condition.

Advanced Model Answer

Public transport, when it's reliable — and that qualification matters. The main advantage is that the time is usable: I can read or work rather than concentrating on traffic, so an hour on a train isn't really lost. It's also cheaper once you count fuel, parking and maintenance. I'd choose a car for journeys poorly served by public routes, or when I'm carrying things, or travelling late at night, or when I'm heading somewhere with a young child and a lot of luggage, since the flexibility of a car just makes that kind of trip easier.

Key words and phrases

the time is usable
the period can be used usefully

What are the benefits of travelling by public transport?

Simple Model Answer

Public transport moves many people using less road and parking space. It can reduce traffic and pollution, save households money and provide independence for people who cannot drive. However, those benefits depend on the service working properly. A bus that is unreliable may look efficient on paper but will not persuade people to leave their cars behind.

Key words and phrases

on paper
In theory or according to a plan, rather than in real practice.

Advanced Model Answer

The main one is efficiency of space. A single bus replaces perhaps fifty cars, so congestion, pollution and the amount of land given over to roads and parking all fall dramatically. There's a household benefit too, since running a car is one of the largest expenses most families have. And it provides genuine independence to people who can't drive — the elderly, teenagers and anyone with a disability — which is easy to overlook if you've always had a car. None of this works, though, if the service itself is unreliable — the efficiency is only theoretical if the bus never turns up on time.

Key words and phrases

efficiency of space
how well the available area is used
genuine independence
the real ability to act without depending on others

In your opinion, in what ways can governments encourage people to use public transport?

Simple Model Answer

First, make services frequent, safe and easy to use, with tickets that work across different transport types. People need a realistic alternative before restrictions on driving are introduced. Charges may help afterwards, but applying them first can make people angry without changing travel habits. Good service should be the main reason to switch, not simply punishment for driving.

Key words and phrases

switch
Change from one choice or method to another.

Advanced Model Answer

By making it genuinely competitive, which means frequency above all, since people will accept a great deal if they never have to check a timetable, and integrated ticketing across buses and trains matters, as does safety, particularly at night. Beyond that, some restriction on driving is usually necessary, such as congestion charges. But imposing the restriction first, before the alternative's actually good, simply makes people resentful and turns the whole policy into a political liability.

Key words and phrases

integrated ticketing
one system for paying for connected journeys
makes people resentful
causes people to feel angry about unfair treatment
