Activity

A trip you took by bike

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a trip you took by bike

You should say: When and where the trip was Who you went with Why you went by bike And explain how you felt about it.

OUTLINE

>>> Mekong Delta cycling
2-day cycling trip along Mekong Delta w/ cousin, February this year
flew to Can Tho, hired bikes, cycled through villages, orchards, canal paths
chose bike bc area not accessible by car, paths too narrow
cycling slow enough to stop for floating market, mat weaver, farmer gave free dragon fruit
legs aching + sunburnt by day 2 but felt like really experienced the delta
wouldn't have noticed half of it on any other form of transport

Simple Model Answer

A bike trip I enjoyed was a two-day ride in the Mekong Delta with my cousin in February. We flew to Can Tho and hired bicycles so we could explore small villages and paths beside the canals.

We chose bikes because many of those paths were too narrow for cars. Distances between villages were also short, so cycling allowed us to move at a comfortable speed and notice more. We passed fruit gardens, people making mats and children swimming near their homes.

Because we could stop whenever we wanted, we had several interesting conversations. One fruit farmer gave us dragon fruit without asking for payment. His friendliness became my favourite part of the trip. By the end, my legs ached and I had spent too much time in the sun, but I felt pleased with the experience. Travelling slowly helped me understand local life better than watching from a bus window. I would choose a bicycle again for a place like this, where the small details are an important part of the visit.

Key words and phrases

canals
Narrow waterways made or used for boats, drainage or water supply.
move at a comfortable speed
Travel at a pace that feels easy and manageable.

Advanced Model Answer

I'll tell you about a two-day cycling trip I took along the Mekong Delta with my cousin, back in February this year.

We flew down to Can Tho and hired bikes for two days, cycling through small villages, past fruit orchards and along the narrow paths beside the canals that the delta is famous for.

We chose to go by bike specifically because a lot of that area simply isn't accessible by car - the paths between villages are far too narrow, and a lot of local life happens right beside the water, so cycling let us get close to things a bus or taxi never could. It was also just more practical: the distances between villages are short, so a bike was the perfect speed for actually seeing things rather than rushing past them.

Cycling that slowly meant we could stop whenever something caught our eye - a floating market, someone weaving mats by the roadside, kids swimming in the canal. We ended up talking to a fruit farmer who insisted on giving us free dragon fruit, which became the highlight of the whole trip.

By the end of the second day my legs were aching and I was completely sunburnt, but I felt like I'd actually experienced the delta rather than just looked at it through a window. Honestly, I don't think I'd have noticed half of what we saw if we'd taken any other form of transport.

Key words and phrases

narrow paths beside the canals
Small walkways along the waterways.
get close to things
Move near enough to see details.
the perfect speed for actually seeing things
Slow enough to observe the surroundings properly.
caught our eye
Attracted our attention.
the highlight of the whole trip
The most enjoyable part of the trip.
