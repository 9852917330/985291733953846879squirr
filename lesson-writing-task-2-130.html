Health

Why more children are overweight

Causes and effects

Prompt

The percentage of overweight children in western society has increased by almost 20% in the last ten years. Discuss the causes and effects of this disturbing trend.

Model answer

The sharp rise in childhood obesity is not the result of a single poor choice. It reflects changes in the food environment, family routines and children's daily activity, and its consequences extend well beyond appearance or short-term fitness.

One major cause is the widespread availability of inexpensive, energy-dense food. Highly processed meals and snacks are convenient for time-pressed households, while sophisticated marketing makes them especially attractive to children. Portion sizes have also increased, meaning that excessive calorie intake can become normal before a child understands nutrition. At the same time, many neighbourhoods and schools provide fewer opportunities for safe, unstructured physical activity. Screen-based entertainment has therefore displaced a substantial amount of active play, particularly where parents are reluctant to let children play outdoors unsupervised.

The immediate effects include reduced stamina, sleep problems and a higher likelihood of social stigma. More seriously, obesity during childhood can establish patterns that persist into adulthood. Children who become accustomed to sugary food and sedentary routines may find those habits difficult to reverse later. This increases the risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and joint problems at a younger age than previous generations experienced.

There are also psychological and social consequences. Overweight children may face bullying or exclusion, which can damage confidence and encourage further withdrawal from sport. Families and health systems then carry a growing financial burden as preventable conditions require long-term treatment. The trend therefore creates a cycle of physical and emotional disadvantage rather than an isolated medical issue.

In conclusion, childhood obesity is driven by unhealthy food environments and declining everyday activity. Its effects are cumulative, affecting health, self-esteem and public expenditure. Because the causes are structural as well as personal, the problem should be addressed through coordinated action by families, schools, food producers and governments.

Key words and phrases

energy-dense food
Food containing many calories per serving or gram.
displaced a substantial amount of active play
Replaced much time previously spent moving and playing.
establish patterns that persist into adulthood
Create habits that continue after childhood.
creates a cycle of physical and emotional disadvantage
Physical and emotional difficulties repeatedly worsen each other.
public expenditure
Money spent by government.
