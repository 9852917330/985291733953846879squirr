Work & Employment

Is a compulsory retirement age out of date?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

As populations age, some countries are considering abolishing compulsory retirement ages. Do you think a fixed retirement age is out of date, or should it be maintained?

Model answer

As people live longer and remain healthy for longer, several countries are debating whether a fixed retirement age still makes sense. In my opinion compulsory retirement is out of date and should be replaced by more flexible arrangements.

The case for keeping a fixed age is not without merit. It allows employers to plan, and it opens positions for younger staff who would otherwise wait years for promotion. In physically demanding or safety-critical occupations, such as construction, surgery or flying aircraft, there is also a reasonable argument for setting a limit rather than assessing each individual.

Nevertheless, the principle is difficult to defend. Forcing a competent employee to stop working on a particular birthday is a form of age discrimination, since it judges a person by their date of birth rather than by their performance. It is also wasteful, because experienced staff take decades of knowledge with them and many are dismissed while still at the height of their abilities. From a national perspective it is unsustainable, as a shrinking workforce cannot support a rapidly growing number of pensioners.

The sensible alternative is to remove the fixed age and rely instead on regular assessment of health and competence, combined with the option of reduced hours. Those who wish to leave at sixty should be free to do so, provided their pension allows it, and those who wish to continue should be able to.

In conclusion, a compulsory retirement age belongs to an era of shorter lives and larger young populations, and I believe it should now give way to individual choice.

Key words and phrases

opens positions for younger staff
Creates job vacancies for younger employees.
assessing each individual
Judging each person separately.
at the height of their abilities
At their strongest level of skill.
regular assessment of health and competence
Repeated checks of wellbeing and work ability.
