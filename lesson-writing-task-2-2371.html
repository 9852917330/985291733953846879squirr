Business & Companies

Should big firms provide local facilities?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

All large companies should provide sports and social facilities for the local community. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Large employers draw workers, traffic and land from the places they operate in, which raises the question of what they should give back. I partly agree that they should fund local facilities, but requiring every large company to build them would be the wrong way to arrange it.

There is a solid argument for the obligation. Big firms benefit from local infrastructure that the public paid for, and they impose costs through congestion and pressure on housing, so contributing to the community is closer to settling a bill than to charity. They also have the resources: a sports hall costs a large corporation very little and may be beyond a small town entirely. The benefits return to the company as well, since healthier employees take fewer sick days, and firms with a good local reputation find it easier to recruit and to obtain planning permission.

The objection is that companies are poor providers of public services. Their core purpose is to produce goods and employ people, and facilities built by a firm depend on its profits, so a downturn closes the swimming pool the town has come to rely on. Provision would also be arbitrary, since districts with a large employer would gain while poorer areas with none receive nothing at all. Companies naturally build what suits their own staff rather than what a community needs, and control over local amenities gives a private firm influence that no elected body has granted it.

The better arrangement is financial rather than physical. Companies should pay fair taxes and, where a development imposes real costs, contribute to a fund that the local authority spends according to need. Sponsorship and opening existing facilities to residents are welcome additions.

In conclusion, large companies owe something to the places that host them, and many give generously. Requiring each to build facilities produces uneven and unreliable provision, so paying towards services that the community decides on serves everyone better.

Key words and phrases

obtain planning permission
Receive official approval for a building or development.
receive nothing at all
Get no benefit or payment.
no elected body has granted it
No democratically chosen authority has approved it.
paying towards services that the community decides on
Contributing money to publicly chosen services.
