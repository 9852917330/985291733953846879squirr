Education

What is school actually for?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people say that the main purpose of school is to turn children into good citizens and workers, rather than to benefit them as individuals. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

States pay for education out of taxes, so it is natural to ask what they are buying. I partly agree that schools exist to produce capable citizens and workers, but I think the statement sets up a division that does not hold.

The social purpose is undeniable. No government funds twelve years of teaching out of pure generosity. Schools deliver the literacy and numeracy an economy needs, and they also teach queuing, punctuality, sharing and disagreeing without violence, which is where the habits of living together are actually learned. A country whose schools failed at this would find both its workplaces and its politics unmanageable.

The individual purpose is equally real, however. A child who learns to read gains a private life of books that has nothing to do with any employer. Music, sport and history repay nobody in productivity, yet they are what many adults look back on. Education also lets a child discover what they are good at, which is the difference between a chosen life and an assigned one.

What makes the statement misleading is that these aims mostly produce each other. A confident, curious adult is a better employee than an obedient one, and a person who has been encouraged to think for themselves makes a better citizen than someone trained only to comply. The two purposes only separate at the extremes, where a system drills children for examinations and turns out graduates who can pass tests but cannot think.

In conclusion, schools do serve society, and there is nothing shameful in saying so. Yet they serve it best by developing individuals rather than by shaping them for a role, so the choice the statement offers is a false one.

Key words and phrases

the habits of living together
Everyday ways of sharing life with others.
discover what they are good at
Identify their own strengths.
drills children for examinations
Trains pupils through repeated exam exercises.
