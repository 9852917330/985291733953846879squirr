Crime & Punishment

Prison or rehabilitation?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people believe that criminals should be sent to prison, while others think they should receive education and job training instead. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

When someone is convicted of a crime, society must decide how to protect the public while reducing the likelihood of further offending. Some people favour imprisonment, whereas others prefer education and job training. In my opinion, these approaches should be combined according to the seriousness of the offence and the individual's circumstances.

The strongest argument for prison is public protection. Someone who poses a serious danger may need to be detained, and a substantial offence should have meaningful consequences. Imprisonment can also demonstrate that the law recognises the harm suffered by victims. However, the need for punishment does not by itself explain what should happen during a sentence or how a person should prepare for release.

Education and training address a different part of the problem. Some offenders face difficulties with literacy, qualifications or finding legitimate employment. If these barriers remain unchanged, release may return them to the same circumstances that contributed to their offending. Teaching reading, supporting recognised qualifications and helping people develop practical work skills can make lawful employment more realistic. The value of a programme should be judged by its quality and results rather than by the assumption that any classroom activity will prevent crime.

I would therefore reserve imprisonment for cases where the seriousness of the offence or the risk to others justifies it. Suitable lower-risk offenders could receive supervised community sentences with relevant support. In prison, education should be accompanied by planning for housing, treatment where needed and employment after release. These measures do not excuse the original offence; they aim to prevent another victim being harmed.

In conclusion, prison and rehabilitation serve different but compatible purposes. A fair system should impose proportionate consequences while giving offenders a realistic opportunity to return to society without committing further crimes.

Key words and phrases

public protection
Keeping people safe from harm.
meaningful consequences
Results that make an action carry real responsibility.
legitimate employment
Lawful work through which someone earns money.
recognised qualifications
Certificates or skills accepted by relevant employers or institutions.
supervised community sentences
Court-ordered penalties carried out in the community under oversight.
proportionate consequences
Penalties that match the seriousness of the wrongdoing.
