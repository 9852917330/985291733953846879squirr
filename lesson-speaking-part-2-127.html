An art and craft activity you did at school

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an art and craft activity you did at school

You should say: What it was What you did for this activity Who you did it with And explain how you felt about this activity.

Simple Model Answer

A craft activity I tried at primary school was origami, the art of making shapes by folding paper. Many children in my class enjoyed it, and I wanted to learn because their paper animals looked impressive.

I asked my desk mate to teach me. He was good at it and chose a paper crane as our first project. He showed each fold and told me to follow the steps carefully. However, my paper quickly became uneven, and the finished shape did not look like a bird. He explained it again patiently, but my second attempt was not much better.

I felt disappointed at the time. Looking back, I think I was rushing because I wanted a good result immediately. Origami requires careful, accurate folds, not speed. The experience helped me understand why skilled paper artists deserve respect. A simple-looking shape can take a lot of patience. I would like to try again one day, but next time I would work more slowly and enjoy learning rather than expecting to succeed at once.

Key words and phrases

paper crane
A folded-paper model of a long-legged bird.
accurate folds

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a craft activity I did back in primary school - origami. I probably don't need to explain much, since it's already so popular all over the world; it's basically the art of folding paper into a particular shape or animal.

It was all the rage at my old school, because it takes real skill and patience to fold a decent-looking figure - it felt like a proper challenge for us as kids. I got swept up in the hype too, so I asked my desk mate to show me the ropes. He was easily one of the best in our class at it. He decided to teach me the simplest figure first, the crane, and made it look effortless. He told me to just follow the steps exactly and I'd end up with a paper crane.

Easier said than done, though - mine came out misshapen beyond recognition. I genuinely had no idea what I was doing wrong. Even when he patiently showed me a second time, it barely improved, and eventually he just gave up on me. I was pretty disappointed in myself, to be honest.

In retrospect, I think I was just too careless and in too much of a rush - I wanted to get it right straightaway, but origami really isn't about speed, it's about patience and precision. That's probably why I admire origami artists so much now. I might give it another go someday, but next time I'll take my time and actually relax into it.
