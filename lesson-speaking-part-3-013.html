Celebrations

Parties in your country and what happens at them

Part 3

Prompt

What types of parties are popular in your country? What sort of activities do people do at parties? What do you think of attending parties?

What types of parties are popular in your country?

Simple Model Answer

Weddings are especially important and can have hundreds of guests. Children's birthdays, New Year gatherings and company celebrations are common too. However, younger people often prefer small, relaxed events. Large parties, particularly weddings, can cost a lot, so meeting a few friends may be a more practical choice for them.

Key words and phrases

practical choice
An option that works well with the time, money or resources available.

Advanced Model Answer

Weddings are the biggest by some distance — they're often enormous, with hundreds of guests, and families save for years to hold them. Birthday parties are common for children, rather less so for adults. Then there are family gatherings around New Year and traditional festivals, which matter more than birthdays to most people. Companies hold year-end celebrations too, which are half social and half obligation — you're expected to show up even when you'd rather not. Younger people increasingly prefer small informal gatherings to large formal ones, partly because the enormous weddings are getting harder to afford.

Key words and phrases

half social and half obligation
partly enjoyable company and partly a duty

What sort of activities do people do at parties?

Simple Model Answer

Most parties involve a meal, conversation, photos and music, sometimes including karaoke. Weddings may also have speeches and traditional activities. I think a good party needs some organisation so guests feel comfortable, but enough free time for them to talk. If every minute is planned, people may not really enjoy being together.

Key words and phrases

free time
Time without a fixed task or organised activity.

Advanced Model Answer

Eating's central, since most gatherings are built around a meal and the food's often the point rather than an accompaniment. Beyond that, people talk, take an enormous number of photographs, and there's usually music, sometimes karaoke. At weddings there are speeches and formal rituals too. The best parties in my experience have just enough structure to stop people standing around awkwardly, without scheduling every minute so tightly that nobody can simply sit and talk. Get that balance wrong in either direction and people start checking their phones.

Key words and phrases

just enough structure
the minimum organisation needed
checking their phones
looking at messages or information on their phones

What do you think of attending parties?

Simple Model Answer

I enjoy smaller parties where I can have a proper conversation, perhaps with ten or fifteen people. At a huge party, loud music and many short introductions make it harder to connect. I may be glad I attended, but still feel that I barely spoke to anyone. So the size matters more than the party itself.

Key words and phrases

connect
Build a meaningful or friendly relationship with someone.

Advanced Model Answer

I enjoy them when they're small enough for actual conversation, perhaps ten or fifteen people. Large parties I find harder work: the music's too loud to talk properly, and you end up having the same brief exchange twenty times without really speaking to anyone. I'm glad I went, usually, but I often leave feeling I've been social rather than connected with anybody in particular, which is a slightly hollow feeling. Small gatherings don't have that problem, so it really comes down to numbers rather than parties as a whole.

Key words and phrases

the same brief exchange twenty times
repeating a short conversation with many people
been social rather than connected with anybody
spent time among people without forming a close bond
