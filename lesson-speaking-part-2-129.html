Activity

An indoor game that you enjoyed as a child

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an indoor game that you enjoyed as a child.

You should say: - what the game was - where you played it - who played this game with you - and why you liked it

Simple Model Answer

An indoor game I enjoyed as a child was chess. A primary school classmate taught me when I was about seven or eight. He had a small chess set, and we played during breaks once I understood how the pieces moved.

Chess is played by two people. Each player tries to protect their king while putting the other king into a position where it cannot escape. Later, my parents bought me my own set for my birthday. I taught my younger brother, and I also joined the school chess club.

Practising against older pupils helped me improve. In my final primary year, I joined the five-person school team and competed against other schools in our town. I liked the challenge of thinking ahead instead of choosing the first move that looked good. Winning felt exciting, but losing was useful too because I could see my mistakes. Chess taught me to be patient and to congratulate an opponent who played better. It was enjoyable, but it also helped me develop a more careful way of thinking.

Key words and phrases

thinking ahead
Considering what may happen next before deciding what to do.
opponent
The person you are playing or competing against.

Advanced Model Answer

As a child I enjoyed playing chess. I think chess is probably the best known board game in the world. It's a game for two players, and the aim is to defeat the other player by taking his or her pieces and eventually trapping his King. This final move is called checkmate.

I remember that it was a classmate of mine at primary school who first taught me to play chess. He had a small, portable chess set, and once I knew how each piece moved, we started playing at break and lunch times; we played in our classroom or outside on the school playground. Later my parents bought me my own chess set as a birthday present so that I could play at home. I taught my younger brother to play, and at some point I joined the school chess club.

There I had the chance to hone my skills against some of the older pupils, and in my final year of primary school I made it onto the school team. There were five of us on the team, and we competed against children from other primary schools in the same town. I liked playing chess because I enjoyed the challenge of thinking ahead and trying to outwit my opponent. I was probably seven or eight years old when I started playing, and it seemed like a very mysterious and intellectual game at that time.

Also, although I loved winning, chess taught me to learn from my losses and to congratulate the person who had beaten me.
