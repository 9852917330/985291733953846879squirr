Activity

A short trip that was special to you

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a short trip that was special to you

You should say: when it was where you went who you went with And explain why it was special.

OUTLINE

short bike ride last Saturday w/ three friends around Hoan Kiem/Sword Lake
met Ly Thai To statue 5:30am still dark, looped lake before sunrise
misty quiet, photographers taking ao dai photos by water, stopped for banh mi on kerb

short trip ~2hrs but stuck with me as favourite morning this year

Simple Model Answer

A short trip that was special to me was a bike ride around Hoan Kiem Lake last Saturday. I went with three friends, and we met at the Ly Thai To statue at half past five in the morning.

It was still dark and a little misty when we started. We cycled around the lake several times while the city was quiet. Some photographers were taking pictures of women wearing ao dai by the water. Around six, we stopped at a street stall for banh mi and ate breakfast together.

I liked the trip because it showed me a calmer side of Hanoi. There was little traffic, and we could enjoy the view while chatting. The exercise did not feel like hard work because we were having fun. As the streets became busier around seven, we cycled home by a different route and noticed places we usually ignored. The whole outing lasted only about two hours, but it became one of my favourite mornings of the year. It reminded me that a memorable trip does not have to be long or expensive.

Key words and phrases

a different route
Another way of travelling between places.
outing
A short journey or visit made for enjoyment.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to describe a short bike ride I went on last Saturday with three friends, around Hoan Kiem Lake - or Sword Lake, as it's also known - right in the middle of Hanoi.

We met at the Ly Thai To statue at half past five, while it was still pretty dark, and cycled a full loop of the lake a few times before the sun properly came up. What I remember most is how quiet and misty it was - there were already a couple of photographers set up, taking pictures of women in ao dai by the water, which I thought was such a lovely scene to stumble across that early. Around six, we stopped at a street stall for banh mi and just sat on the kerb eating breakfast while the city slowly woke up around us.

I really enjoyed that ride for quite a few reasons. Mainly, it let me see a side of Hanoi I never usually notice - no traffic, no noise, just the lake and a few other early risers out jogging or doing tai chi. It was also a nice bit of exercise without it feeling like a chore, since we were chatting the whole way round instead of really thinking about the effort. And once the streets did start filling up with traffic around seven, we cycled home a completely different way, just to see a few streets none of us had really paid attention to before.

It wasn't a long trip by any means, maybe two hours all together, but it's stuck with me as one of my favourite mornings this year - sometimes the simplest plans end up being the ones you remember best.
