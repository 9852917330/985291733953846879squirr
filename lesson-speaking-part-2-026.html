Person

Someone you admire who is much older than you

Part 2

Prompt

Describe someone you admire who is much older than you

You should say: who this person is how long you have known him/ her what qualities he/ she has And say why you admire him/ her.

OUTLINE

>>> my grandmother
grandmother, almost 90, a huge influence my whole life, moved in with us after grandfather died
kind and wise - turn to her for advice; specific example: ran into a neighbour's burning house at 70 to help pull a man out, without a second thought
raised 4 kids incl. my father; specific example: comforted me after I failed an exam by telling me about failing her own teacher-training exam twice before passing
admire her unconditional love, she's the closest thing I have to a mentor and has shaped my whole outlook on life

Simple Model Answer

An older person I admire is my grandmother, who is almost ninety. She moved in with us after my grandfather died, so I spent much of my childhood with her. She is kind and wise, and I still ask her for advice.

One event showed how brave she is. When a neighbour's house caught fire, she helped bring out an elderly man who could not walk alone. She was about seventy herself, but she acted while other people were still panicking.

She is also very good at helping people feel better. When I failed an important exam, she sat with me over tea and explained that she had failed her teacher-training exam twice before passing. Hearing this helped me see my own problem differently. She raised four children and is still the person our family turns to for support. I admire her because her love does not depend on my success. Spending time with her makes me calmer and reminds me that one failure is not the end.

Key words and phrases

turns to for support
Goes to someone when help or comfort is needed.
does not depend on my success
Is there whether I succeed or fail.

Advanced Model Answer

The first person who comes to mind is my grandmother. She's almost ninety now, and she's been a huge influence on me for as long as I can remember — literally, since I've known her my whole life. She actually moved in with us not long after my grandfather passed away, so I got to spend basically my entire childhood and my teenage years growing up right alongside her.

She's kind and wise, which is exactly why I still turn to her for advice whenever I'm stuck on something. I remember one time that really impressed me — a neighbour's house caught fire late one night, and while everyone else just stood outside panicking, my grandmother, who was about seventy at the time, ran straight back in to help drag out an elderly man who couldn't walk on his own. She never makes a big deal of it, but I still think about how without a second thought she just went in.

She raised four children of her own, one of whom is my father, and she's still the person the whole family turns to when something's wrong. When I failed an important exam a couple of years ago and felt like a total failure, she just made a pot of tea, sat with me on the porch, and told me how she'd failed her own teacher-training exam twice before finally passing on the third attempt — and somehow, by the time she'd finished, it all felt a lot less enormous than it had five minutes earlier.

I could talk about her for hours, but if I had to pick one reason I admire her most, it's that her love for me has never once had any conditions attached. She's the closest thing I have to a mentor, and even now, talking to her genuinely calms me down when everything else feels chaotic. I think she's shaped my whole outlook on life, and there's a real bond between us that I don't think will ever fade.
