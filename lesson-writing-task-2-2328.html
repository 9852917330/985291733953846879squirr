Sport & Leisure

Games on handheld devices

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Entertainment such as electronic games on handheld devices is harmful to individuals and to the society they live in. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Phones have put an endless supply of games into every pocket, and the criticism that follows them is severe. I partly agree, because the harm lies less in the games themselves than in how these particular ones are built.

The defence is reasonable. Games demand attention, planning and quick judgement, and studies of regular players show improvements in spatial reasoning and reaction time. They are a cheap and harmless way to fill a commute, and multiplayer games sustain friendships across distance, which matters to people who are isolated. Treating them as worthless while accepting television as normal is a double standard rather than an argument.

The concerns are still legitimate. Handheld games are designed by companies paid for attention, so they use daily rewards, streaks and near-misses borrowed directly from gambling. The purpose is to make stopping difficult, and for a minority it succeeds completely: schoolwork, sleep and exercise disappear. Many now include purchases dressed as chance, which introduces children to gambling before they can recognise it. The social cost is subtler, since hours absorbed in a screen are hours taken from somewhere else, most often from sleep, movement and the company of other people.

The distinction that matters is between playing and being kept playing. A game with an ending is entertainment; a game engineered never to finish is a product designed against its user. This points to remedies rather than bans: regulating purchases aimed at children, requiring companies to disclose how the rewards are timed, and teaching families to set limits.

In conclusion, portable games are not harmful in themselves, and they give millions of people pleasure and connection. The commercial design behind many of them is harmful, and that is what regulation should be aimed at rather than the games as a whole.

Key words and phrases

a double standard rather than an argument
Applying unequal rules rather than offering sound reasoning.
disclose how the rewards are timed
Explain when and how game rewards appear.
what regulation should be aimed at
The problem rules should specifically address.
