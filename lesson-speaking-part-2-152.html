Situation

A change in your life

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a change in your life.

You should say: What the change was When it happened Why it happened And explain how you have benefited from this change

Simple Model Answer

A change I made in my life was becoming more active and improving my eating habits at seventeen. I was in my final year of high school and spent many evenings revising while eating fast food and snacks. Over time, I gained weight and felt less comfortable.

I spoke to my aunt, who is a nutritionist, and began making changes. I cooked more meals at home, drank water instead of soft drinks and joined a local gym. I also tried to sleep earlier and sometimes cycled before school. Keeping the routine took effort because it was different from how I had lived before.

Over six months, my weight changed from around sixty-five to fifty kilos. For me, though, the most important result was feeling stronger and more confident. I stopped avoiding photographs and felt happier wearing clothes I already owned. This was a personal experience rather than a plan for anyone else. It taught me that lasting change depends on daily habits and patience, not simply wanting a quick result.

Key words and phrases

nutritionist
Someone trained to advise people about food and nutrition.
lasting change
A difference that continues for a long time.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about one of the greatest transformations in my life, which happened when I was seventeen, in my final year of high school. Back then I weighed around sixty-five kilos, and after about six months of continuous dieting and working out, I lost fifteen kilos and got down to a weight I actually felt comfortable with.

The reason it happened was that my eating habits had got completely out of control. As a teenager I mainly lived on fast food, things like fried chicken, burgers and crisps, and because I had so many exams to prepare for, I'd often stay up late revising and snacking at the same time. Unsurprisingly, I put on weight fairly quickly, and one morning I stepped on the bathroom scale and it read sixty-five kilos. That's when I decided enough was enough.

After talking to my aunt, who's a nutritionist, I realised that losing weight really just comes down to diet and exercise, there's no shortcut. So I cleared my fridge of junk food, started cooking homemade meals, and swapped soft drinks for at least two litres of water a day. I also signed up at my local gym, trained three times a week, and got into the habit of going to bed early and waking up at five to cycle before school.

After six months of sticking to that routine, my weight had dropped to fifty kilos, but honestly, the number on the scale wasn't even the best part. I felt stronger and happier than I had in years, and I stopped getting ill all the time. Most importantly, I got my confidence back. I used to run and hide whenever someone pulled out a camera, but now I don't mind being photographed at all. Best of all, I can finally fit into my old dresses and skinny jeans again.
