Advertising & Consumerism

Does advertising make everyone the same?

Opinion

Prompt

Some people argue that advertising is discouraging people from being different individuals, and makes people to be or seem to be the same. Do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Advertising often encourages people to buy the same popular products, but it does not completely remove individuality. I partly agree that advertising promotes conformity, especially through global brands and social media. However, consumers still interpret messages differently and can use products to express personal preferences.

The pressure to conform is real. Companies repeat images of the ideal home, body or lifestyle until these standards appear normal. Influencers then display the same clothes, phones and beauty products to millions of followers. Young consumers may purchase these items mainly to avoid feeling excluded. In this way, advertising can turn social approval into a sales tool and narrow the range of choices that seem acceptable.

Nevertheless, people are not passive. Income, age, culture and personal values shape how they respond to a campaign. The same advertisement may attract one person and annoy another. Digital markets also make specialised products easier to find, so individuals can choose independent designers, niche music or goods linked to particular interests. Advertising may influence the options people notice, but it rarely determines every final decision.

Platforms and regulators can also require clear labels for paid promotion, helping audiences separate personal recommendation from commercial persuasion. It also discourages hidden commercial influence in everyday online content.

In conclusion, advertising contributes to similar tastes by promoting a limited set of highly visible lifestyles. Yet it cannot erase personal judgement or cultural difference. The best response is not to blame advertising for all conformity, but to improve media literacy and give consumers a wider and more transparent range of choices.

Key words and phrases

until these standards appear normal
Until people accept these expectations as usual.
mainly to avoid feeling excluded
Mostly to prevent social rejection.
turn social approval into a sales tool
Use people's wish for acceptance to sell.
separate personal recommendation from commercial persuasion
Distinguish friendly advice from sales messages.
a wider and more transparent range of choices
More options with clearer information.
