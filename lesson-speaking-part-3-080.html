Sports & Exercise

Attending sporting events and funding sport

Part 3

Prompt

Do you often go to sporting events? Are you very competitive? Do you agree or disagree that governments should spend large sums on major sporting events?

Do you often go to sporting events?

Simple Model Answer

Not regularly, but I sometimes attend major events. The shared reaction of thousands of fans creates an atmosphere television cannot copy, even if I see less detail than at home. However, tickets, travel and food can make one match expensive. So I enjoy the experience, but cost limits how often I can go.

Key words and phrases

limits
Restricts the amount or frequency of something.

Advanced Model Answer

Not often, but I go when I can for the bigger ones. What television genuinely can't reproduce is the atmosphere — thousands of people reacting simultaneously, and the sense that you're part of something rather than just watching it. You actually see less of the detail than you would at home, but you feel far more. The obstacle's cost, though: between tickets, travel and food, a single match's become an expensive day out.

Key words and phrases

feel far more
experience a much stronger feeling of
an expensive day out
an outing that costs a great deal

Are you very competitive?

Simple Model Answer

I mainly compete with my previous performance. Running faster or doing a task better than last month motivates me without making everything about beating others. Direct comparison is less useful because someone will always be better. Personal progress gives me a clearer and more realistic target, although I still enjoy challenging myself.

Key words and phrases

challenging myself
Trying something difficult to test or improve my ability.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd say I'm competitive with myself rather than with other people. I like measuring progress — running slightly faster than last month, doing something better than I did before — and that keeps me motivated without much downside. Competing directly with others I find less useful, because it turns everything into a question of status, and there's always somebody better than you. Comparing yourself with your previous self is the version you can actually win.

Key words and phrases

a question of status
an issue of being seen as important by others
the version you can actually win
the level of competition where you can realistically succeed

Do you agree or disagree that governments should spend large sums on major sporting events?

Simple Model Answer

I partly agree because major events can improve facilities and create national pride. However, costs often rise beyond the plan, and stadiums may be unused afterwards. That is difficult to accept when essential services need money. Governments should explain spending openly and show how every venue will benefit the public long after the event.

Key words and phrases

venue
Places where events or activities are held.

Advanced Model Answer

I partly agree, though I'm sceptical. The arguments for it are real — infrastructure gets built, the country gets attention, and there's genuine national pride. But the costs regularly run far over budget, and cities are left with stadiums nobody uses. It's very hard to defend when hospitals are short of staff. If a government does bid, I'd say the spending should be transparent and every venue should have a clear use afterwards, not just for the two weeks of the event itself.

Key words and phrases

genuine national pride
sincere pride in your country
run far over budget
cost much more than the planned amount
