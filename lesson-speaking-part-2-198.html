Object & Favourite

An environment law

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an environmental law.

You should say: what it is how you first learned about it who benefits from it And explain how you feel about this law

OUTLINE

Simple Model Answer

An environmental rule I am interested in concerns sorting household waste. I first learned about it through posters near my flat and reminders in our building's group chat. The message was to separate organic waste, general rubbish and recyclable materials.

The system uses different bins so that people know where to put each type. Before learning about it, I had not thought carefully about how difficult mixed rubbish can be for collectors to handle. Separating it at home seemed like a practical way for residents to help.

I like the idea because it connects a large environmental problem with something people can do every day. Public bins and clear instructions are important; simply telling people to care is not enough. The campaign around the rule also encouraged conversations about waste in my building. I do not remember the exact official name, but its main purpose is clear to me. It aims to make disposal more organised and help people take responsibility for the rubbish they produce, rather than leaving the whole problem to someone else.

Key words and phrases

recyclable materials
Things that can be processed and used to make new products.
disposal
The process of getting rid of unwanted things or waste.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a rule the government introduced a few years ago that's had a genuinely positive effect on how people in Vietnam deal with rubbish. I don't remember its exact official name, but basically it requires households to sort their waste into three separate bins — organic waste, general waste, and anything recyclable — and the city's had to install thousands of these colour-coded bins in public areas to make it work.

I first found out about it through a poster campaign near my apartment, and then later through my building's group chat, which kept sending reminders about which colour bin was for what. Everyone benefits from it, really, but I'd say it's mostly the local environment and the waste collectors themselves — before this, a lot of collectors had to sort through mixed, often quite unpleasant rubbish by hand.

Before the law, waste disposal was a nightmare to manage for local authorities, mainly because people had very little environmental awareness — rubbish just got dumped into rivers, left on pavements, or thrown wherever was convenient. It did real damage to the environment, on top of making a lot of neighbourhoods look and smell awful.

Since the law came in, and with a fair bit of campaigning alongside it, there's been a real shift in how people think about rubbish. There's now an effective and practical solution for getting rid of everyday waste, and people's awareness of the whole issue has grown a lot over the years. I'm honestly quite happy about how this law's helped tackle the waste problem here, rather than everyone just talking about it without anything actually changing.
