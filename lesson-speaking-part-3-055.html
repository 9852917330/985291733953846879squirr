History

What the past teaches, and what may come next

Prompt

What do you think we can learn by studying events of the past? What important events do you think might take place in the future?

What do you think we can learn by studying events of the past?

Simple Model Answer

History helps us understand earlier mistakes and how societies developed. Governments can compare past financial crises or conflicts with present problems, rather than assume everything is new. We do not always use the lessons well, but studying them still gives useful context. History also helps explain who we are and how our communities came to be.

Key words and phrases

context
Background information that helps explain a situation.

Advanced Model Answer

I think we can learn a lot by studying history. Just as individual people learn from their mistakes, societies can learn from the mistakes made by previous governments or leaders. For example, from what I've read in the newspapers, many economists are looking back to the time of the Great Depression, around 80 years ago, in order to understand the financial crisis that is currently affecting many countries around the world. Even if we don't always learn from mistakes, I think it's fascinating to study history because it gives us an insight into who we are and where we come from.

Key words and phrases

learn from mistakes
use an error to do better next time
an insight into who we are
a useful understanding of our own nature

What important events do you think might take place in the future?

Simple Model Answer

It is very hard to predict major events. People in the distant past could not easily imagine later achievements such as walking on the moon. Slow changes, like an ageing population, are easier to expect than sudden discoveries or crises. I hope future science will find better treatments for serious diseases and help people live longer.

Key words and phrases

an ageing population
A population in which older people form a growing share.

Advanced Model Answer

It's really difficult to predict what will happen in the future; most of the big, historic events of the past would have been impossible to foresee. For example, I don't think that anyone living 100 years ago could have imagined that people would one day walk on the moon. That's not true of every kind of change, though — slow trends like an ageing population are fairly predictable decades in advance; it's the sudden breakthroughs and shocks that catch everyone out. If I had to guess, I'd like to think that scientists will invent cures for diseases like cancer, and we'll all live longer.

Key words and phrases

impossible to foresee
not possible to predict
slow trends like an ageing population
gradual changes such as more elderly people in society
invent cures for diseases
discover treatments that end illnesses
