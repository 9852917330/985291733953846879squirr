Education

Does class size matter in language learning?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people argue that small classes for language students are beneficial, whereas others say numbers do not matter. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Model answer

Language schools often advertise small classes as their main advantage, while others insist that the number of students is far less important than how they are taught. In my view size matters more in language teaching than in most other subjects.

The argument for small groups rests on speaking time. A language is learned by producing it, and in a class of thirty each student may speak for under a minute in an hour, whereas in a group of eight the same lesson gives them ten times as much practice. Correction also becomes possible: a teacher with eight students can hear individual errors and address them, while a teacher with thirty can only correct the class as a whole. Smaller groups are less intimidating too, and learners who are afraid of appearing foolish in front of a large audience often stay silent for an entire term.

Those who say numbers do not matter point to method. A skilled teacher can divide a large class into pairs so that half the room is speaking at once, and a poor teacher with six students may still lecture at them. Motivation matters more than either: a determined learner who practises daily will progress in any class, while a reluctant one will not improve in a group of four.

My own position accepts this second point without accepting its conclusion. Method and motivation are decisive, but a large class limits what even a good teacher can do, because speaking time is divided by the number of people present.

In conclusion, teaching quality matters most, yet class size sets a ceiling on it. Small groups are worth paying for in language learning specifically, because the subject cannot be learned by listening.

Key words and phrases

correct the class as a whole
Give general feedback to everyone together.
stay silent for an entire term
Never speak during a whole school term.
improve in a group of four
Develop skills while working with three others.
cannot be learned by listening
Requires active practice, not only hearing explanations.
