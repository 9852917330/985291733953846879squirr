Education

When should school begin?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think that children should start school at a very early age, but others believe that children should not go to school until they are older. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Model answer

School starting ages vary widely, from four in Britain to seven in much of Scandinavia. The disagreement is genuine, and my own view is that early attendance is valuable provided that what happens in the classroom suits the age of the children in it.

Those in favour of an early start point to the pace of early development. Vocabulary and social behaviour develop fastest before the age of six, and a structured setting exposes children to more language, more books and more negotiation with other children than many homes can provide. The effect is largest for children from disadvantaged families, for whom nursery and early school close a gap that otherwise only widens with time. Early attendance also allows parents to return to work.

The opposing argument is that formal instruction too early does more harm than good. A four-year-old asked to sit still and form letters may simply learn that school is unpleasant, and countries that begin reading instruction at seven catch up within a few years while reporting fewer children who dislike school. Long days away from home at that age can also be genuinely tiring for a small child.

My position is that this debate confuses starting age with teaching method. The evidence against early schooling concerns formal lessons and testing, not attendance itself, and countries with the best results send children early but spend those first years on play, talk and stories rather than on worksheets and assessment.

In conclusion, an early start benefits children, particularly the least advantaged, provided the early years are built around play and language. The mistake is not sending a four-year-old to school but treating a four-year-old like a pupil of ten.

Key words and phrases

only widens with time
The difference keeps becoming larger.
genuinely tiring for a small child
Causing real fatigue in a young child.
worksheets and assessment
Written exercises and checks of learning.
treating a four-year-old like a pupil of ten
Expecting adult-like schoolwork from a very young child.
