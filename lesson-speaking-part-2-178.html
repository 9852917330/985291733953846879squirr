Situation

A time when you were really busy

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you were really busy.

You should say: - when this was - why you were so busy - how you managed the situation - and explain how you felt at the time.

Simple Model Answer

A time I was extremely busy was during my Master's degree about ten years ago. I had several assignments, including one three-thousand-word essay that I found particularly difficult. Unfortunately, I kept delaying it until only two days remained.

I then had to do everything quickly: read sources, choose my argument and write a final version. I felt worried that I would miss the deadline. To manage, I limited myself to ten useful sources instead of trying to read too much. I highlighted relevant ideas, made short notes and began writing the main sections before the introduction.

The pressure forced me to work in a more organised way, and I submitted the assignment on time. I even received a reasonable mark. However, I was exhausted afterwards and felt more relieved than proud. The experience showed me that I could focus under pressure, but it also revealed the cost of leaving work too late. I would rather use the efficient methods I discovered while starting earlier, instead of depending on another stressful two-day rush.

Advanced Model Answer

I want to tell you about the time I left a university assignment until the very last minute and had to work flat out to get it finished. This was about ten years ago, during my Master's degree. We had several 3,000-word assignments to write for different modules, but there was one in particular that I really struggled with.

I'd been procrastinating and putting it off for weeks, if I'm honest, and in the end I left myself just two days to get the whole thing done - finding and reading relevant books and articles, deciding on my thesis and title, and producing a final draft to hand in before the deadline. Looking back, I don't know what I was thinking, because for most of those two days I was in a genuine state of panic, convinced I wouldn't make it in time.

Oddly enough, though, that pressure ended up forcing me to work in a much more efficient way than I normally would. My first step was narrowing down the general area I wanted to write about, then I limited myself to just ten sources instead of trying to read everything. I printed the most relevant pages, highlighted the key paragraphs, and jotted down notes to summarise the ideas I found useful. After that I just started writing the main body straightaway, leaving the title and introduction until last.

I did hand it in on time, and I actually got a decent mark, but I remember feeling completely drained afterwards - more relieved than proud, really. It taught me that I work best under pressure, but also that cutting it that fine isn't something I want to repeat too often.
