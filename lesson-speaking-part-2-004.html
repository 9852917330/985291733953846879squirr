A family member who has had an important influence on you

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a family member who has had an important influence on you

You should say: Who the person is; What kind of person he or she is; What this person has done to influence you; And explain why you think this person is important to you.

OUTLINE

>>> my mother
mother played essential role in life, most of who I am is down to her
in 40s, maintains youthful look, compassionate kind-hearted, gives valuable advice, stands by me
clearest influence: piano story age 16 - wanted to quit, mother made me stick it out a year
learned not to quit when things get hard - stuck with me since, through exams and jobs
determination and perseverance something to look up to, piano lesson reminds me to be as strong as her

Simple Model Answer

My mother has had the biggest influence on me. She is in her forties and is a very kind person. She takes good care of our family and always listens when I need advice. Sometimes she expects everything to be perfect, but I know she wants the best for us.

One experience explains her influence clearly. When I was about sixteen, I wanted to stop learning the piano. I thought I was improving too slowly, and the lessons had become boring. My mother asked me to continue for just one more year. She explained that stopping whenever something became difficult could turn into a bad habit.

I followed her advice. By the end of that year, I was playing better and actually enjoying the piano. That experience taught me to be patient. Since then, I have remembered her advice during exams and job applications. She has helped me understand that progress takes time, and this lesson still affects my decisions today.

Key words and phrases

turn into a bad habit
Become an unhelpful action that you repeat regularly.
progress takes time
Improving usually happens gradually rather than immediately.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about my mother, who's played an essential role in my life. She's my guardian, my friend, and my role model, and if I'm honest, most of the person I've become is down to her.

My mother's in her forties now, but she still maintains a youthful look. In terms of personality, she's an extremely compassionate and kind-hearted person who always takes care of her children. She's really a mother and a friend to me, as she always gives me valuable advice and is willing to stand by me through all the ups and downs of my life. Although she sometimes drives me crazy because she's such a perfectionist, I still hold so much pride and respect for her.

The clearest way she's influenced me is through her strong will, and there's one moment I always come back to. When I was about sixteen, I wanted to quit learning the piano because I wasn't improving fast enough and I found it boring. My mother sat me down and told me that giving up the moment something got difficult would become a habit if I let it, and she made me promise to stick with it for just one more year. I did, and by the end of that year I'd actually started to enjoy it — and that lesson about not quitting when things get hard has stuck with me ever since, through exams, job applications, everything.

Her determination and perseverance are really something for us to look up to, and that piano lesson is exactly why I keep reminding myself every day to be as strong as my mother — a real-life "wonder woman".
