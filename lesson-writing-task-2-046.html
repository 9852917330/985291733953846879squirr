Culture & Traditions

Does contact between countries erode identity?

Opinion

Prompt

There is a disagreement on the impact of increased business and culture contact between countries on a country's identity. What is your opinion?

Model answer

Closer business and cultural contact between countries can weaken certain traditions, but it does not inevitably destroy national identity. In my view, international contact can strengthen rather than erase national identity when governments and communities protect what is distinctive and allow culture to develop naturally.

The main risk is cultural homogenisation. Global companies sell similar products in many countries, international media promote the same celebrities and younger people often adopt foreign fashions or expressions. Under commercial pressure, small local businesses, minority languages and traditional crafts may struggle to survive. If these trends continue without support, visible differences between societies can gradually become less pronounced.

However, contact with other countries can also increase interest in local culture. Tourism creates demand for regional food, music, architecture and festivals. International audiences may give artists and small producers new markets, while digital platforms allow communities to document and share traditions that were previously known only locally. Exposure to other cultures can even make people more conscious of their own identity because comparison shows them what is distinctive about it.

The outcome therefore depends on policy and public choice. Schools can teach local history and language, governments can support cultural institutions, and planning rules can protect important buildings and neighbourhoods. At the same time, identity should not be treated as something fixed. Cultures have always borrowed ideas from one another, and selective change can keep traditions relevant to younger generations.

In conclusion, international business and cultural exchange create some pressure towards uniformity, but they also provide resources and audiences for local culture. Countries can preserve distinctive traditions while engaging with the world on their own terms.

cultural homogenisation

become less pronounced

make people more conscious of their own identity
