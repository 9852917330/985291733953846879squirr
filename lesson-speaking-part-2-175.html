Situation

A time when you were looking at the sky

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you were looking at the sky

You should say: When and where you looked at the sky Who you looked at sky with How you felt when looking at it And explain why you looked at the sky

Simple Model Answer

A time I paid close attention to the sky was during a trip to London with my mother a couple of summers ago. We were travelling from the airport to our hotel after leaving the intense summer heat of Hanoi.

When we first arrived, the sky was bright and sunny. I had expected grey weather, so I was pleasantly surprised. I opened the taxi window to enjoy the cool air. However, halfway through the journey, thick clouds appeared and the light changed quickly. By the time we stepped out at the hotel, it was raining heavily.

That evening, my mother and I sat by the window and watched the rain. The sky looked dark and grey, very different from the sunny photographs I had imagined before the trip. I felt a little disappointed, but the quiet time together was still pleasant. I kept looking because I was surprised by how quickly the weather had changed. It became one of those ordinary travel memories that stay with you, even though nothing particularly dramatic happened.

Key words and phrases

paid close attention
Watched or listened very carefully.
pleasantly surprised
Pleased because something was better than expected.

Advanced Model Answer

A couple of summers ago I had a great opportunity to escape the scorching heat of a Hanoi summer and visit London with my mother. I remember looking up at the sky properly, really noticing it, on the drive from the airport to our hotel - something I don't think I'd ever consciously done back home.

We'd landed on a rare sunny day, which apparently isn't typical for London at all - I'd been told to expect grey skies almost the whole time. So when we stepped out of the airport into bright sunshine, I was pleasantly surprised, and I remember opening the taxi window just to feel the breeze on my face.

By the time we were halfway to the hotel, though, the sky had completely changed. Thick grey clouds had rolled in from nowhere, and it looked ready to pour down any moment. I kept glancing up, half fascinated and half worried we'd get soaked before we even checked in - which, sure enough, we did, the second we stepped out of the taxi.

That evening my mother and I sat by the hotel window for a long while, watching the rain come down in sheets and the sky turn a heavy shade of grey. Neither of us said much; we just watched it together, which was actually a nice way to end an odd first day. I think I kept looking up mainly out of disbelief - it was hard to square that dull, overcast and grey sky with the postcard pictures I'd seen of London. It wasn't unpleasant exactly, more a quiet kind of disappointment, one of those small moments that somehow sums up the whole trip.
