Environment

Who should control fresh water?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think that people should be given the right to use fresh water as they like. Others believe governments should strictly control the use of fresh water. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Fresh water is both a basic necessity and a limited resource, and there is disagreement over whether households should use it freely or whether governments should regulate consumption. I believe some control is unavoidable, though it must be designed carefully.

The argument for free use rests on the idea that water is a right rather than a commodity. People need it to drink, cook and stay clean, and rationing it risks harming the poorest households first, since a family that cannot afford a metered supply may cut back on washing before anything else. There is also a fairness objection: domestic users account for a small fraction of consumption, so restricting them while agriculture and industry continue as before places the burden on the wrong people.

The case for control is simply that demand can exceed supply. Aquifers in northern India and the American west are being pumped faster than rainfall replaces them, and once a water table falls the damage takes decades to reverse. Where no limits exist, water tends to be used on whatever is most profitable rather than most necessary, which is why arid regions end up growing thirsty crops for export.

My view is that governments must regulate, but should aim at the largest users rather than at families. Charging industry and irrigation the real cost of water, while guaranteeing every household an affordable basic allowance, cuts consumption without threatening anybody's health.

In conclusion, unrestricted use is not sustainable in regions where water is scarce, and the alternative is not rationing showers but pricing large-scale use properly. Handled this way, control protects the resource while leaving ordinary people with what they actually need.

Key words and phrases

places the burden on the wrong people
Makes the wrong group bear responsibility.
pumped faster than rainfall replaces them
Groundwater is removed faster than rain restores it.
growing thirsty crops for export
Producing water-intensive crops for overseas buyers.
threatening anybody's health
Putting someone's physical wellbeing at risk.
