A place you visited

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a place you visited

You should say: where it was (or is) when you visited it what it looked like And explain why you visited it.

Simple Model Answer

A place I visited is Sapa, a mountain town in northern Vietnam. I went there with university friends two years ago. We travelled in October and stayed for three days after a colleague showed me beautiful photos of the area.

The town has narrow streets on hillsides and is surrounded by fields built in steps along the mountains. In the morning, mist covered much of the valley before slowly clearing. We spent our days walking between villages, talking with Hmong and Dao families and trying food in small family restaurants.

The main difference from Hanoi was the slower pace of life. People seemed less hurried, and even the market was busy for only part of the morning. On our last evening, we watched the sunset from a nearby hill. The valley turned orange, and the view was wonderful. I enjoyed both the scenery and the opportunity to experience local life. I would like to return in another season to see how the fields and mountains change throughout the year.

Key words and phrases

mist
A thin cloud of tiny water drops near the ground.
slower pace of life
A way of living with less hurry and pressure.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to describe a small mountain town called Sapa, in the north of Vietnam, which I visited two years ago with my university friends.

We went in October, just before the rice terraces turn from green to gold, and stayed for three days. Sapa sits high up in the mountains, so the town itself is full of narrow, sloping streets, and it's surrounded by layer upon layer of terraced fields cut into the hillsides. Early in the morning, the whole valley is usually covered in mist, and it only clears a couple of hours later.

I decided to visit mainly because a colleague had shown me photos of the terraces and I couldn't quite believe a landscape like that existed in Vietnam. We spent most of our time trekking between villages, chatting with local Hmong and Dao families, and eating in tiny family-run restaurants that served food I'd never tried before.

What struck me most was how different the pace of life felt compared to Hanoi. Nobody seemed to be in a rush, and even the market only really got busy for a couple of hours each morning. On our last evening we watched the sunset from a hill just outside town, and the whole valley turned a deep orange colour, which is still one of the most beautiful sights I've ever seen.

I'd love to go back one day, ideally in a different season, just to see how much the landscape changes throughout the year.
