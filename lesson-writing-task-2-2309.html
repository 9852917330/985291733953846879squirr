Work & Employment

Should employers care how staff dress?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that employers should not be concerned about the way their employees dress, but should be concerned about the quality of their work. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Offices that once required suits now accept jeans, and many employers say they judge output alone. I largely agree with that position, though appearance still carries weight in a limited set of jobs.

Output is the honest measure. A programmer writes the same code in a hooded top as in a jacket, and an accountant's figures do not improve with a tie. Judging staff by clothing is also unreliable in a way that quietly damages a company, because it favours those who can afford expensive clothes and those whose background matches the manager's, so capable people are overlooked for reasons unrelated to ability. Relaxed rules bring practical gains too: staff are more comfortable, save money, and firms that dropped their dress codes have found recruitment easier rather than standards lower.

There are exceptions, and they are not trivial. Where employees meet clients, patients or the public, dress is part of the service: a surgeon in a stained shirt or a lawyer in beachwear damages the trust a client needs to feel. Uniforms also serve identification and safety, which is why steel-capped boots and high-visibility jackets are requirements rather than preferences. In these cases the employer is protecting the customer, not imposing taste.

The sensible position is therefore a narrow standard rather than none. A company can reasonably ask for clean, safe clothing that suits the setting, and should explain the reason behind any rule it sets. What it should not do is police style, since a preference for formality is a matter of habit, not of performance.

In conclusion, quality of work is the proper measure and employers should concentrate on it. Dress codes are justified only where safety or a client relationship depends on them, and beyond that they achieve very little.

Key words and phrases

requirements rather than preferences
Necessary conditions rather than personal tastes.
explain the reason behind any rule it sets
Give a clear justification for each rule.
a matter of habit, not of performance
Based on custom rather than work results.
