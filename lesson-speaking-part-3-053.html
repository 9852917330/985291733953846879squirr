Friendship

How people meet friends, and who finds it easier

Part 3

Prompt

How do people usually meet new friends where you live? Is it easier for adults or children to make new friends and why? How are friendships different from relationships at work or college?

How do people usually meet new friends where you live?

Simple Model Answer

Many friendships begin at school or university because people spend a lot of time together. Adults also meet through work, mutual friends and shared activities such as classes. An activity gives them an easy subject to discuss. Online groups can help too, although regular personal contact often helps those connections become closer and last longer.

Key words and phrases

mutual friends
People who are friends with both of you.

Advanced Model Answer

School and university are where most lasting friendships start, simply because you spend years in the same place with the same people. After that it's work, mutual friends and shared activities such as sports clubs or classes, and shared activity works particularly well because you're not obliged to make conversation from nothing; there's an obvious thing to talk about. Online communities matter too, though those friendships usually need to move offline to last — a group chat on its own rarely turns into a close friendship.

Key words and phrases

shared activity
something people do together
move offline to last
need real-world meetings to continue

Is it easier for adults or children to make new friends and why?

Simple Model Answer

Usually children, because they have repeated free time together and worry less about making an impression. Adults have work schedules, existing friendship groups and sometimes feel embarrassed about needing company. Making new friends as an adult therefore takes deliberate effort and repeated meetings. It is possible, but the right conditions do not happen as automatically.

Key words and phrases

making an impression
Trying to affect how other people see or judge you.

Advanced Model Answer

Children, clearly. They spend repeated unstructured time with the same group, which is exactly the condition friendship needs, and they haven't yet learned to be cautious — a child can meet someone in a park and be friends within an hour. Adults have the opposite situation: fixed schedules and established social circles, plus a self-consciousness about appearing to need people. So adult friendship generally requires deliberate, repeated effort, and a great many people simply never get round to making it.

Key words and phrases

repeated unstructured time
time together on many occasions without a fixed plan
fixed schedules and established social circles
set routines and groups of friends already formed

How are friendships different from relationships at work or college?

Simple Model Answer

Friendship is chosen freely, while work or college relationships often begin because people are placed together. At work, responsibilities and power differences may limit what people can say honestly. Colleagues can become close friends, but it may be easier after changing jobs. Until then, professional roles can create a limit on openness and trust.

Key words and phrases

openness
Willingness to share thoughts and feelings honestly.

Advanced Model Answer

The essential difference is that friendship is voluntary and has no other purpose. At work you're placed with people you didn't choose, and there's a structure around the relationship — someone evaluates someone else, and there are things you can't say honestly. Colleagues can absolutely become genuine friends, but usually only once that structure is removed, or after one of you leaves the organisation. Until then there's a natural ceiling on the trust.

Key words and phrases

that structure is removed
that planned framework is taken away
a natural ceiling on the trust
a limit to how much they can trust each other
