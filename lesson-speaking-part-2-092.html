Object & Favourite

A useful website that you often visit

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a useful website that you often visit.

You should say: What the contents of this website are How you first found this website How often you visit this website And explain how this website helps you.

Simple Model Answer

A useful website I often visit is Cookpad. It allows ordinary people to share recipes, usually with photographs showing each stage. I am not a very confident cook, so I use it when deciding what to make for dinner.

My friend Minh first sent me a link after I complained that my meals had little flavour. It was a recipe for fish cooked in caramel sauce, and it worked well on my first attempt. Since then, I have checked the site three or four times a week, often on my way home from work.

I like the everyday recipes, including ideas for using leftover vegetables. The photos make the instructions easier to follow, and comments from other users often suggest useful changes. The site has helped me cook more instead of ordering takeaway. My family has noticed the improvement, and my mother even asked me for a recipe recently. That made me proud. Cookpad is useful because it turns a daily problem into an opportunity to learn something practical.

Key words and phrases

leftover vegetables
Vegetables remaining after a previous meal or cooking session.
ordering takeaway
Buying prepared food to eat somewhere else, often at home.

Advanced Model Answer

The website I want to talk about is called Cookpad, a recipe-sharing site where home cooks upload their own dishes with step-by-step photos. I'm not a confident cook at all, so it's become something I check almost every time I'm planning what to make for dinner.

I first found it through my friend Minh, who's a genuinely good cook. I was complaining that everything I made tasted bland, and she sent me a link to a recipe for braised fish in caramel sauce that turned out perfectly on the first try. I was honestly shocked it worked, and I've been using the site ever since.

The content is mostly ordinary home recipes rather than anything fancy — things like what to do with leftover vegetables, or how my grandmother's generation used to make certain dishes, written by real people rather than professional chefs. Each recipe usually comes with clear step-by-step photos and a comments section where other users mention any changes they made, which is often more useful than the recipe itself.

I probably check it three or four times a week, usually on my way home from work when I'm trying to decide what to cook. It's genuinely useful because it's saved me from ordering takeaway more times than I can count, and it's slowly made me a much more confident cook. My family has definitely noticed the difference — my mother even asked me for a recipe last month, which has never happened before.
