Object & Favourite

A prize that you would like to win

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a prize that you would like to win.

You should say: - what the prize is for - how you know about it - what you would have to do to win it - and why you would like to win this prize.

Simple Model Answer

A prize I would like to win is Employee of the Year at my company. The directors give it to one staff member every December for making a particularly valuable contribution during the year. I learned about it soon after joining three years ago.

Some colleagues were discussing possible winners, so I asked how the award worked. There are no completely fixed rules, but reliable attendance, arriving on time and meeting deadlines are important. Winners usually do something extra as well. For example, last year's winner created a new service for customers.

I would like to receive the prize because it would show that senior managers appreciate my work. It might also help me progress in my career. More personally, it would make me feel that my effort had achieved something worthwhile. I know I would need to do more than simply finish my usual tasks. Thinking about the award encourages me to look for useful ideas and ways to support the company, rather than only focusing on my own daily workload.

Key words and phrases

contribution
Something useful that a person gives or does to help a group.
meeting deadlines
Completing work by the time it is due.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about a prize I'd love to win: the Employee of the Year award at the company I work for. Every December, the directors give it to one member of staff who has made an outstanding contribution to the business over the previous twelve months. I first heard about it during my induction, just after I'd got the job three years ago.

It was early December, and a couple of my colleagues were chatting about who might win it that year. I was curious, so I asked them to explain the rules properly. As I said, it's given for outstanding contribution to the business, but there isn't an exact definition of what that actually means.

From what I can tell, you need to hit the basics first: good attendance and punctuality, and meeting your targets and deadlines. On top of that, the winner usually needs to have done something genuinely creative — last year's winner, for instance, designed a completely new service for our clients, and the directors were clearly impressed.

I'd love to win Employee of the Year because it would mean my work had finally been recognised by the people at the top, and that would definitely help me move forward in my career. But honestly, beyond the practical side, it would just give me a real sense of personal achievement — proof that the extra effort hasn't gone unnoticed.

Key words and phrases

made an outstanding contribution
Provided unusually valuable help.
good attendance and punctuality
Coming regularly and arriving on time.
meeting your targets and deadlines
Reaching your goals by their due dates.
recognised by the people at the top
Praised by senior people.
move forward in my career
Progress in my professional work.
personal achievement
Something meaningful I achieved myself.
