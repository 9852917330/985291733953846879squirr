Something you learned in a place or from someone

Part 2

Prompt

Describe something you learned in a place or from someone

Simple Model Answer

Something I learned from another person was the value of volunteering. A woman in my neighbourhood, Ms Lan, runs a small group that visits older people in local nursing homes. I helped her for a few months.

She explained that some residents rarely received visitors and needed someone willing to sit and listen. She was patient and genuinely enjoyed their company. Watching her helped me understand that the work was about building a human connection, not simply completing a good deed for others to notice.

At first, I thought mainly about what I was giving up, such as free afternoons. Later, I realised how much I gained. I became more confident speaking with unfamiliar people and learned from residents whose lives had been very different from mine. I also made friends. Ms Lan never treated the visits as an unpleasant duty, and her attitude influenced me. I still volunteer occasionally, although not as often as she does. She taught me that useful service can benefit both the person receiving support and the person offering it.

Key words and phrases

nursing homes
Places where older or unwell people live and receive care.
a good deed
A kind or helpful action.

Advanced Model Answer

Something I learned from a woman in my neighbourhood, Ms. Lan, is the value of doing voluntary work — and doing it properly, not just for show.

Ms. Lan was born during one of the most turbulent periods in Vietnam's modern history, and now, in her late fifties, she runs her own small volunteer group. A handful of young people, including me for a while, used to help her visit elderly residents in nursing homes around the town who didn't get many visitors.

I remember her explaining why she does it, sitting on a plastic stool outside one of the nursing homes: 'Spending time with the elderly matters — a lot of them don't have relatives who visit, so I try to fill that gap. I've always had a rapport with older people; I'm patient, and I actually enjoy sitting and talking with them.'

Working alongside her for those few months taught me something I hadn't expected — that volunteering isn't really about what you're giving up, it's about what you get back. I went in thinking I was doing the residents a favour, but I came away having gained more confidence talking to people I didn't know, and I'd made a couple of friends I still see now.

What stayed with me most, though, was realising how much you can learn from someone who's lived through far more than you have. Ms. Lan never once made it sound like a chore, and I think that rubbed off on me — I still volunteer occasionally now, even though I'm nowhere near as dedicated as she is.
