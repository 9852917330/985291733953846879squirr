Books & Reading

Why people write books, and books against films

Part 3

Prompt

Why do you think some people write books? What type of books do you read often? Do you prefer to read a book or watch a movie on the same subject and why?

Why do you think some people write books?

Simple Model Answer

People write for different reasons. Some have a story they want to tell; others want to share knowledge or change people's opinions. Writing can also help someone understand a difficult experience, much like a diary does. Money is not the main reward for most writers, because relatively few books earn a large amount.

Key words and phrases

reward
Something valuable or satisfying you get from an activity.

Advanced Model Answer

Different motives, and they rarely overlap much. Some write to tell a story they can't stop thinking about. Others want to preserve knowledge or influence an argument, which accounts for most non-fiction. A great deal of writing is also a way of working something out — people write about a difficult period of their life partly in order to understand it, the way keeping a diary forces you to name what you're actually feeling. What almost none of them do it for is money, though, since very few books earn much at all.

Key words and phrases

a way of working something out
a method for finding an answer

What type of books do you read often?

Simple Model Answer

I often read books based on research, especially about education, psychology and technology. I want ideas I can use. I also value novels because they let me experience a character's thoughts and decisions, rather than just read advice about behaviour. Both types teach me something, so I would find it difficult to stop reading either.

Key words and phrases

based on research
Using information gathered through careful study.

Advanced Model Answer

Mostly research-based non-fiction, particularly on education, psychology and technology, because I want ideas I can apply to what I'm doing. But I read novels regularly too, and I've come to value them differently. Non-fiction tells you about people from the outside, whereas fiction puts you inside someone's reasoning for several hundred pages — a novel about a difficult marriage will teach you something a relationship self-help book simply can't, because you feel the decision being made rather than being told the theory behind it. I'd find it hard to give up either.

Key words and phrases

from the outside
from the viewpoint of someone not involved
give up either
stop doing either one of the two things

Do you prefer to read a book or watch a movie on the same subject and why?

Simple Model Answer

I generally prefer a book when I want to understand something in detail. It has more time to explain a character's thoughts, while a film must shorten the story. However, films use music, scenery and acting to create feelings very quickly. So I prefer books for depth, but I don't think films are always less valuable.

Key words and phrases

in detail
With a lot of information about the different parts.

Advanced Model Answer

The book, generally, if I want to understand something properly. A novel can spend a whole chapter inside a character's thinking, whereas a film has to convey the same thing through an expression lasting two seconds. Films also compress ruthlessly — a five-hundred-page plot becomes two hours, and most of the internal reasoning simply can't survive that cut. That said, film does things prose can't: landscape, music and performance carry an immediate emotional force that writing has to work considerably harder to achieve, so I wouldn't say one form is simply better than the other.

Key words and phrases

compress ruthlessly
shorten it as much as possible without hesitation
an immediate emotional force
a strong feeling produced straight away
