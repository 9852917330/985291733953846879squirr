Communication

Ways of communicating, and how technology changed them

Part 3

Prompt

What are the different ways people communicate? Do you prefer to write a letter to a friend or send an email and why? How has technology affected the way people communicate?

What are the different ways people communicate?

Simple Model Answer

People communicate in person, by phone, messages, email and video calls, as well as through formal documents. They usually choose according to the situation: a text for plans, or a call for something urgent. Difficult conversations may need a face-to-face meeting. However, these habits vary between generations and change as new technology becomes normal.

Key words and phrases

face-to-face
With people physically present together.

Advanced Model Answer

The obvious ones are face-to-face conversation, phone calls, messaging apps, email and video calls, plus formal written documents at work. What interests me is that people have developed quite specific unwritten rules about which to use. A message is fine for arranging something, a phone call's for when it's urgent, and anything difficult or emotional really needs a face-to-face conversation. Using the wrong channel — resigning by email, for instance — is read as a statement in itself. Those rules aren't fixed, though: video calls have blurred the line, and what counts as acceptable varies a lot by generation.

Key words and phrases

the wrong channel
an unsuitable way to communicate
varies a lot by generation
is very different for older and younger people

Do you prefer to write a letter to a friend or send an email and why?

Simple Model Answer

For ordinary news, I prefer email because it is quick and easy to find later. For something important, like expressing sympathy or giving special thanks, I might write a letter. The time and care involved make it feel more personal. An email can also be thoughtful, but people often write those much more quickly.

Key words and phrases

expressing sympathy
Showing care for someone who is experiencing sadness or difficulty.

Advanced Model Answer

Email, for anything ordinary, because it's instant and I can find it again months later. But I'd write an actual letter for something significant, like a condolence or properly thanking someone. The reason is that a letter takes effort, and the effort is part of the message — the person knows you sat down and gave them twenty minutes, not two. An email can carry that too, if you genuinely slow down and write it properly, but most of us don't, which is exactly why the letter still stands out.

Key words and phrases

the effort is part of the message
the work put in helps show that someone cares
stands out
is easily noticed among other things

How has technology affected the way people communicate?

Simple Model Answer

Technology lets people communicate instantly across the world, which helps families stay close. However, people may expect immediate replies and become less patient. Written messages also hide tone, so misunderstandings are easier. For something sensitive or difficult, hearing a voice or seeing a face is still valuable; sending more messages may not solve the problem.

Key words and phrases

tone
The feeling or attitude shown by how someone speaks.

Advanced Model Answer

It's made communication constant and global, which is largely positive, since families spread across countries can speak every day. The costs are subtler, though. We've become less patient, because anything slower than instant feels broken, and a lot of communication has moved to text, which strips out tone completely — so misunderstandings happen constantly over messages that would've been perfectly clear spoken aloud. Difficult conversations still need a real voice or a face, and no amount of messaging replaces that.

Key words and phrases

strips out tone
removes the feeling expressed by someone's voice
spoken aloud
said so others can hear it
no amount of messaging
even sending many messages cannot achieve this
