Situation

An occasion that someone or something made noise

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an occasion that someone or something made noise

You should say: - When this happened - Who this person was/what this thing was - What kinds of noise was And explain what you did when you heard the noise.

Simple Model Answer

An occasion involving noise was when the flat above mine was renovated last year. Work began around seven each morning, and drilling into the floor was particularly loud. I was preparing lessons at home, so concentrating became difficult.

At first, I tried to tolerate it. However, after several days, the work continued outside the building's permitted hours. I chose a quiet Saturday morning to speak to the neighbour rather than complain angrily to the workers. I explained how the noise affected my work and sleep and asked whether the loudest jobs could happen in the middle of the day.

He apologised and agreed on a clearer timetable with the contractor. I then worked at a library during the two-hour drilling period, and the early starts stopped. The solution helped both of us, and we remained friendly. I learned that a complaint is easier to accept when it includes a reasonable suggestion. Choosing the right moment and speaking calmly worked better than letting frustration build until the conversation became an argument.

Key words and phrases

permitted hours
The times when an activity is officially allowed.
contractor
A person or company hired to carry out work, such as building repairs.

Advanced Model Answer

I remember a period last year when the apartment above mine was being renovated. Work started at about seven every morning, and the loudest sound came from an electric drill on the concrete floor. Since I was preparing lessons from home at the time, the noise made it almost impossible to concentrate.

At first I tried to put up with it, telling myself renovation work doesn't last forever. But after several days the drilling was still going on well outside the building's permitted hours, so I decided to speak to the neighbour myself, calmly, rather than confronting the workers directly. I explained the noise was disrupting my work and my sleep, and asked if the loudest jobs could be kept to the middle of the day.

He was genuinely apologetic and arranged a clearer schedule with the contractor. From then on, I'd work from a library during the two-hour drilling window, and the workers stopped starting so early. What I took from it was that a complaint lands better when it comes with a reasonable solution instead of anger, and that addressing a problem directly beats letting frustration build up until you snap. Looking back, what really made the conversation work wasn't even what I said, it was the timing - I waited until a Saturday morning, when he wasn't about to rush off anywhere, and framed it as a practical scheduling question rather than a personal complaint.

I suspect if I'd gone up there on the third morning, tired and irritated, it would've turned into an argument instead. We still say hello to each other now, which almost certainly wouldn't be the case if I'd handled it badly - and when you share a wall with someone, that matters more than winning the argument.
