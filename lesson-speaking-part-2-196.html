#062
Object & Favourite

A rule at your school that you agree or disagree with

Part 2
Model answer audio
Press Play to listen

Prompt

Describe a rule at your school that you agree or disagree with. You should say: - What the rule is; - What will happen when students break the rule; - Why school makes the rule; - Why you agree or not.

OUTLINE

>>> school gender-separation rule
one rule couldn't stand boys girls not allowed seen together sit next each other
break rule caught near opposite gender warning supervisor suspended three days week
rumour couples skipped school secretly failed entrance exams principal worried neglect studies
personally think took way too far agree study main priority nothing wrong spending time together
boys girls different strengths Maths Physics Literature languages mixing helped everyone learn faster
separation pointless caused constant friction better explain consequences than keeping genders apart

Model answer

Vietnamese high schools are known for imposing a lot of strict rules on students, and mine was no exception. The one rule I couldn't stand was this: boys and girls weren't allowed to be seen together, or even sit next to each other, in class or during break time.

If you broke the rule, or you were just caught standing near someone of the opposite gender, you'd get a warning from the supervisor. If it happened again, you could be suspended for anything from three days to a week, so it wasn't taken lightly at all.

I do know the reasoning behind it. The rumour was that a few couples used to skip school to meet up secretly, and ended up failing their university entrance exams because of it. The principal was worried that teenagers falling in love would neglect their studies, so the rule was there to stop students from developing an intimate relationship that might distract them.

Personally, though, I think the school took it way too far. I agree that studying should be our main priority, but there's nothing wrong with boys and girls simply spending time together — most of the time it's just friendship, nothing more. Boys and girls often have different strengths too; the boys in my class tended to be stronger at Maths and Physics, while the girls were better at Literature and languages, so if anything, mixing us up would've helped everyone learn faster.

To me, the whole separation was pointless. It didn't just stop us enjoying our school years properly, it also caused constant friction between students and teachers. If the school really wanted to help us focus, a much better approach would've been to explain the consequences of poor results clearly, rather than keeping the genders apart like that.
