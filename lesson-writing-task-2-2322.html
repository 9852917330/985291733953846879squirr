Technology & Science

How technology changed children's free time

Advantages / Disadvantages

Prompt

New technologies have changed the way children spend their free time. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?

Model answer

A generation ago children filled their afternoons with bicycles and board games; now most of that time belongs to screens. The change brings real gains, but I believe the disadvantages currently outweigh them.

The advantages should not be dismissed. A child with a tablet has access to explanations, languages and skills that no local library could supply, and many learn coding, drawing or editing entirely on their own. Games develop planning and quick decision-making, and online play keeps friendships alive across distance, which matters greatly to children who are isolated, disabled or living far from anyone their own age. Technology has also given quiet children a way of finding others who share their interests.

The costs, though, are physical, social and psychological at once. Time on a screen is time not spent moving, and childhood obesity and poor posture have risen alongside it. Sleep suffers, because devices are used late and delay it. Social skills develop through unsupervised play, where children argue, negotiate and repair things themselves, and a generation with less of that experience is less practised at handling ordinary friction. Social media adds a further layer, since children now compare themselves with an endless supply of edited lives, and rates of anxiety among teenagers have climbed accordingly.

The deepest problem is design. These products are built by companies whose income depends on attention, so they are engineered to be difficult to stop using. Adults struggle against them, and expecting a ten-year-old to succeed is an unreasonable demand disguised as a lesson in self-control.

In conclusion, technology has widened what children can learn and how they stay in touch, and no sensible parent would remove it entirely. The harm to sleep, activity and confidence is presently larger, so the answer lies in limits set by adults rather than by the child.

Key words and phrases

less practised at handling ordinary friction
Less experienced at managing everyday disagreements.
