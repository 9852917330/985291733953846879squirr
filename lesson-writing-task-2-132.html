Housing

More people living alone

Positive / Negative

Prompt

In some countries, many more people are choosing to live alone nowadays than in the past. Do you think this is a positive or negative development?

Model answer

In recent years it has become far more normal for people to live alone, particularly in large cities in the developed world. In my opinion, this trend brings genuine benefits at a personal and economic level, but it also carries a real social and financial cost.

The rise in one-person households can be seen as positive for both personal and economic reasons. On an individual level, people who live alone tend to become more independent and self-reliant than those who share a home with family, since there is no one else to cook, clean, pay the bills or manage the budget for them. From an economic perspective, the same trend creates greater demand for housing, which benefits the construction industry, estate agents and the many businesses that rely on people furnishing a home of their own.

However, the personal and economic arguments above can be viewed from the opposite angle. Rather than feeling more independent, people who live alone may instead experience loneliness, isolation and worry, since they miss out on the emotional support and daily conversation that family or flatmates would normally provide. They must also bear the weight of every household bill and decision without anyone to share it.

The same reversal applies to the economic argument: greater demand for housing tends to push up property prices and rents, so while the construction industry and estate agents may gain, the wider population, including people who live alone, ends up facing higher housing costs than before.

In conclusion, living alone offers genuine personal and economic benefits, but these come paired with a real emotional and financial cost. Whether the trend is ultimately positive probably depends on whether it is freely chosen: independence that people actively want looks very different from an isolation they were simply unable to avoid.

Considering money, jobs and resource use.
greater demand for housing
