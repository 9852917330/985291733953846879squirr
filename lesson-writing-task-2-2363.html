Animals & Wildlife

Is it too late to save species?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

It is true that some human activities have negative effects on plant and animal species. Some people say it is too late to address this problem, while others argue that effective measures can still be taken. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Species are disappearing at a rate not seen for millions of years, and opinions divide between resignation and the belief that action still works. Both readings have evidence behind them, and my own view is that the pessimism describes the past while the optimism describes what remains.

The pessimistic case is not unreasonable. Extinction is permanent, so everything already lost is beyond recovery, and the causes are still accelerating: forests are cleared, oceans warm and habitats fragment faster than protection can be arranged. Climate change adds a difficulty that conservation alone cannot answer, since a protected reserve is useless if the climate inside it no longer suits the species it was built for. Political attention is also short, and governments that promise targets routinely miss them.

The opposing view rests on cases where intervention has worked. Whale populations have recovered since commercial hunting was banned, several birds of prey returned after pesticides were restricted, and protected marine areas refill with fish within a decade. Even severely reduced populations have been rebuilt, which shows that decline is not the same thing as inevitability. Renewable energy and improved farming are reducing the pressure at its source.

My own position is that the argument confuses two claims. It is genuinely too late to save what has already gone, and too late to prevent all future loss. It is not too late for the vast majority of species still living, and each degree of warming avoided and each forest protected changes how much is lost rather than whether anything is.

In conclusion, some damage is irreversible and pretending otherwise helps nobody. Treating the whole problem as hopeless is a mistake, because the evidence shows recovery wherever pressure is removed, and giving up guarantees the outcome that is feared.

Key words and phrases

decline is not the same thing as inevitability
Decline does not mean disappearance is unavoidable.
giving up guarantees the outcome that is feared
Stopping efforts makes the feared result certain.
