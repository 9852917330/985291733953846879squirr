Environment

Why so little gets recycled

Problem / Solution

Prompt

In many countries, there is not enough recycling of waste materials such as paper, glass and cans. What are the reasons for this, and what can be done to solve the problem?

Model answer

Most households claim to support recycling, yet the proportion of material actually recovered remains low in many countries. The causes are practical and economic rather than a lack of goodwill, and the remedies have to work on both.

Confusion is the first obstacle. Rules differ between districts and change without explanation, packaging is rarely labelled clearly, and a resident who cannot tell whether a container is acceptable puts it in the ordinary bin to be safe. Convenience is the second: where recycling requires washing items, separating them into four containers and carrying them to a distant point, participation falls sharply, particularly among people in flats with no space for the bins.

The economics matter more than most people realise. Recycled material must compete with new material, and while oil is cheap new plastic is cheaper than recovered plastic, so collected waste sometimes has no buyer. Products are also designed without recovery in mind: mixed plastics, glued laminates and coated cartons cannot be separated at any reasonable cost. Meanwhile landfill and incineration remain the cheap option for local authorities in many places.

Effective measures exist. Deposit schemes on bottles and cans, where a small sum is refunded on return, achieve recovery rates above ninety per cent wherever they operate, because they attach a price to the object. Simple, standardised rules across a country and clear symbols on packaging remove the confusion, and frequent collection from the doorstep removes the effort. The strongest measure is to make producers responsible for the cost of disposal, which pushes them to redesign packaging rather than leaving councils to deal with it. Charging by the weight of unsorted rubbish also works, provided low-income households are protected.

In conclusion, recycling rates are low because the system is confusing, inconvenient and often uneconomic. Deposit schemes, uniform rules and charging producers for what they create would change all three.

Key words and phrases

cannot be separated at any reasonable cost
Sorting costs would be too high.
make producers responsible for the cost of disposal
Require manufacturers to pay for waste treatment.
charging producers for what they create
Making businesses pay for their output's impacts.
