Work & Employment

What really produces success?

Opinion

Prompt

Some people who have been successful in life do not attribute their success to the theoretical knowledge they learned at university. In your opinion, what are the main factors that contribute to a person's achievement?

Model answer

Many successful people say that they owe little to the theoretical knowledge they acquired at university. In my opinion academic study contributes more than they admit, but personal qualities and opportunity explain the greater part of achievement.

There is some truth in the sceptical view. University courses often teach models that are simplified or outdated, and much of the detail is forgotten within a few years. Several of the most successful entrepreneurs of the past generation left university without finishing, which suggests that formal qualifications are not essential in every field.

Nevertheless, the value of higher education is easy to underestimate because it is indirect. A degree teaches people to structure an argument, evaluate evidence and work independently to a deadline, and these habits remain long after the content has faded. Certain professions are also impossible without it: nobody would accept a surgeon or a structural engineer who had learned informally.

The factors that matter most, however, are personal. Persistence appears repeatedly in accounts of successful careers, since most achievement follows a period of failure that others would not have tolerated. The ability to work with people is equally important, because almost nothing significant is accomplished alone. Opportunity should not be ignored either: family wealth, contacts and country of birth determine what is realistically available, and much of what is called merit is partly luck.

In conclusion, university provides useful habits of thought rather than directly usable knowledge, which is why graduates often underestimate it. Persistence, cooperation and circumstance still account for a far larger share of what people achieve.

Key words and phrases

not essential in every field
Not required in all types of work.
learned informally
Learned outside formal classes or qualifications.
almost nothing significant is accomplished alone
Most important achievements require other people's help.
circumstance
The situation and conditions affecting an event.
