Environment & Climate

Global greenhouse gas emissions

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The charts compare the sources of global greenhouse gas emissions in 2002 and emissions from electricity generation.

Model answer

The two pie charts compare the shares of global greenhouse gas emissions produced by three groups of countries in 2002 and 2030, while the bar chart shows projected carbon dioxide emissions from different sectors in the same two years.

Overall, developing countries will overtake OECD countries to become the largest of the three groups in terms of greenhouse gas emissions by 2030. While industry is responsible for the highest amount of these emissions, the consumer and transportation sectors will experience the greatest increases.

As can be seen in the two pie charts, developing countries are expected to account for a bigger share of emissions in 2030, 48%, compared to 38% in 2002. In contrast, OECD countries are projected to make up a lower proportion, 43%, 9 percentage points lower than in 2002. Countries with transition economies are estimated to take up the remaining 9% in 2030, 1 percentage point less than in 2002.

The bar chart reveals that carbon dioxide emissions will expand to varying degrees in different sectors by 2030. Industry will continue to be the major producer of carbon dioxide, rising to more than 10 billion tonnes in 2030. Next comes the consumer sector, which is projected to more than double its emissions from less than 4 billion to 8 billion or more tonnes. Transportation, the third largest producer, will see huge growth, to 6 billion tonnes, triple the amount in 2002. By comparison, waste combustion and other sectors make up a much lower proportion, each producing an estimated 1 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide.
