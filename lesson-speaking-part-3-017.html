Children

Children's activities in a city and getting them interested

Part 3

Prompt

What kinds of children's activities are there available in your city? What do you need to do to interest a child in such activities? How do you think adults should improve their relations with children?

What kinds of children's activities are there available in your city?

Simple Model Answer

There are sports clubs, swimming pools, art and music classes, libraries and parks. Some larger cities also have science museums and computer workshops. However, not every child can use them. Fees may be too high, and many activities are far from outer neighbourhoods, so travel time and money can limit access.

Key words and phrases

access
The opportunity or ability to use something.

Advanced Model Answer

Quite a lot — sports clubs, swimming pools, music and art classes, libraries with children's sections, and public parks. Larger cities also have science museums and coding workshops, which have become popular recently. The problem isn't availability but access, though: plenty of the good ones charge fees a low-income family can't manage, and they're concentrated in central districts, so a child in an outer suburb faces a long journey just to get there, on top of the fee itself.

Key words and phrases

concentrated in central districts
mainly located near the middle of the city
faces a long journey
must travel a long distance

What do you need to do to interest a child in such activities?

Simple Model Answer

I'd begin with something the child already enjoys. A child interested in dinosaurs may like a museum more than a general lecture. It also helps to remove pressure and praise trying rather than winning. Some children need time, or may become interested only after seeing a friend enjoy the same activity.

Key words and phrases

remove pressure
Stop making someone feel that they must succeed or perform.

Advanced Model Answer

Start with something connected to what they already like, rather than what you think they should enjoy — a child obsessed with dinosaurs will happily sit through a museum trip that a "get some culture" lecture would never achieve. Then remove the pressure, because a child who feels they're being assessed will usually refuse, and praising effort rather than results matters a great deal, since a child praised for being talented stops attempting anything difficult. It doesn't always work straightaway, though — plenty of children need to see a friend enjoying something first before they're willing to try it themselves.

Key words and phrases

praising effort rather than results
telling someone their hard work matters more than their score

How do you think adults should improve their relations with children?

Simple Model Answer

Adults should listen seriously, explain rules and keep promises. Something that seems small to an adult may be important to a child, so dismissing it can damage trust. Listening does not mean allowing everything, though. Clear, consistent limits also help children feel safe because they know what to expect.

Key words and phrases

consistent limits
Rules that stay clear and are applied in a similar way each time.

Advanced Model Answer

The most effective thing is listening properly — not just waiting for your turn to reply, but taking what they say seriously, including complaints that seem trivial to an adult. Explaining rules helps too, since "because I said so" only produces compliance while an adult's actually watching. And keeping promises matters, because children remember broken ones for years. None of this means removing boundaries, though; consistent limits are part of what makes a child feel secure in the first place.

Key words and phrases

taking what they say seriously
listening to their ideas with respect
consistent limits
rules that remain clear and do not keep changing
