Education

Should students evaluate their teachers?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people believe that high school students should be encouraged to evaluate and criticise their teachers in order to improve the quality of education. Others think this would result in a loss of respect and discipline in the classroom. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Some people believe that allowing secondary students to evaluate their teachers would raise standards, while others fear it would undermine respect and discipline. In my opinion student feedback is valuable, but only if it is collected carefully and used appropriately.

The argument in favour is that students observe teaching more closely than anyone else. An inspector visits a classroom once every few years, whereas pupils sit through every lesson and know precisely who explains clearly, who marks work promptly and who does not prepare. Without their views, a weak teacher may continue unnoticed for decades. Being consulted also encourages students to take their own learning more seriously, since it treats them as participants rather than as passive recipients.

The objection is equally reasonable. Adolescents may reward teachers who are entertaining or lenient and criticise those who set demanding work, so the teachers most likely to raise attainment could be penalised. There is also evidence from universities that such evaluations reflect prejudice about a teacher's gender, age or accent rather than teaching quality. If feedback is public, or affects pay directly, the authority a teacher needs to maintain order may be damaged.

The sensible approach is therefore a compromise. Feedback should be anonymous, gathered through specific questions about clarity and fairness rather than general popularity, and given to the teacher and head of department as one source of evidence among several.

In conclusion, I support consulting students, since they possess information nobody else has. It should inform professional judgement, however, rather than function as a public verdict on individual teachers.

Key words and phrases

continue unnoticed for decades
Remain undetected for many years.
as participants rather than as passive recipients
Actively involved rather than only receiving information.
reward teachers who are entertaining or lenient
Favour amusing teachers or those with easy rules.
specific questions about clarity and fairness
Focused questions about understandable and fair teaching.
one source of evidence among several
One piece of information alongside other evidence.
