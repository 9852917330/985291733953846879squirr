Transport

Rails or roads?

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people believe the government should spend money on building train and subway lines to reduce traffic congestion. Others think that building more and wider roads is the better solution. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Governments facing congestion must choose between investing in rail and expanding the road network. Both approaches have been tried extensively, and the evidence points strongly towards rail in large cities.

Those who favour roads argue from flexibility and cost. A road serves cars, buses, lorries and emergency vehicles alike, and it can be built in stages wherever demand appears, whereas a metro line takes a decade and enormous capital before carrying a single passenger. Roads also reach places rail cannot justify, and for freight and for residents of low-density areas there is no practical alternative to driving.

The argument for rail rests on capacity. A single metro line can move as many people per hour as a dozen lanes of traffic, on a fraction of the land, which matters most in exactly the dense areas where congestion is worst. There is also a well-documented problem with the road solution: added capacity fills up. When a motorway is widened, journeys that were previously avoided become attractive, traffic grows to fill the new lanes, and within a few years the delay returns to where it started. Rail does not generate congestion in the same way.

My own view is that this asymmetry decides the question for cities. Widening roads treats the symptom and reliably recreates the problem, while a rail line removes cars permanently by offering a faster option, and it does so without demolishing the streets it is meant to serve.

In conclusion, roads remain necessary for freight and for areas too thinly populated for rail. In congested cities, however, investment should go to trains and metros, because road building has repeatedly been shown to create the traffic it was intended to relieve.

Key words and phrases

no practical alternative to driving
No other workable transport option.
returns to where it started
Goes back to its original condition.
demolishing the streets it is meant to serve
Destroying neighbourhoods while claiming to improve their transport.
create the traffic it was intended to relieve
Encourage additional driving instead of reducing congestion.
