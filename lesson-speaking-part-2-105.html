Object & Favourite

An invention that changed people's lives

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an invention that changed people's lives

You should say: What it was Who invented it When it was invented And explain how it changed people's lives

OUTLINE

>>> the telephone

Simple Model Answer

An invention that changed people's lives is the telephone. It made it possible to speak to someone far away instead of waiting for a letter. Alexander Graham Bell is closely associated with its development in the nineteenth century.

My grandmother used to explain how slowly family news travelled when she was young. If someone in another city became ill, a letter could take days to arrive. A telephone call allowed people to share important information almost immediately. This made a major difference to both family life and work.

The telephone affects my own life every day. I call my parents, solve problems with colleagues and tell friends when I am running late. Modern mobile phones do many other things, but direct communication remains one of their most useful functions. What impresses me is that a basic idea about sending sound became something people depend on around the world. We often take it for granted because it is so familiar, but life would be much less convenient without the ability to hear another person's voice from a distance.

Key words and phrases

running late
Expecting to arrive after the planned time.
take it for granted
Fail to notice or appreciate something because it seems normal.

Advanced Model Answer

The invention I want to describe is the telephone, which I think is one of the most life-changing inventions there's ever been, even though we barely think about it these days because it's just always there.

As far as I know, it was Alexander Graham Bell who invented it, back in 1876, after years of experimenting with ways to send sound down a wire. Interestingly, another inventor, Elisha Gray, filed a similar patent on almost the exact same day, so the two of them were basically racing each other without even knowing it.

The reason it changed people's lives so completely is obvious once you think about what came before it. My grandmother used to tell me that when she was young, if a relative in another city fell ill, the family found out by letter, and by the time it arrived, things had often already changed completely. A phone call turned something that used to take days into something that takes seconds.

It's changed how I live too, though in a completely different way from my grandmother's generation. I rely on my phone for almost everything now — calling my parents every Sunday, sorting out problems at work before they get worse, or letting a friend know I'm running late. It's strange that all of that traces back to one man in a lab in Boston over a century ago.

What impresses me most is how something so simple — turning sound into an electrical signal and back again — ended up reshaping how the world communicates. Almost every invention that's come after it, from the mobile phone to video calls, is just a more advanced version of the same basic idea.
