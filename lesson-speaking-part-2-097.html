Object & Favourite

An educational television programme that you liked

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an educational television programme that you liked.

You should say: - what the programme was about - when and where you saw it - how you found out about this programme - and why you liked it.

Simple Model Answer

An educational programme I enjoyed was Planet Earth, a nature documentary shown on the BBC. It has eleven episodes about different environments, including mountains, deserts, caves and forests. I first watched it years ago after seeing its beautiful television trailers.

The programme was narrated by David Attenborough, whose voice I recognised from other nature documentaries. Later, someone gave me the DVD collection for Christmas, so I watched the whole series again. I enjoyed being able to return to my favourite episodes.

The photography impressed me most. The crew filmed animals and landscapes in places I had never seen. In the episode about freshwater, for example, there were animals from Japan, the Nile and the Amazon. I wondered how the team managed to reach such locations and film so clearly. The series taught me about unfamiliar species and showed how varied the planet is. It was entertaining without being only entertainment. After watching, I wanted to learn more about nature and the places where these animals live.

narrated

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about one of my favourite educational TV programmes, a nature documentary called Planet Earth. It has eleven episodes, and each one covers a different habitat, like mountains, caves, deserts and jungles, using spectacular footage of animals, plants and landscapes from all over the world. I first saw it years ago, when it was broadcast on the BBC.

I think it went out every Sunday evening, which was honestly the perfect slot for a programme like that. A few years later, someone gave me the whole box set on DVD for Christmas, so I ended up watching every episode again from the start. I actually found out about it before it aired, because the trailers were shown constantly in the weeks leading up to the first episode, and they were impossible to ignore — beautiful shots of nature set to dramatic music.

I also recognised the narrator's voice straight away — it's David Attenborough, and I don't think there's a nature programme from the last twenty years he hasn't narrated. It was those trailers that had me hooked from the first episode. What I liked most, though, was the stunning photography and how the series covered the whole planet rather than just one place. In the Fresh Water episode, for example, they filmed giant salamanders in Japan, crocodiles in the Nile, and river dolphins in the Amazon.

I remember being amazed that a film crew could get access to locations like that. I picked up all sorts of facts about animals I'd never heard of, and the programme genuinely opened my eyes to how much of the planet I hadn't seen.
