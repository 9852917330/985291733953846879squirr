Language & Communication

Learning a language means learning a culture

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people argue that learning a second language necessarily involves learning about the culture of the countries where it is spoken. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Some people believe that it is impossible to learn a foreign language properly without also studying the culture of the countries in which it is spoken. I largely agree with this view, although the amount of cultural knowledge required depends on why the language is being learned.

Language and culture are difficult to separate because meaning depends on context. Idioms, humour, levels of politeness and forms of address all reflect how a society thinks, and a learner who ignores them will produce sentences that are grammatically correct but socially wrong. A student who translates directly from Vietnamese into English, for example, may sound abrupt or excessively formal without realising it. Cultural knowledge also aids memory, since vocabulary met in films, songs and real situations is retained far better than words memorised from a list.

Culture also supplies the motivation that long-term study requires. Most people who reach a high level have developed a genuine interest in the literature, music or history of the language they are learning, and this is what carries them through the difficult intermediate stage.

I would qualify this in one respect. English is now used mainly between people who are not native speakers, so an engineer writing technical reports needs clarity and accuracy far more than familiarity with British or American customs. For such learners, cultural study is useful rather than essential.

In conclusion, I agree that culture is an important part of language learning for anyone who wants to communicate naturally, but it matters much less for those who need a language purely as a professional tool.

Key words and phrases

levels of politeness and forms of address
Ways of speaking respectfully to different people.
sound abrupt or excessively formal
Seem rude or unnecessarily stiff.
memorised from a list
Learned by recalling written items.
the difficult intermediate stage
The challenging period between beginner and advanced ability.
