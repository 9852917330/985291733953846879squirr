Object & Favourite

An advertisement you have seen recently

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an advertisement you have seen recently.

You should say: - When and where you saw it; - What kind of advertisement it was; - What the contents of the advertisements were; - How you felt about it.

Simple Model Answer

An advertisement I saw recently was for Blue Electronics. I came across it on Facebook last week, although I had seen similar versions on television before. It showed people dressed as blue household machines, including a fridge and a washing machine.

They smiled and danced while a short tune played repeatedly. The style was unusual and very noticeable. I think the company wanted viewers to remember the colour, the music and the brand together. It certainly worked on me because I could still remember the tune afterwards.

My feelings about it are mixed. I find it quite funny, but the song can also be annoying when repeated too often. My parents prefer advertisements that explain products clearly and think this one is too dramatic. People my age may be more used to this kind of online humour. I would not say it taught me much about the machines themselves, but it created a strong impression. It shows that an advertisement can be memorable even when viewers do not all enjoy it.

Advanced Model Answer

I want to talk about an advert for Blue Electronics, one of the biggest electronics brands in Vietnam. I saw the latest one just last week, scrolling through Facebook late at night, though I'd already seen versions of it on TV plenty of times before.

The advert shows people dressed up as blue household appliances — a fridge, a washing machine, a rice cooker — smiling and dancing to a really catchy, slightly annoying tune, while staring almost too intensely at the camera. I think the whole point is to create a big impression and make the products feel more appealing, maybe even a bit more like friendly characters than just machines.

Honestly, I think they're also trying to get the tune stuck in your head on purpose, so that whenever you hear it again, you automatically think of Blue Electronics. It's a bit of a strange approach, but I have to admit it's clever, because I still remember the tune weeks later, even though I never wanted to.

What's interesting is that I don't think this kind of ad works for everyone. My parents find it a bit over the top and much prefer straightforward, informative ads that just explain the product clearly. People my age, though, tend to find it funny rather than annoying — I think we're just more used to this kind of advertising online.
