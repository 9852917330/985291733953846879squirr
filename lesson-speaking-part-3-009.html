Books & Reading

The books people read and encouraging children to read

Part 3

Prompt

What kind of books do you read? What types of books do children like? Should we encourage children to read books and why?

What kind of books do you read?

Simple Model Answer

I mainly read factual books about psychology, education and technology because they give me useful ideas. I also read novels sometimes. Stories help me understand a person's thoughts from the inside, which is different from reading explanations about people. I try to include both, although in some months I read almost only non-fiction.

Key words and phrases

non-fiction
Writing about real people, events or information.

Advanced Model Answer

Mostly non-fiction — psychology, education and technology are the areas I keep coming back to — with a novel now and then. The non-fiction's partly practical, since I want ideas I can actually use. But I've come to think fiction does something non-fiction can't: spending four hundred pages inside somebody else's reasoning is genuinely different from reading about people from the outside. I try to keep a balance for that reason, though I'll admit some months the non-fiction pile wins completely and I don't touch a novel at all, which probably isn't the healthiest ratio.

Key words and phrases

inside somebody else's reasoning
seeing the steps another person uses to think

What types of books do children like?

Simple Model Answer

It depends on age. Young children often enjoy pictures, funny stories and repetition. Older children may prefer adventures, while teenagers choose fantasy or mysteries. Across these ages, children usually read more when they can choose for themselves. Even a joke book or comic can help; forcing a more serious choice may reduce their interest.

Key words and phrases

repetition
Something happening or being said again several times.

Advanced Model Answer

It changes fast with age. Young children want pictures, repetition and humour, and will happily hear the same story fifty times without getting bored of it. Slightly older ones move to adventure and anything a little frightening. Teenagers tend towards fantasy and mystery, or books connected to films and games they already know. What holds across all ages is that children read far more when they've chosen the book themselves, even if the choice seems unambitious to an adult — a graphic novel or a joke book still counts as reading, and insisting on something more "worthy" usually just kills the habit.

Key words and phrases

chosen the book themselves
selected the book by their own choice
kills the habit
makes people stop doing it regularly

Should we encourage children to read books and why?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, reading helps children build vocabulary and concentrate, which supports other school subjects too. However, we should make it enjoyable rather than another duty. Keeping books nearby, reading together and letting children choose are useful approaches. If they dislike a book, allowing them to try another may help them keep enjoying reading.

Key words and phrases

vocabulary
The words a person knows and uses.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, but how you encourage matters enormously. Reading builds vocabulary and concentration in a way screens generally don't, and children who read well find every other subject easier. The mistake is turning it into an obligation — comprehension tests, required pages, reading logs — because that reliably makes children associate books with work rather than pleasure. What works is keeping books within reach, reading alongside them, and letting them abandon anything they are not enjoying without any sense of failure.

Key words and phrases

turning it into an obligation
making it feel like something they must do
letting them abandon anything they are not enjoying
allowing them to stop reading what they do not like
