Situation

A time when you saw an interesting animal

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you saw an interesting animal

You should say: - What animal it was - When and Where you saw it And explain how you feel about this animal

Simple Model Answer

A time I saw an interesting animal was on a mountain trip near Hanoi when I was about fourteen. I was with classmates, and halfway up the path we suddenly met a group of wild monkeys.

There were around a dozen, and at first we stopped in surprise. They watched us and moved closer, apparently interested in whether we had food. One friend threw a few biscuit pieces, and the monkeys quickly gathered around them. Later, we saw one take a bag of crisps from a tourist and disappear into the trees.

What interested me most was their hands. They looked surprisingly similar to small human hands, with fingers and wrinkles. The animals were entertaining to watch, but the stolen food reminded me that they were still wild and could behave unexpectedly. I had not expected the walk to include any wildlife, so the encounter stayed in my memory. Afterwards, I read a little about evolution because I wanted to understand our connection with monkeys better. A brief meeting on a path had created a new curiosity.

Key words and phrases

encounter
An unexpected meeting or experience.
evolution
The gradual development and change of living things over many generations.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the time I saw a troop of wild monkeys on a school trip when I was about fourteen. We'd gone to climb a mountain on the outskirts of Hanoi with a few classmates, and about halfway up, a group of small monkeys suddenly appeared in the middle of the path ahead of us.

At first we all froze - I hadn't expected to see any wildlife at all, let alone a dozen monkeys just sitting there watching us. They came a bit closer and started staring us down, almost as if they were sizing us up to see whether we had any food. One of my friends had a bag of biscuits, so we ended up tossing a few scraps their way, and within seconds they were fighting each other over the crumbs.

What struck me most was how human their hands looked - small and wrinkled, almost like a tiny version of our own. It made them seem oddly familiar rather than wild, and I remember thinking that must be part of why people find them so endlessly entertaining to watch. At the same time, I quickly learned they're not as harmless as they look. Later on the same trip, I saw a monkey snatch a bag of crisps straight out of a tourist's hand and disappear into the trees before anyone could react, so there's clearly a cheeky, opportunistic side to them too.

Looking back, it's one of those small experiences that's stuck with me ever since. It genuinely made me curious about how closely we're related to them, and I ended up reading a bit about evolution afterwards, just because of that one encounter on the mountain.
