Culture & Traditions

Traditional dress and national identity

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

People in many countries no longer wear their traditional costume. Some people argue that they will therefore forget their history and traditions. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

In most countries, traditional dress is now worn only at weddings, festivals and official ceremonies. Some people fear that this means a nation's history and customs will gradually be forgotten. I disagree with this view, because culture is preserved by far more than clothing.

It is easy to see why the change has happened. Traditional garments were designed for a way of life that no longer exists. They are often expensive, difficult to clean and impractical for commuting, office work or factory shifts, whereas modern clothing is cheap, comfortable and suitable for almost any activity. People have therefore abandoned traditional dress for practical reasons, not because they have lost interest in their heritage.

More importantly, a nation's history is carried by things that are much harder to lose than a style of dress. Language, food, religion, music, festivals, architecture and family customs all transmit culture from one generation to the next, and none of them depends on what people wear to work. Vietnam and Japan are good examples: everyday clothing in both countries is almost entirely Western, yet their languages, cuisines and traditional celebrations remain firmly intact.

I would accept that traditional costume has real value and should not disappear completely. Governments and schools can protect it by teaching children its history, wearing it on national holidays and supporting the craftspeople who still make it. Wearing it occasionally, however, is enough to keep the knowledge alive.

In conclusion, the decline of traditional dress reflects practical convenience rather than cultural loss, and I do not believe it will cause people to forget their history.

Key words and phrases

impractical for
Not useful or workable for.
transmit culture from one generation to the next
Pass traditions and knowledge from older to younger people.
keep the knowledge alive
Ensure skills or traditions continue to be known.
reflects practical convenience rather than cultural loss
Shows a useful practical choice, not lost identity.
