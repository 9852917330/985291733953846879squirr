Object & Favourite

A plant, flower or tree that is famous or important

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a plant, flower or tree that is famous or important

You should say: - what type of plant, flower or tree it is - where it grows or is usually seen - how it is used in your country - and why it is famous or important

Simple Model Answer

An important tree I know about is the oak, especially the Major Oak in Sherwood Forest near Nottingham. This famous tree is thought to be around a thousand years old. I first saw it on a school trip when I was about ten.

Its trunk and branches are very wide, which made it seem enormous to me. I remember thinking about all the history that had happened while the same tree continued growing. It is also connected with stories about Robin Hood, who is said to have met his men there.

Oak trees have a practical importance too. Their strong wood is used for furniture and parts of buildings. My family has an oak dining table that we use every day. The Major Oak, however, is protected as a landmark rather than used for wood. I like it because it combines natural beauty with history. Standing beside something so old helped me think differently about time and why preserving important trees matters for future visitors.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about oak trees, and I'll also describe a famous example called the Major Oak. This enormous tree stands in Sherwood Forest near Nottingham and is thought to be around a thousand years old. I'd probably say it's the most impressive tree I've ever seen, mainly just because of its sheer size.

Oak trees are common in England. They grow in woodland, fields, parks and gardens, and their strong timber's traditionally been used for furniture and the structural beams of buildings. I've got several pieces of oak furniture at home myself, including the dining table we eat at every day, so the tree's also got a practical connection to everyday life for me.

The Major Oak's different because it's been preserved as a landmark rather than cut down for timber. Its trunk and branches are exceptionally broad, and visitors can immediately sense its age just standing next to it. I first saw it on a school trip when I was about ten, and I was struck by the idea that this exact living tree had survived through so many centuries of history, long before any of my own family's history even began.

It's also tied to the legend of Robin Hood, who's said to have used it as a meeting place for his men. Whether or not that story's literally true, the Major Oak's become a powerful symbol of English history, strength and continuity.
