Technology & Science

Storing knowledge online

Advantages & Disadvantages

Prompt

In the past, knowledge was stored in books. Today, people store knowledge on the internet. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Model answer

Human knowledge was once held in printed volumes on library shelves; it now sits on servers and is retrieved in seconds. In my view the advantages of this shift are decisive, although it has introduced problems that printed books never had.

The most important gain is access. A medical paper that would once have existed in a few hundred university libraries can now be read by a doctor in a rural clinic with a phone, and a student who cannot afford textbooks can study from free encyclopaedias and lecture recordings. Digital storage also allows knowledge to be corrected quickly. When a scientific finding is overturned, an online reference is updated within days, whereas a printed edition carries the error for years until the next reprint.

Searchability matters just as much. Finding one statistic in a printed collection could take an afternoon; the same search now takes a moment, which frees researchers to spend their time thinking rather than hunting for material.

The drawbacks are real, however. Anyone can publish online, so accurate work sits beside deliberate falsehood with no obvious difference in appearance, and readers who have never been taught to check a source are easily misled. Digital records are also less permanent than they look. Websites are taken down, formats become unreadable and whole archives can be lost when a company closes, while a book printed two centuries ago remains legible on the shelf.

In conclusion, the benefits clearly outweigh the costs, because open access to reliable information changes far more lives than any library system ever could. The disadvantages are nonetheless worth taking seriously, and they argue for teaching people to evaluate what they read and for funding proper digital archives rather than for returning to print.
