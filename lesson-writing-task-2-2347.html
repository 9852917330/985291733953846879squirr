Economy & Money

Growth against the environment

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people believe that economic growth is essential for ending global poverty and hunger, while others argue that it damages the environment. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Growth has lifted more people out of poverty than any other force and has also driven the emissions now changing the climate. Both observations are correct, and my own view is that the argument mistakes the amount of growth for the kind of growth.

The case for growth is supported by the record. Hundreds of millions of people left extreme poverty within a single generation as economies in Asia expanded, and it was industry and trade rather than aid that did it. Growth also pays for the things that end hunger permanently: clinics, roads, irrigation and schools all require tax revenue, and a government of a poor country cannot redistribute what has not been produced. Telling such countries to stop developing, having industrialised freely ourselves, is difficult to defend.

The environmental objection is equally grounded. Rising output has meant rising emissions, cleared forests and exhausted fisheries, and the planet cannot supply every person with the consumption levels of a wealthy country. There is also a fairness point, in that the poorest nations contributed least to the damage and are suffering the worst of the consequences first.

The way out is that growth and emissions are separable. Several developed economies have expanded while their emissions fell, because energy came from wind and solar rather than coal and because services grew faster than heavy industry. Renewable power is now cheaper than fossil fuel in most places, so poorer countries can develop without repeating our route if the finance and technology reach them.

In conclusion, growth remains the most reliable route out of poverty and should not be abandoned. What has to end is growth measured by consumption of materials, and wealthy nations owe poorer ones the investment that makes cleaner development affordable.

Key words and phrases

cannot redistribute what has not been produced
Wealth must exist before it can be shared.
suffering the worst of the consequences first
Experiencing the most serious harm earliest.
develop without repeating our route
Progress without following our damaging development path.
