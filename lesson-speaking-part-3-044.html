Whether life is becoming safer or more dangerous

Part 3

Prompt

In what ways is life becoming safer, and in what ways is it becoming more dangerous? Should people always avoid dangers, or is it a good idea to take a risk? What risk should people try to avoid?

In what ways is life becoming safer, and in what ways is it becoming more dangerous?

Simple Model Answer

Medicine, safer buildings and workplace rules have reduced many traditional dangers. However, new risks such as cybercrime and climate problems can affect whole systems. A failure in one country may disrupt supplies elsewhere very quickly. These dangers are harder to avoid personally because being careful cannot protect you from every failure in a bank or transport network.

Key words and phrases

disrupt
Interrupt something and stop it working normally.

Advanced Model Answer

It's become dramatically safer in the most fundamental respects. Medicine has eliminated diseases that once routinely killed children, building and fire regulations have reduced accidents enormously, and workplace deaths are now a small fraction of what they were. The new dangers are different in character — they're systemic rather than personal. Cybercrime, climate instability and highly connected supply chains mean a failure in one place can spread globally within days. The catch is that this kind of danger is much harder to protect yourself from individually; no amount of personal caution stops a bank's systems going down or a supply chain breaking.

Key words and phrases

systemic rather than personal
caused by the whole system instead of one person's choices
no amount of personal caution
however careful one person is, it cannot prevent this

Should people always avoid dangers, or is it a good idea to take a risk?

Simple Model Answer

People should not avoid every risk. Starting a business, moving abroad or learning a new skill all involve possible failure. What matters is understanding the downside and deciding whether the benefit is worth it. Acting without considering consequences is different. Even careful decisions can go wrong, but that is not a reason to avoid all opportunities.

Key words and phrases

downside
The negative part or possible disadvantage of something.

Advanced Model Answer

Avoiding all risk is neither possible nor desirable. Nothing worth doing — starting a business, moving abroad, learning something in public — comes without the possibility of failure, and a person who avoids all of it ends up with a very narrow life. The useful distinction is between informed and reckless risk. Informed means you understand the downside and you've decided it's worth taking; reckless means you simply never looked at it in the first place. That said, the line isn't always obvious in the moment — plenty of reckless decisions felt informed right up until they went wrong.

Key words and phrases

informed and reckless risk
a danger understood in advance versus one taken carelessly
isn't always obvious in the moment
is difficult to see clearly while it is happening

What risk should people try to avoid?

Simple Model Answer

I would avoid risks with lasting harm and very little benefit. Dangerous driving is a clear example: saving a few minutes is not worth risking a life. Taking on very expensive debt or ignoring a serious health problem can also have major consequences. Risks are easier to accept when a mistake can be repaired or recovered from.

Key words and phrases

consequences
The results or effects of an action.

Advanced Model Answer

The ones where the potential harm is permanent and the benefit is small — that's the test I'd apply. Dangerous driving is the obvious example, since you save a few minutes and risk your life. High-interest debt belongs in the same category, as does ignoring a serious medical symptom. If the worst realistic outcome cannot be undone, the reward has to be genuinely substantial to justify it, whereas a risk you can recover from is worth taking more freely.

Key words and phrases

the worst realistic outcome cannot be undone
the most serious likely result would be permanent
genuinely substantial
truly large or important
