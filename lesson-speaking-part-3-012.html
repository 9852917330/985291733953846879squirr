#012
Celebrations

Festivals in the working year, and new ones

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think new festivals will be introduced in the future? Why do you think festivals are important in the working year? Would you agree that the original significance of the festivals is often lost today?

Model answer

Do you think new festivals will be introduced in the future?

Answer 1

Almost certainly, because it's happened continuously throughout history — most traditions people assume are ancient were actually invented at a specific point. I'd expect new ones around environmental themes and around local identity, as places try to distinguish themselves. Many will begin commercially, like Black Friday, which was straightforwardly invented to sell things. Whether any of them becomes genuinely meaningful depends on whether communities take them over and add their own customs, which is exactly how the older festivals came about in the first place. Plenty will just fade out again, though — most invented celebrations never catch on beyond a marketing campaign.

Why do you think festivals are important in the working year?

Answer 2

They interrupt routine, which matters more than it sounds. Without fixed occasions people simply don't stop, since there's always a reason to keep working. A festival gives everyone permission to rest at the same time, which is the key point, because a holiday is far less valuable if everyone you'd want to see is stuck at work instead. They also bring extended families together, often the only time all year it happens. Not everyone gets that rest equally, though — people in hospitals, transport and hospitality often work through the festival precisely so everyone else can enjoy it.

Would you agree that the original significance of the festivals is often lost today?

Answer 3

Partly, though I'd be careful with the word "lost". Commercial promotion certainly overshadows the original meaning — plenty of people now think of a festival mainly in terms of the sales and the food rather than the event it commemorates. But traditions have always changed, and the version people call authentic was itself a modification of something earlier. What survives in practice is usually the social function of bringing families together, and that seems remarkably resistant to commercialisation, even when everything wrapped around it has become thoroughly commercial.
