Trees

Trees in your hometown and why they matter

Prompt

Are there many trees in your hometown? What are the benefits of planting more trees? Do cities have enough trees?

Are there many trees in your hometown?

Simple Model Answer

There are many large, older trees in some parts of my hometown. Newer areas often have fewer, so the amount of green space depends on which neighbourhood you are in.

Key words and phrases

green space
An area with trees, grass or other plants.

Advanced Model Answer

Some older parts of my hometown have mature trees, but the newer districts often don't have as many. It's pretty uneven, so how much greenery you get depends a lot on where people live.

Key words and phrases

where people live
The places people call home.

What are the benefits of planting more trees?

Simple Model Answer

Trees provide shade, make places cooler, take in some pollution and help with rainwater. They also support wildlife and make streets nicer, which helps people enjoy living in the city.

Key words and phrases

wildlife
Animals and other living things that live naturally in an area.

Advanced Model Answer

Trees reduce heat, absorb some pollution, manage rainwater and make streets more pleasant. They're good for urban wildlife too, and overall they improve quality of life.

Key words and phrases

make streets more pleasant
Make roads and pavements more enjoyable.
improve quality of life
Help people live better and more comfortably.

Do cities have enough trees?

Simple Model Answer

I don't think many cities have enough healthy trees. Planting new ones helps, but cities also need to care for them over time and protect them when building work happens nearby.

Key words and phrases

protect
Keep something safe from harm or damage.

Advanced Model Answer

Many cities don't have enough healthy, well-maintained trees. Planting campaigns help, but long-term care and protection during construction are equally important.

Key words and phrases

long-term care
Care provided over an extended period.
equally important
Important to the same degree.
