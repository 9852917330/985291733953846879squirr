Object & Favourite

Your favourite season of the year

Part 2

Prompt

Describe your favourite season of the year.

You should say: - What the weather is like. - Why you like this season. - What activities you usually do during this season. - How different kinds of weather in this season affect people.

Simple Model Answer

My favourite season in Vietnam is spring. I enjoy the mild weather because it is more comfortable than the hottest summer days or the coldest winter ones. It is a pleasant time to spend longer outdoors.

I especially like seeing flowers and fresh growth in parks and gardens. Peach and apricot blossoms are closely connected with the season for me. Spring also brings Tet, when my family gathers and relatives return from work or study elsewhere. We share traditional meals and have more time together than usual.

My friends and I often plan a trip to see flowers outside the city. I also return to cycling and enjoy evening walks without feeling too hot or cold. The comfortable weather seems to help my grandmother too. She struggles more in extreme temperatures but is noticeably more active during spring. That is another reason I appreciate it. For me, the season combines pleasant surroundings, family traditions and opportunities to be outside. It makes ordinary days feel more cheerful without needing a special event every time.

Key words and phrases

mild weather
Weather that is not very hot or very cold.
extreme temperatures
Conditions that are unusually hot or cold.

Advanced Model Answer

My favourite season has to be spring. In Vietnam it usually starts around the end of December and runs through to about the middle of March, and honestly, it's the one time of year when the weather doesn't really bother me either way — it's not roasting like summer or freezing like winter, just genuinely comfortable, day and night.

What I love most about spring is how much nature changes. Cherry and apricot trees are in full blossom, and you start seeing daffodils and marigolds everywhere in the parks near my flat. The mornings are full of birdsong too, which you barely notice the rest of the year. And of course, spring is when we celebrate Tet, which for my family means a proper reunion — my cousins come back from wherever they're studying or working, and every meal for about a week is full of delicious traditional dishes.

Activity-wise, this is the season I actually get outside the most. My friends and I usually go on at least one flower-viewing trip to somewhere just outside the city, and I try to get back into cycling once the weather's mild enough not to make it miserable. It's also just a nicer season for walking around in the evenings without either sweating or shivering.

As for how the weather affects people, I've definitely noticed that the extreme seasons hit people harder than spring does — my grandmother, for instance, really struggles in both the height of summer and the depths of winter, but during spring she's noticeably more comfortable and active. It's a small thing, but it's part of why spring's my favourite time of year in Vietnam.
