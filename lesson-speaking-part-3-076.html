Seasons

Whether the seasons still shape behaviour

Prompt

Do you think the seasons still influence people's behaviour?

Do you think the seasons still influence people's behaviour?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, seasons still change clothes, mood and leisure habits. People spend more time outside in summer and socialise indoors in winter. However, food and work depend less on seasons now because of supermarkets and indoor jobs. The influence has become less directly economic, but limited winter daylight can still have a real effect on how people feel and behave.

Key words and phrases

daylight
Natural light during the daytime.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, though much less than they used to. People still adjust their clothing, routines and free time according to daylight and temperature — in summer we eat outdoors and travel more, whereas in winter people stay in and socialise at home. Where the influence's weakened considerably is food and work: supermarkets sell strawberries in December, and most people now work indoors under artificial light, so the farming calendar that once governed everything has almost no effect on daily life. I'd say the seasons now shape our mood and habits far more than our economy, though that's arguably a bigger effect than people realise — plenty of low mood in winter genuinely does trace back to a lack of daylight.

Key words and phrases

weakened considerably
became much less strong
artificial light
light produced by lamps or electronic devices
the farming calendar
the yearly schedule for planting and harvesting
our mood and habits
the way we feel and what we usually do
a lack of daylight
not having enough natural light during the day
