Someone who is a good parent

Part 2

Prompt

Describe someone who is a good parent.

You should say: - Who the person is. - How you know him/her. - What the person does. - Why you think he/she is good.

Simple Model Answer

Someone I think is a good parent is my best friend's father. He is a doctor, so he has a busy schedule and often works at night. I visit their home quite often, although I usually learn about him through my friend's stories.

What I admire is the way he respects his daughter's choices. He does not expect her to become a doctor just because he is one. Instead, he supports her dream of being a singer. When she receives poor grades, she can tell him honestly because she knows he will help her improve rather than simply get angry.

He also makes time for family despite his work. At weekends, he teaches her cooking and practical skills. Sometimes they go to the cinema and talk about the film afterwards. I think these conversations help them understand each other. He acts as both a parent and a trusted friend. His example has taught me that good parenting means offering guidance while allowing a child to develop their own interests.

Advanced Model Answer

Speaking of a role model in parenting, I'd like to talk about my best friend's father. He's a doctor, and because of his tight working schedule, I don't actually get that many chances to talk to him directly, even though I'm round at their house pretty often. Still, I feel like I know him really well through the stories my best friend tells me — and honestly, how well-rounded and grounded she turned out proves to me that he's done a brilliant job raising her.

Vietnamese parents get a reputation for being “tiger parents”, but he's very much the exception. He respects his daughter's decisions and never forces her into anything against her will. Unlike a lot of doctors, who'd probably expect their kid to follow them into medicine, he's always pushed her to pursue her dream of becoming a singer instead of following the family line of work. Even when she messes up and comes home with bad grades, she's never afraid to tell him, because to her he isn't just a parent; he's a genuine friend who gives her honest advice and helps her figure out how to do better.

Being a doctor is nowhere near a nine-to-five job — he's on night shifts several times a week — but he still manages to take brilliant care of his daughter. He's clearly worked out how to keep a proper work-life balance: he clears his entire weekend for family time, teaching her cooking and basic survival skills, and sometimes taking her to the cinema so they can chat about the film afterwards and whatever it made them think about.

All in all, he's exactly the kind of parent I hope to become myself one day.
