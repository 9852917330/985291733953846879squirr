Transport

Should cyclists pass a test?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some experts propose that cyclists should be required to pass a test before they are allowed to use public roads. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

As cycling has grown in cities, so have complaints about riders who ignore signals and ride on pavements. I disagree with compulsory testing, because it would prevent far more cycling than it would prevent danger, though training itself is valuable.

The argument for a test is reasonable in principle. Roads are shared, and a cyclist who does not understand junctions or signalling endangers pedestrians and themselves. Drivers must prove competence before being allowed out, and consistency suggests the same should apply to anyone using the same space. A test would also ensure that riders know the rules they are accused of breaking.

The objection is one of proportion. A bicycle weighs a few kilograms and travels at fifteen miles an hour; a car weighs well over a tonne and can travel at seventy. Testing exists because of the harm a vehicle can do to others, and cyclists injure almost nobody except themselves. Applying the same procedure to both ignores that difference entirely.

The practical consequences would be worse than the problem. A test costs money and time, and would stop exactly the people cycling should be encouraging: children, low-income workers and older riders. Cities gain enormously from more cyclists through cleaner air, less congestion and better public health, and any measure that reduces the number costs more in illness than it saves in accidents. Enforcement would also be impossible without licensing every bicycle, which no country has managed cheaply. What does work is free voluntary training, taught in schools, along with cycle lanes separated from traffic, since most conflict arises where riders and vehicles are forced to compete for the same narrow strip of road.

In conclusion, cyclists should certainly know the rules, and training deserves public funding. A compulsory test would deter cycling without making roads meaningfully safer, so better infrastructure is the more sensible investment.

Key words and phrases

injure almost nobody except themselves
Cause little physical harm to other people.
better infrastructure is the more sensible investment
Improved facilities offer a better use of money.
