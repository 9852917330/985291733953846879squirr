Are libraries still necessary?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Students today can access information online, so libraries are no longer necessary. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Since almost every text a student needs can now be opened on a laptop, some people conclude that library buildings have outlived their purpose. I disagree, because this argument mistakes a library for a store of books when its real value lies elsewhere.

The first thing a library provides is a place to work. Many students have nowhere at home that is quiet, warm and free of interruption, particularly those sharing a room or living with young children, and a desk in a reading room is the only environment in which sustained study is possible for them. Removing that would disadvantage precisely the students who already have the least support.

Libraries also solve the problem the internet creates. Searching online produces thousands of results of wildly varying reliability, and a first-year student rarely knows which to trust. Librarians teach people how to judge a source, how to search a specialist database and how to reference properly, and universities pay for subscriptions to journals that are locked behind expensive paywalls and cannot simply be found through a search engine.

I accept that the traditional function of lending printed books has genuinely declined, and that libraries which do nothing else look increasingly redundant. The response, though, has been to change rather than to close: many now offer computers, study rooms, language help and community classes, and their visitor numbers have risen rather than fallen.

In conclusion, I strongly disagree that libraries are unnecessary. Online access has removed one of their functions while making the others more valuable, because a student who can reach everything needs help more than ever in deciding what is worth reading at all.

Key words and phrases

disadvantage precisely the students
Create difficulties for those particular learners.
locked behind expensive paywalls
Accessible only after expensive subscription payments.
risen rather than fallen
Increased instead of decreased.
worth reading at all
Valuable enough to spend time reading.
