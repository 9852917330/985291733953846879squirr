Change

Why older people resist change

Prompt

Why do some older people resist change? Is technological change always positive?

Why do some older people resist change?

Simple Model Answer

They may worry about losing skills they have spent years developing. When a familiar method changes, an expert suddenly needs help, which can feel uncomfortable. Changes are also harder when nobody provides training or explains the reason. Younger people may have more practice with new technology, but resistance is not simply about being old.

Key words and phrases

resistance
Unwillingness to accept or support a change.

Advanced Model Answer

I think it's less about age than about competence. Someone who's spent forty years becoming genuinely skilled at something has a great deal to lose when the method changes overnight — they go from being the expert to being the person who needs help, and that's uncomfortable at any age. It's also often handled badly: new systems get introduced with no training and no explanation of why the old way was inadequate, which naturally feels like a judgement on everything they have done. Age plays some part too, admittedly — younger people have simply had more practice adapting to new tools throughout their lives, so it isn't purely about who's more competent.

Key words and phrases

handled badly
dealt with in an unhelpful way
a judgement on everything they have done
a criticism of everything they have accomplished

Is technological change always positive?

Simple Model Answer

No. Technology has improved healthcare and made it easier to contact family abroad, but it also creates problems. People can lose jobs before learning new skills, and privacy may become weaker. We should ask whether a change improves life and who pays the cost, rather than assume everything new is automatically better.

Key words and phrases

privacy
Control over your personal information and private life.

Advanced Model Answer

No, and I think we're too quick to assume it is. Technology's clearly improved healthcare, communication and access to information — nobody wants to give up video calls with family abroad. But it's also eliminated jobs faster than people can retrain, weakened privacy considerably, and created dependencies we don't fully understand. The test should be the outcome and who benefits from it, not simply whether something's new. And the people who benefit are rarely the people who carry the cost, which is why the argument gets so heated.

Key words and phrases

eliminated jobs faster than people can retrain
removed positions more rapidly than workers could learn new skills
who benefits from it
which people gain from it
carry the cost
bear the expense or negative effects
