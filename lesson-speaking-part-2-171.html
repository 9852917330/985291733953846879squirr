Situation

A time when you had to do something in a hurry

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you had to do something in a hurry.

You should say: - what you were doing - when this was - why you had to hurry - and explain how you felt at that time

Simple Model Answer

A time I had to hurry was before a work trip about five years ago. My colleagues and I had an early flight to a conference in Da Nang. A senior colleague had agreed to collect me at half past five in the morning.

I had set an alarm, but apparently switched it off without waking properly. When the doorbell rang at five thirty, I was still in bed. I jumped up, got dressed, washed quickly and brushed my teeth in a rush. Luckily, my suitcase was already packed from the night before.

I apologised repeatedly and ran to the car. We reached the airport with very little time left, and the rest of the group had been waiting. I felt embarrassed because my mistake had affected other people, not only me. Since then, I have used two separate alarms before early journeys. I also leave my phone away from the bed so I must stand up to turn it off. It was an unpleasant morning, but it helped me become more careful about preparation and punctuality.

Key words and phrases

in a rush
Doing something quickly because there is little time.
punctuality
The habit of arriving or doing things at the correct time.

Advanced Model Answer

About five years ago, I had to get ready in a great hurry for a work trip, and it's a morning I still cringe about. A few colleagues and I had to catch an early flight to a conference in Da Nang, and a senior colleague had offered to collect me from home at half past five in the morning.

The problem was that I'd overslept. I'd set an alarm the night before, but I must have switched it off in my sleep without ever properly waking up. At exactly 5.30, the doorbell rang, and I looked at the clock in complete shock, I hadn't even got out of bed yet. I jumped up, got dressed in what must have been record time, splashed water on my face, brushed my teeth in about ten seconds and shoved my shoes on without bothering to tie the laces properly.

Fortunately, I'd packed my suitcase the night before, so at least I didn't have to think about that. I grabbed it, apologised to my colleague about six times on the way down, and jumped into the car. We ended up reaching the airport with only minutes to spare, and since the rest of the group had already been standing around waiting for me, I felt absolutely mortified walking up to them.

The whole incident taught me a pretty practical lesson. Before any early journey now, I always set two independent alarms, and I make sure to leave my phone far enough from the bed that I physically have to get up to switch it off.
