Education

Why girls choose arts and boys choose science

Reasons / Opinion

Prompt

In schools and universities, girls tend to choose arts subjects while boys prefer science. What are the reasons for this trend, and do you think it should be changed?

Model answer

Physics and engineering lecture halls remain largely male, while literature and languages are largely female. The reasons have little to do with ability, and the pattern should be changed because of what it costs both sexes.

The influences begin early. Children are handed different toys and different praise: construction sets and puzzles for boys, drawing and stories for girls, and by the age of seven many children already describe science as something boys do. Teachers, without intending it, ask boys the harder questions in maths and reward girls for neatness. Textbooks and news coverage supply almost no female engineers, so girls have nobody obvious to imagine themselves as. By the time subjects are chosen at sixteen, the decision feels personal but has been shaped for a decade.

Two further factors matter at that point. Girls who are equally good at both often choose the arts because they expect the sciences to be an uncomfortable environment, and reports of harassment in male-dominated fields confirm this. Boys, meanwhile, avoid languages and literature because those subjects are treated as unmasculine, which is a loss that receives far less attention.

The pattern deserves to be changed for three reasons. It wastes talent a country needs, since shortages of engineers exist alongside capable girls who were steered away. It also limits earnings, because technical work pays more, so the divide quietly widens the pay gap. Products designed by uniform teams fail predictably: crash tests built around male bodies made cars less safe for women.

In conclusion, the split arises from expectations set in childhood rather than from aptitude. Changing it through early exposure, visible role models and better teaching practice would benefit both the economy and the individuals currently choosing by habit.

Key words and phrases

ask boys the harder questions in maths
Give boys more challenging maths tasks.
nobody obvious to imagine themselves as
Lacking relatable role models.
a loss that receives far less attention
A disadvantage that receives less public notice.
