A lesson that you enjoyed

Simple Model Answer

A lesson I enjoyed was an English class at university. Our normal teacher was ill, so a substitute teacher called Thomas Hall came instead. He spent the lesson explaining how films could help us learn a language.

He said that following conversations in a film could introduce vocabulary and grammar in a natural context. At first, my classmates and I were doubtful because we were used to studying with textbooks. Then we watched a short clip and answered some questions. The activity was easier and more useful than I had expected.

What I liked most was discovering a method that suited my interests. Traditional study sometimes made me lose motivation, but I already enjoyed watching films. After that lesson, I began choosing English versions and paying attention to useful expressions. Over time, I noticed that I understood more words and felt more confident when writing. I still use films alongside other study methods. The lesson was memorable because it changed something I already enjoyed into an extra opportunity to practise English.

Key words and phrases

substitute teacher
A teacher who temporarily takes the place of another teacher.
natural context
A real or realistic situation that helps explain how language is used.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a really enjoyable lesson I had back at university, in one of my English classes.

Our regular teacher was off sick one day, so the school sent in a substitute, a guy called Thomas Hall, who delivered an amazing lecture about how he thought we should all be learning languages by watching films rather than textbooks. For two hours, he explained how films can help you immerse yourself in a language - you pick up vocabulary and grammar naturally just by following the dialogue, and he reckoned that in the long run, that does more for your overall level than any textbook could.

At first, we were all a bit sceptical, to be honest, but after watching a short clip and trying a quick test on it, we were pretty much convinced - learning through films wasn't nearly as hard as we'd assumed, and it was something we could actually use to improve at school.

What I liked most was that this method genuinely worked for me. I'd always found traditional study a bit demotivating, and my results never really reflected the effort I put in. But once I started watching my favourite films in English instead, things clicked, and my vocabulary grew fast, and I got a lot more confident writing longer essays. I still put it into practice now, actually, whenever I've got a new film to watch.
