Technology

Life then and now, and the effect of electrical equipment

Part 3

Prompt

What are the main differences between life in the past and life today? What are the impacts of electrical equipment on our life? How will technology change our life in future?

What are the main differences between life in the past and life today?

Simple Model Answer

Life is faster and offers more choices now. Information arrives quickly, travel takes less time and machines do much household work. In the past, people often had stronger local routines and knew neighbours better. However, they also had fewer options and spent more time on tiring tasks. So I see benefits and losses rather than one period being completely better.

Key words and phrases

household work
Tasks needed to run a home, such as cooking and cleaning.

Advanced Model Answer

The clearest difference is speed and choice. Information arrives instantly, travel takes hours rather than days, and household technology has removed a great deal of the physical labour that used to fill people's time. What people had in the past was a stronger local routine — you knew your neighbours and life followed a predictable rhythm. The trade-off is that they had far fewer options, and everyday tasks were exhausting in a way that's hard to imagine now.

Key words and phrases

a stronger local routine
more established everyday habits within the local area

What are the impacts of electrical equipment on our life?

Simple Model Answer

Appliances have saved enormous time and effort, particularly for those who did most cooking and washing. Refrigerators also make food storage safer and reduce waste. However, these machines use energy and make us dependent on electricity. Another problem is repair: some modern products are difficult to fix, so people replace them instead, creating extra cost and waste.

Key words and phrases

dependent on
Needing something in order to function or manage.

Advanced Model Answer

Household appliances have transformed daily life more than people usually acknowledge, particularly for women, since washing and cooking once occupied most of the day. Refrigeration alone changed diet and health enormously by making it safe to store food. The costs are energy consumption and a kind of dependence — very few of us could manage a week without electricity. There's also the repair problem: modern appliances are sealed units, so when one fails, you replace it rather than fix it.

Key words and phrases

the repair problem
difficulty fixing damaged devices

How will technology change our life in future?

Simple Model Answer

I expect more routine tasks to become automatic and services to suit individual users more closely. The main difficulties may concern jobs and control rather than the technology itself. Workers may lose roles before retraining, and people need ways to question important automated decisions. A system choosing someone for a loan or interview should not be impossible to challenge.

Key words and phrases

automated decisions
Choices made by a computer system with little or no direct human involvement.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd expect automation to take over most routine decisions — scheduling, purchasing, basic diagnosis, the kind of thing a call-centre worker or a data-entry clerk does today — and services to become far more personalised. The genuine questions aren't technical, though. One is employment: if systems handle routine analytical work, many middle-income jobs disappear faster than people can retrain. The other is control. When a system decides who gets a loan or an interview, somebody needs to be able to explain and challenge that decision.

Key words and phrases

far more personalised
much more closely suited to each person's needs
faster than people can retrain
more quickly than workers can learn different skills
explain and challenge that decision
give reasons for that choice and question it
