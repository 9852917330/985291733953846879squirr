Education

Education as a lifelong process

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

In the past, education was a short period of training, but today people treat it as a lifelong process. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is often observed that education is no longer confined to childhood and early adulthood, but continues throughout a person's working life. I agree that this change has taken place, and I believe it is both necessary and beneficial.

The main reason is that knowledge and technology now change far faster than they once did. A person who trained as an accountant or a mechanic forty years ago could expect the skills learned at twenty to remain useful until retirement. Today, software, regulations and equipment are replaced every few years, and entire occupations disappear. An employee who stops learning becomes unemployable long before reaching pension age, so continuous training is simply a condition of remaining in work.

A second reason is that access to learning has become far easier. Online courses, professional qualifications and evening classes allow an adult to study while working, which was rarely possible in the past. Employers increasingly fund this training themselves, since retraining existing staff is cheaper than recruiting new ones.

There are benefits beyond employment. Adults who continue to study tend to be more confident and more adaptable, and there is evidence that sustained mental activity helps to protect memory in later life. Many people also return to education for subjects they were unable to pursue when young, which is a genuine improvement in quality of life.

In conclusion, I agree that education has become a lifelong process. This is an unavoidable consequence of rapid technological change, but it also gives adults opportunities that earlier generations never had.

Key words and phrases

unemployable long before
Unable to find work much earlier.
simply a condition of remaining in work
Simply necessary to keep a job.
retraining existing staff
Teaching current employees new skills.
sustained mental activity
Thinking and learning regularly over time.
protect memory in later life
Help people remember well as they age.
