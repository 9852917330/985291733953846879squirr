Education

Should children be encouraged to watch television?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Children can learn effectively by watching television. Therefore, they should be encouraged to watch television regularly at home and at school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Television can undoubtedly teach children things that a classroom cannot show them. I agree with the first half of this statement but disagree with the conclusion drawn from it, because the value of television depends entirely on what is watched and for how long.

The learning argument is sound within limits. A documentary can take a child to the deep ocean or inside an erupting volcano, and a chemistry programme can show a reaction that no school laboratory could safely run. Well-made children's programmes have measurably improved vocabulary and number skills, particularly among children whose parents read to them little, so for those families television closes a gap rather than creating one.

The recommendation that follows does not hold, however. Almost none of what children actually watch is educational, and encouraging regular viewing in practice means encouraging entertainment. Television is also passive in a way that matters: a child watching an experiment learns less than a child performing one, because understanding is built by doing something and getting it wrong. Hours in front of a screen additionally displace reading, play and conversation, which are the activities that develop language fastest.

The school proposal is weaker still. Class time is scarce, and a teacher who can question, correct and adapt to a confused pupil does something no broadcast can. Television belongs in a lesson as an occasional illustration, not as a substitute for teaching.

In conclusion, children do learn from good programmes, and those programmes deserve support. The conclusion that viewing should be encouraged regularly is wrong, because it treats television as uniformly educational when the value lies in a narrow band of what is broadcast.

Key words and phrases

closes a gap rather than creating one
Reduces an existing difference rather than adding one.
displace reading, play and conversation
Take time away from other important activities.
a substitute for teaching
Something used instead of proper instruction.
a narrow band of what is broadcast
Only a small selection of available programmes.
