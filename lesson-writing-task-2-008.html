Animals & Wildlife

Animal experimentation

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people strongly oppose animal experimentation, believing that it causes suffering to animals, while others consider it necessary. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Animal experimentation divides opinion sharply. Some people regard it as an unacceptable source of suffering, while others consider it necessary for medical research. In my view, animal studies should be permitted only when there is a strong scientific justification and no validated alternative can answer the same question.

The ethical objection deserves serious consideration. Laboratory procedures can cause pain, distress and confinement, and animals cannot consent to these experiences. Critics also question whether every experiment produces useful evidence. A treatment that appears safe in one species may behave differently in humans. Researchers should therefore explain why the proposed study is relevant rather than assume that using animals automatically makes a product safe.

Supporters argue that some research questions involve interactions between organs and biological systems that remain difficult to reproduce fully. Animal studies have contributed to medical knowledge, and carefully justified work may still provide information that other methods cannot yet supply. For example, understanding how a treatment affects several body systems may require more than an isolated cell experiment. This is a conditional argument for research, however, not a defence of every existing practice.

The appropriate response is strict assessment and continued investment in alternatives. Human cell models, organ-based laboratory systems and computer methods should replace animal procedures wherever they can provide reliable evidence. Where animal research remains justified, the number of animals and the suffering involved should be minimised, and independent reviewers should examine the expected benefits.

In conclusion, neither unrestricted experimentation nor an immediate ban on all animal studies is convincing. A responsible approach requires scientific necessity, effective oversight and a clear commitment to replacing animal use whenever reliable alternatives become available.

Key words and phrases

scientific justification
A clear reason supported by research needs.
validated alternative
Another method whose reliability has been properly checked.
ethical objection
A criticism based on what is morally acceptable.
strict assessment
A careful examination using demanding standards.
reliable evidence
Information that can reasonably be trusted.
independent reviewers
People who evaluate work without being controlled by its authors.
