Object & Favourite

An interesting song you like

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an interesting song you like

You should say: What it is Which country the song comes from What story the song tells And explain why you think it is interesting.

OUTLINE

Simple Model Answer

A song I like is Bonjour Vietnam, sung by Pham Quynh Anh. She was born in Belgium to Vietnamese parents, and the song is in French. I first heard it at secondary school when it received a lot of attention in the media.

The song describes someone living abroad who wants to understand Vietnam and her family background. She imagines visiting the country she has heard about from her parents and other sources. Although I did not understand French at first, I loved the melody and the singer's voice.

Wanting to understand the words encouraged me to begin learning French. Once I understood the meaning, the song became even more special. It reminded me that there was still much about my own country that I had not explored. I keep it in my phone's favourite playlist and sometimes listen when I miss home. I like how a gentle song can connect language, family and a sense of belonging. It helped me appreciate Vietnam through the feelings of someone looking at it from far away.

Key words and phrases

family background
The history and origins of a person's family.
a sense of belonging
A feeling of being connected to a place or group.

Advanced Model Answer

One of my favourite songs is "Bonjour Vietnam", sung by Pham Quynh Anh. The song's in French, though it's really about Vietnam. It came out years ago, but it's still sitting in my favourites playlist on my phone.

I first came across this song back in secondary school, when it was suddenly discussed all over the media — it's pretty rare for a song about Vietnamese identity to get that kind of attention. It's about a Vietnamese woman living abroad who's desperate to learn more about her roots, and who dreams of going to Vietnam herself one day to see whether it's really like the country her parents and the media had always described.

The first time I heard it, I didn't understand a single word of French, but I was hooked by the melody and the singer's voice straight away. That's actually what pushed me to start learning French properly — I wanted to know exactly what the lyrics meant. Once I understood them, the song became even more beautiful to me, because it made me realise there was still so much about my own country I hadn't discovered yet.

I suppose you could say my love for Vietnam was rekindled by a song written by someone who'd never really lived here. I still put it on sometimes when I'm feeling a bit homesick, and it does the job every time.
