Education

Teachers or parents: who shapes a child more?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Once students go to school, teachers have a greater influence on their intellectual and social development than parents. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

School occupies six hours of a child's day for a dozen years, so the claim that teachers take over from parents is not unreasonable. I disagree with it, though the balance shifts more than many parents realise.

Teachers clearly hold advantages in the intellectual half of the argument. They are trained to explain, they know the order in which ideas should be introduced, and they can identify a difficulty that a parent would miss entirely. A gifted teacher can turn a child towards a subject for life, and for children whose homes contain few books and no help with study, school is the only place learning happens at all. Socially, too, the classroom supplies what a family cannot: a crowd of equals, with the negotiating, queuing and losing that go with it.

Parents nonetheless remain the stronger influence for structural reasons. They are there before school, after it, and long after it has ended, and their effect is cumulative rather than annual. They decide the things that determine whether teaching works: sleep, meals, whether homework is possible, whether reading happens at home, and whether school is spoken about with respect. Children also copy behaviour rather than instruction, and the model they see daily is at home. Values around honesty, money and how people are treated are absorbed there, and a teacher who contradicts them rarely wins.

The two are not really in competition. Teachers work with what parents send them, and the pupils who advance fastest almost always have both, which is why parental involvement predicts results more reliably than the choice of school.

In conclusion, teachers have a greater influence on academic knowledge and on how a child behaves among equals. Parents remain more powerful overall, because their influence is constant, early and long, and the best outcomes come from the two working in the same direction.

Key words and phrases

copy behaviour rather than instruction
Follow what people do rather than what they say.
their influence is constant, early and long
Their effect starts young and continues for years.
