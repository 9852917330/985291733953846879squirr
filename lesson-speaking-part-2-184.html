Situation

An experience that you taught a friend or a relative

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a time when you taught a skill to a friend or relative.

You should say: - When it happened and what it was; - Who was taught by you; - Why you taught someone something; - How difficult it was for you.

Simple Model Answer

An experience of teaching a relative was showing my mother how to use Facebook last year. She is in her late forties and had spent much of her life working and looking after our family. New technology often made her nervous.

Many of her friends had begun using Facebook and Zalo, so she wanted to learn how to send messages, share photographs and see their updates. We sat together one afternoon, and I showed her how to open the app, log in and use the main functions. She carefully wrote the steps in a notebook.

After practising a few times, she could manage without my help. I clearly remember her smile when she posted her first photo. Teaching her was easier than I had expected because she paid close attention and was willing to try. The most important result was that she could reconnect with old friends and feel more involved in their lives. I was pleased to give something back to someone who had spent so much time teaching and supporting me.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the time I taught my mother how to use Facebook to send messages and keep in touch with people. My mum's in her late forties, and she never went to university - she started working straight after high school, which was pretty normal for her generation. Ever since she got married, her whole world has basically revolved around the family; she's always made sure my dad and I are well looked after, the house is tidy, all that.

Because of that, she never really had time to keep up with new technology, and she'd get a bit flustered whenever she had to deal with smartphones, laptops or smart TVs. Last year she asked me whether I knew how to post photos and updates on Facebook, since a lot of her friends had started using it, along with apps like Zalo, to stay in touch.

Of course I said yes, and I sat down with her one afternoon to show her the basics - how to open the app, log in, and use the main features. She was so thorough about it that she actually wrote everything down step by step in a notebook, as if she were taking notes for an exam. After a few practice runs, she could manage it completely on her own, and I remember the huge grin on her face when she posted her first photo.

Honestly, I didn't find it difficult to teach her at all - she picked it up faster than I expected. What mattered more was that it let her catch up with her old friends and stay a little more in the loop with what's going on in the world, which I think made her genuinely happier.
