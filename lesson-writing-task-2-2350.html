Language & Communication

Are screens damaging literacy?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

There is a view that the widespread use of computers and mobile phones has contributed to a decline in reading and writing abilities among young people. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Teachers and employers regularly complain that young people cannot write a paragraph or read a long text. I partly agree that devices are involved, but the problem concerns stamina and register rather than literacy itself.

Young people today probably read and write more words than any earlier generation, since messaging, posts and comments are all text. What has changed is the shape of it. Screens train the eye to scan for the useful line and move on, which works for a notice and fails for an argument, so students struggle with anything requiring sustained attention on a single page. Writing has shifted in the same direction: quick exchanges reward brevity and speed, and formal structure is used so rarely that it is never practised. Predictive text and automatic correction remove the effort that once fixed spelling in the memory, and now errors are corrected without ever being noticed.

There are reasons for scepticism about the decline, however. Complaints about the writing of the young are centuries old. Measured literacy rates have risen, not fallen, and today's students write for real readers rather than only for teachers, which usually improves clarity. Adapting language to the situation is a skill in itself, and a student who writes casually to friends and formally in an examination is demonstrating range rather than ignorance.

The honest conclusion is that devices have changed the balance of practice. Reading many short fragments is not the same exercise as reading a chapter, and schools now have to teach deliberately what used to develop by itself: long reading, planning, and revising a draft.

In conclusion, phones and computers have not destroyed literacy, and young people use written language constantly. They have weakened concentration and formal writing, so the remedy is practice at length rather than restrictions on technology.

Key words and phrases

sustained attention on a single page
Concentrating continuously on one piece of text.
errors are corrected without ever being noticed
Automatic correction hides mistakes from the writer.
demonstrating range rather than ignorance
Showing flexibility rather than lack of knowledge.
practice at length rather than restrictions on technology
Extended writing practice instead of limiting devices.
