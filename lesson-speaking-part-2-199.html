Object & Favourite

A useful maths lesson

Part 2

Prompt

Describe something important that you learned in a maths lesson at primary school

You should say: - what it was - when you learned it - how you learned it - and explain how it became important in your future life.

Simple Model Answer

Something important I learned in primary school maths was the multiplication tables from one to twelve. I was about nine, in the year before my final primary year. We called them times tables.

Our teacher began with the twos and had us repeat the answers aloud. We gradually practised the other numbers until we could remember them without working out each calculation again. There were regular tests, and I wanted good marks partly because I liked the teacher. At the time, I did not fully understand why memorising the tables mattered so much.

Now I use them in ordinary situations. For example, I can quickly work out that six times seven is forty-two. The twelve times table is also helpful when calculating monthly payments or savings over a year. Having the answers ready in my mind saves time and makes larger calculations easier. Looking back, I am grateful that we practised so often. It was a basic lesson, but it gave me a skill I have continued using long after leaving the classroom.

Advanced Model Answer

Let me talk about something I learned in a maths lesson at primary school: the multiplication tables for the numbers 1 to 12. I remember doing this in the penultimate year of primary school, when I was maybe nine years old. As children, we just called them 'times tables'. As far as I remember, we started with the number 2 and worked through multiplying it by every number from 1 to 12. Our teacher had us go through the tables speaking aloud — one times two is two, two times two is four, three times two is six, and so on. We gradually worked our way through the tables for every number, until we knew the answers by heart.

I think our teacher also gave us regular tests to check we'd learned them properly. I'm not sure I really understood at the time why learning times tables by rote mattered so much, but I remember enjoying the challenge, and I was keen to get good marks — partly because I liked the teacher and wanted to do well for her.

Years later, I realise how useful it is to have those tables just sitting in my head. It means I can do quick calculations without having to think. The other day, for instance, I needed to multiply six by seven for some reason I can't remember, and the answer, forty-two, just popped into my head. Another example is whenever I'm working out bill payments or savings over a twelve-month period — I find myself reaching for the twelve times table without noticing.

In situations like that, I'm genuinely glad my primary school teachers were so insistent about learning to multiply by heart.
