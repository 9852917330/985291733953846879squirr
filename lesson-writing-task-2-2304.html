Health

Prevention or treatment?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

It is more important to spend public money on promoting a healthy lifestyle in order to prevent illness than on treating people who are already ill. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Health budgets everywhere are growing faster than the economies that fund them, which makes the balance between prevention and treatment a practical question rather than a philosophical one. I largely agree that prevention deserves a greater share, though it cannot come at the expense of care for those already ill.

The argument for prevention is mainly arithmetic. A large proportion of the conditions filling hospitals — type 2 diabetes, heart disease, several cancers, lung disease — is driven by smoking, diet, inactivity and alcohol, and preventing a case costs a fraction of treating one for decades. Measures such as vaccination, clean water, tobacco control and sugar levies have produced the largest gains in life expectancy in history, far exceeding anything achieved by treatment. Prevention also delivers something treatment cannot, since a person who never develops a disease keeps the years rather than surviving them.

The opposing consideration is that treatment cannot be reduced. People are ill now, and a government that redirected money from hospitals to campaigns would be abandoning them; illness also arrives through accident, infection and inheritance, none of which any lifestyle prevents. There is a further difficulty in that prevention pays off over decades while ministers are judged over years, so the savings appear long after the spending.

The strongest objection concerns what prevention means. Advice and campaigns achieve very little on their own and tend to reach the people already healthy, which widens the gap between rich and poor rather than closing it. What works is structural: taxes, regulation of advertising, safe places to walk and cycle, decent housing and school meals.

In conclusion, prevention protects more lives per pound than treatment and should receive a larger and protected share of health spending. Treatment must be funded fully alongside it, and prevention must mean changing conditions rather than issuing advice.

Key words and phrases

keeps the years rather than surviving them
Enjoys extra years instead of merely remaining alive.
the savings appear long after the spending
Financial benefits arrive much later than initial costs.
changing conditions rather than issuing advice
Improve real circumstances, not just give recommendations.
