Sport & Leisure

Elite training centres or public sports facilities

Positive or Negative

Prompt

Some countries achieve international success by building specialised facilities to train top athletes instead of providing sports facilities that everyone can use. Is it a positive or negative development?

Model answer

Some governments concentrate their sports budgets on specialised training centres for a small number of elite competitors, rather than on facilities that the general public can use. In my opinion this is a negative development, although it does bring certain benefits.

The advantages are not difficult to identify. Success at the Olympic Games or a World Cup generates national pride and international recognition, and it can encourage young people to take up a sport when they see a compatriot winning. Elite athletes also require specialist coaching, sports science and equipment that ordinary facilities cannot possibly provide, so without dedicated centres a country will not compete at the highest level.

However, the costs of this approach are considerable. Elite programmes are extremely expensive and benefit a very small number of people, whereas the same money spent on local swimming pools, running tracks and sports halls would improve the health of an entire population. This matters because obesity, diabetes and heart disease are now among the largest burdens on public health budgets, and regular exercise is the cheapest way of reducing them. Spending public money on a handful of professional athletes while ordinary citizens have nowhere affordable to exercise is difficult to justify.

There is also a flaw in the argument that elite success inspires participation. Studies following major sporting events have generally found little lasting increase in the number of people exercising, largely because those who are inspired discover that there is no accessible pool or pitch nearby.

In conclusion, I regard this as a negative trend. Governments should fund facilities that serve the whole community, and support elite athletes from a much smaller share of the budget.

Key words and phrases

generates national pride and international recognition
Makes citizens proud and attracts attention abroad.
at the highest level
Among the strongest or most advanced performers.
benefit a very small number of people
Help only a few individuals.
burdens on public health budgets
Expenses placing pressure on government healthcare funding.
nowhere affordable to exercise
No exercise space within their budget.
no accessible pool or pitch nearby
No usable swimming pool or sports field close by.
