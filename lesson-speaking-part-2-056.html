Place

A tall building in your hometown you like or dislike

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a tall building in your hometown you like or dislike.

You should say: Where it is What it is used for What it looks like And explain why you like or dislike it.

Simple Model Answer

A tall building I like is the Bitexco Financial Tower in District 1 of Ho Chi Minh City. It is near the Saigon River and is easy to recognise from many places downtown. The building contains offices, including those of financial companies.

It is known for its unusual design, which is intended to look like a lotus bud. There is also a landing area for helicopters projecting from one side. Visitors can go to the Skydeck on the forty-ninth floor to look over the city.

My favourite memory is taking my uncle there when he visited from Australia a few years ago. We arrived around sunset and stood quietly by the windows for several minutes. We could see the river and buildings spread out below us. The view was much more impressive than I had expected. Now, whenever I see the tower, I remember that evening. I like it partly for its appearance, but mainly because it reminds me of a special experience with a family member.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about the Bitexco Financial Tower, which is probably the most recognisable tall building in Ho Chi Minh City.

It's right in the centre of District 1, close to the Saigon River, and it towers over pretty much everything else nearby, so you can spot it from most parts of downtown. It's mainly an office building — a lot of banks and financial companies have their headquarters there — but what it's really famous for is the Saigon Skydeck, an observation deck on the 49th floor that's open to the public, plus a helipad sticking out from one side like a little disc.

As for what it looks like, the whole design is meant to represent a lotus bud, which is Vietnam's national flower, and it tapers into a spiral shape as it rises. Though if I'm honest, I've always thought it looks more like a stack of coins, or corn on the cob, than a flower.

I like it because of one particular memory. A few years back, my uncle flew in from Australia, and I took him up to the Skydeck one evening right as the sun was setting. Neither of us said much for the first few minutes — you can see the whole city spread out below, with the river cutting straight through the middle of it, and it really is a breathtaking view. Now, whenever I spot the building's outline on the skyline, from wherever I happen to be in the city, it takes me straight back to that evening, and that's really why I've grown so fond of it.
