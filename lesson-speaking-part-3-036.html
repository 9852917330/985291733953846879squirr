Education

How many subjects schools teach, and changes to the system

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think too many subjects are taught at school? Have there been any major changes in the education system in your country? Should everyone be entitled to go to university?

Do you think too many subjects are taught at school?

Simple Model Answer

Sometimes there are so many subjects that students only memorise enough to pass tests. I would give key subjects such as languages, maths and science enough time for real understanding. However, schools should still offer some variety. Without trying art or music, children may never discover that they have a talent for them.

Key words and phrases

talent
A natural or developed ability to do something well.

Advanced Model Answer

In some systems, yes. There's a genuine tension here: breadth lets children discover what they're actually good at, but an overcrowded timetable means nothing gets studied in real depth, and students end up memorising for exams and forgetting immediately afterwards. I'd rather see fewer subjects taught properly — the core ones, language, maths and science, need enough time for real understanding, and I'd protect those before adding anything new. I wouldn't cut breadth completely, though; a child who never tries art or music simply never finds out whether that's where their talent lies.

Key words and phrases

forgetting immediately afterwards
not remembering it soon after
that's where their talent lies
that is the area in which they are especially skilled

Have there been any major changes in the education system in your country?

Simple Model Answer

Technology is a major change: students now use digital resources and can find information easily. There is also more testing, which can push teachers towards exam content. English and international education have become more important too. However, these opportunities are unequal, since studying abroad or following certain programmes still depends strongly on family income.

Key words and phrases

digital resources
Learning materials available through computers or the internet.

Advanced Model Answer

Several. The most obvious is technology, since classrooms use digital resources routinely now and students have access to information their teachers never had. Assessment's also become far more central, with much more frequent testing, which has advantages but does narrow what teachers cover, since anything not tested tends to get squeezed out. There's far more international influence too: English is taught earlier, international curricula have spread, and studying abroad's now a realistic ambition for many more families than it was a generation ago. Not every family benefits equally, though — that international option still depends heavily on household income.

Key words and phrases

narrow what teachers cover
reduce the number of topics teachers teach
household income
the money earned by people living in one home

Should everyone be entitled to go to university?

Simple Model Answer

Everyone should have a fair chance if university suits them, and lack of money should not block that. However, a degree is not the best route for everyone. Some graduates take jobs that do not need one while still owing money. Strong practical training deserves equal respect because skilled work is valuable too.

Key words and phrases

practical training
Education that teaches the skills needed for a particular kind of work.

Advanced Model Answer

Everyone should have a fair opportunity, in the sense that money should never be the barrier. But entitlement's a different thing from everyone actually going. A degree isn't the right path for every person, and countries that pushed participation very high often ended up with graduates stuck in jobs that never needed a degree in the first place, carrying student debt for it. Strong vocational training deserves equal status — a skilled electrician is not a failed academic.

Key words and phrases

a fair opportunity
the same reasonable chance to succeed
student debt
money owed because someone borrowed to pay for education
a skilled electrician is not a failed academic
being good at electrical work is valuable, not a sign of poor study
