Environment & Climate

Transport carbon emissions

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The table below shows carbon dioxide emissions from transport in three European countries in 1994 and 2004.

Model answer

The table compares the amount of carbon dioxide produced by transport in Germany, France and the United Kingdom in 1994 and 2004, with the totals broken down by type of transport.

Overall, road transport was responsible for the majority of emissions in all three countries, and it remained the biggest source throughout. Germany was the only one of the three to cut its total emissions, while both the UK and France failed to reduce carbon dioxide emissions from transport between 1994 and 2004.

Germany, which had higher emissions than either the United Kingdom or France, saw its total decrease slightly from 47.2 to 46.7 million tonnes. By comparison, France recorded an increase of four million tonnes from 34.7 million in 1994, while the United Kingdom saw a smaller rise of 2.5 million tonnes over the same period.

In the United Kingdom, road transport produced 32.6 million tonnes of emissions in 2004, 2 million more than ten years earlier, while the other three transport sources showed no remarkable growth. A similar pattern was seen in France, where road transport added 3.6 million tonnes to the total emissions within ten years. Germany, by contrast, was the only country of the three to experience a drop in road transport emissions, and its other transport sources also had a lower emission volume, with the exception of civil aviation, where the amount rose to 1.2 million tonnes.
