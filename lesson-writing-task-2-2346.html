Sport & Leisure

Games instead of sport

Reasons / Beneficial or Harmful

Prompt

Nowadays, many children spend a great deal of time playing computer games and less time taking part in sport. Why is this the case? Is this a beneficial or harmful trend?

Model answer

Children who once spent afternoons on a pitch now spend them on a console. Several practical changes explain the shift, and I regard it as harmful, chiefly because of what the body loses.

Availability is the first cause. A game is always ready, requires no equipment, no travel and no other players who happen to be free, whereas sport demands a pitch, a coach and a fixed time. Cities have made this worse by building over the open ground children used to play on, and parents who fear traffic and strangers now keep children indoors. Cost matters too, since organised sport carries fees that many families cannot meet, while a game already installed is free. The games themselves are also more compelling than they were. They are designed by companies whose income depends on time spent, so progress, rewards and friends inside the game make stopping difficult, and a child who loses at football feels it publicly while a game lets you try again immediately and privately.

There are benefits worth acknowledging: strategy games train planning, online play maintains friendships, and children who dislike competitive sport are no longer excluded from everything.

The costs are heavier. Physical activity in childhood determines bone strength, heart health and weight for the rest of life, and none of it can be recovered later. Sport also teaches losing, cooperation and the value of practice in a way that a screen cannot reproduce because nothing physical is at stake. Sitting for hours affects sleep and posture, and inactive children usually become inactive adults, which is why obesity and diabetes now appear at ages that were once unheard of.

In conclusion, games have grown because they are cheap, always available and skilfully designed, while sport has become harder to access. The trend is harmful, and the remedy lies in free local facilities and safe streets rather than in criticising children.

Key words and phrases

free local facilities and safe streets
Nearby spaces available without charge and danger.
