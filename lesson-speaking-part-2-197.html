Object & Favourite

A type of weather you like

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a type of weather you like

You should say: what kind of weather it is when this weather usually occurs what you usually do during this weather And explain why you like this type of weather

Simple Model Answer

A type of weather I like is late autumn in Hanoi, especially the last few weeks of October. I have lived here all my life and find the weather difficult during much of the year. Summer can be extremely hot and then suddenly rainy.

In late autumn, the sky is often blue and there is a gentle breeze. It becomes comfortable to sit outside or go cycling without feeling too hot. That makes me more willing to plan picnics and spend time outdoors with friends.

It is also a time when my family and neighbours sometimes gather for a barbecue. My father brings out his old cassette player, people talk about football, and someone usually starts playing music. The gathering can be noisy, but I enjoy the friendly atmosphere. What makes this weather special is partly the contrast with hotter or wetter months. A few pleasant weeks encourage everyone to leave the house and enjoy being together. For me, comfortable weather is not only about temperature; it creates opportunities for the kinds of shared evenings I remember happily.

Advanced Model Answer

I was born in Hanoi and I've lived here my whole life. The city's known for a lot of things — the street food, the mad rush-hour traffic — but it's also famous for how unpredictable the weather can be. In summer the mornings can be scorching hot, the kind where you don't want to leave the house, and then out of nowhere it'll be raining cats and dogs by the afternoon. After more than twenty years here, I've worked out exactly when it's worth planning a picnic or a bike ride with friends: late autumn.

The last couple of weeks of October are pretty much ideal for anything outdoors. There's a clear blue sky most days, and after a brutally hot summer, it's such a change of scenery to sit outside in a gentle breeze without sweating through your shirt. People here genuinely say Hanoi looks its best during this stretch of the year, and I'd agree.

It's also when I make a real effort to spend time with my parents and the neighbours. We usually get a few families together and have a barbecue out on the street. My dad drags out this ancient cassette player he's oddly proud of — probably because nobody else in the neighbourhood still owns one — and he sits there listening to the football results with his mates over a beer, while the neighbours turn on some music and sing along. It's a bit chaotic, honestly, like a small Viking feast, but I love being part of it.

Living somewhere the weather's this harsh and unpredictable for most of the year, I think two or three weeks of genuinely perfect weather, spent properly enjoying time with family and neighbours, is something worth appreciating.
