Object & Favourite

A science subject you studied at school

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a science subject you studied at school.

You should say: - what you learned about - what the teacher was like - what you thought of the teacher - and how you felt about it.

Simple Model Answer

A science subject I studied at high school was Biology. I began learning it about six years ago because it was a compulsory subject. We studied topics such as cells, the human body and genetics.

Our teacher, Linh, was strict but explained ideas using everyday examples. For instance, she described digestion by discussing what happens after we eat lunch. Even with her help, I found the first lessons difficult. I expected the subject to be complicated, and my poor mid-term result made me less confident.

However, I continued practising. Linh advised me to summarise each chapter in my own words instead of only reading the textbook again. This helped me understand the ideas rather than just remember labels. By the end of the term, my result had improved greatly. I felt proud because I had worked through something difficult. Biology also made me more curious about how living things function. I still enjoy reading about science, partly because that class taught me that a challenging subject can become interesting with the right approach.

Key words and phrases

compulsory subject
A subject that students are required to study.
in my own words
Using my own way of expressing an idea rather than copying it.

Advanced Model Answer

There were quite a few science subjects I studied years ago, but the one I'd like to talk about is Biology, which I had the chance to learn at high school. This was about six years ago, right at the start of the school year, when I was absolutely swamped with new subjects. Biology happened to be one of the compulsory subjects that year, so I didn't really have a choice about taking it.

We covered things like how the human body works, cells, and later on genetics, which I found genuinely interesting once I got into it. My teacher was a woman called Linh, and she was one of the most respected teachers in the school — quite strict, but she always backed it up with real examples, like explaining digestion by walking us through what happens after we eat lunch, rather than just making us memorise diagrams.

Honestly, at first I struggled. I was under the impression that Biology would be complicated, and in the first few lessons I couldn't understand half of what she taught, which knocked my confidence and showed up badly in my mid-term results. But I didn't give up, and kept working at it day by day until it gradually started to click.

By the end of the term, I'd passed with flying colours, and I honestly think that came down to Linh's practical advice on how to study more effectively — she taught me to summarise each chapter in my own words instead of just re-reading the textbook. Looking back, Biology wouldn't have been an easy subject to pass without that kind of determination and perseverance, and it's actually one of the reasons I still enjoy reading about science now.
