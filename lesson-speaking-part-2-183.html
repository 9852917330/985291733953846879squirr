Situation

An experience that you had when you left home

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an experience that you had when you left home

You should say: - What is it - When did it happen - And how do you feel about that experience

Simple Model Answer

An experience I had away from home was getting lost in a forest on a company trip about a year ago. We were staying near Hanoi, around eighty kilometres from home, for two days.

After an evening of food, singing and too many drinks, I suggested exploring the forest beside our campsite. About ten colleagues agreed. We used phone lights and walked until the campfire was no longer visible. After roughly twenty minutes, nobody knew the way back. Finding a large banyan tree and a small temple made the situation feel even stranger, especially when someone began telling a ghost story.

We became frightened and called colleagues who had stayed at camp. They contacted the caretakers, who knew the location and told us to wait. About fifteen minutes later, they arrived and guided us back. We felt relieved and embarrassed. Later, we laughed about the evening, but I realised how foolish the decision had been. The experience taught me not to confuse an exciting idea with a sensible one, particularly when exploring an unfamiliar place at night.

Key words and phrases

campsite
A place where people stay in tents or camp outdoors.
caretakers
People responsible for looking after a place or property.

Advanced Model Answer

I'm going to talk about the time I got lost in a forest about eighty kilometres from home. This was around a year ago, on a two-day company trip to a resort near Hanoi.

The first evening was great fun - eating, drinking and singing with my colleagues until late, and most of us had had a few too many drinks, which is really the only reason what happened next made any sense. Our campsite was right next to a huge forest, and in that state I announced what seemed like a brilliant idea: 'Let's go and explore it.' Ridiculous as it sounds, everyone who could still walk straight was surprisingly up for it.

So about ten of us set off, using our phone torches to see. At first, while we could still see the campfire behind us, it felt exciting, even though I was leading the way with no idea where I was heading. After twenty minutes of wandering, we found ourselves in the middle of nowhere, and worse, none of us could remember the way back. Further on we found a huge banyan tree with a small temple beside it, lit in an eerie reddish glow, and right then a colleague chose that exact moment to tell a ghost story about a haunted temple back home - creepy enough that someone screamed.

We instantly regretted the whole idea, but there wasn't much to do except call the people back at camp. Luckily a few were still awake, and they found the caretakers, who knew exactly where that tree was and told us to stay put. So we huddled by the temple, feeling equal parts scared and stupid, until they turned up about fifteen minutes later and walked us back. We were exhausted and more than a little embarrassed, but somehow stayed up half the night laughing about it. I don't think I'll ever forget that trip - one of the most ridiculous nights of my life, and, oddly, one of the best.
