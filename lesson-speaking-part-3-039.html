Emotions

What excites people, and staying calm

Part 3

Prompt

What generally makes people in your country excited? Are people in your country generally calm, or do they get excited easily? How do you think people show their excitement?

What generally makes people in your country excited?

Simple Model Answer

National football victories, weddings, New Year celebrations and personal achievements often create excitement. People also get excited about events they have waited for, such as a concert. Usually they need both expectation and a personal reason to care. However, interests vary: someone may ignore a huge national event but feel thrilled about getting their first job.

Key words and phrases

personal achievements
Goals or successes that matter in your own life.

Advanced Model Answer

Sport's probably the biggest one — a national football victory genuinely brings people out onto the streets in a way almost nothing else does. Beyond that, family celebrations like weddings and New Year, and personal milestones such as exam results or a first job. Announcements about things people have been waiting for, a concert finally being confirmed, say, also generate a surprising amount of excitement. The common thread is anticipation combined with emotional investment; excitement seems to need both. It's not universal, though — plenty of people find big national events completely underwhelming, and get far more excited by something purely personal instead.

Key words and phrases

anticipation combined with emotional investment
looking forward to something and caring greatly about it
completely underwhelming
much less impressive than expected

Are people in your country generally calm, or do they get excited easily?

Simple Model Answer

It depends on the setting. People may control their feelings at work or around strangers but show much more excitement at a match or family celebration. Culture affects when strong emotion seems acceptable. Personality matters too, though, because some people remain quiet in almost every situation. I would not describe a whole country in only one way.

Key words and phrases

setting
The place or situation in which something happens.

Advanced Model Answer

It depends enormously on the setting, which I suspect is true of most cultures. In formal situations, at work, with people they don't know well, dealing with officials, people are quite restrained, and showing strong emotion would seem out of place. But at a festival, a football match or a family gathering, exactly the same people can be extremely expressive. So it's less about temperament than about where the culture permits emotion. That's not the whole story, though; some individuals stay reserved wherever they are, festival or office, so personality still matters on top of context.

Key words and phrases

out of place
uncomfortable because one does not seem to belong
where the culture permits emotion
in a setting where showing feelings is accepted
personality still matters
a person's individual character also affects the result

How do you think people show their excitement?

Simple Model Answer

People often smile, move their hands more and speak faster or louder. They also want to share their news immediately, as if excitement needs someone to hear it. Online, people use messages and emojis instead. Those reactions may be more carefully chosen, while a face-to-face response often happens before someone has time to plan it.

Key words and phrases

response
What someone says or does after something happens.

Advanced Model Answer

The physical signs are fairly universal — speaking faster and louder, smiling, gesturing more, and an obvious need to tell somebody immediately. That last part's interesting, because excitement seems to need an audience in a way other emotions don't. Online it looks different, expressed through messages and emojis, but I'd say it reads as more deliberate, more composed than the real thing. Face-to-face reactions are harder to fake because they happen before you've had time to think about how you want to look.

Key words and phrases

Face-to-face reactions are harder to fake
people's reactions in person are more difficult to pretend
