A school that you went to when you were a child

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a school that you went to when you were a child.

You should say: - where the school was - when you went there - what the school and the teachers were like - and explain whether you enjoyed your time there.

Simple Model Answer

A school I attended as a child was a small state primary school in my hometown. It was close to our house, so I walked there with my older sister almost every day from the age of five to eleven.

The school had six classrooms, a small library, a sports field and a large hall for assemblies. It seemed enormous when I was young, although it looked much smaller when I returned years later. The teachers were caring but expected us to work hard. We felt comfortable asking questions, even when our answers were wrong.

My clearest memory is taking part in a school play in my final year. I only had a few lines, but speaking in front of the school and our parents frightened me. Luckily, I remembered everything. Afterwards, I felt proud and more confident. The school gave me useful knowledge and good friendships, but that performance taught me another lesson: confidence can grow when you try something that feels difficult at first.

Key words and phrases

state primary school
A school for young children funded by the government.
assemblies
Meetings where many or all students in a school gather together.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the primary school I went to in the town where I grew up. It was a small state school within walking distance of my family home, so between the ages of five and eleven, I walked there with my older sister almost every single day, rain or shine.

The school itself wasn't anything fancy — just six classrooms, one for each year group, plus a small library, a sports field and a big assembly hall where we all gathered on Monday mornings. At the time it felt huge to me, but when I went back and had a look years later, I realised how compact it actually was. The teachers were caring but firm; they never let us get away with being lazy, but they also made the classroom feel safe enough that we weren't afraid to put our hands up and get things wrong.

What I remember most clearly, though, is taking part in the school play in my final year. I had a small speaking part, just a handful of lines, but I had to say them in front of the whole school and all the parents, and I was terrified beforehand. Somehow I didn't forget a single word, and walking off stage afterwards gave me a genuine sense of achievement that I still remember now.

Looking back, I've got really fond memories of that school. It gave me some close friendships and a strong academic foundation, but more than that, it taught me that confidence isn't something you're born with — it's something you build gradually, usually by doing things that scare you a bit first.

Key words and phrases

within walking distance
close enough to reach on foot
caring but firm
kind but clear about rules and limits
a genuine sense of achievement
a real feeling of success after completing something
a strong academic foundation
Strong basic knowledge and study skills.
