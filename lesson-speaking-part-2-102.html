Object & Favourite

An impressive story you heard from other people

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an impressive story you heard from other people.

You should say: - what the story was - when you heard it - who told you the story - and explain why you were impressed by it.

Simple Model Answer

An impressive story I heard was about a former colleague's father. She told me over lunch about two years ago. He had trained as a teacher, but financial difficulties forced him to become a long-distance lorry driver, a job he did for nineteen years.

What surprised her was discovering that he had continued studying throughout that period. At night, he read in his cab under a small light. He completed a history degree by studying at a distance and posting assignments from places along his driving route. He did this quietly, without telling the family at first.

He finished at fifty-eight and did not plan to change jobs. When asked why he had studied, he explained that learning was an important part of who he was. I found this moving because we often connect education with money or promotion. He had continued for personal reasons, despite tiredness and a difficult schedule. His patience impressed me, but his love of learning impressed me even more. The story changed how I think about the purpose of education.

Key words and phrases

cab
The part of a lorry where the driver sits.
studying at a distance
Learning away from a college, using materials and assignments sent remotely.

Advanced Model Answer

The story that's stayed with me longest is one a former colleague told me about her father, and I heard it over lunch about two years ago.

Her father had trained as a teacher, but when the family's finances collapsed he took work as a long-distance lorry driver, which he ended up doing for nineteen years. What she only found out after he'd retired was that he'd spent almost every night of those years reading in the cab under a little clip-on light, and had quietly worked his way through a correspondence degree in history, submitting the assignments from post offices along his route.

He never told anyone while he was doing it — not even her mother knew until years later. He didn't use the qualification either; he was fifty-eight by the time he finished, and he had no intention of changing careers at that point. When she asked him why he'd bothered, he apparently said that the driving was something he did, and the reading was who he was, and he hadn't wanted to lose the second one.

I was impressed for two reasons. The obvious one is the persistence: nineteen years of study with no deadline, no classmates and no reward at the end. But what struck me more was the reason. We usually assume people study in order to get something out of it — a promotion, a new career, a certificate to hang on a wall — and he'd done it purely to remain himself, which I found unexpectedly moving.

Key words and phrases

the family's finances collapsed
The household lost financial security.
worked his way through
