Technology & Science

Computers and children's development

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people believe that computers do not help children learn more effectively, and that using them has a negative effect on children's physical and mental development. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes claimed that computers do not improve children's learning and that they damage physical and mental development. I disagree with the first part of this statement, although I accept that excessive screen use causes genuine harm.

The educational benefits are difficult to dismiss. Software can adapt to a child's level, providing extra practice where it is needed, and immediate feedback helps learners correct mistakes before they become habits. Simulations allow students to observe chemical reactions or historical reconstructions that no classroom could provide, and children with dyslexia or impaired sight can use tools that make written material accessible for the first time.

The concerns about development, however, deserve to be taken seriously. Children who spend many hours seated exercise less, and rates of obesity and poor posture have risen accordingly. Screen use before bed disrupts sleep, which affects concentration the following day. There is also reasonable evidence linking heavy social media use among adolescents, particularly girls, with anxiety and low self-esteem.

The important distinction is between purposeful and unlimited use. A child using educational software for an hour is in an entirely different situation from one scrolling through videos for five hours, and research on this subject is frequently criticised for confusing the two.

In conclusion, I disagree that computers fail to help children learn, since the evidence points clearly in the opposite direction. The real problem is the quantity and the type of screen time, and the sensible response is to regulate both rather than to avoid technology altogether.
