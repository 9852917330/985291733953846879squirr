Countryside

The advantages of country living, and how it has changed

Prompt

What advantages does living in the countryside have, compared to living in a city? Have areas of countryside in your country changed since you were a child? Do you think that visits to the countryside should be something that schools offer to their children?

What advantages does living in the countryside have, compared to living in a city?

Simple Model Answer

The countryside usually offers cleaner air, less traffic and a slower way of life. In small villages, neighbours often know each other, which can create a friendly community. However, it also has disadvantages. Jobs may be harder to find, and good schools or hospitals can be far away, so the benefits depend on a person's needs.

Key words and phrases

way of life
The usual habits and conditions in which people live.

Advanced Model Answer

I think there are some clear advantages to living in the countryside. With the clean air and less traffic or industry, the countryside's generally a much healthier place to live than the average city, and there's a stronger sense of community too — in a small village everyone tends to know everyone else, and the pace of life is slower, with people more laid back and friendly, compared to the hustle and bustle of city life. It's not without its downsides, though: jobs are scarcer, and basic services like hospitals or good schools are often a long drive away.

Key words and phrases

the pace of life
how quickly daily life seems to move
the hustle and bustle
Busy, noisy activity.
basic services
essential help such as water, transport, or healthcare

Have areas of countryside in your country changed since you were a child?

Simple Model Answer

The protected hills, woods and paths I know have changed less than I expected. However, villages now have better roads, more cafes and guesthouses, and more weekend visitors. Tourism provides useful income, but some villages feel designed mainly for visitors rather than local families. So the landscape may stay familiar while daily life changes around it.

Key words and phrases

landscape
The natural features and appearance of an area.

Advanced Model Answer

Less than I expected, in the area I know best, because a good deal of it's protected from development — the hills, footpaths and woodland are still pretty much as I remember them. What has changed is the infrastructure around the villages: better roads, a good many more cafes and guesthouses, and considerably more visitors at weekends than there used to be. That's brought welcome income to places that were losing their young people, though a few villages now feel arranged for tourists rather than actually lived in.

Key words and phrases

losing their young people
seeing young residents move away

Do you think that visits to the countryside should be something that schools offer to their children?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, especially for children who mainly know city life. They can exercise outdoors and learn about animals and plants directly. Some might never experience the countryside otherwise. The difficulty is cost, particularly for longer trips. Schools with limited money may only manage a short visit, so the opportunity is not equally available to everyone.

Key words and phrases

directly
Through personal experience rather than through someone else's description.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, I think it's a great idea for schools to take children on trips to the countryside, especially if those children live in a large town or city. A visit like that can be both relaxing and educational — children get to walk in the fresh air, and see animals and plants they might never have come across otherwise. If schools don't offer this, some children will never see life outside the city. The main obstacle is usually cost, though: a proper residential trip isn't cheap, and schools in poorer areas often can't manage more than a single day out a year.

Key words and phrases

a single day out
one short outing
