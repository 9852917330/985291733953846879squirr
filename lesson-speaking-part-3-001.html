Advertising

Game shows and whether adverts get noticed

Prompt

Do you think there are too many game shows on TV nowadays and why? Do you think that people pay attention to adverts on TV and why?

Do you think there are too many game shows on TV nowadays and why?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, I think there are too many. Game shows are popular and cheaper to make than dramas, so channels keep producing similar versions. For example, Who Wants to Be a Millionaire? has appeared in many countries. I'd rather watch a film or a new drama, although I do enjoy some shows that test knowledge rather than depend mainly on luck.

Key words and phrases

depend mainly on luck
Be decided mostly by chance rather than ability.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, there are far too many game shows on TV for my liking. I suppose the channels show these programmes because they're popular, and they must be cheap to make compared with drama, which is why they've multiplied so quickly. A good example is 'Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?', which has been sold to broadcasters across the world and repeated in dozens of local versions. Personally, I'm not a fan, and I'd much rather watch a film or an original drama series. That said, I'd make an exception for the handful that test real knowledge rather than luck, since at least those reward something more than guessing.

Key words and phrases

far too many
a much larger number than needed
multiplied so quickly
increased in number very rapidly
reward something more than guessing
give credit for more than a lucky answer

Do you think that people pay attention to adverts on TV and why?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, at least to some degree. Adverts make products familiar and connect them with a lifestyle people want. We may not buy immediately, but remember the name later. Many viewers skip adverts, so companies also pay to show products inside programmes. That way, the message can reach people even when they are not actively watching an advert.

Key words and phrases

to some degree
Partly, although not completely.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, I think we're all influenced to some extent by television advertising; otherwise companies wouldn't spend so much money on it. Advertisers create memorable images and link products with desirable lifestyles, so a brand starts to feel familiar before you've even used it. Even when viewers don't buy immediately, an advert can plant a seed and affect a later decision — that's the whole point of a long-running campaign. How closely people pay attention obviously varies, though, and most of us skip adverts whenever we can, which is exactly why advertisers have moved towards sponsorship and product placement, since you can't skip what's built into the programme itself.

Key words and phrases

to some extent
Partly, but not completely.
long-running campaign
a planned public effort that continues for a long time
product placement
showing a branded item within a film or programme as advertising
