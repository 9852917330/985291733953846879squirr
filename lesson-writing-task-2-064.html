Work & Employment

Sending new doctors and teachers to rural areas

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Good facilities can be found in cities but not in the countryside. Some people therefore argue that newly qualified doctors and teachers should be required to work in rural areas. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Rural areas in most countries have far fewer hospitals and schools than cities, and it is sometimes proposed that newly qualified doctors and teachers should be required to work there. I agree that rural communities need these professionals urgently, but I disagree that compulsion is the right way to achieve it.

The need is undeniable. A village without a doctor means that treatable conditions become serious, and a school without qualified teachers condemns a generation of children to limited opportunities. Newly qualified professionals also gain genuinely from rural work: a doctor in a small clinic sees a far wider range of conditions and must make decisions independently, which develops competence more quickly than a junior post in a large hospital.

Compulsion, however, tends to fail. Professionals forced into rural posts usually count the days until they can leave, and unwilling staff provide poor care and depart the moment their term ends, so the community faces constant turnover instead of continuity. Skilled people may also emigrate rather than accept the requirement, which makes the shortage worse.

The better approach is to make rural work genuinely attractive. Higher salaries, subsidised housing, cancellation of student loans, guaranteed promotion afterwards and decent equipment have all been shown to work. Recruiting students from rural areas is particularly effective, since they already have family and friends outside the cities and are far more likely to return home and stay.

In conclusion, I agree that rural communities deserve properly qualified doctors and teachers, and that the current imbalance is unacceptable. Incentives, however, will retain such people where obligation will not.

Key words and phrases

treatable conditions become serious
Manageable illnesses worsen without timely care.
count the days until they can leave
Eagerly wait for the chance to quit.
depart the moment their term ends
Leave immediately after their required service.
constant turnover instead of continuity
Staff repeatedly leave instead of remaining long term.
cancellation of student loans
Removing the obligation to repay education debts.
