Cities

Migration from the countryside to the cities

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think large-scale migration from the countryside to cities is positive or negative? Do you think that the possibility of working from home via the internet will lead to many people going back to the countryside? In what ways do the new megacities of Asia, Africa, and South America differ from older ones such as London or New York?

Do you think large-scale migration from the countryside to cities is positive or negative?

Simple Model Answer

It can benefit individuals because cities offer better jobs, wages and education. However, rapid movement puts pressure on both places. Cities may not build homes and services quickly enough, while rural areas lose young workers. I think moving is a reasonable choice, but governments need to manage the speed of growth and support the communities affected.

Key words and phrases

puts pressure on
Creates extra demands that are difficult to manage.

Advanced Model Answer

On balance, positive for the individuals, and difficult for the places at both ends. People move because cities offer work, wages and schooling that simply don't exist in rural areas, and it'd be patronising to say they shouldn't. The problem's speed: when a city grows faster than its housing and transport can keep up, migrants end up in informal settlements with poor sanitation, and rural areas lose exactly the young workers they needed to keep going.

Key words and phrases

informal settlements
areas where people live without officially planned housing

Do you think that the possibility of working from home via the internet will lead to many people going back to the countryside?

Simple Model Answer

Some people will move back, but probably not most. Online work can give office workers cheaper homes and more space. However, many jobs require people to be present, and rural areas may lack schools, hospitals or good internet. Living far from others can also be lonely. So remote work creates more choice, rather than a complete return to country life.

Key words and phrases

remote work
Work done away from a usual workplace, often using the internet.

Advanced Model Answer

Some, but not the large-scale reversal people predicted. Remote work does let a certain group — office workers with decent salaries — choose cheaper housing and more space, and there was a visible movement in that direction just after the pandemic. But it stalls for practical reasons: schools, hospitals, patchy internet, and the fact that most jobs still cannot be done remotely. There's also isolation, which people underestimate until they've actually experienced it, so the shift looks real but partial rather than any kind of wholesale return to rural life.

Key words and phrases

patchy internet
internet service that is unreliable or not available everywhere
most jobs still cannot be done remotely
the majority of jobs still need workers to be present

In what ways do the new megacities of Asia, Africa, and South America differ from older ones such as London or New York?

Simple Model Answer

Many newer megacities have grown much faster than their roads, transport and basic services. Cities such as Lagos or Dhaka have gained millions of residents in a short time. Older cities developed more gradually, giving systems longer to grow. They still have inequality, but usually have more established institutions and transport networks.

Key words and phrases

basic services
Essential facilities such as clean water, sanitation and public transport.

Advanced Model Answer

The main difference is the speed of growth relative to infrastructure. London expanded over centuries, so its transport and sanitation had time to develop alongside the population. Cities like Lagos or Dhaka have added millions of people within a few decades, so a large proportion of residents end up in informal housing that the city never really planned for. Older cities have serious inequality too, and it'd be wrong to romanticise them — but they generally do possess mature institutions and transport networks that the newer megacities are still very much in the middle of building.

Key words and phrases

the speed of growth relative to infrastructure
how fast a city expands compared with its roads and services
mature institutions
well-developed public organisations and systems
