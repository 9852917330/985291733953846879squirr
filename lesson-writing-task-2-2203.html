Cities & Urban Life

Public spaces in towns and cities

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

It is important for all towns and cities to have large public spaces such as squares and parks. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?

Model answer

It is often argued that every town and city should provide substantial open areas such as parks and public squares. I completely agree with this view, because such spaces improve residents' health, strengthen communities and make urban environments far more liveable.

The health benefits are the most obvious. City dwellers spend most of the day indoors and seated, and a nearby park is the cheapest and most accessible place to walk, run, cycle or play sport. Free access matters enormously, since low-income families cannot afford gym memberships. Green space also improves air quality and lowers summer temperatures, which is increasingly important as cities grow hotter, and there is now considerable evidence that regular contact with nature reduces stress and anxiety.

Public spaces also perform a social function that no private facility can replace. A square or park is one of the very few places where people of different ages, incomes and backgrounds meet without having to spend money. Markets, festivals, concerts and public celebrations all require open ground, and communities that have such places tend to be more cohesive and less isolated.

The usual objection is that land in city centres is extremely expensive and could be used for housing or offices. This is a genuine difficulty, but it is short-sighted. Property near parks consistently commands higher prices, and cities famous for their open spaces, such as London and New York, have found that these areas repay their cost many times over through tourism, health savings and residential appeal.

In conclusion, I strongly agree that large public spaces are essential, and planners should protect them even when the pressure to build is intense.

Key words and phrases

without having to spend money
Without needing to pay.
more cohesive
More closely connected and united.
short-sighted
Ignoring future consequences for immediate benefits.
repay their cost many times over
Produce benefits far greater than their expense.
the pressure to build is intense
There is a very strong demand for construction.
