Family

Family likeness, and family against friends

Part 3

Prompt

In what ways can a family be similar to each other? In terms of her personality, are people more influenced by their family or by their friends? Do you think daughters are always more similar to their mothers than to male relatives?

In what ways can a family be similar to each other?

Simple Model Answer

Families can share physical features, such as height or facial appearance. They also copy habits, expressions, humour and ways of handling arguments after years together. Ideas about money and work can pass between generations without formal teaching. However, family influence is not complete; a child may develop a very different personality from the people who raised them.

Key words and phrases

physical features
Visible parts of a person's appearance, such as face shape or height.

Advanced Model Answer

The obvious similarities are physical — height, features, the way people walk. But what strikes me more is the behavioural side: families share a sense of humour, particular expressions, how loudly they talk and how they handle disagreement. Some of that's genetic, but a great deal is simply absorbed from years of proximity. Values around money, work and hospitality also pass down remarkably reliably, usually without anyone deliberately teaching them and often without the family noticing it's happening. It doesn't hold for every trait, though — a strong-willed child in a quiet family can turn out nothing like the people who raised them.

Key words and phrases

absorbed from years of proximity
learned gradually from being near someone for many years
a strong-willed child in a quiet family
a determined child among relatives who are generally quiet

In terms of her personality, are people more influenced by their family or by their friends?

Simple Model Answer

It changes with age. Family shapes early habits and values, while friends become especially influential during the teenage years. Adults often keep family values but adopt ways of speaking or thinking from friends. Some people resist both influences strongly, though. I think personality develops through a mixture rather than coming entirely from one group.

Key words and phrases

influential
Having a strong effect on what people think or do.

Advanced Model Answer

It shifts with age, which I think is the key point. Family dominates early childhood, setting the basic values and habits, and those tend to persist. During adolescence friends become far more influential — teenagers adjust their language, clothing and often their ambitions to match their group. In adulthood people usually settle somewhere between the two, keeping the family's underlying values while having acquired much of their manner and outlook from their peers. It's not a clean split, though; a strong personality can resist both influences and stay fairly constant from childhood onward.

Key words and phrases

to match their group
to behave like the people around them
a strong personality can resist both influences
someone with strong character can avoid both effects

Do you think daughters are always more similar to their mothers than to male relatives?

Simple Model Answer

No. A daughter can resemble her father or another male relative in appearance or personality. Mothers and daughters may copy each other's habits if they spend a lot of time together, but that is not a rule. The person providing most early care may have a stronger influence, regardless of whether they are male or female.

Key words and phrases

resemble
Look or behave like someone else.

Advanced Model Answer

No, and I think that assumption comes from expectation rather than observation. Genetics doesn't work that way — a daughter can easily inherit her father's temperament or her grandfather's build. What may create an appearance of similarity is that mothers and daughters often spend more time together, so mannerisms get copied. But plenty of families would tell you that the daughter is unmistakably like her father, and nobody in those families finds it surprising. The one place the stereotype has some basis is early childcare, since whoever spends the most hours with a baby shapes it a little more than the other parent does.

Key words and phrases

expectation rather than observation
what someone assumes will happen rather than what they actually see
unmistakably like her father
clearly resembles her father
