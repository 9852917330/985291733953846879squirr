Transport

Getting to work, and how transport has improved

Part 3

Prompt

Do you think getting to work is easier or more difficult than it used to be? How have methods of transport improved? Are there any problems that have been created by new transport systems?

Do you think getting to work is easier or more difficult than it used to be?

Simple Model Answer

It depends on the place. New metro lines and live travel information can make commuting easier to plan. However, traffic has grown and cities have spread, so many people travel further than their parents did. My impression is that planning tools have improved while the journey itself may still take longer, especially where transport investment has not kept up.

Key words and phrases

commuting
Regular travel between home and work or study.

Advanced Model Answer

It depends almost entirely on where you live. In cities with new metro lines it's genuinely easier — you can check live times, plan a route on your phone and know within a few minutes when you'll arrive, which removed a lot of the old uncertainty. But congestion has grown faster than the infrastructure in most places, and cities have spread outwards, so people now commute much further than their parents did. My honest impression is that the technology improved while the journey itself got longer.

Key words and phrases

spread outwards
expand into areas farther from the centre

How have methods of transport improved?

Simple Model Answer

Vehicles have better safety features, such as airbags and automatic braking, and many are becoming cleaner. Digital tickets, route planning and live information also reduce uncertainty about a journey. Comfort may not have improved as quickly, but knowing how to travel and when to arrive is much easier. I think safety and coordination are the biggest gains.

Key words and phrases

coordination
Organising different parts so they work well together.

Advanced Model Answer

Safety is the clearest one — cars have airbags, crumple zones and automatic braking, and road deaths have fallen substantially in most developed countries despite far more traffic. Vehicles are also cleaner, with electric buses replacing diesel in a lot of cities. The less visible improvement is coordination: digital ticketing, live arrival information and route planning have made journeys predictable in a way they simply were not twenty years ago. Comfort hasn't improved anywhere near as fast, but on the essentials, transport keeps getting better.

Key words and phrases

predictable in a way they simply were not
became reliable in a way they were not before

Are there any problems that have been created by new transport systems?

Simple Model Answer

Yes. More roads can encourage extra driving instead of permanently reducing traffic. Large roads may also divide neighbourhoods, often affecting poorer communities. Ride-hailing can add vehicles and draw people away from buses. So a system that seems convenient for one journey may create wider problems. Governments need to examine the overall effects, not only individual travel times.

Key words and phrases

Ride-hailing
Using an app or service to request a car and driver.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, and some are counter-intuitive. Building new roads tends to generate additional traffic rather than relieving it, because easier driving encourages more of it — engineers call this induced demand. Major roads also cut neighbourhoods in half, and the communities affected are usually the poorest. Ride-hailing has had a similar effect, adding vehicles to congested streets while drawing passengers away from public transport. So a project that looks like progress on paper — a wider road, a new app — can still leave a city worse off once you look at the wider effects rather than just journey times.

Key words and phrases

induced demand
extra travel caused when more road space is provided
drawing passengers away from public transport
causing people to stop using buses or trains
looks like progress on paper
appears successful in official plans or statistics
