Crime & Punishment

Prison or community service?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people believe that all offenders should be sent to prison, while others argue that there are more effective alternatives for those who commit minor crimes, such as community service. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Prisons in most countries hold more people than they were built for, and a large share of those people are serving short sentences for minor offences. My own view is that imprisonment should be reserved for the dangerous, with community penalties used for the rest.

The argument for imprisoning everyone rests on consistency and deterrence. If minor offences bring no serious consequence, the law appears optional, and victims of theft or vandalism reasonably feel that a few hours of unpaid work does not answer what was done to them. Prison also removes the offender from the streets, which is the one thing that stops the behaviour for as long as the sentence lasts.

The case for alternatives is supported by what happens afterwards. Short sentences are long enough to cost a person their job, their housing and often their family, and short enough to teach them nothing, so they leave with fewer reasons to stay honest than when they arrived. Prison also functions as an education in crime supplied at public expense, since a first-time offender spends months among experienced ones. Reoffending rates after community sentences are consistently lower, and the cost is a fraction of a prison place.

Community penalties also do something imprisonment cannot: they keep the offender working and paying tax, allow treatment for addiction or mental illness where that is the real cause, and let the debt be repaid visibly to the neighbourhood that was harmed. The essential condition is that they are supervised strictly enough to be taken seriously.

In conclusion, prison remains necessary for violent and repeat offenders, and no system can do without it. For minor crimes it is expensive, damaging and largely ineffective, so penalties that keep people in work and under supervision protect the public more successfully.

Key words and phrases

for as long as the sentence lasts
Throughout the entire punishment period.
an education in crime supplied at public expense
Learning criminal skills in a taxpayer-funded prison.
supervised strictly enough to be taken seriously
Monitored closely enough for the punishment to matter.
