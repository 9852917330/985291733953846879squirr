Transport

Should people be encouraged into cities?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

The best way to solve traffic and transport problems is to encourage people to live in cities rather than in suburbs or the countryside. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Suburban living generates far more travel per household than city living does, which makes concentrating the population look like an obvious remedy. I partly agree, since density genuinely reduces the need to travel, but calling it the best solution overstates what it can achieve.

The transport case for density is strong. A city dweller can walk to a shop, cycle to work or take a train that runs every few minutes, because enough passengers exist to justify the service, whereas a suburb of detached houses cannot support a bus route at all. Journeys are also shorter: the same trip that takes twenty minutes by car in a spread-out area takes five on foot in a dense one. Fewer vehicles means fewer emissions and accidents, and the land saved from roads and car parks can be used for housing.

The proposal has serious limits, however. People live in suburbs largely because city housing is expensive and cramped, so encouraging a move without building affordable family homes in the centre simply pushes prices higher and moves the problem. Cities also become congested in their own way, and a district that gains residents without gaining transport capacity exchanges long journeys for crowded ones. Not everyone can move in any case, since farming, tourism and much industry are located where they are, and emptying rural areas has consequences of its own.

More effective measures work on the journeys rather than the addresses. Building homes near stations, allowing shops and offices within residential districts, improving rural bus services and letting people work remotely for part of the week all shorten travel without requiring anybody to relocate.

In conclusion, denser cities do reduce transport demand and deserve encouragement through planning and housing policy. Density alone will not solve the problem, so it belongs among several measures rather than ahead of them.

Key words and phrases

building affordable family homes in the centre
Providing reasonably priced central housing for families.
exchanges long journeys for crowded ones
Replaces lengthy travel with overcrowded transport.
shorten travel without requiring anybody to relocate
Reduce journey times without making people move home.
