Technology & Science

Space research or living standards on Earth?

Opinion

Prompt

Millions of dollars are spent on space research every year. Some people argue that the money should be spent on improving living standards on Earth. Do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Space research requires enormous public investment, while many people still lack adequate housing, healthcare and education. Although these social needs deserve priority, I do not agree that governments should abandon space programmes. A limited and carefully directed budget can produce direct practical benefits on Earth and support valuable long-term research.

The strongest argument against space spending is opportunity cost. Money used for a distant mission cannot be spent on hospitals, schools or clean water. In poorer countries, these services can save lives immediately, whereas the benefits of space exploration may seem uncertain or remote. Governments should therefore avoid prestige projects that consume large sums without a clear scientific or public purpose.

However, space research is not simply an expensive adventure. Satellite technology supports weather forecasting, navigation, communications and disaster management. It helps farmers monitor crops, allows emergency services to track storms and gives scientists reliable evidence about climate change. Research carried out for space missions has also contributed to advances in materials, computing and medicine. These benefits are shared far beyond the space industry.

Space science is also a long-term investment. Satellites have recorded polar ice and sea temperatures for more than forty years, and a continuous record of that kind cannot be created once a crisis has already begun; the expertise and international cooperation behind such programmes take decades to build. Missions should still be reviewed independently, so that governments can show what each one delivers when it competes with pressing social needs.

In conclusion, basic living standards should receive the largest share of public money, but this does not make space research wasteful. A balanced funding model can meet urgent needs while preserving a programme that delivers practical technology and scientific knowledge.

avoid prestige projects

A balanced funding model
