Situation

A positive change that you made to your life

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a positive change that you made to your life.

You should say: - When it happened - Where it happened - What the change was And explain how you have benefited from this change (or, explain how you feel about this change)

Simple Model Answer

A positive change I made was moving from real estate into teaching. About six months ago, I was still working as a property agent. I had done that job for around a year and a half, but I realised it did not suit me.

I became interested in teaching IELTS and began studying the exam. At first, the amount of material felt overwhelming, so I asked friends for help. One friend had the same goal, and we decided to prepare together. We set study rules and encouraged each other to keep working. After about two months, we took the test on the same day.

We are now both working as IELTS teachers. I still need to learn more about lesson planning and teaching methods, but I enjoy the work much more than my previous job. It gives me opportunities to improve my own knowledge while helping students. Changing careers involved uncertainty, yet I feel it was the right decision. I learned that progress in a job is not enough if I cannot imagine being happy doing it for years.

Key words and phrases

overwhelming
Feeling too large or difficult to manage comfortably.
Changing careers
Moving into a different type of work.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to tell you about a life-changing decision I made a while back: switching my entire career path. About six months ago I was still working as a real-estate agent. I'd been in that job for about a year and a half and was making decent progress, but the more I got into it, the more I realised it just didn't suit me. When I pictured myself five years down the line still doing the same thing, I honestly couldn't see myself being happy, so that's why I quit and decided to try my luck in a completely unrelated field: teaching.

As I looked into it, I discovered that teaching IELTS in Hanoi could actually offer quite a lucrative income, so I started learning absolutely everything I could about the exam. It was arduous at first, I felt overwhelmed by all the self-study materials I found online, and I knew straight away that I couldn't manage it alone, so I asked a few friends for help. One of them turned out to want exactly the same thing, so we ended up studying together.

Mainly what we did was keep each other motivated and disciplined: we set out a few ground rules, and whoever broke one had to face some kind of harsh punishment, like buying the other coffee for a week. After about two months of that, we sat the test on the same day, and now we're both working as IELTS teachers.

Of course, as a new teacher I still have to figure a lot of things out, and I've had to do a lot of research to build up a deep understanding of different teaching methods, but in retrospect, I'd say I made the right move. The job's a lot of fun, and it's opened doors to plenty of opportunities for self-improvement, so all in all, this has genuinely been a positive change in my life.
