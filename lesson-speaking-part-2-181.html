Situation

An achievement that you are proud of

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an achievement that you are proud of.

You should say: - What you do; - When, where, and how you do it; - How difficult it is; - Why you are proud of it.

Simple Model Answer

An achievement I am proud of is passing my university entrance exam. Earlier in high school, I was not a particularly hardworking student. I mainly wanted to finish school, but my final-year Literature teacher changed my attitude.

Her lessons made me interested in learning and encouraged me to consider becoming a teacher. I decided to apply to the University of Education, although my earlier results meant I would need to improve. The entrance exam covered Maths, Literature and English. Languages were easier for me, but Maths required a lot of practice.

I completed extra algebra exercises and often asked teachers to explain problems. Some evenings, I felt tired and wanted to stop, but their encouragement helped me continue. When I learned that I had been accepted, I felt extremely happy. The result mattered, but I was also proud of changing my habits and working towards a difficult goal. It showed me that my earlier performance did not have to decide my future. With effort and help, I could move towards something I genuinely wanted.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about one of my personal achievements that I take pride in - passing my university entrance exam. It's known to be nerve-wracking and pretty challenging, especially if you're hoping to get into one of the more prestigious schools.

To be honest, back in high school I was not much of a diligent student. I didn't really appreciate hard work or self-improvement - all I wanted was to graduate and be done with it. But in my final year, I had a Literature teacher who completely changed that. The way she taught each lesson was genuinely thought-provoking, and she sort of woke up this desire in me to actually improve myself. Around the same time, I'd also started thinking seriously about becoming a teacher myself one day.

I knew it wouldn't be easy for someone with my academic record to get into the University of Education, but once I'd set my heart on something, I tend to go to great lengths to get it. The exam covered three core subjects - Maths, Literature and English. Languages came naturally to me, but Maths definitely didn't, so I ended up studying around the clock, doing extra algebra exercises and constantly going to my teachers with questions. There were plenty of evenings when I was exhausted and ready to give up, but their encouragement kept me going.

In the end, the hard work paid off. I was over the moon when I found out I'd been accepted. I was proud of myself, not just for the result, but for the determination it took to step out of my comfort zone and make my dream come true.
