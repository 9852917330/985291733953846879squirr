Entering competitions

Part 3

Prompt

Do you enjoy entering competitions? Have you entered any other competitions?

Do you enjoy entering competitions?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, when a competition tests a skill and helps me improve. A deadline makes me prepare more seriously. For example, a photography competition once encouraged me to practise every weekend. I am less interested in events decided by luck because there is little to learn. Improving matters more to me than my final position.

Key words and phrases

deadline
The time by which something must be finished.

Advanced Model Answer

I do, provided the competition tests a genuine skill and gives you something back for entering. What I like most is the deadline, because it forces a level of preparation I'd never manage on my own — I once signed up for a local photography competition and it was the only thing that got me out shooting every weekend for a month. I'm much less keen on events decided largely by luck, a prize draw, say, since there's nothing to learn from the result either way. For me the point should be that you're better afterwards than you were before, not simply where you finished.

Key words and phrases

tests a genuine skill
checks an ability a person actually needs
better afterwards than you were before
having improved because of the experience

Have you entered any other competitions?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, I entered school competitions such as essay writing and public speaking. I did not often win, but preparing taught me to organise my time, handle pressure and learn from mistakes. Those skills have been more useful than the results themselves, although winning occasionally would also have been nice.

Key words and phrases

handle pressure
Stay effective when a situation is demanding or stressful.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, mostly academic and language competitions when I was at school — essay writing and public speaking, that sort of thing. Honestly, I didn't win very often, but the preparation was where the real value lay. I learned to plan my time properly, to perform under pressure, and to look at a poor result and work out what actually went wrong instead of simply feeling discouraged. Those habits turned out to matter far more than any of the results ever did, though I'll admit it would've been nice to win occasionally too.

Key words and phrases

plan my time properly
organise how I use my time well
perform under pressure
do a task well despite stress
work out what actually went wrong
understand the true cause of the problem
feeling discouraged
losing confidence or enthusiasm
