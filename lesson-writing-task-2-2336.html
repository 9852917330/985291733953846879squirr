Sport & Leisure

Traditional games against modern ones

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people think traditional games are better than modern games in helping children develop their abilities. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Skipping ropes and hide-and-seek have been replaced in most childhoods by consoles and phones. I partly agree that the older games develop a child more completely, but the advantage lies in how they are played rather than in their age.

Traditional games teach through the conditions they impose. Children playing outside must agree the rules themselves, settle disputes without a referee and include whoever turns up, which is where negotiation and fairness are actually learned. The games involve running, climbing and balancing, so physical development happens without anyone calling it exercise. Simple equipment also forces invention, since a stick becomes several different things, and boredom between games pushes children into making something up.

Modern games are stronger than critics allow, however. Complex titles require planning, resource management and rapid decisions under pressure, and children who play together online must coordinate with people they cannot see, which is a genuine social skill. Games are also the entry point through which many children discover coding and design, and they include those whose disability or geography rules out the playground.

The real difference is the amount of freedom involved. A traditional game is shaped by the children playing it; a commercial game is shaped by designers who decide the goals, the rewards and when the next one arrives. That is why the modern version is easier to enjoy and harder to stop, and why it develops fewer of the abilities that come from having to organise the fun yourself.

In conclusion, traditional games do more for physical health, social negotiation and imagination, so the preference for them is justified. Modern games train useful skills of their own, and the sensible aim is a childhood containing both rather than nostalgia for one of them.

Key words and phrases

pushes children into making something up
Encourages children to invent their own activity.
easier to enjoy and harder to stop
Offers easy pleasure but is difficult to leave.
having to organise the fun yourself
Needing to create your own entertainment.
nostalgia for one of them
Affectionate longing for one older approach.
