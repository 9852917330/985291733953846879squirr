Environment & Nature

Water transport compared with other forms

Part 3

Prompt

How does water transport compare with other forms of transport? What are the advantages and disadvantages of water transport? How important is it for a town or city to be located near a river or the sea?

How does water transport compare with other forms of transport?

Simple Model Answer

Water transport is much slower than flying but usually cheaper for heavy goods. That makes it useful for large shipments that are not urgent. It is less flexible than road transport because boats depend on ports and rivers. Goods often need a truck for the final journey, so shipping is not ideal for direct, fast deliveries.

Key words and phrases

shipments
Large amounts of goods sent from one place to another.

Advanced Model Answer

It occupies a specific niche. It's far slower than air freight — weeks rather than hours — but the cost per tonne is a fraction, which is why almost all international trade in bulk goods goes by sea. Compared with road transport it's much less flexible, because you're tied to ports and navigable rivers, and something else has to cover the final leg of the journey to the actual destination. So it suits bulk, low-value cargo far better than anything urgent or door-to-door.

Key words and phrases

the cost per tonne is a fraction
moving each tonne costs only a small share as much
something else has to cover the final leg
another type of transport must finish the journey

What are the advantages and disadvantages of water transport?

Simple Model Answer

Ships carry huge loads using less fuel per tonne than many smaller road vehicles, and they reduce lorry traffic. However, they are slow and follow limited routes. They can also cause serious pollution, especially through fuel emissions or oil spills, while ports may damage coastal habitats. So being efficient does not mean water transport has no environmental costs.

Key words and phrases

fuel emissions
Polluting gases released when fuel is burned.

Advanced Model Answer

The advantages are capacity and fuel efficiency: a single container ship carries what would otherwise need thousands of lorries, using far less fuel per tonne, and it keeps freight traffic off the roads. The disadvantages are speed, inflexible routes and environmental risk, since an oil spill causes damage lasting decades and building ports destroys coastal habitat. Shipping also burns some of the dirtiest fuel available, which is only now being regulated properly — it's not that the industry can't clean itself up, it simply hasn't had much incentive to until now.

Key words and phrases

building ports destroys coastal habitat
constructing harbours harms natural areas beside the sea
some of the dirtiest fuel available
one of the fuels that creates the most pollution

How important is it for a town or city to be located near a river or the sea?

Simple Model Answer

It used to be extremely important because water provided trade routes and a supply of fresh water. Roads and air transport have reduced that dependence, although rivers and coasts still support tourism and industry. However, flooding is a growing risk for some coastal cities. A location that once created wealth may now require expensive protection.

Key words and phrases

dependence
The need to rely on someone or something.

Advanced Model Answer

Historically it was decisive, since almost every major old city sits on water because that was the only practical way to trade and to obtain fresh water. Today it matters far less for transport, as roads and air freight have reduced the dependence, but it hasn't disappeared — river and coastal cities still do well out of tourism and industry. The complication is climate: coastal cities now face flooding, so the location that created them has become a liability, and today's advantage can quietly turn into tomorrow's expensive problem.

Key words and phrases

reduced the dependence
made people less reliant on it
