A person who has apologised to you

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a person who has apologised to you

You should say: when it was who the person was why this person apologised and explain how you felt about it.

OUTLINE

Simple Model Answer

Someone who apologised to me was my friend Duy. Last month, he borrowed my car for a day. I care a lot about my car, so I asked him to drive carefully. He promised that he would.

When he returned that evening, he looked upset. He explained that another driver had hit the back of my car in traffic. This pushed it into the vehicle in front. He said sorry several times and offered to take it to a mechanic the next morning.

At first, I felt unhappy about the damage. However, I understood that Duy had not caused the accident, and his apology was clearly honest. The next day, he came with me to get the car repaired. Luckily, the damage was not serious or very expensive. I accepted his apology, and we stayed friends. This experience reminded me that accidents can happen even when someone tries to be careful. Being honest and helping to solve the problem matter more than simply getting angry.

Key words and phrases

accepted his apology
Agreed to forgive him after he said sorry.
solve the problem
Find a way to deal with something that has gone wrong.

Advanced Model Answer

I think we all make mistakes now and then in life, sometimes on purpose and sometimes completely by accident, but we usually end up regretting them afterwards. I always try to apologise when I've done something wrong, and naturally, I expect the same from other people.

So, last month, my friend Duy borrowed my car for the day. He knows perfectly well how possessive I am about it, so I asked him to take good care of it, especially since he's not the greatest driver — it's actually the reason his own dad won't buy him a car of his own.

He promised he'd drive carefully, but when he brought it back that evening he looked really down. He told me someone had hit the car from behind while he was stuck in traffic, which pushed him straight into the car in front. He felt terrible about it and apologised over and over, and he promised he'd get it fixed first thing the next morning.

I did feel sorry for him, though I was also pretty gutted about the damage, but somewhere in the back of my mind I knew it wasn't really his fault. I usually lose my temper fairly easily, but his apology was so sincere that I just couldn't stay angry with him for long.

The next day, he came with me to the mechanic, and luckily the damage wasn't too bad or expensive to fix. He apologised again and promised he'd be more careful in future. We're still good friends now, and honestly, I think receiving an apology and being willing to forgive someone is one of the best ways to keep a friendship strong.
