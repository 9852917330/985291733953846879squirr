Place

A shopping street you would like to go

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a shopping street you would like to visit.

You should say: What is this place? Where is it situated? Why is it so popular?

Simple Model Answer

A shopping street I would like to visit again is Nguyen Trai Street in District 5 of Ho Chi Minh City. It is about twenty minutes from my home by scooter. I first discovered it during my first year at university.

I had just finished a difficult exam period when my flatmates persuaded me to go out. We walked around without a plan and found the street. At first, I thought the crowds would make me uncomfortable. Instead, I enjoyed the lively atmosphere. There were stalls selling grilled corn and spring rolls beside shops with inexpensive, fashionable clothes.

The street is popular with students because prices are affordable and many places stay open late. I now go only once or twice a year, but I still think of it when I need a break from work or study. Browsing shops and eating street food helps me relax. Next time my younger cousins visit, I would like to take them there and share a place that has given me many enjoyable afternoons.

Key words and phrases

lively atmosphere
A feeling of activity, energy and enjoyment in a place.
Browsing shops
Looking around shops without necessarily planning to buy something.

Advanced Model Answer

Right, today I want to talk about Nguyen Trai Street, one of the busiest shopping streets in Ho Chi Minh City.

It's in District 5, about twenty minutes by scooter from where I live. I first went there years ago, in my first year at university, right after a brutal exam period. A couple of my flatmates practically dragged me out of the house to chill out and clear my head, and we ended up just wandering around the neighbourhood with no real plan — that's how we stumbled on it.

Honestly, I wasn't expecting much. I'd always found busy, crowded streets a bit overwhelming, so I assumed I'd want to leave after ten minutes. It turned out to be the opposite — there were stalls selling grilled corn and fried spring rolls right next to little shops full of cheap, trendy clothes, and the whole street had this buzzing, festival-like energy I hadn't expected at all. I think it's especially popular with students, since everything's so cheap and most stalls stay open really late.

These days I don't get there anywhere near as often as I'd like — maybe once or twice a year — but whenever exams or work get on top of me, it's still the first place that comes to mind to relieve everyday stress. There's something about eating street food and browsing stalls with no agenda that clears my head better than anything else. I'd really like to take my younger cousins there next time they visit, actually, just to see it through their eyes.
