Food & Diet

Should everyone be a vegetarian?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people think everyone should be a vegetarian, because we do not need to eat meat to have a healthy diet. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes claimed that people should give up meat altogether, on the grounds that a healthy diet does not require it. While I accept that most of us eat far more meat than we need, I disagree with the idea that everyone should become a vegetarian.

There are certainly good arguments for eating less meat. Livestock farming uses enormous quantities of land, water and grain, and produces a significant share of global greenhouse gas emissions. There is also strong medical evidence linking high consumption of red and processed meat with heart disease and certain cancers. A diet based mainly on vegetables, grains and pulses is cheaper, and it is perfectly possible to obtain enough protein from beans, nuts and dairy products.

Nevertheless, a universal rule would be neither practical nor fair. Meat and fish remain the most reliable sources of iron and vitamin B12, and deficiencies are common among people who remove them without careful planning. This is a particular risk for children, pregnant women and families in poorer countries, where the range of alternative foods is limited. Food is also central to culture, and asking every community to abandon dishes that have been prepared for centuries would be an unreasonable interference in private life.

In my opinion, the sensible response is moderation rather than prohibition. Governments should educate the public about the health and environmental costs of excessive meat consumption, and make plant-based options widely available, while leaving the final decision to the individual.

In conclusion, I agree that we should all eat less meat, but I disagree that vegetarianism should be required of everyone.

Key words and phrases

a universal rule
a rule that applies equally to everyone
deficiencies are common
Many people lack necessary nutrients.
abandon dishes that have been prepared for centuries
Stop making foods with long cultural histories.
an unreasonable interference
An unjustified intrusion into people's choices.
moderation rather than prohibition
Keeping quantities sensible instead of banning consumption.
