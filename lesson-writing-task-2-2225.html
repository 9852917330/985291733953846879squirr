Sport & Leisure

School sport or professional performance?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Government money should be used to support children in school for sports rather than to support professional sports and arts that perform for the general public. To what extent do you agree?

Model answer

Public budgets for sport and culture are limited, and it is sometimes argued that all of the money should go to school children rather than to professionals who perform for an audience. I agree that school provision deserves priority, but not that professional funding should be withdrawn entirely.

The case for schools is strongest on grounds of reach. Money spent on a stadium benefits the few thousand who attend, whereas money spent on school pitches, equipment and coaching reaches every child in the country, including those whose families could never pay for a club membership. The health argument reinforces this. Habits formed before the age of twelve tend to last, so a child who learns to swim or enjoys running is far less likely to become an inactive adult, and the saving to health services from that single change is difficult to overstate.

School sport also does work that professional sport cannot. Losing a match and returning to training the following week teaches persistence, and a mixed-ability team teaches children to depend on people who are not like them. These lessons come from taking part rather than watching.

I would not go as far as the statement, however. Professional sport and the arts are what give children something to aim at, and a national orchestra or athletics squad also supports a wider network of teachers, coaches and technicians whose skills eventually reach schools. Removing that funding would weaken the base as well as the top.

In conclusion, I largely agree that children in school should come first, because participation delivers more health and social value per pound than performance does. The right approach is to shift the balance decisively towards schools while keeping enough professional support to give young people something to aspire to.

Key words and phrases

difficult to overstate
So important that exaggerating its value is difficult.
taking part rather than watching
Actively participating instead of being a spectator.
weaken the base as well as the top
Harm ordinary participation as well as elite performance.
give young people something to aspire to
Provide an inspiring goal for young people.
