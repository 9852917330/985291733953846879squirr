Situation

A decision that you disagreed with

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a decision that you disagreed with

You should say: Who made this decision What it was How you told him/her And explain why you disagreed with the decision

OUTLINE

>>> 1st argument w/ parents over a sleepover
important milestone in life, 1st real argument w/ parents, mummy's boy, strict mother
best friend's birthday sleepover invite, mother said no straight away
raised voice at mother for 1st time, stood ground, felt old enough be responsible for self
disagreed bc treated like a kid when friends allowed, mother came round, let me go
taught me to speak up for myself calmly but firmly

Simple Model Answer

A decision I disagreed with was my mother's refusal to let me attend a friend's birthday sleepover. This happened about three weeks ago. I was sixteen and had never stayed overnight at a friend's home because my parents were very protective.

When my best friend invited me, I was excited. However, my mother immediately said no. I became frustrated and raised my voice, telling her I was old enough to look after myself for one night. She was surprised and initially became even stricter.

Afterwards, I explained why I thought the decision was unfair. My friends were allowed to attend, and I wanted a chance to show that I could behave responsibly. Eventually, she understood my point and gave permission. I was pleased, but I also recognised that shouting had not helped. The experience mattered because it was one of the first times I clearly expressed a different opinion to my parents. It taught me that speaking up is important, but doing so calmly makes it easier for someone else to listen.

Key words and phrases

sleepover
A visit where someone stays overnight at a friend's home.
speaking up
Expressing your opinion instead of remaining silent.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about an important milestone in my life, which was the first real argument I ever had with my parents. I've always been a bit of a mummy's boy, especially with my mother, who's definitely the stricter of the two. She's so direct that it's usually her way or the highway, and she's always been incredibly protective of me, to the point where she'd never once let me stay over at a friend's house.

This all came to a head about three weeks ago, when my best friend invited me to his birthday sleepover. I was thrilled, it would have been my first time staying at a friend's place overnight, but my excitement didn't last long, because the moment I mentioned it to my mum, she said no straight away.

Filled with frustration, I actually raised my voice at her for the first time in my life and said, "Mum, I'm sixteen, not six, I can look after myself for one night." She was so taken aback by my tone that she told me off even harder at first, but I stood my ground. I felt it was about time I told her how I really felt, that I was old enough to be responsible for myself and that she needed to loosen the reins a little.

I disagreed with her decision simply because I didn't think it was fair to treat me like a little kid when all my friends were already allowed to do this kind of thing. In the end, she understood where I was coming from and let me go. I'll never forget that evening, not just because it was the night I finally stood up to my mother, but because it taught me that speaking up for myself, calmly but firmly, is something I still need to work on.

Key words and phrases

an important milestone in my life
a major event showing progress in my life
her way or the highway
she insists things must be done exactly as she wants
protective of me
wanting to keep me safe from harm
stood my ground
refused to change my position despite pressure
be responsible for
have the duty to take care of
