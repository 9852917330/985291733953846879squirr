Object & Favourite

An important letter you received

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an important letter you received.

You should say: - who sent the letter - what it was about - why it was important - and explain how you felt about it.

OUTLINE

>>> grandmother's handwritten letter

sent by post unusual everyone used phone two sides thin paper careful handwriting

Simple Model Answer

An important letter I received was a handwritten one from my grandmother. She sent it a few months before she died. It was unusual because by then our family normally spoke on the phone rather than writing letters.

The letter covered two pages in careful handwriting. Most of it was news about her garden and neighbours. However, the final paragraph offered advice. She said she had noticed how much I worried about decisions and reminded me that situations often change more than we expect.

At first, I read it quickly and put it in a drawer. After she died, I found it again and understood its value differently. It was something she had written herself, in her own words. I felt a little guilty that I had phoned instead of sending a proper reply, but I was grateful to have it. I still keep it in my desk and read it occasionally. It reminds me of her care and of the importance of recording things that someone may need much later.

Key words and phrases

in her own words
Expressed exactly as she chose to say it.
occasionally
Sometimes, but not very often.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a handwritten letter I received from my grandmother, which arrived a few months before she died.

She'd sent it by post, which was unusual even then, because everyone in the family used the phone by that point. The letter itself was quite short — two sides of thin paper in very careful handwriting, because her eyesight was failing. Most of it was ordinary news about the garden and the neighbours, but the last paragraph was different. She wrote that she'd watched me worry over decisions for years, and that in her experience almost nothing turned out to be as permanent as it felt at the time.

It became important for reasons that had nothing to do with what she'd intended. At the time I read it quickly and put it in a drawer. After she died I found it again, and it was suddenly the only thing I had in her own words, rather than just my memory of her voice.

I felt slightly guilty, if I'm honest, because I never replied properly — I phoned instead, which isn't the same thing. But I also felt grateful that she'd bothered. A text would've just disappeared into a phone somewhere, whereas that letter's still in my desk, and I take it out occasionally.

It taught me something practical too: if something matters, write it down, because the person receiving it might need it much later than you'd expect.

Key words and phrases

turned out to be
