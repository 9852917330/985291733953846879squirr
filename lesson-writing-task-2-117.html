Government & Politics

Is international aid a moral obligation?

Discussion

Prompt

The advocates of international aid believe that countries have a moral obligation to help each other, while the opponents consider it unnecessary, because money is misspent by the governments that receive it. Discuss these two points of view and give your opinion.

Model answer

Supporters of international aid argue that wealthy countries have a moral duty to assist poorer ones, while critics point to corruption and waste. Both concerns are valid. In my view, aid remains necessary, but it should be targeted, transparent and designed to build local capacity rather than permanent dependence.

The strongest case for aid arises during emergencies. After war, drought or an earthquake, outside funding can provide food, medicine, clean water and temporary shelter far more quickly than a damaged government can. Longer-term support can also fund vaccination, teacher training and basic infrastructure. These investments save lives and may prevent instability from spreading across borders.

Critics are right that money can be diverted by officials, spent on unsuitable projects or tied to the donor's commercial interests. Large transfers made without local oversight may weaken accountability and encourage governments to rely on external finance. The answer, however, is not to abandon assistance. Donors should publish contracts, audit outcomes, work with reliable local organisations and release funds in stages. Wherever possible, aid should transfer skills and strengthen institutions that can continue the work.

Aid can also affect a government's own incentives. When a large share of the budget arrives as foreign transfers, ministers face less pressure to build effective domestic tax collection, since public spending no longer depends entirely on what citizens are willing to pay. Aid that is tied to measurable progress on revenue collection, rather than simply replacing it, is more likely to leave a government financially self-reliant once the programme ends.

In conclusion, international aid is justified both morally and practically, but its quality matters more than the headline amount. Carefully monitored programmes that respond to local priorities can deliver lasting benefits instead of short-term publicity.

Key words and phrases

strengthen institutions that can continue the work
Improve organisations able to sustain the effort.
