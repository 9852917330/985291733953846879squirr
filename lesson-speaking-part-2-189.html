An occasion where somebody gave you positive suggestions

Part 2

Prompt

Describe an occasion when someone gave you useful suggestions.

You should say: - When you received - Who gave you the suggestions - What you did - Why the suggestions were positive/how you felt.

Simple Model Answer

An occasion when someone gave me useful suggestions was in my final university year about three years ago. My dissertation deadline was a month away, but I had hardly started. I kept telling myself I would manage at the last moment.

My best friend recognised the problem because he was working on a similar topic. He suggested dividing the dissertation into smaller sections and giving each one its own deadline. For example, I could complete the review of previous research first, then move to the methods section. He also offered to check my progress every few days.

I followed the plan, and it changed how the task felt. Instead of one enormous job, I had smaller pieces that seemed possible to finish. I submitted on time and graduated successfully. I still use the same method at work when a large project feels difficult to begin. I am grateful because his advice was practical and supportive rather than simply critical. He helped me change a habit that could have caused a serious problem, and the benefit continued after university.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the time my best friend gave me some genuinely positive suggestions about how to beat procrastination, advice that, looking back, probably saved my degree.

This was about three years ago, in my final year at university, right before my dissertation was due. I had a serious procrastination problem back then, my Word document was still completely blank even though the deadline was only a month away. Weirdly, I wasn't even that worried about it; I was overconfident, telling myself I'd somehow pull it together at the last minute, the way I always had with smaller assignments.

Luckily, my best friend, who was doing his dissertation on a similar topic, could see exactly what I was doing and refused to just let it slide. He sat me down one evening and suggested breaking the dissertation down into smaller chunks, with a proper deadline for each section, the literature review one week, the methodology the next, and so on. He even offered to check in with me every few days to make sure I was actually keeping to it.

I did exactly what he suggested, and it honestly transformed how I worked. Instead of one terrifying deadline looming in the distance, I had a series of manageable ones I could actually picture finishing. I ended up submitting everything on time and graduated with flying colours, which felt almost unbelievable given where I'd started. I still use that same approach at work now, breaking big projects into smaller stages with their own deadlines, and it's proved useful again and again. I'm genuinely grateful for that advice, without it, I dread to think how differently things might have turned out.
