Person

A person who knows a lot

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a knowledgeable person.

You should say: - Who the person is; - What the person does; - What kinds of things he knows; - What you learn from him/her.

Simple Model Answer

A person who knows a lot is my grandmother. She is in her late seventies and used to work as a pharmacist. However, her knowledge is not limited to medicine. She can also talk about history, plants and things happening in the world.

She reads the newspaper every morning and always has a book nearby. She also does a crossword every day. What I like is that she does not try to impress people with difficult words. She explains things simply when her knowledge can help. For example, when my mother was ill, she helped us understand the doctor's explanation and think of useful questions to ask.

My grandmother has taught me to stay curious. Whenever I visit, she shares something new from a book or documentary. She believes people should continue learning at every age. Her example encourages me to read something new each week. I admire her because she shows that learning can be a lifelong habit, rather than something that ends after school.

Key words and phrases

stay curious
Keep wanting to learn and understand new things.
a lifelong habit
Something you do regularly throughout your life.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to tell you about my grandmother, who is easily the most knowledgeable person I know. She's in her late seventies now, and she spent most of her working life as a pharmacist, but what strikes me is how much she knows well beyond her own field.

She reads constantly — newspapers in the morning, then whatever book she's currently working through, and she still does the crossword every single day without fail. Ask her about history, plants, traditional medicine or even current politics, and she'll usually have a well-informed opinion rather than just a vague guess.

What I find most impressive is that she never shows off about it. She only brings up what she knows when it's actually useful, like when my mother was ill and my grandmother could explain exactly what the doctor's diagnosis meant and which questions we should ask.

I've learned a huge amount from her over the years, mostly about staying curious. She still says that the day you stop learning is the day you start getting old, and she genuinely lives by that. Whenever I visit, she'll have some new fact or story ready, usually something she picked up from a book or a documentary, and she always explains it in a way that's easy to follow rather than talking down to you.

Honestly, she's the reason I still try to read something new every week myself. Knowledge like hers doesn't come from one qualification; it comes from decades of genuine curiosity about the world.

Key words and phrases

have a well-informed opinion
hold a view based on enough reliable information
the day you stop learning
the point when you no longer try to learn
talking down to you
speaking as though the other person is less intelligent
genuine curiosity about the world
a sincere desire to understand the world
