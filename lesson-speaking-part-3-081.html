Sports & Exercise

Exercise and health, and compulsory sport at school

Part 3

Prompt

How can regular exercise improve your health? Should schools make sport compulsory? How have the types of exercise people do changed in recent years?

How can regular exercise improve your health?

Simple Model Answer

Regular exercise supports the heart, muscles and bones, and can help sleep and mood. These mental benefits matter as well as physical fitness. The key is keeping it up over time, rather than training extremely hard for a short period and stopping. A manageable activity done regularly is more useful than an ambitious plan someone cannot maintain.

Key words and phrases

keeping it up
Continuing an activity or habit over time.

Advanced Model Answer

Exercise improves health in two ways, and people usually only think about one of them. Physically, it's well documented — it strengthens the heart and circulation, preserves muscle and bone density, and cuts the risk of diabetes and several cancers quite substantially. What people underestimate is the mental side: it's one of the most effective treatments there is for mild anxiety, and it improves sleep, which then affects everything else you do that day. The catch is that it's consistency rather than intensity that actually delivers these benefits — someone who walks briskly most days for years ends up healthier than someone who trains hard for a month and then burns out and gives up.

Key words and phrases

consistency rather than intensity
doing something regularly rather than very intensely once
burns out and gives up
becomes too tired to continue and stops

Should schools make sport compulsory?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, to some extent, because children who most need activity might otherwise avoid it. However, schools should offer more than competitive team games. Swimming, cycling or dance may appeal to different pupils. Requiring movement is easier to support when children have a reasonable choice and do not feel forced into a sport they hate.

Key words and phrases

appeal to
Seem interesting or attractive to someone.

Advanced Model Answer

I think so, up to a point. Left to choose, the children who most need physical activity are exactly the ones who'll opt out, so making it compulsory reaches them. What I'd change is the range on offer, though. Traditional school sport means competitive team games, which suits maybe half the pupils and actively puts off the rest. If schools offered swimming, cycling or dance as alternatives too, compulsion would be far easier to justify.

Key words and phrases

opt out
choose not to take part
far easier to justify
much simpler to explain as reasonable

How have the types of exercise people do changed in recent years?

Simple Model Answer

More people choose individual activities such as gym training, running or yoga because these fit changing work schedules. Apps and watches also make progress easy to measure. That motivates some people but makes others worry too much about numbers. Since work now involves more sitting, exercise often needs to be planned rather than happening naturally during the day.

Key words and phrases

work schedules
The times and arrangements for when people work.

Advanced Model Answer

Exercise has become much more individual and much more measured. Gym training, running and yoga have grown at the expense of organised team sports, largely because they fit around unpredictable working hours in a way a Tuesday-night five-a-side match doesn't. Apps and watches have added a tracking element that didn't exist before, which motivates some people and makes others slightly obsessive about the numbers. The underlying reason is that work has become sedentary, so movement now has to be deliberately scheduled rather than happening naturally.

Key words and phrases

a tracking element
a feature that records progress or location
deliberately scheduled
planned in advance on purpose
