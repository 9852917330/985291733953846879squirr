#019
Health

Teen smoking in Britain

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The table below describes the proportion of smokers in males and females (between the age of 15 and 20) in Britain. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer

The table shows the proportions of males and females aged between 15 and 20 who were smokers in Britain over a 30-year period.

It is clear from the table that the 15-to-20-year-old population in Britain had a much higher percentage of smokers by the end of the last century than in the early 1970s.

In 1970, smoking was not very popular among youngsters aged 15 to 20, with only 10 per cent of males and 5 per cent of females smoking. The following decade saw a dramatic change: the smoking rate among males leapt by nearly 13 percentage points every five years to reach 35% in 1980, compared with slower growth among females, whose rate rose by approximately 8 points every five years to 22% in 1980.

Over the period between 1980 and 1990, smoking remained popular among young males, accounting for over a third of the 15-to-20 male population, while young females showed a growing interest in smoking, pushing their smoking rate first to 34% in 1985 and then to 37% in 1990, even overtaking their male counterparts.

Over the final years of the last century, smoking became less common in the 15-20 age group, both among males and among females. In 2000, an estimated 27% to 28% of people in this age group were reported to be smokers, lower than in previous years, though still more than three times higher than 30 years earlier.

Key phrase meanings

leapt by nearly 13 percentage points
The percentage rose by almost 13 points.
even overtaking their male counterparts
Even exceeding the corresponding male figures.
accounting for over a third of
Forming more than one third.
showed a growing interest in smoking
Became more interested in smoking.
