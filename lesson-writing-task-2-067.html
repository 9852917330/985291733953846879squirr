Education

Broad study or specialisation?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

It is argued that university students should study a full range of subjects instead of specialising in one particular area. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes argued that university students should study a wide range of subjects rather than concentrating on a single field. I partly agree: some breadth is valuable, but depth is what a degree exists to provide.

The case for breadth is reasonable. Employers increasingly want graduates who can do several things, since most jobs now combine tasks that once belonged to separate roles — a marketing position may require statistics, design and writing. Studying different subjects also develops flexible thinking, because each discipline approaches problems differently, and a student who has encountered more than one way of reasoning adapts more easily when their field changes.

Nevertheless, a degree spread across many subjects risks producing graduates who know a little about everything and are qualified for nothing. Serious understanding of any discipline takes years, and the value of a graduate lies precisely in having gone further into a subject than a person could manage alone. Nobody would want a doctor or a structural engineer whose training had been broad but shallow. Employers also value the qualification because it certifies depth; a degree guaranteeing only general familiarity would tell them very little.

The sensible arrangement, in my view, is the one many universities already use: a specialised main subject supported by a smaller number of optional courses outside it. That gives students genuine expertise while ensuring they can write clearly, handle data and understand something of a second field.

In conclusion, I agree that students benefit from studying beyond their own discipline, but this should supplement specialisation rather than replace it.

Key words and phrases

more than one way of reasoning
Several approaches to thinking through problems.
having gone further into a subject
Having studied a topic in greater depth.
it certifies depth
It formally confirms detailed specialist knowledge.
supplement specialisation rather than replace it
Add to expert training without removing it.
