Leisure & Holidays

Leisure time in the past and hobbies in the future

Prompt

Did people have more or less leisure time in the past? Do you think hobbies will change much in the future?

Did people have more or less leisure time in the past?

Simple Model Answer

Many people in my grandparents' generation had less free time. Household jobs took longer without modern appliances, and working six days a week was common. They often used the remaining time simply to rest. However, money made a large difference: wealthy families with paid help could enjoy much more leisure, so the past was not the same for everyone.

Key words and phrases

appliances
Machines used for household tasks, such as washing or cooking.

Advanced Model Answer

When my grandparents were young, I think they generally had less leisure time than we do now. They didn't have the gadgets we take for granted, so ordinary tasks simply took longer — cooking a meal from scratch instead of using a microwave, washing clothes by hand, that sort of thing. Most people also worked six days a week, so whatever free time they had was mostly spent resting rather than doing anything organised. I'd say that's a bit of a generalisation, though, because it depended enormously on money — a wealthier household with servants would have had plenty of leisure even back then.

Key words and phrases

less leisure time
fewer hours free from work
the gadgets we take for granted
devices we use without appreciating how useful they are
plenty of leisure
a large amount of free time

Do you think hobbies will change much in the future?

Simple Model Answer

The basic hobbies may stay similar: people will still cook, garden, play music and enjoy sport. The internet is changing how they learn and share those activities. Someone can now follow a video lesson or show a result to people worldwide. So I expect familiar hobbies to continue, but with different tools and social connections.

Key words and phrases

social connections
Relationships or links with other people.

Advanced Model Answer

No, I don't think hobbies will change all that much in the future — I'm sure people will still play games and sports much as they always have. Where I do think things are shifting is the Internet, which is changing the way we spend our free time. What I suspect will happen is that the old hobbies survive but move partly online: people still cook, garden and play music, but now they learn it from videos and share the results with strangers, which is genuinely new. The hobbies themselves probably won't change; how we learn and show them off will.

Key words and phrases

the old hobbies survive
traditional free-time activities continue to exist
share the results with strangers
show what you made or achieved to people you do not know
