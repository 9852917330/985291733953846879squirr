Object & Favourite

A traditional product in your country

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a traditional product in your country.

You should say: - What it is; - When was the first time you tried it; - How to make it; - How you like it.

Simple Model Answer

A traditional product that is important in Vietnam is rice. It has been part of my diet for as long as I can remember. My mother says I ate soft rice porridge when I was a baby, before I could eat normal family meals.

Rice begins in the fields, where farmers grow and later harvest the plants. At home, we usually wash the grains and put them in a rice cooker with water. My grandmother prefers cooking rice in a pot on the stove because she likes the texture better.

In my family, we eat plain rice with fish, vegetables and other dishes. I enjoy it because it goes well with many different foods. Rice is important beyond our kitchen too. Many families earn their living by growing it, and it appears in traditional celebrations connected with farming and harvests. To me, it represents everyday Vietnamese life. It may seem like a simple food, but it connects family meals, work and traditions across the country.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd love to talk about rice, which is probably the most important agricultural plant in my country. I genuinely can't remember the first time I tried it, because it's the first solid food most Vietnamese babies eat — my mother says I was having soft rice porridge before I could even sit up properly, so it's been part of my life since before I have any real memories.

As for how it's made, it obviously starts in the paddy fields — farmers plant the seedlings by hand, flood the fields, and then harvest and dry the grain months later. At home, though, making it is much simpler: you just wash the rice a couple of times until the water runs clear, then cook it in a rice cooker with the right amount of water. My grandmother always insisted on doing it the traditional way, in a pot on the stove, because she said the rice cooker version never had quite the same texture.

Rice turns up in almost every meal in my house — usually a bowl of plain white rice alongside fish, vegetables and a bit of fish sauce. What I love is that it goes with almost anything; it's simple, but it never gets boring no matter what you serve alongside it.

Beyond the dinner table, though, rice really is woven into the culture here. Millions of families still depend on growing rice to make a living, and Vietnam has become one of the world's biggest rice exporters. A lot of traditional festivals and ceremonies still centre around farmers hoping for a good harvest, and children here grow up eating rice from the moment they're weaned, so it's genuinely one of the strongest symbols of Vietnamese life.
