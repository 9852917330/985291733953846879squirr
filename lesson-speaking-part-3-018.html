Cities

City life against rural life, and making cities easier

Prompt

How is life different in cities compared to rural areas? How do you think life in big cities could be made easier? What do you think cities will be like in 50 years time?

How is life different in cities compared to rural areas?

Simple Model Answer

City life is usually more expensive and competitive, especially for housing and jobs. Rural areas often feel slower and more connected because neighbours know one another. However, they may have fewer hospitals, jobs and reliable internet services. Both places have difficulties; country problems may simply be less visible than those in a crowded city.

Key words and phrases

Rural areas
Places in the countryside, outside towns and cities.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd point to the cost of living first — housing alone is far more expensive in cities than in rural areas, and pretty much everything else costs more too. There's also more competition, for jobs and for space generally, simply because there are more people chasing the same things. Rural life moves a lot slower, people know their neighbours, and there's a sense of community that's harder to find in a big, anonymous city. It's not all one-sided, though — rural areas usually have far fewer services, from hospitals to decent internet, and isolation and lack of opportunity are real problems there too, just quieter ones than city crime.

Key words and phrases

the cost of living
The money needed for everyday essentials.
a sense of community
Feeling connected with people around you.

How do you think life in big cities could be made easier?

Simple Model Answer

Cities need good public transport, green spaces and housing that ordinary workers can afford. These make travel easier and give people somewhere to relax. Better planning can bring homes and jobs closer together. However, major improvements cost a lot and take years, so residents may have to live with imperfect solutions for some time.

Key words and phrases

can afford
Have enough money to pay for something.

Advanced Model Answer

In my opinion cities need to be well-planned, above all. Good public transport can definitely make life easier, because with so many people around, just getting from one side of the city to the other can be genuinely stressful otherwise. Public parks and green spaces matter too, since people need somewhere to actually relax rather than just commute and work. Affordable housing has to be part of it as well, because when ordinary workers can't live anywhere near their jobs, everything else gets harder. None of this is quick or cheap, though — a proper transit system takes decades and enormous budgets to build, so most cities are stuck living with compromises for a long time before things genuinely improve.

Key words and phrases

make life easier
reduce difficulty in everyday living

What do you think cities will be like in 50 years time?

Simple Model Answer

I expect cleaner vehicles and better transport, and perhaps more people working from home. That could reduce some pollution and crowding. However, growing populations may mean that homelessness, unemployment and crime remain problems. Cities may also become hotter, so I think future planning will need to deal with both population growth and heat.

Key words and phrases

unemployment
The situation of people being unable to find work.

Advanced Model Answer

I imagine cities will be less polluted because we'll have electric cars and far better public transport by then. More people might work from home too, so maybe cities won't feel quite so packed during the day. But I think there'll probably still be real problems, because more and more people are migrating to cities in search of work, so I'm not especially optimistic about issues like crime, homelessness and unemployment going away on their own. I'd also expect cities to be a good deal hotter, which is something planners are only just beginning to design for.

Key words and phrases

less polluted
with fewer harmful substances in the air or water
migrating to cities
moving from rural areas to towns and cities
