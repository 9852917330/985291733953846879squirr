The classroom of the future and what makes a good teacher

Prompt

How will classrooms change in the future? What qualities make a good teacher?

How will classrooms change in the future?

Simple Model Answer

I expect more digital tools for routine practice, marking and activities matched to each student's level. Teachers will still matter because many children need guidance and a clear structure. Older, highly motivated students may prefer more independent online study. So classrooms will probably combine technology and personal teaching, with the balance depending on students' ages and needs.

Key words and phrases

guidance
Help and advice about how to do something.

Advanced Model Answer

I expect a mixture rather than a revolution. Digital tools will handle the routine parts — drilling vocabulary, marking exercises, adjusting difficulty to each student — because software can do that better and faster than one teacher with thirty pupils. But the classroom itself won't disappear: remote teaching during the pandemic showed fairly clearly that most children don't learn well without a teacher physically present, and that the weakest students suffer most when that structure disappears. It's not universal, though — a small number of highly motivated older students genuinely thrive with self-paced online courses, so the mix will probably look different by age group.

Key words and phrases

adjusting difficulty to each student
changing the level to suit each learner
the weakest students suffer most
students having the most difficulty experience the greatest harm
self-paced online courses
internet classes students complete at their own speed

What qualities make a good teacher?

Simple Model Answer

A good teacher understands the subject, notices when students are confused and expects effort without making mistakes feel shameful. Students need to feel safe asking questions. These qualities require attention to each person, not just an entertaining personality. That is why class size matters: even an excellent teacher struggles to support too many learners individually.

Key words and phrases

individually
Separately, with attention to each person.

Advanced Model Answer

Three things stand out for me. First, they know the subject properly, because you can only explain something simply if you actually understand it deeply yourself. Second, they notice when a student is lost, which sounds obvious but is surprisingly rare in practice. And third, they set high expectations while making it safe to be wrong — if pupils are afraid of looking foolish, they stop asking questions, and the learning stops right along with it. None of that requires charisma particularly; it requires sustained attention to the individual student, which is exactly why class size matters so much — even the best teacher can't give thirty-five children that kind of attention.

Key words and phrases

notice when a student is lost
see when a learner does not understand
making it safe to be wrong
allowing mistakes without fear of embarrassment
class size matters
the number of students together affects learning
