Leisure & Holidays

Television as relaxation, and leisure against stress

Part 3

Prompt

Is watching television a good way of relaxing? What leisure activities would you recommend as a way of combating stress? Do young people get enough physical exercise these days?

Is watching television a good way of relaxing?

Simple Model Answer

It can be helpful after a tiring day, especially if you choose a programme you enjoy. However, watching deliberately for an hour is different from leaving the TV on all evening. Long, automatic viewing may waste time without leaving you rested. The same problem can happen with phones, so the way we use screens matters more than the device.

Key words and phrases

deliberately
With a clear purpose or conscious choice.

Advanced Model Answer

It can be, particularly after a mentally demanding day when you want something that requires no effort. But I'd distinguish between choosing a programme and just leaving the television on. Deliberate viewing for an hour is genuinely restful, whereas automatic viewing for four hours tends to leave people feeling worse — you've lost the evening and you're not actually rested. The same applies to scrolling on a phone, which has largely replaced it for younger people, so the habit hasn't disappeared, it's just moved to a smaller screen.

Key words and phrases

automatic viewing
watching without actively choosing or paying attention
moved to a smaller screen
changed to watching on a phone or small device

What leisure activities would you recommend as a way of combating stress?

Simple Model Answer

Physical activities such as walking, swimming and cycling can help. Cooking, gardening or music may also work because they hold your attention, while social activities reduce loneliness. The best choice is something you actually enjoy and will repeat. If it feels like another duty, it is less likely to continue when life becomes busy.

Key words and phrases

loneliness
The unhappy feeling of lacking the company or connection you want.

Advanced Model Answer

Anything physical first of all — walking, swimming, cycling — because exercise works on the body's stress response directly rather than merely distracting you from it. Beyond that, I'd add anything that needs enough concentration to occupy your mind, like cooking, gardening or playing an instrument, plus anything social, since isolation makes stress considerably worse. The most important thing, though, is that it's something the person will actually keep doing every week rather than in theory, because an activity that feels like a chore rarely survives a busy week.

Key words and phrases

works on the body's stress response directly
directly changes the body's physical reaction to stress
a chore
an unpleasant duty

Do young people get enough physical exercise these days?

Simple Model Answer

Many do not, but it is not simply because they are lazy. Study, unsafe streets and easy access to screens reduce natural opportunities to move. Schools need to provide activity deliberately. Offering only competitive team games leaves some pupils out, so choices such as dance or climbing may help more young people find something they enjoy.

Key words and phrases

competitive
Involving an effort to win or perform better than others.

Advanced Model Answer

Most don't, and I don't think it's mainly laziness. Study takes longer than it used to, cities aren't really built for walking or cycling safely, and screens are simply more available. The activity that used to happen automatically — walking to school, playing outside — has been designed out of daily life. So it now has to be provided deliberately, and schools that only offer competitive team sports miss most of the pupils who need it. The exception is schools that also offer non-competitive options like dance or climbing — they tend to reach far more of the class.

Key words and phrases

designed out of daily life
removed through the design of modern routines
miss most of the pupils who need it
fail to reach most students who require help
