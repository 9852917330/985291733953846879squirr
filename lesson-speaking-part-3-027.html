Consumerism & Possessions

Valuable possessions and changing attitudes to keeping things

Part 3

Prompt

What are the valuable personal possessions in your country? How has your attitude towards keeping personal possessions changed? Why do you think people keep valuable things?

What are the valuable personal possessions in your country?

Simple Model Answer

For most families, a home or land is the most valuable possession. Gold and jewellery also matter, often as savings or wedding gifts. Vehicles, phones and laptops are useful too. Younger people may value a good computer more than gold because of the work it allows them to do, so priorities differ between generations.

Key words and phrases

priorities
The things people consider most important.

Advanced Model Answer

Property's by far the most valuable thing most families own — a house or a piece of land usually represents a lifetime's savings. Beyond that, gold and jewellery are traditionally important, partly because older generations trust them more than banks, and they're often given as wedding gifts. Vehicles matter too, and increasingly phones and laptops, though those are valued for what they let you do rather than as assets in themselves. That said, younger people often rate a good laptop above gold, so what counts as valuable is starting to split along generational lines.

Key words and phrases

a lifetime's savings
the money saved over an entire lifetime
assets in themselves
valuable things in their own right
split along generational lines
different according to people's age groups

How has your attitude towards keeping personal possessions changed?

Simple Model Answer

I keep fewer things now. When I was younger, I saved almost everything in case it became useful. Moving house showed me how much I never used. Now I mainly keep useful items and things with personal meaning, such as family gifts. I am not perfect, though; I still have an old jacket I cannot quite give away.

Key words and phrases

personal meaning
Importance connected with your own memories or feelings.

Advanced Model Answer

I've become far more selective. When I was younger I kept almost everything, partly on the assumption that it might be useful one day and partly because throwing things away felt wasteful. What changed my mind was moving house a few times and realising how much I was carrying around that I'd never even touched. Now I keep two categories only: things I actually use, and things that carry real personal meaning, like photographs or gifts from family. I'll admit I still break my own rule occasionally — I've kept a jacket I never wear simply because I can't quite part with it.

Key words and phrases

carry real personal meaning
have importance connected to someone's own life
can't quite part with it
cannot quite make myself give it away

Why do you think people keep valuable things?

Simple Model Answer

People may keep valuable things as savings, as family memories or to show success. A family ring, for example, can matter because of who owned it rather than its price. These emotional and financial reasons often overlap. Buying things only to impress others seems less useful to me, especially if the owner never actually uses them.

Key words and phrases

overlap
Be partly connected or have something in common.

Advanced Model Answer

Several reasons, and they're quite different from one another. Some people keep valuables purely as an investment, since gold and property hold their worth when currency doesn't. Others keep family heirlooms, where the point isn't the price at all but the connection to a grandparent or a particular period of family history — my mother still has her mother's wedding ring and would never sell it whatever it was worth. And then there's status: an expensive watch signals success to other people. I'd say the emotional and financial motives are strongest and they often overlap, though buying purely for status, things you'll never actually use, is a less healthy version of the same instinct.

Key words and phrases

family heirlooms
valuable objects passed from one generation to the next
overlap
have some features in common
