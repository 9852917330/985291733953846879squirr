Object & Favourite

A book you liked to read in your childhood

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a book you liked to read in your childhood

You should say: who wrote this book what it is about when you read it and explain why you liked it.

Simple Model Answer

A book I enjoyed as a child was Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone by J.K. Rowling. It tells the story of a young boy who discovers that he is a wizard and goes to a magical school called Hogwarts.

At school, Harry becomes friends with Ron and Hermione. Together, they face problems and learn more about the dangerous Lord Voldemort. I liked the magic, but the friendship between the three children interested me just as much.

I started reading the book when I was about eight. It was much longer than the books I usually read, so finishing it took a long time. However, I felt proud whenever I completed another chapter. I did not understand every detail, but I wanted to find out what would happen next. The story also made me feel more confident about making friends at my own school. Some of those friendships have continued until today. That combination of an exciting story and happy childhood memories is why the book still matters to me.

Key words and phrases

wizard
A character in stories who can use magic.
completed another chapter
Finished reading one more section of a book.

Advanced Model Answer

Well, I'd like to tell you about the book I was really fond of when I was little — Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone. It's a fantasy novel by the British author J.K. Rowling, and she's become really popular all over the world thanks to this one series alone.

The story follows the school life of a young wizard, Harry Potter, and how he ends up best friends with Ron and Hermione after a pretty rocky start on the train to Hogwarts. There's also a villain lurking in the background for most of the book, Lord Voldemort, though you don't actually find out how dangerous he really is until near the very end, when he turns out to have been hiding in plain sight the whole time.

I started reading it when I was about eight. At that age I was still working hard to improve my reading skills, and Harry Potter was a thick book for a kid my size to carry around, so it honestly took me ages to get through the whole thing, though I remember feeling proud every time I finished another chapter.

I didn't understand every detail back then, but I loved the friendship between Harry, Ron and Hermione, and I think it actually helped me feel more confident about making friends at my own school — some of whom I still keep in touch with today. That's the main reason this book still means so much to me, even all these years later.
