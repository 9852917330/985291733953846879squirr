Situation

A difficult choice that you made

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a difficult choice that you made.

You should say: what the choice was when this happened what choice you made And explain why the choice was a difficult one.

Simple Model Answer

A difficult choice I made was deciding which parent to live with after their divorce. This happened when I was a child, about ten years ago. They had been arguing, and life at home had become very uncomfortable.

I loved both of them, so choosing felt almost impossible. I did not want either parent to think I cared less about them. We spent a long time discussing what would be best for me. In the end, I decided to stay with my mother because she could look after me day to day, and I felt she needed support after the separation.

My father was upset at first, but he eventually accepted the choice. I think it was painful for everyone, not just me. The hardest part was losing the everyday experience of living with both parents under one roof. I missed seeing my father daily for a long time. Looking back, I understand why the decision was necessary, but it remains one of the most emotionally difficult choices I have ever faced.

Advanced Model Answer

One time when I found it really difficult to make up my mind was back when I was a child, about ten years ago. My parents were not getting on well with each other, and the atmosphere at home became pretty unbearable. In the end they got divorced, and I was left not knowing which of them I should go and live with.

That was a genuinely difficult period for me, because so much changed at once, and mostly not for the better. Choosing who to live with felt almost impossible, since I loved both of my parents equally and we'd built up so many memories together as a family. In the end, we sat down as a family and spent ages talking it through, trying to work out what would actually be best for me.

I finally decided to stay with my mother, mainly because she'd be able to take better care of me day to day, and because I felt she needed my support more after the divorce. My father was upset at first, but he came round and agreed it was the right choice, for my sake. Looking back, I think it wasn't just a hard decision for me, it was hard for both of my parents too.

Part of what made it so difficult was that I'd always wanted to live under the same roof as both of my parents, so having to pick just one felt like a kind of loss in itself. Not getting to see my father every single day made me quite sad for a long time, and even now I'd say it's one of the toughest choices I've ever had to make.
