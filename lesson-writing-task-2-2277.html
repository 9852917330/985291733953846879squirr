Education

Adults who cannot read or write

Problem / Solution

Prompt

Despite better access to education, many adults today still cannot read or write. In what ways are they disadvantaged? What can governments do to help them?

Model answer

Schooling has spread widely over the past century, yet a substantial number of adults still cannot read a form or write a sentence. Their difficulties reach well beyond employment, and governments can address them with measures that are neither complicated nor expensive.

The most obvious disadvantage is economic. Almost every job above manual labour now requires reading instructions, filling in records or using a screen, so illiterate adults are pushed into insecure and poorly paid work, and are the first to be dismissed when it disappears. They also lose money quietly, because they cannot check a contract, a payslip or a loan agreement and must trust whoever is holding the paper.

The social cost is heavier and less visible. An adult who cannot read struggles to follow a prescription, understand a diagnosis or vote on the basis of anything written, which narrows both health and citizenship. Many arrange their lives around hiding the problem, claiming they have forgotten their glasses or asking a relative to deal with letters, and that daily effort to conceal it produces real shame. Their children suffer too, since nobody reads with them at home.

Governments can act in three ways. Free classes must be built for adults rather than borrowed from schools: in the evening, near workplaces, in small groups, and taught with the documents learners actually face, such as bank forms and medical letters. Second, employers should be paid to release staff for a few hours a week, which removes the choice between wages and lessons. Third, public information should be offered in spoken and pictorial form, so that people are not excluded while they learn.

In conclusion, illiteracy limits earnings, health and confidence rather than reading alone. Practical adult classes, workplace support and accessible public information would reduce that disadvantage substantially.

Key words and phrases

trust whoever is holding the paper
Depend on someone else to read the document.
daily effort to conceal it
Regular attempts to hide the difficulty.
choice between wages and lessons
Having to choose between earning and studying.
