Health

UK obesity trends

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The graphs below summarise the results of a survey carried out by the Department of Health about the overweight population in Britain with projections. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer

The graphs give information about the numbers of adults and children classified as obese in Britain, together with projections up to 2010, and about the link between obesity in children and in their parents.

Overall, the projected figures for 2010 are higher than the 2003 estimates for all four groups. The projections show men outnumbering women and girls outnumbering boys, and children with at least one parent classified as obese are more likely to be obese than those whose parents have a healthy weight.

The first two graphs show a marked rise in obesity in Britain by 2010. The number of men classified as obese is estimated to be as high as 6.8 million in 2010, over 50% higher than in 2003. The increase among women with the same problem tends to be smaller, rising almost 30% from 4.7 million to 6 million.

The picture is different for children. The chart suggests that the number of girls classified as obese was projected to increase more during the period 2003-2010, to more than 900 thousand, 100 thousand more than boys with the same problem, although in 2003 there were more boys than girls suffering from obesity, over 700 thousand versus about 690 thousand.

As shown in the third graph, children in households where both parents are obese are twice as likely to be obese as those in households where one parent is obese (12.5%), and five times as likely as those whose parents have a healthy weight (5%).

Key words and phrases

projected figures
Values estimated for a future date at the time the forecast was made.
a marked rise in obesity
A clear increase in severe overweight.
estimated to be as high as
Thought to reach as much as.
was projected to
Was expected to reach a future level according to a forecast.
twice as likely to be obese as
Having twice the chance of obesity.
five times as likely as
Having five times the chance.
