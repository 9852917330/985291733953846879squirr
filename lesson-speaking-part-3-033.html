Decisions

Why decisions matter and how to make good ones

Part 3

Prompt

Why is decision-making important in our lives? How can we make good decisions? Do you think parents should allow their children to make their own decisions?

Why is decision-making important in our lives?

Simple Model Answer

Our lives develop through many decisions about work, relationships and health. Small choices can have large effects when repeated for years, such as exercising regularly. Making good decisions cannot remove bad luck, but it improves our chances. It also helps avoid the regret of realising we never properly considered something important.

Key words and phrases

regret
Sadness about a choice or action you wish had been different.

Advanced Model Answer

Because the shape of your life is essentially the accumulation of decisions — what you study, where you work, who you spend time with, how you look after your health. Individually most of them seem small, but they compound over decades; someone who exercises three times a week for twenty years ends up in a completely different place physically from someone who always meant to start next month. Good decision-making doesn't guarantee a good outcome, since luck plays an enormous part, but it improves the odds and reduces the particular kind of regret that comes from never having thought about it at all.

Key words and phrases

the accumulation of decisions
many choices added together over time
compound over decades
build on each other over many years

How can we make good decisions?

Simple Model Answer

First, be clear about your goal. Then gather information from different sources and consider what could go wrong. For an important choice, it helps to wait a night and ask someone who has no personal interest in the result. These steps do not guarantee success, but they make the decision better informed.

Key words and phrases

better informed
Based on more useful or reliable knowledge.

Advanced Model Answer

A few things help consistently. Being clear about what you're actually trying to achieve, because a lot of bad decisions come from answering the wrong question — optimising for the cheapest option when what you actually wanted was reliability, for example. Gathering information from sources that don't all agree with each other helps too, and so does deliberately asking what could go wrong, uncomfortable as that is. For anything major I'd add two more: sleep on it, and ask someone with no stake in the outcome. None of this removes risk entirely, though — even a carefully made decision can still turn out badly.

Key words and phrases

answering the wrong question
responding to an issue other than the one that matters
sleep on it
wait until the next day before deciding
no stake in the outcome
no personal interest in how it ends

Do you think parents should allow their children to make their own decisions?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, with more freedom as they grow. A small child can choose clothes or spend pocket money, where mistakes are not very costly. Teenagers can make larger choices about study and time. Parents should neither control everything nor ignore serious risks. The level of responsibility needs to match the child's age and ability.

Key words and phrases

pocket money
A small amount of money regularly given to a child to spend.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, but gradually, with the stakes matched to the age. A young child can choose their own clothes or how to spend their pocket money — if the choice is a poor one, the cost is small and the lesson's real. A teenager can choose their subjects and manage their own time. What parents shouldn't do is either decide everything, which produces adults who've never practised deciding anything, or step back entirely on things that genuinely cannot be undone — a tattoo, say, or dropping out of school at sixteen.

Key words and phrases

genuinely cannot be undone
truly impossible to reverse
