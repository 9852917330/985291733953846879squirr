Language & Communication

Will the skill of writing disappear?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

As the use of mobile phones and computers increases, fewer people write letters. Some people believe that the skill of writing will disappear completely. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

As mobile phones and computers have replaced handwritten letters, some people predict that the ability to write will eventually disappear. I disagree with this view, because technology has changed how we write rather than whether we write at all.

There is no doubt that certain forms of writing are dying out. Personal letters are now rare, handwriting is deteriorating because most people use a keyboard, and features such as voice messages and predictive text allow users to communicate with very little effort. Schools in several countries have already reduced the time spent teaching cursive script, and some have removed it from the curriculum altogether.

Nevertheless, most people now write far more than any previous generation did. A typical office worker produces dozens of emails, reports and messages every day, and social media consists almost entirely of written text. The medium has changed, but the underlying skill of organising an idea and expressing it clearly is required far more often than it was when letters were posted once a month.

That skill also remains essential where it matters most. Contracts, medical records, academic papers, journalism and job applications all depend on precise writing, and employers regularly identify it as the ability they find most lacking in graduates. Voice technology cannot replace it, because a spoken sentence and a written argument are structured quite differently.

In conclusion, handwriting and traditional letters are certainly in decline, and that particular loss is worth regretting. I do not believe, however, that writing itself will disappear. If anything, digital communication has made the ability to write clearly more valuable than before.
