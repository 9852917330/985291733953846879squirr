Food & Diet

Cheaper vegetables or costlier junk food?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some argue that subsidising fruit and vegetables is a good way for governments to make healthy food cheaper, while others maintain that taxing junk food would work better. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Diet-related illness now consumes a large share of health budgets, and price is one of the few levers a government can pull. Both approaches have evidence behind them, and my own view is that they work properly only together.

Subsidy has the advantage of pushing rather than punishing. Fresh produce is expensive relative to processed food, particularly for households buying calories on a fixed budget, so lowering its price removes a real barrier rather than adding a burden. Schemes that give low-income families vouchers for fruit and vegetables have raised consumption measurably. Politically it is far easier to introduce, since nobody objects to cheaper food, and it helps the poorest households rather than charging them. It also supports growers.

Taxation has stronger evidence behind it. Levies on sugary drinks have reduced sugar consumption in every country that has tried them, partly because buyers switch and partly because manufacturers reformulate products to avoid the charge, which changes what is on the shelf for everyone. Tax also raises revenue instead of costing it, and that money can fund the subsidy. The principle is familiar from tobacco: where a product imposes costs on the health service, the price should carry some of that cost.

The objections are equally clear. Subsidy alone is expensive and does not stop anybody buying what they were buying already. Taxation alone falls hardest on the poor, who spend more of their income on food and have fewest alternatives, and it looks like punishing people for being unable to afford anything better.

In conclusion, taxing unhealthy products changes both purchases and recipes, while subsidies make the healthy option genuinely affordable. Used separately each has a serious weakness, so the sensible policy is to tax the harm and spend the revenue on the alternative.

Key words and phrases

helps the poorest households rather than charging them
Supports low-income families instead of adding costs.
the price should carry some of that cost
The selling price should reflect some resulting harm.
