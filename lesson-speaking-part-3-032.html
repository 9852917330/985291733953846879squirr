Decisions

Choices at different stages of life

Part 3

Prompt

What are the typical choices people make at different stages of their lives? Should important choices be made by parents rather than by young adults? Why do some people like to discuss choices with other people?

What are the typical choices people make at different stages of their lives?

Simple Model Answer

Children choose friends and how much effort to give school. Young adults make major choices about education, careers and moving away. Later, people decide about homes, children and retirement. Older adults often have more experience, but that does not guarantee better judgement. The order also varies, so not everyone follows exactly the same life path.

Key words and phrases

life path
The course a person's life takes through choices and events.

Advanced Model Answer

They follow a fairly predictable sequence. Children choose their friends and how much effort to put into school, which seems minor but shapes a lot more than people realise. Young adults face the biggest decisions — what to study, which career to enter, whether to move away — and they make these with the least experience of anyone. Later choices, around housing, children and retirement, are financially bigger but usually much better informed, simply because by then people have some sense of how their own judgement performs. That's not a fixed rule, though: some people are still making decisions just as badly informed at fifty as they were at twenty.

Key words and phrases

the least experience
having less practical experience than anyone else
just as badly informed
lacking reliable facts to the same degree

Should important choices be made by parents rather than by young adults?

Simple Model Answer

Parents should offer advice, but young adults usually need to make the final choice. Being forced into a career can create lasting unhappiness. Deciding for yourself also develops judgement. However, parents should still step in when a choice is seriously dangerous or cannot easily be reversed, rather than leave a young person without protection.

Key words and phrases

reversed
Changed back or undone.

Advanced Model Answer

Parents should advise, and their experience is genuinely valuable, but the final decision has to belong to the young person. There's a practical reason for this: someone pushed into a career they didn't choose tends to resent it — I know someone who did medicine because his parents insisted, and he's spent a decade being competent at a job he never wanted. Making a decision and living with the consequences is how judgement develops, and without that you get adults who've never really practised deciding anything. The exception is anything genuinely dangerous or irreversible, where a parent's veto is reasonable whatever the teenager says.

Key words and phrases

living with the consequences
dealing with the real effects of a decision
a parent's veto
a parent's power to reject a decision

Why do some people like to discuss choices with other people?

Simple Model Answer

Talking helps people explain their reasoning and notice mistakes. Someone else may suggest an option they missed; a friend once reminded me to consider travel time, not just a flat's rent. Discussion can also make a decision feel less frightening. However, advice may be biased if the other person benefits from a particular result.

Key words and phrases

biased
Unfairly influenced by one person's interests or preferences.

Advanced Model Answer

Partly because talking forces you to actually articulate the reasoning, and you often notice a flaw the moment you say it out loud. Another person also brings options you hadn't considered and questions the assumptions you'd stopped noticing — a friend once pointed out I was choosing a flat based purely on rent, having completely forgotten how long the commute would be. There's an emotional element too, since a decision feels less frightening once someone else has heard it. The risk is asking people who have a stake in the outcome, because their advice will quietly serve their own interests rather than yours.

Key words and phrases

the assumptions you'd stopped noticing
ideas you accepted without questioning them
have a stake in the outcome
be personally affected by the result
