Environment

Should air travel be restricted?

Opinion

Prompt

Some people believe that air travel should be restricted because it causes serious pollution and will use up the world's resources. Do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

The environmental impact of air travel has attracted growing attention as more people use planes for work and leisure. Some therefore argue that restrictions should be imposed on flying. There are valid concerns behind this view, although a complete restriction would be neither practical nor necessary.

The first concern is that aircraft are responsible for a significant share of air pollution. Planes consume large quantities of fuel while taking off, landing and taxiing, and they produce emissions at high altitude. Some might suggest that air travel is the only suitable mode of transport for long-distance journeys. However, trains can often provide an effective alternative on shorter routes while using less energy and causing less pollution.

Aircraft also create noise pollution. Although the precise impact of noise on daily life is difficult to measure, persistent exposure can increase anxiety and annoyance. For people living near airports or beneath flight paths, this noise can become a serious and continuing disturbance.

Nevertheless, these problems do not mean that air travel should be heavily restricted. A more balanced response is to make aviation cleaner and more efficient. For example, airlines can replace older engines with cleaner models, improve flight planning and use fuller aircraft so that more passengers are carried with the same amount of fuel. Governments can also support high-speed rail where it is a realistic substitute.

In light of these considerations, the environmental damage caused by aviation deserves serious attention. However, targeted technological improvements and sensible transport policies are preferable to broad restrictions on air travel.

Key words and phrases

a significant share of
A substantial portion of something.
persistent exposure can increase anxiety and annoyance
Repeated exposure can increase worry and irritation.
a serious and continuing disturbance
A major disruption that does not stop.
a realistic substitute
Another option that can actually work.
targeted technological improvements
Technical changes addressing specific problems.
