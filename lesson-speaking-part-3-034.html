Dreams

What dreams tell us and whether to interpret them

Part 3

Prompt

What can dreams tell us about ourselves? Why are dreams considered important in many cultures? Do you think it is helpful to interpret someone's dreams for them?

What can dreams tell us about ourselves?

Simple Model Answer

Dreams may show what we have recently experienced or worried about. Before an exam, for example, I repeatedly dreamed about missing a flight. That suggested anxiety rather than a secret prediction. I don't think every object in a dream has one fixed meaning, but a repeated dream may still encourage us to consider our feelings.

Key words and phrases

anxiety
A feeling of worry or nervousness.

Advanced Model Answer

Something, but less than people would like. Dreams clearly draw on recent experience — you dream about whatever you've been worrying about, so they can point you towards a concern you haven't consciously admitted to yourself. I once had the same anxiety dream about missing a flight every night for a week before a big exam, which wasn't exactly subtle. What I'm sceptical about is the idea of fixed symbols, where a particular object always means one thing — there's no real evidence for that. That said, I wouldn't dismiss dreams completely; one that keeps coming back is at least worth paying attention to.

Key words and phrases

fixed symbols
symbols with meanings that never change
keeps coming back
returns repeatedly

Why are dreams considered important in many cultures?

Simple Model Answer

Dreams can feel very real and emotional, so people may think they have a special source or message. Many cultures have treated them as warnings or messages from ancestors. They can also give people a way to discuss fears. Modern explanations are usually more cautious, but the strong feelings that dreams create still matter to people.

Key words and phrases

ancestors
Family members or people from earlier generations.

Advanced Model Answer

Because they feel significant. A dream is vivid, emotionally intense and completely outside your control, so it's natural to assume it came from somewhere else rather than from your own mind. Historically, many cultures treated dreams as messages from ancestors or warnings about the future, and important decisions, going to war, choosing a leader, were sometimes taken on that basis. They also served a social function, giving communities a shared language for fears that were difficult to state directly. Modern psychology takes a far more modest view of what dreams can actually tell us, but the emotional pull hasn't gone away just because the science caught up.

Key words and phrases

completely outside your control
not something you can influence at all
modest view
a limited and careful opinion

Do you think it is helpful to interpret someone's dreams for them?

Simple Model Answer

It can help if it starts a conversation about the person's feelings or worries. However, I would not claim that one interpretation is certainly true. Someone might make an important decision based on a meaning that is only a guess. Exploring possibilities together is safer than telling them a dream has a single fixed message.

Key words and phrases

interpretation
One possible explanation of what something means.

Advanced Model Answer

It can be, but it depends entirely on how it's done. If it opens a conversation about what someone's anxious about, that's useful, and the dream's simply the way in. What I'd avoid is presenting an interpretation as fact, because telling somebody confidently what their dream means can create a false certainty they then act on — deciding to leave a job because a dream book said water means change, say. A therapist explores it together with the person; a fixed reading from a book does the opposite.

Key words and phrases

a false certainty
confidence that is not supported by evidence
a fixed reading from a book
one unchanging interpretation written in a book
