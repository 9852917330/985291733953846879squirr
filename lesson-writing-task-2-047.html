Society

Military service or community service?

Opinion

Prompt

What are the benefits of requiring young people to serve in the army? Does participation in community work qualify as an alternative?

Model answer

Some countries still require young people to complete a period of military service. In my opinion this brings certain genuine benefits, but community service would achieve most of the same aims without the drawbacks.

The advantages of military training are not difficult to identify. Recruits become considerably fitter, and they learn discipline, punctuality and how to function under pressure, which many young people have never previously been required to do. There is a social benefit as well: conscription mixes young men from different regions and backgrounds who would otherwise never meet, which strengthens national cohesion in a way little else does. Employers frequently regard former conscripts as reliable, punctual and well organised, and in some countries the experience is treated almost as a qualification in itself.

The objections, however, are serious. Military training is designed to prepare people for combat, and not everyone is suited to that environment; a significant number find the stress genuinely damaging. It also removes a year or two from a young person's education or career at exactly the point when those years matter most.

Community service seems to me a better instrument for the same purpose. Working in hospitals, care homes, conservation projects or disaster relief teaches responsibility and teamwork just as effectively, and it exposes young people to parts of society they would otherwise never see. It also produces something of immediate value, whereas peacetime military training produces a readiness that is rarely used.

In conclusion, military service genuinely does develop discipline and physical fitness, and I would not dismiss it. Community service, however, delivers comparable benefits at a far lower personal cost, and I would prefer it as the general requirement.
