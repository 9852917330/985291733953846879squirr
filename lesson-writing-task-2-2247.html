Cities & Urban Life

Building outwards or upwards

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think that it is best to live in a horizontal city while others think of a vertical city. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Model answer

As populations grow, cities must decide whether to spread outwards in low buildings or upwards in towers. Both models have committed supporters, and my own view is that the vertical approach is the more responsible one for large cities.

Those who prefer low-rise living point to what happens at ground level. Streets of houses two or three storeys high keep front doors, gardens and pavements close together, so neighbours meet and children can play outside within sight of a window. Such districts are also easier to adapt, since a family can extend a house as it grows. Towers, by contrast, can leave residents isolated behind a lift door, with no space of their own outdoors.

The argument for building upwards is a matter of arithmetic. Housing the same population across a wider area means longer journeys, more roads, more pipes and more cable, all of which cost money and produce emissions, and it consumes farmland at the edges. Density does the opposite: where enough people live within walking distance, shops, clinics and a bus route become viable, and a resident may not need a car at all. Compact cities consistently record lower emissions per person than sprawling ones.

My view is that the objections to towers describe bad design rather than height itself. Buildings with generous shared gardens, ground-floor shops and well-maintained lifts produce sociable neighbourhoods, whereas towers surrounded by car parks fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the storeys.

In conclusion, low-rise districts are pleasant but cannot house growing populations without consuming the countryside. Building upwards is preferable, provided cities invest in the shared spaces that make density liveable rather than merely efficient.
