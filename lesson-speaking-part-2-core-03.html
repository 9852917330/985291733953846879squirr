Starting Over in a New City

Core Story

Prompt

Describe a difficult challenge you faced.

You should say: - what the challenge was - why it was difficult - what you did to deal with it - and explain what you learned from it.

OUTLINE

>>> Starting Over in a New City
major challenge • unfamiliar big city • rebuild daily life from scratch
housing • transport • faster pace • very few local contacts
one neighbourhood at a time • weekend sports group • talk to colleagues
weekly routine • shopping, exercise, meal prep • small daily wins
confidence follows action • adaptability through repetition

Simple Model Answer

A difficult challenge was moving to a large city where I knew almost nobody. I was excited, but the first few weeks were harder than I expected. I needed an affordable place to live, had to understand public transport and was not used to the busy lifestyle.

The biggest problem was having no familiar people nearby. Even small difficulties felt serious when I had nobody to ask. I dealt with this by learning one neighbourhood at a time and joining a weekend sports group. I also talked more to colleagues instead of always going straight home. A regular routine for shopping, exercise and cooking helped me feel organised.

After a couple of months, the city began to feel like home. I did not suddenly stop being nervous. I simply became more confident as I solved everyday problems. The experience taught me that sometimes you need to take action before confidence develops.

Key words and phrases

affordable
Not too expensive for your budget.
take action
Do something practical to improve or change a situation.

Advanced Model Answer

One difficult challenge I faced was moving to a large city where I knew almost nobody and had to build a routine from scratch.

The move itself was exciting, but the first few weeks were surprisingly difficult. I had to find affordable accommodation, learn the transport system and adjust to a much faster pace of life. The hardest part, however, was the lack of familiar people. Even simple problems felt bigger because I had nobody nearby to ask for help.

I dealt with it by making the situation more manageable. I learned one neighbourhood at a time, joined a weekend sports group and made a point of speaking to colleagues instead of going straight home after work. I also kept a basic weekly routine for shopping, exercise and meal preparation, which gave the new place a sense of structure.

After a couple of months, the city stopped feeling intimidating and started to feel like home. The main lesson I learned was that confidence often comes after action, not before it. I did not suddenly become fearless; I simply became more capable because I kept solving small problems every day.

Key words and phrases

build a routine from scratch
Create daily habits without an existing plan.
a much faster pace of life
Everyday activities happen much more quickly.
making the situation more manageable
Making the problem easier to handle.
a sense of structure
A feeling that things are organised.
confidence often comes after action
Doing something can make you feel more capable.
