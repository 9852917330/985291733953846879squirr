Object & Favourite

Something special that you saved money to buy

Part 2

Prompt

Describe something special that you saved money to buy

You should say: - What was this thing. - Why was it special to you. - How much did it cost you. - How you felt after you bought it.

Simple Model Answer

Something special I saved for was a clock necklace for my best friend Julie. I bought it at the end of grade ten, before she left to study abroad. I wanted a gift that would remind her of our friendship.

After visiting several shops, I found a vintage-style necklace with a small clock pendant. It could be customised, so I chose details and added a message on the back. Before giving it to her, I set the clock to Vietnamese time. I hoped that seeing the time back home would make her think of her family and friends.

The necklace cost about twenty dollars. That was a lot for me as a school student relying on pocket money, so saving enough took a month. When she opened it, she was delighted. I felt proud because I had chosen something personal rather than simply buying an expensive-looking object. The gift represented our connection even though we would be far apart. I hoped it would remind her that she still had someone thinking about her at home.

Key words and phrases

pendant
A small decorative object hanging from a necklace.
customised
Made or changed according to a particular person's choices.

Advanced Model Answer

I want to tell you about a clock necklace I bought for my best friend, Julie, at the end of grade ten, when she was leaving to study abroad. I wanted something she'd think of me by, so I spent weeks going shop to shop to find the right one.

Julie's always been more like a sister than a friend, so an ordinary present wasn't going to cut it — I didn't want something pretty but pointless, or useful but with no feeling behind it. Then one day I found a vintage necklace with a little clock pendant and knew it was right. It suited her style, and because it was custom-made, I could choose the numbers and have a message engraved on the back.

The detail I'm proudest of is that I set the clock to Vietnamese time before I gave it to her, even though she'd be living on the other side of the world. That way, whenever she checked the time and it looked wrong, she'd have to stop and remember why — which meant thinking of home, and of me, if only for a second.

It only cost about twenty dollars — tiny, but as a high schooler living on pocket money, it was a small fortune. I skipped breakfast for a month to afford it, though Julie never found out.

When I gave it to her, she loved it, and I felt properly proud of myself — not just for the gift, but because I'd managed to give my best friend something that meant something, on a budget that shouldn't have stretched that far. I hope that little clock keeps us connected, even with an ocean between us.
