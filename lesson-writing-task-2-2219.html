Health

Preventing illness through policy

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Some people think that governments should focus on reducing environmental pollution and housing problems to help people to prevent illness and disease. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

It is sometimes argued that the best way to improve public health is not to build more hospitals but to clean the air and fix people's homes. I largely agree with this, because the causes of much modern illness lie outside the health service altogether.

The evidence on air quality is difficult to dismiss. Fine particles from traffic and coal burning penetrate deep into the lungs, and cities with heavy pollution record markedly higher rates of asthma, heart disease and stroke. A government that lowers emissions therefore prevents thousands of admissions that would otherwise have to be treated, which is both cheaper and kinder than treating the consequences afterwards.

Housing works in the same way. Damp and cold homes are directly linked to chest infections and childhood asthma, while overcrowding spreads infectious disease and undermines the sleep and study that children need. Insulating an old housing estate is unglamorous work, but it removes a cause of illness permanently rather than managing it year after year.

I would qualify my agreement in one respect. Prevention cannot replace treatment, because clean air does nothing for a person who has already developed cancer or been injured in an accident, and a government that diverted its entire health budget into housing would leave those patients with nowhere to go. The sensible position is that prevention deserves a far larger share of spending than it usually receives, not that it should take the place of medical care.

In conclusion, I agree that reducing pollution and improving housing should be central to health policy, since both remove causes of disease rather than symptoms. A country that acts on them will need fewer hospital beds in twenty years, but it will still need the beds it has today.

Key words and phrases

treating the consequences afterwards
Dealing with damage after it has happened.
managing it year after year
Handling a continuing condition over many years.
take the place of medical care
Replace professional health treatment.
the beds it has today
The hospital capacity currently available.
