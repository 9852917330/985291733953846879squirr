Health

Why older patients ignore exercise advice

Problem / Solution

Prompt

Many doctors recommend that older people should take regular exercise, but most patients do not follow this advice. What are the reasons for this? What can be done to solve this problem?

Model answer

Doctors know that movement does more for an ageing body than most medication, yet the advice is rarely acted on. The reasons are practical and psychological rather than a lack of willingness, and the remedies have to remove the obstacles rather than repeat the instruction.

Fear is the first barrier. Older patients worry about falling, about pain and about making an existing condition worse, and told simply to exercise they have no idea what is safe, so doing nothing feels like the cautious choice. Vague advice makes this worse: an instruction to be more active carries no starting point, no amount and no way of knowing whether it is working.

Circumstances add to it. Many older people live with arthritis or breathlessness that makes the first weeks genuinely unpleasant, and the benefits arrive months later while the discomfort arrives immediately. Facilities are often unsuitable, since gyms are designed for the young and can be intimidating, and getting to one may be impossible without a car. Cost matters on a pension. There is also a belief, still common, that slowing down is simply what age requires, and those who exercise alone find little to sustain the habit.

The solutions follow directly. Doctors should prescribe activity as precisely as medicine, naming the type, the amount and the progression, and refer patients to supervised classes rather than leaving them to arrange it. Programmes designed for older bodies — chair exercises, walking groups, swimming — work far better than general facilities, and free or subsidised places in community centres remove the cost. Group classes matter most, because the company is what brings people back, and families and local councils can help by making pavements, benches and safe routes available.

In conclusion, older patients ignore the advice because it is vague, frightening and hard to act on. Specific prescriptions, supervised group classes and facilities built for their age rather than borrowed from the young would change that.

Key words and phrases

doing nothing feels like the cautious choice
Inaction seems safer than taking a risk.
slowing down is simply what age requires
Assuming older people naturally must become inactive.
the company is what brings people back
Social contact motivates people to return.
