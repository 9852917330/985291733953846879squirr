Trade & Industry

Rotterdam port exports

Academic Task 1

Prompt

The charts give information about the amount of exports (millions of tonnes) moving through Rotterdam port in Holland to various global destinations in 2002 and 2012; and also the % rates of tax imposed on these exports by the receiving countries. Write a report summarising the information. Select and describe the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Model answer

The charts show the quantity of exports being transported through Rotterdam over a ten-year period, and the level of taxation imposed on the goods at their destination.

Overall, total export volumes rose slightly over the decade, but the amount going to most individual zones declined. The figures also show that the two destinations with growing export volumes also had lower tax rates in 2012.

Total exports reached 18,600 million tonnes by 2012. Europe – the largest destination – declined from 6,900 million to 6,400 million, and the USA – the second highest – fell by 300 million tonnes, or about 7%, to 3,900 million. China dipped slightly to 2,300 million, and Latin America dropped from 1,800 to 1,500 million, while the ‘other’ zones remained static at 350 million. By marked contrast, volumes to Asia Pacific and Australasia more than trebled, hitting 2,550 and 2,300 million tonnes respectively.

Turning to taxation, we see that tax increased considerably in all the zones that showed a decline in volumes, reaching 2% in the USA, China and Latin America, and rising from 5% to 8% in Europe. The ‘other’ zones kept tax stable at 2%, while the growth zones reduced tax massively, from 5% to 2% in Asia Pacific and from 4% to 1% in Australasia.

Key words and phrases

By marked contrast
Showing a very clear difference.
more than trebled
Became over three times as much.
respectively
In the same order as the things already mentioned.
kept tax stable at 2%
Left the tax rate unchanged at 2%.
