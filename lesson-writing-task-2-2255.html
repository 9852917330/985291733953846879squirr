Environment

Flights or cars?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Long distance flight uses more fuel than cars and brings pollution to the environment. We should discourage non-essential flights rather than limit the use of cars. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Aviation and road transport both damage the atmosphere, and it is argued that policy should target unnecessary flying rather than driving. I partly agree, because flying is harder to make clean, but I do not accept that cars should therefore be left alone.

The case for concentrating on flights is strong in one respect. A single return flight between Europe and Asia produces more carbon per passenger than a year of typical local driving, and the emissions are released high in the atmosphere, where their warming effect is greater. Aviation fuel is also untaxed in most countries, so tickets are artificially cheap, and the technology to replace kerosene on long routes does not yet exist at scale. Discouraging the least necessary journeys is therefore one of the few tools available.

However, treating this as an alternative to reducing car use misreads the arithmetic. Road transport produces several times more emissions in total than aviation, simply because so many more people drive so much more often, and a small reduction in a very large number outweighs a large reduction in a small one. Flying is also concentrated among wealthier travellers, so a policy aimed only at flights leaves most of the problem untouched while appearing decisive.

Cars are additionally the easier problem to solve. Electric vehicles, better buses and cycle lanes already work, whereas no equivalent exists for a long-haul aircraft, so progress is available now on the road.

In conclusion, I agree that unnecessary flights deserve discouragement, since aviation is difficult to decarbonise and currently undertaxed. Presenting this as a reason to spare cars is mistaken, because driving causes the larger share of the damage and is the part we already know how to fix.

Key words and phrases

does not yet exist at scale
Is not available in large quantities yet.
leaves most of the problem untouched
Does not address most of the difficulty.
progress is available now
Improvement is possible immediately.
the part we already know how to fix
The problem with an already-known solution.
