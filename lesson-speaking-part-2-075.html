Object & Favourite

A piece of artwork that you have seen before

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a piece of artwork that you have seen before.

You should say: Where you saw it What it was about When you saw it And explain how you felt about it

Simple Model Answer

A piece of art I like is the Mona Lisa, a portrait painted by Leonardo da Vinci. I first saw a picture of it in an art textbook when I was about fifteen. Even though it was only a printed image, it caught my attention.

The painting shows a woman with long hair and a calm face. At first, I mainly noticed her unusual expression. The more I looked, the more I wondered whether she was smiling. I kept turning back to the page because her expression seemed difficult to understand.

That mystery is the main reason I like the painting. People have different ideas about the woman and her smile, and I find those discussions interesting. I am not an art expert, so I cannot explain every detail of the technique. However, I think a good painting can make an ordinary viewer stop and feel curious. The Mona Lisa did that for me. It helped me understand that a picture does not need a dramatic scene to hold someone's interest.

Key words and phrases

portrait
A painting or photograph of a person, especially their face.
caught my attention
Made me notice and become interested in something.

Advanced Model Answer

Today I'd like to tell you about one of the most famous paintings in the world, and my personal favourite — the portrait of the Mona Lisa. It was painted by Leonardo da Vinci, the Italian artist, back in the fifteenth century.

I first came across it in one of my high school art textbooks, when I was about fifteen, and I honestly couldn't take my eyes off it — there was something mysterious about it I couldn't quite explain. At first sight, all I really noticed was a long-haired woman with a gentle, slightly rounded face and no eyebrows at all, which I found a bit strange at the time. The longer I looked, though, the more it felt like she was actually smiling at me specifically, and I remember flicking back to the page more than once just to check I wasn't imagining it.

Some people say Mona Lisa was purely imaginary, while others believe she was actually Leonardo's wife. Honestly, it doesn't really matter to me either way, because to me the Mona Lisa is a masterpiece that attracts people with that one magical smile, and that's the main reason I'm such a fan of it.

As for the actual truth behind the portrait, I guess we'll never know for certain, but I think that mystery is exactly what makes the whole thing all the more intriguing, and it's probably why the painting still draws such huge crowds today.
