Sport & Leisure

Team sports or individual sports?

Discuss Both Views

Prompt

Some people think it is more beneficial to take part in team sports such as football, while others think individual sports such as swimming are better. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

Football and swimming both keep a person fit, yet they teach quite different things. Each has a serious claim, and my own view is that the better choice depends on what a particular person needs rather than on the sports themselves.

Team sports offer lessons that cannot be learned alone. A player must read what teammates are doing, accept a role that may not be glamorous and keep going when a match is lost through somebody else's mistake, which is closer to how most work and family life actually feels. The social pull is also practical: turning up matters because other people depend on it, so team players tend to keep exercising when individuals give up. For children who are shy or new to an area, a team supplies friendships that would otherwise take years.

Individual sports have their own strengths. Progress depends entirely on the person, so improvement is visible and honestly earned, and that produces a durable kind of confidence. There is no substitute to hide behind and no captain to blame, which builds self-discipline. They are also more flexible, since a swimmer or runner trains at any hour without organising twenty other people, and they can be continued into old age long after the body has stopped tolerating collisions. Children who dislike competition or find crowds difficult often thrive here after failing in a team.

The choice therefore depends on the individual. A child who avoids other people benefits from a team; one who is constantly compared with others benefits from a sport where the only opponent is a previous time.

In conclusion, team sports develop cooperation and keep people participating, while individual sports build discipline and last a lifetime. The greatest benefit comes from matching the sport to the person, and ideally from experiencing both.

Key words and phrases

the body has stopped tolerating collisions
Physical impacts have become harder to withstand.
the only opponent is a previous time
Competing against your own earlier performance.
