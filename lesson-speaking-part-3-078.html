Natural ability against developing a skill

Part 3

Prompt

Could you comment on the idea that children shouldn't waste their time trying to develop a skill if they lack natural ability? Is it important to learn a skill? What do you think are the most important skills for children to learn in the modern world?

Could you comment on the idea that children shouldn't waste their time trying to develop a skill if they lack natural ability?

Simple Model Answer

I disagree. Natural ability can affect how quickly a child improves, but practice still makes a difference. A talented child who never works may progress less than someone who keeps trying. Learning also teaches that effort produces results, which helps elsewhere. Talent may matter more at the very highest level, but that is no reason to discourage most children.

Key words and phrases

Natural ability
A skill or strength someone appears to have without much training.

Advanced Model Answer

I disagree fairly strongly. Natural ability affects how quickly someone improves, not whether they can improve at all, and most people vastly overestimate how much talent explains. A child who practises consistently will overtake a naturally gifted one who doesn't, in almost any field. There's a wider point too: the value of learning an instrument isn't only the music, it's discovering that sustained effort produces results, and that lesson transfers to everything else a child attempts. I'd make one exception, though — right at the elite level, in top-tier sport or music, natural ability probably does start to matter more, but that's a tiny fraction of children and no reason to hold the rest back.

Key words and phrases

overestimate how much talent explains
think natural ability matters more than it actually does
sustained effort
work continued for a long time

Is it important to learn a skill?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, because learning a skill teaches us how improvement works. Progress can be slow or uneven, and a difficult period does not mean we should stop. Once people understand that, they may handle challenges better in other areas. The original skill might become less useful, but the habit of careful, repeated practice can stay valuable throughout life.

Key words and phrases

repeated practice
Doing an activity many times to improve at it.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, and the process matters more than the specific skill itself. Learning something difficult teaches you what your own progress actually looks like — that it's slow, uneven, and that plateaus are normal rather than a signal to stop. People who've been through that once are usually much harder to discourage later on, in completely unrelated areas. The particular skill may well become obsolete — nobody needs my childhood recorder-playing now — but the habit of deliberate practice stays useful for a lifetime.

What do you think are the most important skills for children to learn in the modern world?

Simple Model Answer

Reading, writing and maths remain basic. Children also need to judge information carefully, communicate and work with others. Learning how to learn matters because their jobs may change several times. These skills should be practised throughout ordinary lessons rather than taught once in a separate activity, so children can use them in different real situations.

Key words and phrases

judge information
Decide how trustworthy, useful or accurate information is.

Advanced Model Answer

The foundations haven't changed — reading, writing and maths still determine what everything else is built on. Beyond that I'd prioritise the ability to evaluate information, because children now encounter enormous amounts of it with no filter. Communication and working with others matter too, since almost nothing significant gets done alone, and increasingly learning how to learn, since most of them will change occupation several times over a career. The one thing I'd push back on is treating these as one-off modules — they only really stick when they're built into ordinary lessons.

Key words and phrases

the ability to evaluate information
being able to judge whether facts are reliable
learning how to learn
understanding methods that help you gain new knowledge
one-off modules
classes completed once without regular continuation
