Work & Employment

Why some people work harder

Two-Part Question

Prompt

In both education and employment, some people work harder than others. Why do some people work harder than others? Is working hard always a good thing?

Model answer

In any classroom or office, effort varies far more than ability does. The reasons lie in circumstance and motivation rather than character, and hard work is valuable only while it remains attached to a purpose.

Necessity is the first explanation. A student whose family has sacrificed to pay the fees, or an employee supporting relatives, works from a pressure that a comfortable colleague simply does not feel. Interest is the second and stronger one: people who find the subject absorbing put in hours that do not feel like effort, while those in the wrong course or job spend most of their energy on getting started. Upbringing matters too, since children raised where persistence was expected treat difficulty as normal rather than as a signal to stop.

There is also a question of belief. People work hard when they expect the effort to change something, which is why students who think ability is fixed give up quickly, and why employees in workplaces where promotion follows connections rather than results stop trying for reasons that are entirely rational.

Hard work is not always good, however. Beyond a certain point the returns fall sharply, and long hours produce exhaustion, poor judgement and errors that undo the extra time. Effort can also be misdirected, since somebody working twelve hours on the wrong task achieves less than a colleague who thinks first. The heaviest cost is personal, because health, family and friendship are what usually pay for the additional hours, and nobody recovers that time later.

In conclusion, people work hard because of financial pressure, genuine interest, upbringing and a belief that effort matters. Hard work is admirable when it is aimed at something worthwhile and bounded by rest, and harmful once it becomes a habit that no longer serves any goal.

Key words and phrases

spend most of their energy on getting started
Use most effort on initial entry rather than progress.
stop trying for reasons that are entirely rational
Give up because circumstances make continuing unreasonable.
nobody recovers that time later
Lost time cannot be reclaimed.
a habit that no longer serves any goal
A repeated behaviour without a useful purpose.
