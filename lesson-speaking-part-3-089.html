Technology

Advertising online and how it changes what we buy

Part 3

Prompt

What role does advertising play on the internet? How do you think the internet will affect buying patterns in the future? What effect has the internet had on the way people generally communicate with each other?

What role does advertising play on the internet?

Simple Model Answer

Advertising pays for many free services, such as search, email and videos. Companies value being able to reach very specific users, but this often involves collecting personal data. I would like people to have more meaningful control over that information. However, many of us accept conditions without reading them, so we do not always act on our concerns.

Key words and phrases

personal data
Information connected with an identifiable individual.

Advanced Model Answer

It funds almost everything people use for free — search, social media, email, video. That's the bargain, though it's not always made explicit: you're not the customer, you're what's being sold. Targeting has become extraordinarily precise, which advertisers value and which makes many users uncomfortable, because the line between relevance and surveillance is genuinely thin. What I'd want is real control over the data rather than a consent box nobody reads — though I'll admit most people, myself included, still click accept without reading it, so the market clearly isn't demanding that control very loudly.

Key words and phrases

the line between relevance and surveillance
the unclear boundary between helpful suggestions and watching people
click accept without reading it
agree to terms without checking what they say

How do you think the internet will affect buying patterns in the future?

Simple Model Answer

Everyday purchases may become more automatic through subscriptions or systems that reorder items. For bigger purchases, reviews and comparisons can help buyers, although fake reviews create problems. Physical shops may become places to try products before ordering elsewhere. That gives consumers options, but makes it harder for shops to earn enough from providing the experience.

Key words and phrases

comparisons
Ways of examining different options to understand their advantages and disadvantages.

Advanced Model Answer

Routine purchasing will become largely automatic — subscriptions and systems that reorder things before you notice you need them. For anything considered, people will rely more on reviews and comparison, which shifts power towards the consumer, though fake reviews undermine that considerably. Physical shops won't disappear, but their role will change into places where you test or try something before buying it more cheaply elsewhere, which is a very difficult business model to sustain.

Key words and phrases

fake reviews undermine that considerably
false opinions from buyers greatly reduce that trust

What effect has the internet had on the way people generally communicate with each other?

Simple Model Answer

It makes contact quicker, cheaper and global, which is very useful. However, short messages can lose meaning or tone and create misunderstandings. Online communication is also sometimes written for an audience rather than one person, making people less open. We may send far more messages without necessarily understanding each other better, especially in sensitive conversations.

Key words and phrases

sensitive conversations
Discussions where feelings or personal issues need careful handling.

Advanced Model Answer

It's made communication immediate, cheap and global, which has obvious benefits. Two effects concern me, though. Messages have become shorter, so a lot of nuance goes missing and people misread tone constantly. And a great deal of communication is now public performance rather than conversation — people write for an audience, which changes what they're willing to say. So we communicate far more often while arguably understanding each other less well.

Key words and phrases

public performance rather than conversation
acting for an audience instead of speaking sincerely
understanding each other less well
becoming less able to understand one another
