Place

A garden or park you have visited

Part 2

Prompt

Describe a garden/park you have visited

You should say: - Where it was - What it looked like - What you did there - Why you liked it there.

Simple Model Answer

A park I visit is Tao Dan Park in central Ho Chi Minh City. It is about ten minutes' walk from my workplace. It is an old park, with very large trees that provide shade even on hot days.

There are stone paths, flower beds, small lakes and a space used for events. I usually go at about six in the morning and run a few laps before work. At that time, other people are already exercising, playing chess or doing tai chi. I enjoy seeing so many different activities in one place.

One morning before an important exam, I sat on a bench and watched an older woman feeding pigeons. The quiet moment helped me feel much calmer. That is the main reason I keep returning. The park gives me a break from traffic and work without costing anything. Sometimes I exercise there, and sometimes I simply sit under a tree. Either way, I usually leave in a better mood than when I arrived.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd like to talk about Tao Dan Park, which is right in the centre of Ho Chi Minh City, just a ten-minute walk from where I work. It's actually one of the oldest parks in the city — it used to be part of the grounds of the old Governor's Palace back in the French colonial period, so some of the trees there are well over a hundred years old.

As for what it looks like, there are huge, shady trees everywhere, so even at midday it stays cool underneath them, which is rare in a city this hot. Stone paths wind between the flower beds, there are a couple of small lakes with turtles in them, and an old bandstand in the middle that gets used for events now and then. Early in the morning the whole place smells of frangipani, mixed in with birdsong and traffic noise from the streets outside.

I go there most mornings before it gets too hot, usually around six, to run a couple of laps before work. By that time the park's already full — groups of older residents doing tai chi in perfect unison, a few men playing chess under the trees, and other joggers weaving between them like me. I remember one morning last year, right before a big exam, sitting on a bench watching an elderly woman feed the pigeons, and it somehow calmed me down more than any amount of revision had.

That's really why I keep going back — the park does something to your mood that's hard to explain. However stressed I am before I get there, I always leave feeling lighter. It costs nothing, it's open to everyone, and unlike a gym or a cafe, nobody expects anything from you — you can just sit there for twenty minutes and nobody minds. That's what makes Tao Dan special, in a city that otherwise never really slows down.
