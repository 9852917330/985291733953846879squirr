Education

Will computers replace teachers?

Agree / Disagree

Prompt

Computers are now widely used in education, and some people think that teachers will no longer play an important role in the classroom. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

As computers have become central to education, some people predict that teachers will no longer play an important role in the classroom. I disagree with this view, because technology changes what teachers do rather than removing the need for them.

The argument for the prediction is understandable. A well-designed program can explain a concept as clearly as most teachers, repeat it indefinitely without impatience, and adjust its difficulty to each student. Online courses give learners in remote areas access to material that would otherwise be unavailable, and software marks tests instantly, which frees a great deal of time.

Nevertheless, these advantages address only part of what education involves. Most students, particularly younger ones, do not study consistently without someone who notices when they have stopped trying and asks why. A teacher recognises that a child is struggling because of problems at home rather than because the material is difficult, and no program can make that judgement. Motivation is also personal: pupils work hard for teachers they respect and are reluctant to disappoint, and no program can generate that.

Teaching also involves skills that software cannot assess. Discussion, argument and cooperation are learned from other people, and a computer cannot tell a student that their reasoning is superficially plausible but wrong. Experience with remote learning demonstrated this clearly, since results fell sharply and the weakest students suffered most.

In conclusion, computers are valuable tools that have changed how teachers work, but I disagree that they will make teachers unimportant. The human elements of education have become more important, not less.

Key words and phrases

without impatience
Without becoming annoyed by delays.
adjust its difficulty to each student
Make tasks easier or harder for each learner.
notices when they have stopped trying
Recognises when students stop making an effort.
reluctant to disappoint
Not wanting to let someone down.
superficially plausible but wrong
Seems believable at first but is incorrect.
