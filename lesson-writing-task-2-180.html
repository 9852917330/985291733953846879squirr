Transport

Punishing drivers, or other routes to road safety

Discuss both views + opinion

Prompt

Some people think that strict punishments for driving offences are the key to reducing traffic accidents. Others, however, believe that other measures would be more effective in improving road safety. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

Model answer

People have differing views with regard to the question of how to make our roads safer. In my view, both punishments and a range of other measures can be used together to promote better driving habits. On the one hand, strict punishments can certainly help to encourage people to drive more safely. Penalties for dangerous drivers can act as a deterrent, meaning that people avoid repeating the same offence. There are various types of driving penalty, such as small fines, licence suspension, driver awareness courses, and even prison sentences. The aim of these punishments is to show dangerous drivers that their actions have negative consequences. As a result, we would hope that drivers become more disciplined and alert, and that they follow the rules more carefully.

Several other approaches, however, do not rely on punishment at all. Education is probably the most important: a driver who has never been taught to anticipate danger will not necessarily drive more carefully simply because a fine is possible, so schools and extended driving tests could teach hazard awareness before bad habits form, rather than punishing them once the damage is done. Road design can reinforce the same goal. Speed bumps, road bends and warning signs physically slow traffic down in places where drivers might otherwise take risks, and speed cameras extend that effect to stretches of road where no officer is watching.

A different kind of solution is to reduce the number of car journeys that take place at all. When governments and local councils invest in reliable, affordable public transport, they give people a genuine alternative to driving, and a road with fewer cars on it is safer for everyone, regardless of how carefully any individual driver behaves.

In conclusion, while punishment plays a useful role in deterring dangerous drivers, I believe it works best alongside education, safer road design and investment in transport that reduces the need to drive in the first place.
