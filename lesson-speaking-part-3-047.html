Role models for children, and being a child

Part 3

Prompt

Is it important for young children to have a role model? What are the advantages and disadvantages of being a child? How do you imagine traditional relationships changing in the future?

Is it important for young children to have a role model?

Simple Model Answer

Yes, because children understand values better when they see someone practising them. A teacher or relative who works hard and treats people well can be a useful example. I would encourage several role models rather than one perfect hero. Seeing ordinary people's weaknesses also gives children a more realistic picture than a carefully managed celebrity image.

Key words and phrases

role models
People whose behaviour others admire and may try to copy.

Advanced Model Answer

Yes, because abstract values mean very little to a child until they see them embodied in a person. Watching someone they admire work hard or behave decently teaches far more than simply being told to. What I'd argue against is having a single role model, particularly a celebrity, because every public figure eventually disappoints. Several is healthier, and the most useful ones are usually people children actually know, such as a teacher or a relative — someone whose flaws are visible too, not just the polished version a celebrity presents.

Key words and phrases

embodied in a person
shown through a particular individual
flaws are visible too
people can also see that person's weaknesses

What are the advantages and disadvantages of being a child?

Simple Model Answer

Children usually have fewer financial responsibilities and more time to learn. However, they have little control over where they live, their school or how adults treat them. Their happiness depends heavily on the home around them. A stable family can make childhood enjoyable, but children may still want more say in decisions affecting their lives.

Key words and phrases

more say
Have a greater opportunity to influence a decision.

Advanced Model Answer

The advantages are freedom from responsibility and an enormous amount of time to learn — a child can spend a year becoming good at something with no financial pressure at all. The disadvantage is powerlessness. Children don't choose where they live, which school they attend or how they're treated, and their well-being depends almost entirely on the adults around them. That's why a stable home matters so much — though it's worth saying that within a stable home, plenty of children would happily trade some of that freedom for a bit more say in decisions that affect them.

Key words and phrases

powerlessness
the feeling that you cannot change what happens

How do you imagine traditional relationships changing in the future?

Simple Model Answer

I expect family roles to become more equal, with less fixed ideas about who earns money or cares for children. Families may also live further apart and rely more on technology to stay connected. However, messages cannot fully replace relaxed time together. Protecting that time will remain important even as relationships become more flexible.

Key words and phrases

flexible
Able to change to suit different needs or situations.

Advanced Model Answer

I'd expect them to become more equal and less defined by fixed roles — who earns, who cares for the children, who makes decisions. That's already well advanced in many places. Families are also more geographically scattered, so relationships increasingly have to be maintained across distance, which technology makes possible without quite replicating the real thing. What families will need to protect is unstructured time together, since messaging conspicuously fails to provide it.

Key words and phrases

less defined by fixed roles
less controlled by traditional duties for different groups
unstructured time together
time spent together without a planned activity
