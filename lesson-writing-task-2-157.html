Society

Is it ever right to lie?

Opinion

Prompt

Some people think that telling the truth is not always essential. It is necessary to tell lies sometimes. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Model answer

Honesty is essential for trust, but I do not believe that every fact must be stated in every situation. Small lies can sometimes be justified when they prevent unnecessary harm and do not deceive someone about an important decision.

The main reason to value truth is that relationships and institutions depend on reliable information. People need accurate facts when they make choices about health, money, work or family matters. A person who lies for personal gain may damage others and eventually lose credibility. For this reason, honesty should remain the normal rule rather than the exception.

Even so, complete openness can occasionally be cruel or pointless. A gentle compliment may protect someone's feelings, and a surprise party requires temporary secrecy. In more serious cases, a doctor might choose careful language when giving difficult news, although the patient should still receive the information needed to make decisions. The important test is whether the lie is intended to protect another person or merely to protect the liar. A harmless social lie should not be treated in the same way as deliberate deception for personal benefit.

There is also a risk in allowing any exception at all: the reasoning used to justify a kind lie can just as easily excuse a self-interested one. A manager who quietly delays bad news to avoid alarming staff is using the same logic as someone hiding a gambling debt from their family. The exception must therefore stay narrow, judged by its effect on the other person rather than the liar's comfort.

In conclusion, telling the truth is normally the best policy because it creates trust and accountability. Nevertheless, limited exceptions are reasonable when the issue is minor, the intention is kind and no important right is affected. This approach balances honesty with compassion.
